# Properties of Skeletal Muscles

## Length tension relationship
- Contraction of a muscle fibre
  - Shortening of muscle fibre → change in length
  - Generation of force → change in tension
- Length tension graph
  - Length compared to active tension
  - Tension depends on actin myosin overlap
- Point D
  - Overlap absent → no tension
  - Fibre over-stretched
- Point B & C
  - Optimal overlap → maximum tension (T max)
  - Plateau ~ "2μ" wide
- Point A
  - Overlap present but not optimal → less tension
  - Fibre too short, filaments crowd

## Types of tension & length
### Types of tension
- Active
  - Muscle stimulated
  - Actin myosin overlap → cross bridge → tension
- Passive
  - Muscle not stimulated
  - Titin → passive stretching / elasticity
- Total
  - Total = active + passive
### Factors affecting active tension
- Cross bridge formation
- Diameter of muscle fibre
- Length of muscle fibre
- AP frequency
- No. of motor units recruited
### Types of length
- Optimal / resting length
  - Maximum tension generated by all body muscles
- Equilibrium length
  - Muscle cut from bony attachments
### Types of muscle fibres
- Type I → small & red
  - Myoglobin high, slow myosin ATPase
  - Aerobic, O₂ storage +, lactate −
  - Fatigue resistant, slow fatigue resistant units
  - Recruited first (size principle)
  - Sitting, maintaining posture
- Type IIa → large & red
  - Myoglobin high, fast ATPase
  - Aerobic or anaerobic (glycolysis), lactate +
  - Fatigue resistant, fast fatigue resistant units
  - Recruited second → standing
- Type IIb → large & white
  - Myoglobin absent, fast ATPase, O₂ storage −
  - Anaerobic glycolysis → lactate → pain, fatigue
  - Fatigue prone, fast fatiguable units
  - Recruited last → running

## Motor unit
- Single α motor neuron + fibres it innervates
- Fine precise movements
  - Extraocular muscles → 3–6 fibres per unit
- Coarse movements
  - Leg / back muscles → 600 fibres per unit

## Muscle heat
- Produced during contraction & relaxation
- Resting heat → basal metabolic rate
- Contraction heat
  - Activation heat → activation of actin & myosin
  - Shortening heat → overlap of actin & myosin
- Relaxation heat
  - Relaxation proper → isotonic, no tension change
  - Recovery heat → muscle regains original length
- ↑ resting heat
  - Lean body
  - After exercise (weight loss)

## Stimulation of a muscle
### Single muscle twitch
- Stimulus → latent period → contraction → relaxation
- Latent period → after stimulation, before contraction
- Contraction → end of latent period to peak
- Relaxation → after contraction
### Incomplete tetanus
- ↑ frequency of stimulus → ↑ frequency of contraction
- Incomplete relaxation → rippled trace
### Complete tetanus
- ↑↑↑ frequency → sustained contraction, no relaxation
- Due to Ca²⁺ ion accumulation
- Eg: tetanospasmin (Clostridium tetani)
  - → spastic contracted state
  - → body bends like a bow (opisthotonus)
### Tetanising frequency
- Frequency at which complete tetanus is produced
- T_f = 1 / contraction period

## Fibrillation vs fasciculation
- Fibrillation
  - Single muscle fibre
  - Single motor unit (α motor neuron or its axons)
- Fasciculation
  - Multiple muscle fibres
  - Multiple motor units
