# 🖐️ Sensory Receptors

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> A **sensory receptor**[^ety-receptor][^pre-re] is a transducer: it turns one kind of energy (touch, heat, chemicals, light) into action potentials in an **afferent**[^ety-afferent][^pre-ad] fibre, which a chain of neurons carries to the cortex. The chapter answers:
> - ✅ **What** is sensed and **by which receptor** (mechano-, noci-, thermo-, proprio- …; Meissner, Merkel, Pacinian, Ruffini; TRPM vs TRPV)?
> - ✅ **How** does a receptor code a stimulus — **MILD**: Modality, Intensity, Location, Duration?
> - ✅ How is **pain** carried (Aδ fast vs C slow), amplified (hyperalgesia) and suppressed (local anaesthetics, endogenous analgesia, gate control)?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — Sensors, Cables and a Volume Knob

Think of the body as a building wired with alarms. Each **receptor** is a sensor tuned to one thing (smoke, heat, a door opening — **modality**). It reports how strong the signal is by how **fast it beeps**, not how loud (**intensity = frequency**, all-or-none). Where the beep came from depends on how many sensors cover each room (**receptive field, receptor density, two-point discrimination**). Some sensors beep only when the door opens and closes (**rapidly adapting**), others keep beeping while it stays open (**slowly adapting**). Pain has its own **volume knob** in the spinal cord: inflammation turns it up (**hyperalgesia**), touch/TENS and the brain's own opioids turn it down (**gate control, endogenous analgesia**).

```
STIMULUS → RECEPTOR (transducer) → 1st-order neuron → spinal cord (2nd) → thalamus (3rd, relay) → cortex
CODING  = MILD: Modality · Intensity (∝ AP frequency) · Location (receptive field, 2-point) · Duration
TOUCH   = Aβ (Meissner tap · Merkel Braille · Pacinian high-freq vibration · Ruffini stretch)
PAIN    = Aδ fast (glutamate, neospinothalamic) · C slow (substance P, paleospinothalamic)
          ↑ by algogens (bradykinin most potent) · ↓ by LA (Na⁺ channel), PAG opioids/cannabinoids, gate (Aβ)
TEMP    = cold (Aδ + C, TRPM, 10–24 °C) ≫ warm (C, TRPV, 30–45 °C)
ADAPT   = rapid/phasic (Meissner, Pacinian) vs slow/tonic (Merkel, Ruffini, free nerve endings)
```

🧠 The first four name receptors by **what energy** they sense; the last three name them by **where the stimulus comes from** — outside the body, inside it, or far away.[^4]

### Properties — mnemonic **MILD**

1. **Modality**[^ety-modality]: **specific sense activating a receptor.**
2. **Intensity**[^ety-intensity]: **intensity ∝ frequency of action potential** (**amplitude remains constant: all or none law**).[^5]
3. **Location:**
   - **Receptive field**: **area where the stimulus activates the receptor.**
   - **Receptor density**: **number of receptors in a receptive field.**
   - **Two-point discrimination**[^ety-discrimination][^pre-dis]: **minimum distance between 2 points at which they can be seen [felt] separately.**
     - **Receptor density ∝ 1 / two-point discrimination.**
     - **Two-point discrimination: maximum at lips, fingertips; minimum at back region.**[^6]
4. **Duration**: **time gap between start & end of receptor response.**[^7]

### Pain

- **Receptors: Free nerve endings of Aδ & C fibers.**

| | **Aδ fibers (myelinated)** | **C fibers (unmyelinated)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Causes** | **1st pain / fast pain / epicritic**[^ety-epicritic][^pre-epi] **pain** | **2nd pain / slow pain / protopathic**[^ety-protopathic][^pre-proto] **pain** |
| **Pathway** | **Neospinothalamic**[^pre-neo] | **Paleospinothalamic**[^pre-paleo] |
| **Neurotransmitter** | **Glutamate** | **Substance P**[^ety-substance-p] |

🧠 Stub your toe: the sharp, well-localised "ouch" first (Aδ, fast, myelinated), then a dull, spreading ache a second later (C, slow, unmyelinated).[^10]

#### Somatic pain — 1. Hyperalgesia[^ety-hyperalgesia][^pre-hyper][^suf-algesia]

- **Seen in inflammatory conditions.**
- **Algogen**[^ety-algogen][^suf-gen] **→ chemicals that produce pain:** **prostaglandin**[^ety-prostaglandin], **serotonin**, **substance P**, **histamine**, **bradykinin**[^ety-bradykinin][^pre-brady] (**most potent**).

```
Algogens: prostaglandin · serotonin · substance P · histamine · bradykinin (most potent)
        ↓
Free nerve endings (FNE)
        ↓
Sensitization
        ↓
↓ threshold
        ↓
↑ firing at FNE
        ↓
Pain (hyperalgesia)
```

- **Sensitization**[^ety-sensitization] lowers the receptor's threshold, so a stimulus that would normally hurt a little now hurts a lot.[^11]

> [!note] Note — Allodynia
> **Allodynia**[^ety-allodynia][^pre-allo]: **non-nociceptive stimulus → perceived as pain.**[^12]

#### Clinical aspect

**1. Local anaesthetics**[^ety-anaesthetic][^pre-an] **(eg: lignocaine**[^ety-lignocaine][^suf-caine]**):**

```
Local anaesthetic
      ↓ blocks
Voltage-gated Na⁺ channel  (on the free nerve ending, FNE)
      ↓
No action potential from the FNE
      ↓
Analgesia
```

*Local anaesthetics (eg: lignocaine) block the voltage-gated Na⁺ channel on the free nerve ending (FNE) → analgesia (cropped from the handwritten page).*[^13]

**2. Congenital**[^ety-congenital][^pre-con] **insensitivity to pain syndrome (CIPS):** **congenital absence of voltage-gated Na⁺ channel.**[^14]

#### Analgesia[^ety-analgesia]

| Type | **Endogenous**[^ety-endogenous][^pre-endo][^suf-genous] **opioid**[^ety-opioid][^suf-oid] **analgesic system** | **Stress induced analgesic system** |
|---|---|---|
| **Example** | **Acupuncture**[^ety-acupuncture] **therapy** | **Fire walking** |
| **MOA** | **Activation of periaqueductal**[^ety-periaqueductal][^pre-peri] **gray matter (PAG) → release endogenous morphines (eg: endorphins, enkephalins, dynorphins**[^ety-endorphin]**)** | **Activation of PAG (d/t stress) → release of endogenous cannabinoids**[^ety-cannabinoid] **(eg: anandamide**[^ety-anandamide]**)** |
| **Result** | **Pain relief** | **Pain relief** |

```
Acupuncture → PAG → endorphins, enkephalins, dynorphins ─┐
                                                         ├─► PAIN RELIEF
Stress (fire walking) → PAG → endocannabinoids (anandamide)┘
```

🧠 Both systems start in the same place — the PAG — and both end in pain relief; they differ only in the chemical released (opioid peptides vs endocannabinoids).[^15]

#### Visceral pain[^ety-visceral]

- **Unpleasant, poorly localized, referred**[^ety-referred] **pain.**
- **Associated with nausea**[^ety-nausea]**, vomiting & sweating.**
- **Conveyed via C fibers.**
- **Structures:**

| **Pain sensitive** | **Pain insensitive** |
|---|---|
| **Liver capsule** | **Alveoli**[^ety-alveoli] |
| **Parietal pleura**[^ety-pleura] | — |

🧠 The organ's own working tissue (alveoli) feels nothing, but its coverings (liver capsule, parietal pleura) are richly supplied with pain fibres — so stretching or inflaming the covering hurts.[^17]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> **A receptor is a specialised transducer, and the brain trusts the line, not the message.** Each receptor is tuned to one modality; it reports intensity only by firing frequency (the AP itself is all-or-none), location by which fibre fires (receptive field, receptor density), and duration by how it adapts (phasic vs tonic). The fibre it uses decides speed: Aβ for touch, Aδ for sharp pain and cold, C for slow pain and warmth.
>
> Pain is the one sense with a built-in volume control: algogens and sensitisation turn it up (hyperalgesia, allodynia), while Na⁺-channel blockers, the PAG's endogenous opioids/cannabinoids and Aβ-driven inhibitory interneurons (the gate) turn it down — and pain receptors themselves adapt slowly, so the warning keeps sounding.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Mechanoreceptors code touch through Aβ fibres; free nerve endings of Aδ and C fibres respond only to damaging stimuli (first sharp, then dull pain); cold and warm receptors fire within their ranges; phasic receptors report change, tonic ones report steady states.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Tissue injury releases algogens (bradykinin, prostaglandins, histamine, serotonin, substance P) → nociceptors sensitise → threshold falls → hyperalgesia; the body counters with PAG-driven endogenous opioids/cannabinoids (stress-induced analgesia) and the patient rubs the area (gate control).
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Persistent sensitisation gives allodynia (touch or clothing hurts); visceral disease gives poorly localised, referred pain with nausea and sweating; absence of the Na⁺ channel (CIPS) removes pain entirely, so children injure themselves without knowing.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → injury / inflammation → algogen release → sensitisation (↓ threshold) → hyperalgesia → allodynia / chronic pain — or, at the other extreme, congenital absence of Na⁺ channels → no pain → repeated unnoticed injuries
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> NSAIDs remove prostaglandin sensitisation, local anaesthetics block the Na⁺ channel, opioids mimic the PAG system, and TENS/massage exploit the gate — each targets one step of the same chain.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Reading "two-point discrimination is maximum at the fingertips" as "largest distance"**
>
> **What students think:** Maximum two-point discrimination means the two points must be farthest apart at the fingertips.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** "Maximum discrimination" means the **best ability** to tell two points apart — the threshold **distance is smallest** (a few mm) at lips and fingertips because receptor density is highest; on the back the points must be several centimetres apart. Receptor density ∝ 1 / two-point (threshold) distance.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Ability ↑ ↔ distance ↓ ↔ density ↑.
>
> **Trap 2: Intensity is coded by AP size**
>
> **What students think:** A stronger stimulus produces bigger action potentials.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** APs obey the all-or-none law — amplitude stays constant. Intensity is coded by **frequency** of APs (and by recruiting more receptors), as the page states: intensity ∝ frequency.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Louder = faster beeps, not louder beeps."
>
> **Trap 3: Mixing up the fast and slow pain columns**
>
> **What students think:** C fibres carry sharp pain because they are "first" alphabetically or because they are many.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Aδ fibres are myelinated → fast, first, epicritic pain via the neospinothalamic tract with glutamate; C fibres are unmyelinated → slow, second, protopathic pain via the paleospinothalamic tract with substance P. Visceral pain and warmth are C-fibre senses.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "δ = dart (sharp, fast); C = crawl (slow, dull)."
>
> **Trap 4: Endogenous opioid vs stress-induced analgesia**
>
> **What students think:** Both release morphine-like peptides.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Both start in the PAG, but acupuncture-type (endogenous opioid) analgesia releases endorphins, enkephalins and dynorphins, whereas stress-induced analgesia (fire walking) releases endocannabinoids such as anandamide.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Needle → opioid; fire → cannabinoid."
>
> **Trap 5: Adaptation of pain receptors**
>
> **What students think:** All free nerve endings adapt quickly, so pain fades on its own.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Free nerve endings are listed with the **slowly adapting (tonic)** receptors — pain keeps signalling as long as the damage lasts, which is its protective purpose. Pacinian and Meissner corpuscles are the rapid (phasic) ones.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Pain persists; Pacinian passes."

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Neurophysiology = **sensory physiology + motor physiology + higher mental functions**; ascending sensory = afferents/inputs, descending motor = efferents/outputs.
> 2. **General senses** (touch, pain, temperature) — receptor in **skin**; **special senses** (vision, hearing, smell, taste, balance) — receptor in **CNS** (page).
> 3. Pathway: **receptor (1st order) → spinal cord (2nd, spinothalamic) → brainstem → thalamus (3rd, relay station) → cerebral cortex (4th, page)**.
> 4. Classification: **mechano** (touch), **noci** (pain), **thermo** (hot & cold), **proprio** (joint position), **extero** (skin, external), **intero** (osmoreceptors), **tele** (photoreceptors).
> 5. **MILD**: **M**odality · **I**ntensity ∝ AP **frequency** (amplitude constant, all-or-none) · **L**ocation (receptive field, receptor density ∝ 1/two-point discrimination) · **D**uration.
> 6. **Two-point discrimination best at lips and fingertips, poorest on the back.**
> 7. Touch 1st-order neuron = **Aβ (large, myelinated)**. **Meissner** — superficial, lips/fingertips, tap, **low-frequency vibration 5–40 Hz**; **Merkel** — superficial, edges/corners, **Braille**, sustained pressure; **Pacinian (largest)** — deep, pressure, **high-frequency vibration 50–500 Hz**; **Ruffini** — deep, **joint capsule**, skin stretch.
> 8. Pain receptors = **free nerve endings of Aδ & C**. **Aδ** (myelinated) — fast/first/epicritic — neospinothalamic — **glutamate**; **C** (unmyelinated) — slow/second/protopathic — paleospinothalamic — **substance P**.
> 9. **Hyperalgesia** (inflammation): algogens (prostaglandin, serotonin, substance P, histamine, **bradykinin — most potent**) → sensitisation → ↓ threshold → ↑ firing. **Allodynia** = non-nociceptive stimulus felt as pain.
> 10. **Local anaesthetics (lignocaine) block voltage-gated Na⁺ channels**; **CIPS = congenital absence of voltage-gated Na⁺ channel**.
> 11. Analgesia: **endogenous opioid** (acupuncture → PAG → endorphins, enkephalins, dynorphins) vs **stress-induced** (fire walking → PAG → endocannabinoids, anandamide).
> 12. **Massage & TENS → gate control theory** — Aβ fibres excite an inhibitory interneuron that blocks the projection neuron in the substantia gelatinosa.
> 13. **Visceral pain** — poorly localised, referred, with nausea/vomiting/sweating, via **C fibres**; **liver capsule & parietal pleura sensitive, alveoli insensitive**.
> 14. Temperature: **cold receptors ≫ warm**; cold = Aδ + C, **TRPM**, 10–24 °C (nociception < 5 °C); warm = C, **TRPV**, 30–45 °C (> 45 °C); **body temperature → TRPV3**.
> 15. **RARs = phasic (Meissner, Pacinian); SARs = tonic (Merkel, Ruffini, free nerve endings).**

[^5]: Modality is preserved because each receptor connects to its own fibre and cortical area — the *labelled line principle* (Müller's law of specific nerve energies): pressing on the eye is "seen" as light. [^7]: Duration is coded by how long the receptor keeps firing — which depends on its adaptation (see *Adaptation of receptors* below).
[^8]: Aβ fibres (Erlanger–Gasser class) are about 5–12 µm in diameter and conduct at roughly 30–70 m/s ([[Characteristics of Nerve Fibres]]).
[^9]: Meissner corpuscles sit in the dermal papillae of hairless skin and are rapidly adapting (flutter); Merkel discs at the base of the epidermis are slowly adapting; Pacinian corpuscles, deep in the dermis and subcutis, have onion-like lamellae that make them the fastest-adapting receptors (best response ~250 Hz — the 128/256 Hz tuning fork test); Ruffini endings are slowly adapting stretch receptors in the dermis and joint capsules.
[^10]: Guyton: Aδ pain fibres conduct at 6–30 m/s and C fibres at 0.5–2 m/s. C-fibre terminals release glutamate as well as substance P.
[^11]: Prostaglandins and substance P mainly **sensitise** nociceptors rather than activate them (the reason NSAIDs relieve inflammatory pain); bradykinin both activates and sensitises. *Primary* hyperalgesia occurs at the injured site (peripheral sensitisation); *secondary* hyperalgesia in the surrounding normal skin (central sensitisation in the dorsal horn).
[^12]: Classic examples of allodynia: sunburnt skin that hurts under clothing, post-herpetic neuralgia, and migraine scalp tenderness.
[^13]: Local anaesthetics enter the axon in the uncharged form and block the Na⁺ channel from inside in the charged form, preferentially when channels are open (use-dependent block). [^14]: The channel lost in CIPS is Nav1.7 (gene *SCN9A*), expressed in nociceptors; loss-of-function mutations abolish pain, whereas gain-of-function mutations cause erythromelalgia and paroxysmal extreme pain disorder.
[^15]: Guyton's analgesia system: PAG and periventricular grey → nucleus raphe magnus (serotonin) → enkephalin-releasing interneurons of the dorsal horn → presynaptic inhibition of incoming pain fibres. The opioid peptides act on μ, δ and κ receptors; anandamide acts on CB1 receptors. Acupuncture analgesia is reduced by the opioid antagonist naloxone.
[^16]: Proposed by Ronald Melzack and Patrick Wall in *Science*, 1965. The substantia gelatinosa is lamina II of the dorsal horn.
[^17]: Visceral pain is caused by ischaemia, chemicals, spasm and over-distension of hollow organs — cutting or burning the gut is painless. It is referred to skin of the same dermatome because visceral and somatic afferents converge on the same dorsal-horn neurons. The liver parenchyma, visceral pleura and lung parenchyma are insensitive; the liver capsule and parietal pleura (intercostal and phrenic nerves) are pain-sensitive.
[^18]: Guyton: there are 3 to 10 times as many cold receptors as warm receptors in most skin areas. TRPM8 is the menthol/cool receptor; TRPV1 the capsaicin/noxious-heat receptor (> ~43 °C); TRPV3 and TRPV4 respond to warm temperatures around normal body temperature; TRPA1 to noxious cold. [^pre-photo]: **photo-** — light: photoreceptor, photophobia, phototherapy. Don't confuse with **phono-** sound · **phospho-** phosphate (literally "light-bearer").
[^pre-dis]: **dis- / di-** — apart, away: discrimination, dissociation, dilate. Don't confuse with **dys-** bad, difficult · **di-** two (Greek) · **dia-** through.
[^suf-ated]: **-ated / -ate (verb, adjective)** — provided with, having been made: myelinated, saturated, innervated. Don't confuse with **-ate** a salt (glutamate) · **-ase** an enzyme.
[^suf-cle]: **-cle / -cule / -culus** — small (Latin diminutive): corpuscle, molecule, fasciculus, follicle. Don't confuse with **-cele** a swelling or hernia · **-coele** a cavity.
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, above: epicritic, epidermis, epidural. Don't confuse with **endo-** within · **hypo-** below.
[^pre-proto]: **proto-** — first, primitive: protopathic, protoplasm, protozoa. Don't confuse with **pro-** before, forward · **proprio-** one's own.
[^pre-neo]: **neo-** — new: neospinothalamic, neocortex, neonate, neoplasm. Don't confuse with **paleo-** old · **neuro-** nerve.
[^pre-paleo]: **paleo- / palaeo-** — old, ancient: paleospinothalamic, paleocortex, paleocerebellum. Don't confuse with **neo-** new · **archi-** first, most primitive (archicerebellum).
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, excessive: hyperalgesia, hyperaesthesia, hyperpolarisation. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra- / super-** above (Latin).
[^suf-algesia]: **-algesia / -algia** — pain, sense of pain: hyperalgesia, analgesia, neuralgia, myalgia. Don't confuse with **-odynia** pain (allodynia — Greek synonym) · **-aesthesia** sensation in general.
[^suf-gen]: **-gen / -genic** — producer, producing: algogen, pathogen, allergen, pyrogen. Don't confuse with **-genous** born in / produced in (endogenous) · **-gene** a unit of heredity.
[^pre-brady]: **brady-** — slow: bradykinin, bradycardia, bradykinesia. Don't confuse with **brachy-** short · **tachy-** fast.
[^pre-allo]: **allo-** — other, different: allodynia, allograft, allosteric. Don't confuse with **auto-** self · **homo-** same · **hetero-** other (near-synonym).
[^pre-an]: **a- / an-** — without, not: anaesthetic, analgesia, anhidrosis. Don't confuse with **ana-** up, again (anabolism) · **ante-** before · **anti-** against.
[^suf-caine]: **-caine** — the suffix for local anaesthetics, taken from *cocaine* (Spanish *coca* + *-ine*): lignocaine, bupivacaine, procaine. Don't confuse with **-cain** in unrelated words · **-azepam** benzodiazepines.
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with: congenital (born with), conduction, convergence. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** against.
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endogenous, endorphin, endocannabinoid. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside · **ecto-** outer · **en-** in (enkephalin).
[^suf-genous]: **-genous** — produced in, born of: endogenous, exogenous, myogenous. Don't confuse with **-genic** producing (algogenic) · **-genesis** origin.
[^suf-oid]: **-oid** — like, resembling: opioid, cannabinoid, vanilloid, steroid. Don't confuse with **-ode / -ade** · **-oma** a tumour.
[^pre-peri]: **peri-** — around: periaqueductal, perineurium, peristalsis. Don't confuse with **para-** beside · **pre-** before.
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: transcutaneous, transduction, transmission. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **dia-** through (Greek).
