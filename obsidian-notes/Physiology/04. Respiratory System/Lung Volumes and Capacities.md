# 📊 Lung Volumes and Capacities

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The air in the lungs is divided into **four volumes**[^ety-volume] (IRV, TV, ERV, RV) that never overlap, and **capacities**[^ety-capacity] that are sums of two or more volumes (IC, FRC, VC, TLC). They are measured in two ways:
> - ✅ **Time independent = static volumes** (e.g. tidal volume) — how much air; measured by **spirometry**, except the **residual volume**, which needs **body plethysmography**.
> - ✅ **Time dependent = dynamic volumes** (e.g. **FEV₁**) — how fast the air comes out; the **FEV₁/FVC (Tiffeneau–Pinelli) index** and the **flow volume loop** separate **obstructive** from **restrictive** lung disease.

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Tank with Four Layers and a Tap

Think of the lungs as a tank with four stacked layers of air. You can tip out the top three (IRV, TV, ERV) through the tap — that is the **vital capacity**, and a spirometer measures it. The bottom layer (**RV**) never leaves, so the spirometer can never see it — you need a sealed **body box** and Boyle's law to weigh it. Dynamic tests time the tap: a **narrow tap** (obstruction) empties a full tank slowly (FEV₁/FVC ↓); a **small tank** (restriction) empties quickly but holds little (ratio normal or ↑).

```
VOLUMES (never overlap)                     CAPACITIES (sums of volumes)
1. IRV  inspiratory reserve   2–3 L         IC   = IRV + TV
2. TV   tidal volume          500 mL        FRC  = ERV + RV = TLC/2 = 2.5 L   (equilibrium volume)
3. ERV  expiratory reserve    1.3 L         VC   = IRV + TV + ERV
4. RV   residual volume       1.2 L         TLC  = IRV + TV + ERV + RV = 5 L
   ↳ not measured by spirometry → body plethysmography (Boyle P ∝ 1/V)

DYNAMIC: FVC (deep breath → fast, forceful blow for 6 s) · FEV1 = 70–80 % of FVC
OBSTRUCTIVE  FEV1 ↓ / FVC (↓ less) = ratio ↓↓ · loop scooped out (dog-leg)
RESTRICTIVE  FEV1 ↓ / FVC ↓ = ratio normal (early) → FVC ↓↓ = ratio ↑↑ (late) · loop = miniature normal
```

## 🫁 Static Lung Volume & Capacities

![[Lung Volumes and Capacities - spirogram of static lung volumes and capacities.png]]
*Spirogram, as on the page — volume in lung against time: quiet breaths between 2500 and 3000 mL (**normal expiration / normal inspiration**), then a **maximum inspiration** to 6000 mL and a **maximum expiration** to 1200 mL; the boxes beside it build the four volumes and four capacities (cropped from the handwritten page).*

**Volumes:**

| # | Volume | Value (page) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Inspiratory**[^ety-inspiratory][^pre-in] **reserve**[^ety-reserve][^pre-re] **volume (IRV)** | **2–3 L** |
| 2 | **Tidal volume (TV)** | **500 ml** |
| 3 | **Expiratory**[^ety-expiratory][^pre-ex] **reserve volume (ERV)** | **1.3 L** |
| 4 | **Residual**[^ety-residual] **volume (RV)** | **1.2 L** |

**Capacities:**

| Capacity | Formula | Value (page) |
|---|---|---|
| **Inspiratory capacity (IC)** | **IRV + TV** | — |
| **Functional**[^ety-functional] **residual capacity (FRC) / equilibrium**[^ety-equilibrium][^pre-equi] **volume** | **= TLC/2 or ERV + RV** | **2.5 L** |
| **Vital**[^ety-vital] **capacity (VC)** | **IRV + TV + ERV** | — |
| **Total lung capacity (TLC)** | **IRV + TV + ERV + RV** | **5 L** |

> [!tip] Why FRC is called the "equilibrium volume"
> At the end of a quiet expiration no muscle is working: the lung's inward recoil exactly balances the chest wall's outward spring. The volume left at that balance point is the FRC — about half the TLC. It is the lung's resting volume and the O₂ store that buffers each breath.[^1]

**5. Closing**[^ety-closing] **volume:** volume enclosed in **lower dependent**[^ety-dependent][^pre-de] **alveoli** during the **end of expiration**.

- **Closing capacity = closing volume + residual volume.**[^2]

### Investigations[^ety-investigation]

**1. Spirometry**[^ety-spirometry][^pre-spiro][^suf-metry]**:** can **measure all lung volumes except residual volume** — **hence cannot calculate FRC & TLC.**[^3]

![[Lung Volumes and Capacities - spirometer.png]]
*A desktop spirometer with mouthpiece, as pictured on the page (cropped from a small, low-resolution photo on the handwritten page — see the prompt after the body box).*

**2. Body plethysmography**[^ety-plethysmography][^suf-graphy]**:**

- **Use: to measure RV.**
- **Principle: Boyle's law**[^ety-boyle] **P ∝ 1/V.**

![[Lung Volumes and Capacities - body plethysmograph.png]]
*Body plethysmograph ("body box") — the subject sits in a sealed, glass-walled cabin breathing through a mouthpiece with a shutter, beside its computer cart (cropped from a small, low-resolution photo on the handwritten page).*

> [!example]- 🎨 Image prompt — Spirometer and body plethysmograph
> "Clean medical-equipment illustration, white background, two panels. LEFT, titled 'Spirometry — measures all volumes except RV': a seated adult with a nose clip blowing hard into a handheld spirometer mouthpiece connected to a small desktop unit showing a volume–time curve. RIGHT, titled 'Body plethysmography — measures RV (Boyle's law P ∝ 1/V)': the same adult seated inside a closed, transparent body box breathing through a mouthpiece with a shutter, labels 'mouth pressure', 'box pressure', 'shutter'; a computer cart beside the box. Flat vector, soft blues and greys, legible labels, no brand names, no watermark."

**Interpretation (during inspiration against the closed shutter):**

| **Inspiration** | **Volume (V)** | **Pressure (P)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Lungs** | **↑** | **↓** |
| **Chamber** | **↓** | **↑** |

When the subject tries to breathe in against the closed mouthpiece, the chest enlarges, so the gas in the lungs expands (V ↑) and its pressure falls (P ↓); the same enlargement squeezes the air in the sealed chamber (V ↓, P ↑). Measuring the pressure change at the mouth for a known volume change gives the lung volume by Boyle's law.[^4]

**Formula for calculating plethysmography:**

```latex
P_{m_i} \times V_{L_i} \;=\; P_{m_f} \times \left( V_{L_i} + \Delta V \right)
```

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pmᵢ** | **Initial pressure in mouth = atmospheric**[^ety-atmospheric] **pressure = 760 mm Hg** |
| **VLᵢ** | **Initial volume in lung = FRC** |
| **Pm_f** | **Final pressure at mouth** |
| **ΔV** | **Change in volume** |

> [!example] Eg — If inspiratory effort lowers pressure at mouth by 10 mm Hg & expands gas in lung by 50 ml, calculate FRC?
> **Ans:** FRC = ?
> - Pmᵢ = 760 mm Hg · VLᵢ = FRC · Pm_f = 760 − 10 = **750 mm Hg** · ΔV = **50 ml**
> - Pmᵢ × VLᵢ = Pm_f × (VLᵢ + ΔV)
> - 760 × FRC = 750 × (FRC + 50)
> - 760 FRC = 750 FRC + 37 500
> - 10 FRC = 37 500
> - **FRC = 3750 ml**

> [!note] Note — Obsolete methods for calculating RV
> - **Helium**[^ety-helium] **dilution**[^ety-dilution][^pre-di] **technique.**
> - **Nitrogen**[^ety-nitrogen] **washout technique.**[^5]

## ⏱️ Dynamic Lung Volume & Capacities

**1. Forced vital capacity (FVC) estimation:**

```
Deep inspiration ──── followed by ────→ Fast & forceful expiration in 6 sec
```

**2. Forced expiratory volume in first second (FEV₁):**

- **70–80 % of air expired in first second** in healthy individuals.
- **Tiffeneau–Pinelli**[^ety-tiffeneau] **index:**

```latex
\frac{FEV_1}{FVC} = 70\text{–}80\ \%
```

- **Use: to differentiate obstructive**[^ety-obstruction][^pre-ob] **(difficulty in expiration) & restrictive**[^ety-restriction] **(difficulty in inspiration) lung diseases.**[^6]

![[Lung Volumes and Capacities - obstructive vs restrictive FEV1 FVC ratio.png]]
*FEV₁/FVC in the two patterns, as on the page — **obstructive** (emphysema: normal alveoli vs alveoli with emphysema): FEV₁ ↓ / FVC = **ratio ↓↓**; **restrictive** (stiff, thick-walled lung): **early stage** FEV₁ ↓ / FVC ↓ = **ratio normal**; **late stage** FEV₁ ↓ / FVC ↓↓ = **ratio ↑↑** (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Pattern | FEV₁ | FVC | FEV₁/FVC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Obstructive** (e.g. **emphysema**[^ety-emphysema][^pre-em]) | **↓** | — (↓ less) | **Ratio ↓↓** |
| **Restrictive — early stage** | **↓** | **↓** | **Ratio normal** |
| **Restrictive — late stage** | **↓** | **↓↓** | **Ratio ↑↑**[^7] |

> [!info] Note — **GOLD**[^ety-gold] **criteria used for COPD**[^ety-copd][^pre-chron] **are based on FEV₁/FVC.**[^8]

> [!tip] Why the flattened limb tells you where the block is
> An airway **outside** the chest is squeezed by atmospheric pressure when the pressure inside it falls — in **inspiration** — so a floppy extrathoracic obstruction flattens the **inspiratory** limb. An airway **inside** the chest is squeezed by pleural pressure when that becomes positive — in **forced expiration** — so a floppy intrathoracic obstruction flattens the **expiratory** limb. A **fixed** (rigid) narrowing limits flow in both directions.[^10]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> **Static tests ask "how big is the lung?"; dynamic tests ask "how fast can it empty?".** The four volumes stack into capacities, and FRC sits where lung recoil and chest-wall spring balance. A spirometer only sees air that moves, so RV — and therefore FRC and TLC — need Boyle's law in a body box.
>
> Disease splits along the same line. **Obstruction** narrows the tap: the lung is full (TLC, RV ↑) but empties slowly, so FEV₁ falls more than FVC (ratio ↓↓) and the flow–volume loop is scooped. **Restriction** shrinks the tank: everything is small, but what is there leaves fast, so the ratio is normal or ↑ and the loop is a miniature. Where an obstruction lies (inside or outside the chest, fixed or variable) decides which limb of the loop flattens.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> TV 500 mL, IRV 2–3 L, ERV 1.3 L, RV 1.2 L; FRC 2.5 L (= TLC/2), TLC 5 L. A forced breath empties 70–80 % of the FVC in the first second (FEV₁/FVC 70–80 %); the flow–volume loop rises steeply to PEFR and falls in a straight line to RV.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Small-airway disease (early COPD, smoking) first affects the medium and small bronchi — the descending part of the expiratory limb, "most sensitive to obstruction" — and the closing volume rises, while FEV₁/FVC may still be near normal. Early restriction lowers FEV₁ and FVC together, so the ratio stays normal and only the volumes reveal it.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Established obstruction (COPD, asthma): FEV₁/FVC < 70 %, scooped "dog-leg" loop, air trapping with ↑ RV, ↑ FRC, ↑ TLC — GOLD staging by FEV₁. Established restriction (fibrosis): ↓ TLC, ↓ FVC more than FEV₁ → ratio ↑↑, miniature loop. Upper-airway lesions flatten one or both limbs.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal volumes and flows → trigger (smoking, allergen, fibrosis, airway lesion) → small-airway or volume change (↑ closing volume / ↓ VC) → compensation (ratio still normal) → overt obstructive (ratio ↓↓) or restrictive (ratio normal → ↑↑) pattern → respiratory failure
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> The pattern decides the next test and treatment: an obstructive ratio → bronchodilator reversibility (asthma vs COPD) and GOLD staging; a restrictive pattern → confirm with TLC by body plethysmography and look for interstitial or chest-wall disease; a flattened loop → laryngoscopy or bronchoscopy for a fixed or variable airway lesion.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Obstructive lung disease (COPD, asthma, emphysema):** "FEV₁/FVC < 70 %", "scooped out / dog leg loop", "↑ RV, ↑ TLC", "GOLD criteria" → narrowed or collapsing airways slow expiration.
>
> **Restrictive lung disease (fibrosis/ILD):** "FEV₁ and FVC both ↓, ratio normal or ↑", "miniature version of normal loop", "↓ TLC" → small, stiff lungs.
>
> **Variable extrathoracic obstruction:** "Flattened inspiratory limb", "sleep apnoea", "vocal cord palsy" → the extrathoracic airway collapses in inspiration.
>
> **Variable intrathoracic obstruction:** "Flattened expiratory limb", "foreign body in a bronchus", "tracheomalacia" → the intrathoracic airway is compressed in expiration.
>
> **Fixed upper airway obstruction:** "Both limbs flattened — a box-shaped loop", "tracheal stenosis" → rigid narrowing limits flow both ways.
>
> **Spirometry limit:** "Cannot measure RV, FRC or TLC" → needs body plethysmography (or helium dilution / nitrogen washout).
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Flow–volume loop shapes: tall triangle over a semicircle (normal), scooped expiratory limb shifted left (obstructive), small narrow loop shifted right (restrictive), plateau in one or both limbs (upper airway obstruction).
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> FEV₁/FVC normal 70–80 %; GOLD grades COPD by post-bronchodilator FEV₁ % predicted; RV 1.2 L, FRC 2.5 L, TLC 5 L on the page.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "Spirometry measures FRC"**
>
> **What students think:** FRC = ERV + RV, and ERV is on the spirogram, so spirometry gives FRC.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** RV never leaves the lung, so a spirometer cannot measure it — and every capacity containing RV (FRC, TLC) is out of reach. Body plethysmography (Boyle's law) or gas dilution is needed.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "If it contains RV, the spirometer can't see it."
>
> **Trap 2: A low FEV₁ means obstruction**
>
> **What students think:** FEV₁ ↓ = obstructive disease.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** FEV₁ falls in both patterns. The **ratio** decides: obstruction drops FEV₁ much more than FVC (ratio ↓↓); restriction drops both (ratio normal) or FVC more (ratio ↑↑ late).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Never read FEV₁ alone — read FEV₁/FVC first, then the volumes.
>
> **Trap 3: Volume vs capacity**
>
> **What students think:** FRC and VC are "volumes".
>
> **Why this is wrong:** There are only four volumes (IRV, TV, ERV, RV) and they never overlap; a capacity is a sum of two or more volumes (IC, FRC, VC, TLC).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Capacity contains volumes; volumes contain nothing."
>
> **Trap 4: Swapping intrathoracic and extrathoracic loops**
>
> **What students think:** Any upper-airway lesion flattens the expiratory limb.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** A **variable extrathoracic** lesion (sleep apnoea, vocal cord palsy) collapses in **inspiration** — inspiratory limb flattened; a **variable intrathoracic** lesion collapses in **expiration** — expiratory limb flattened; **fixed** lesions flatten both.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**E**xtrathoracic flattens **E**ntry (inspiration); **I**ntrathoracic flattens the **I**-am-leaving limb (expiration)."
>
> **Trap 5: Reading the plethysmography table backwards**
>
> **What students think:** In inspiration both the lung and the box gain volume.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The sealed box has a fixed total volume: when the chest enlarges, the gas in the lungs expands (V ↑, P ↓) and the box air is compressed (V ↓, P ↑) — the two are mirror images, which is exactly what Boyle's law uses.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Lungs ↑V ↓P · chamber ↓V ↑P."

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **Time independent = static** (tidal volume); **time dependent = dynamic** (FEV₁).
> 2. Volumes: **IRV 2–3 L, TV 500 mL, ERV 1.3 L, RV 1.2 L.**
> 3. Capacities: **IC = IRV + TV**; **FRC = ERV + RV = TLC/2 = 2.5 L (equilibrium volume)**; **VC = IRV + TV + ERV**; **TLC = IRV + TV + ERV + RV = 5 L**.
> 4. **Closing volume** = volume in lower dependent alveoli at the end of expiration; **closing capacity = closing volume + RV**.
> 5. **Spirometry measures all volumes except RV → cannot calculate FRC & TLC.**
> 6. **Body plethysmography measures RV** — principle **Boyle's law P ∝ 1/V**; in inspiration lungs V ↑ P ↓, chamber V ↓ P ↑.
> 7. **Pmᵢ × VLᵢ = Pm_f × (VLᵢ + ΔV)**; Pmᵢ = 760 mm Hg, VLᵢ = FRC → worked example **FRC = 3750 mL**.
> 8. Other (page: "obsolete") methods for RV: **helium dilution, nitrogen washout**.
> 9. **FVC** = deep inspiration → fast & forceful expiration in **6 s**; **FEV₁ = 70–80 % of FVC** = **Tiffeneau–Pinelli index**.
> 10. **Obstructive: ratio ↓↓**; **restrictive: ratio normal (early), ↑↑ (late)**; **GOLD criteria for COPD are based on FEV₁/FVC**.
> 11. Flow–volume loop: **FVC = TLC − RV**, **PEFR** at the peak; trachea = early peak, **medium & small bronchi = most sensitive to obstruction**.
> 12. **Obstructive = scooped out / dog leg pattern**; **restrictive = miniature version of normal loop**.
> 13. **Variable intrathoracic** (foreign body) → expiratory limb flat; **variable extrathoracic** (sleep apnoea) → inspiratory limb flat; **fixed UAO** → both flat.

```
Closing capacity = closing volume + RV · spirometry ✗ RV/FRC/TLC → body box (Boyle): 760·FRC = 750·(FRC+50) → 3750 mL
FVC: deep breath → forced blow 6 s · FEV1/FVC (Tiffeneau–Pinelli) 70–80 % · GOLD (COPD) uses FEV1/FVC
OBSTRUCTIVE ratio ↓↓, scooped/dog-leg loop · RESTRICTIVE ratio normal → ↑↑, miniature loop
UAO: variable extrathoracic (OSA) = insp flat · variable intrathoracic (foreign body) = exp flat · fixed = both flat
```

- Ganong's Review of Medical Physiology — Lung volumes, spirometry and flow–volume curves
- West's Respiratory Physiology: The Essentials — Measurement of lung volumes; forced expiration
- ATS/ERS standardisation of spirometry; GOLD report (Global Initiative for Chronic Obstructive Lung Disease)
- `Lung Volumes and Capacities - ….png` files in `attachments/` are crops of the handwritten pages

[^1]: Guyton's adult male values: TV 500 mL, IRV 3000 mL, ERV 1100 mL, RV 1200 mL; IC 3500 mL, FRC 2300 mL, VC 4600 mL, TLC 5800 mL. Volumes are about 20–25 % smaller in women and fall supine. The page's values (IRV 2–3 L, ERV 1.3 L, FRC 2.5 L, TLC 5 L) are kept in the body.
[^2]: Closing volume is measured by the single-breath nitrogen test (the start of "phase IV"). It rises with age, smoking and small-airway disease; closing capacity exceeds FRC in the supine position at about 44 years and upright at about 65 years, so dependent airways close during quiet breathing — one reason PaO₂ falls with age.
[^3]: Spirometry also gives FVC, FEV₁, PEFR and FEF₂₅–₇₅ (the mid-expiratory flow, a marker of small-airway disease). RV/TLC (normally ~25 %) needs a body box or gas dilution.
[^4]: The subject pants or makes an inspiratory effort against a closed shutter at FRC. With no airflow, mouth pressure equals alveolar pressure, so Boyle's law (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ at constant temperature) applied to mouth pressure and the box-derived volume change gives the thoracic gas volume. The plethysmograph measures **all** gas in the thorax, including gas trapped behind closed airways.
[^5]: Helium dilution and nitrogen washout are still widely used to measure **FRC** (RV = FRC − ERV); the page calls them obsolete, which is kept in the body. They measure only the gas that communicates with the airways, so in air trapping (COPD, bullae) they **underestimate** FRC compared with plethysmography — the difference is the trapped gas.
[^6]: ATS/ERS criteria: exhalation should last at least 6 s (or reach a plateau). Obstruction = FEV₁/FVC below 0.70 (or below the lower limit of normal); restriction is confirmed by a reduced **TLC** (< 80 % predicted), not by spirometry alone.
[^7]: In late fibrosis FVC falls more than FEV₁ and the stiff lung's increased radial traction holds the airways wide open, so expiration is fast and FEV₁/FVC can exceed 80–90 % — the page's "ratio ↑↑".
[^8]: GOLD: a **post-bronchodilator FEV₁/FVC < 0.70** confirms persistent airflow limitation (COPD); severity of airflow limitation is then graded by FEV₁ % predicted — GOLD 1 ≥ 80 %, GOLD 2 50–79 %, GOLD 3 30–49 %, GOLD 4 < 30 %.
[^9]: The first part of the expiratory limb (up to PEFR, about the first third of the FVC) is **effort-dependent** and reflects the large airways (trachea); the rest is **effort-independent**, limited by dynamic compression of the medium and small airways — so it is the part that shows obstruction earliest. Normal adult PEFR is roughly 400–600 L/min (about 7–10 L/s).
[^10]: Examples: variable **extrathoracic** — vocal cord paralysis, laryngomalacia, obstructive sleep apnoea; variable **intrathoracic** — tracheomalacia, a tumour of the lower trachea or main bronchus; **fixed** — post-intubation tracheal stenosis, goitre compressing the trachea. The page's "LAO" for the intrathoracic type most likely means *lower airway obstruction*; standard texts call it a variable intrathoracic (upper) airway obstruction.
[^11]: Read the curves on a volume axis that runs from TLC on the left to RV on the right: the obstructive curve sits to the left (hyperinflation — larger TLC and RV), the restrictive curve to the right (small lungs) but with a steep descent because recoil is high.
[^ety-volume]: **volume** — Latin *volumen*, "a roll, a scroll" (*volvere*, to roll) → the bulk of a rolled book, then the space anything occupies. **Differentiate:** *volume* (one undivided compartment of lung air) vs *capacity* (a sum of volumes).
[^ety-capacity]: **capacity** — Latin *capax*, "able to hold" (*capere*, to take) + *-ity*, the quality → how much a container holds; in the lung, always two or more volumes added together.
[^ety-static]: **static** — Greek *statikos*, "causing to stand" (*histanai*, to stand) → measured without regard to time (how much, not how fast).
[^ety-dynamic]: **dynamic** — Greek *dynamis*, "power, force" + *-ic* → measured against time, during forceful movement (how fast).
[^ety-tidal]: **tidal** — Old English *tīd*, "time, season" → the regular ebb and flow of the sea; *tidal volume* ebbs and flows with each quiet breath (500 mL).
[^ety-inspiratory]: **inspiratory** — Latin *in-*, "into" + *spirare*, "to breathe" + *-atory*, "relating to" → relating to breathing in. **Differentiate:** *inspiration* (air in) · *expiration* (air out) · *respiration* (the whole process) · *ventilation* (bulk air movement).
[^ety-reserve]: **reserve** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *servare*, "to keep" → kept back for later use: the extra air that can still be drawn in (IRV) or pushed out (ERV) beyond a quiet breath.
[^ety-expiratory]: **expiratory** — Latin *ex-*, "out" + *spirare*, "to breathe" + *-atory* → relating to breathing out.
[^ety-residual]: **residual** — Latin *residuus*, "left over" (*re-*, "back" + *sedere*, "to sit") → what stays behind; RV is the air that remains even after a maximal expiration.
[^ety-functional]: **functional** — Latin *functio*, "performance" (*fungi*, to perform) + *-al* → relating to working; FRC is the "working" resting volume at which tidal breathing takes place.
[^ety-equilibrium]: **equilibrium** — Latin *aequus*, "equal" + *libra*, "balance, scales" → equal balance: at FRC the lung's inward recoil equals the chest wall's outward spring.
[^ety-vital]: **vital** — Latin *vitalis*, "of life" (*vita*, life) → the "living" capacity; John Hutchinson (1846), who invented the spirometer, named it because it predicted life expectancy.
[^ety-closing]: **closing volume** — Latin *claudere*, "to shut" → the volume at which the small airways of the dependent lung zones begin to shut at the end of expiration.
[^ety-dependent]: **dependent** — Latin *de-*, "down from" + *pendere*, "to hang" → hanging down: the lowest, gravity-dependent zones of the lung (bases when upright, posterior when supine), where airways close first.
[^ety-investigation]: **investigation** — Latin *in-*, "into" + *vestigare*, "to track, follow footprints" (*vestigium*, a footprint) + *-ation* → tracking something down; a test.
[^ety-spirometry]: **spirometry** — Latin *spirare*, "to breathe" + Greek *metron*, "measure" + *-ia* → the measuring of breath; the spirometer was introduced by John Hutchinson (1846).
[^ety-plethysmography]: **plethysmography** — Greek *plēthysmos*, "enlargement, increase" (*plēthein*, to be full) + *-graphia*, "recording" → recording changes in volume; the body plethysmograph ("body box") was developed by Arthur DuBois and colleagues (1956).
[^ety-boyle]: **Boyle's law** — Robert Boyle (1627–1691), Anglo-Irish natural philosopher, who showed (1662) that at constant temperature the pressure of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.
[^ety-atmospheric]: **atmospheric** — Greek *atmos*, "vapour" + *sphaira*, "sphere" + *-ic* → of the sphere of vapour around the earth; atmospheric pressure at sea level = 760 mm Hg.
[^ety-helium]: **helium** — Greek *hēlios*, "sun" + *-ium* → the element first detected in the sun's spectrum (1868); inert and insoluble, so it stays in the lungs and dilutes in proportion to lung volume.
[^ety-dilution]: **dilution** — Latin *dis-* (*di-*), "apart" + *luere*, "to wash" + *-tion* → washing apart, thinning out: a known amount of helium is diluted by the unknown lung volume (C₁V₁ = C₂V₂).
[^ety-nitrogen]: **nitrogen** — Greek *nitron*, "native soda, saltpetre" + *-genēs*, "producing" → "producer of nitre" (Jean-Antoine Chaptal, 1790); in nitrogen washout, 100 % O₂ is breathed and the N₂ washed out of the lung measures its volume.
[^ety-tiffeneau]: **Tiffeneau–Pinelli index** — Robert Tiffeneau (1910–1961) and André Pinelli, French physicians, who in 1947 described the volume expired in the first second (VEMS, *volume expiratoire maximal par seconde*) relative to vital capacity — the FEV₁/FVC ratio.
[^ety-obstruction]: **obstructive** — Latin *ob-*, "against, in the way" + *struere*, "to build" + *-ive* → something built in the way: airflow is blocked, so expiration is difficult. **Differentiate:** *obstructive* (narrow airways — expiration slow) vs *restrictive* (small, stiff lungs — inspiration limited).
[^ety-restriction]: **restrictive** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *stringere*, "to draw tight" + *-ive* → drawn back tight: the lung or chest wall cannot expand, so inspiration is difficult and all volumes shrink.
[^ety-emphysema]: **emphysema** — Greek *en-* (*em-* before *ph*), "in" + *physan*, "to blow, inflate" + *-ma*, the result → "an inflation": air spaces enlarged by destruction of their walls — the classic obstructive lung.
[^ety-gold]: **GOLD** — **G**lobal Initiative for Chronic **O**bstructive **L**ung **D**isease, launched in 1997–98 with the US NHLBI and the WHO; its yearly report defines and stages COPD by spirometry.
[^ety-copd]: **COPD** — **c**hronic **o**bstructive **p**ulmonary **d**isease: Greek *chronos*, "time" → long-lasting; Latin *pulmo*, "lung" → pulmonary. An umbrella for emphysema and chronic bronchitis with persistent airflow limitation.
[^ety-trachea]: **trachea** — Greek *tracheia (artēria)*, "rough (air-pipe)" → named for the rough feel of its cartilage rings; the large airway that sets the early, effort-dependent peak flow.
[^ety-bronchus]: **bronchus** — Greek *bronchos*, "windpipe, throat" → the branches of the trachea; the medium and small bronchi form the effort-independent descending limb.
[^ety-intrathoracic]: **intrathoracic / extrathoracic** — Latin *intra-*, "within" / *extra-*, "outside" + Greek *thōrax*, "breastplate, chest" + *-ic* → inside vs outside the chest: the thoracic inlet divides the trachea into an extrathoracic (neck) and an intrathoracic part.
[^ety-apnoea]: **apnoea** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *pnoē*, "breathing" → cessation of breathing; *obstructive sleep apnoea* = repeated collapse of the pharynx (an extrathoracic airway) in sleep.
[^pre-in]: **in- (directional)** — in, into: inspiratory, inhalation, investigation. Don't confuse with **in- (negative)** not (independent, inert) · **intra-** within.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: reserve (kept back), residual (left back), restrictive (drawn back). Don't confuse with **retro-** behind · **pre-** before.
[^pre-ex]: **ex- / e- / ef-** — out of: expiratory, exhale, effort. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek) · **extra-** beyond, outside (extrathoracic).
[^pre-equi]: **equi-** — equal: equilibrium, equivalent, equimolar. Don't confuse with **iso-** equal (Greek: isovolumetric) · **equine** of a horse.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, down from, away: dependent (hanging down), deflation, deoxygenated. Don't confuse with **di- / dis-** apart (dilution) · **dys-** bad, difficult.
[^pre-spiro]: **spiro-** — breathing (Latin *spirare*): spirometry, spirogram, spirometer. Don't confuse with **spiro- (Greek *speira*)** coil (spirochaete, spironolactone's spiro-ring) · **-pnoea** breathing (Greek).
[^suf-metry]: **-metry / -meter** — measuring / the measuring instrument: spirometry, spirometer, oximetry. Don't confuse with **-graphy** recording (plethysmography) · **-scopy** viewing · **metr-** uterus (metritis).
[^suf-graphy]: **-graphy / -gram / -graph** — the procedure of recording / the record / the instrument: plethysmography, spirogram, plethysmograph. Don't confuse with **-metry** measuring · **-scopy** looking.
[^pre-di]: **di- / dis- (Latin)** — apart, away: dilution (washing apart), diffusion, distension. Don't confuse with **di- (Greek)** two (dioxide) · **dia-** through.
[^pre-ob]: **ob- (oc-, op-)** — against, in the way: obstructive, occlusion, opposing. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from · **ad-** towards.
[^pre-em]: **em- / en-** — in, into (Greek); *en-* becomes *em-* before *p*, *b*, *ph*: emphysema, embolus, empyema. Don't confuse with **e- / ex-** out (Latin) · **an-** without.
[^pre-chron]: **chrono- / chron-** — time: chronic (long-lasting), chronotropic, synchronous. Don't confuse with **acute** (Latin *acutus*, sharp — sudden, short) · **chromo-** colour.
[^pre-broncho]: **broncho-** — the windpipe's branches: bronchus, bronchiole, bronchodilator, bronchoscopy. Don't confuse with **branchio-** gills (branchial arches) · **brachio-** arm.
[^pre-intra]: **intra-** — within: intrathoracic, intrapleural, intrapulmonary. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **extra-** outside.
[^pre-extra]: **extra-** — outside, beyond: extrathoracic, extracellular, extrapulmonary. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek) · **ecto-** outer · **intra-** within.

[^suf-pnoea]: **-pnoea / -pnea** — breathing: apnoea, dyspnoea, tachypnoea, orthopnoea, hyperpnoea. Don't confuse with **pneumo-** air, lung (pneumothorax) · **-spirometry** (Latin breathing root).
