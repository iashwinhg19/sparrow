# Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule

## Counter Current Mechanism
- Requirements
  - 2 tubes lying close to each other
  - Fluid moving in opposite directions
- Seen in
  - Human kidneys (loop of Henle)
  - Testis
  - Intestines
  - Skin (temperature regulation)
- Stork leg in cold water
  - Exchange of heat between vessels
  - Arterial blood warm
  - Cold venous blood → warmed by heat exchange

## Loop of Henle (LOH)
- Receives isotonic fluid from PCT (300 mOsm/L)
- Function → concentration and dilution of urine
- Urine osmolarity range → 50–1400 mOsm/L
  - Average → 100–800 mOsm/L
- Parts → descending LOH, ascending LOH

### Counter current system
- Descending LOH = concentrating segment
  - Permeable to water (aquaporin)
  - Impermeable to Na⁺
  - Countercurrent multiplier
  - 300 → 600 → 900 → 1200
- Ascending LOH = diluting segment
  - Permeable to solutes
  - Impermeable to water
  - 1200 → 900 → 600 → 300 → DCT
  - Na⁺, K⁺, 2Cl⁻ cotransporter
- Loop diuretic (frusemide) ✗ cotransporter
  - → excretion of Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻
- Single effect → Na⁺ moved without H₂O

### Medullary hyperosmolarity
- Created by → countercurrent multiplier
- Purpose → ADH-mediated water reabsorption from CD
- Maintained by → countercurrent exchanger (vasa recta)
- Length of LOH ∝ medullary hyperosmolarity
- Contributed by
  - Na⁺ → 50 %
  - Urea → 50 %, from CD
    - Urea transporters (UT1, UT2)
    - ↑ with high protein diet

### Vasa recta
- Blood vessels along LOH
- Similar osmolarities → 300, 600, 900, 1200

### Reabsorption of Ca²⁺ (thick ascending limb)
- 1 → Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ cotransporter
- 2 → K⁺ recycling → positive charge in lumen
- 3 → + charge repels Ca²⁺ → reabsorbed
- Loop diuretic → calciuria

### Reabsorption of chloride
- Lumen → Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ cotransporter
- Blood side → chloride channel (barttin)
- Bartter syndrome (mutation)
  - Salt & water loss → volume depletion
  - → RAAS activation → ↑ aldosterone
  - → ↑ K⁺ & H⁺ excretion

### Classification based on length
- Juxtamedullary nephron
  - Long LOH
  - 15 % of nephrons
  - Concentration & dilution of urine
- Cortical nephron
  - Short LOH
  - 85 % of nephrons (maximum)

## Distal Convoluted Tubule
### Sodium reabsorption
- 5 % of filtered load
- Blood side → Na⁺ K⁺ pump
- Lumen → Na⁺–Cl⁻ cotransporter (2° active)
  - Mutation → Gitelman's syndrome
  - Blocked by thiazide diuretics

### Calcium reabsorption
- 9 % of filtered load
- Blood side → Na⁺–Ca²⁺ exchanger
- Lumen → TRPV5 channel
  - Up-regulated by PTH
  - Up-regulated by vitamin D

## Juxta Glomerular Apparatus
- DCT in close contact with its glomerulus → JGA

### JG cells
- Line the afferent arteriole
- AKA granular cells → granules → release renin
- Sense pressure changes
- ↑ renin release
  - Low pressure / hypotension in afferent arteriole
  - ↓ Na⁺
  - Sympathetic stimulation
  - Diuretics
  - Hemorrhage
- ↓ renin release
  - ↑ pressure in afferent arteriole
  - Angiotensin II (−ve feedback)
  - ADH

### RAAS
- In response to low BP
- Angiotensinogen (liver) → renin → angiotensin I
- Angiotensin I → ACE (lung) → angiotensin II
- Angiotensin II → ↑ BP
- Angiotensin II → aldosterone (adrenal cortex) → ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption
- Overactive RAAS → hypertension
  - ACE inhibitors block RAAS
  - Direct renin blocker → aliskiren

### Macula densa
- GFR / NaCl sensor → chemical sensor
- Tubuloglomerular feedback
  - ↑ GFR → ↑ NaCl → sensed by macula densa
  - → adenosine → afferent constriction → ↓ GFR

- AKA lacis cells / Polkissen cells
