# Descending Motor Pathways

## Brain → spinal cord → skeletal muscle
- Efferent / descending pathway
- Top-down approach

## Cortex, brainstem & spinal cord pathways
- Cortex & brainstem neurons → upper motor neurons
### Motor cortex
- Primary → Brodmann area 4
  - Execution of movement
- Supplementary → Brodmann area 6
  - Bimanual coordination
- Premotor → Brodmann area 6
  - Postural set-up
  - Mirror neurons → imitation
- Motor cortex → precentral gyrus (frontal lobe)
- Cortical association areas
  - Idea generation & initiation of movement
- Sensory cortex → Brodmann 3, 1, 2
### Motor homunculus
- Penfield homunculus → body part representation
- Area ∝ functionality / use, not size
- Maximum representation
  - Fingers, thumb → writing
  - Lips, tongue → vocalization
- Medial → leg · lateral → face

### Corticospinal (pyramidal) tract
- Recently evolved
- Contralateral → controls opposite side of body
- Controls distal muscles
- Fine, skilled, voluntary movements → writing
- Origin → Betz cells
  - 30 % primary motor cortex
  - 30 % premotor & supplementary cortex
  - 40 % sensory parietal & association areas
  - → sensory-motor integration
- Course
  - Internal capsule (posterior limb)
  - → midbrain → pons → pyramid
  - → decussation (cross over)
  - Lateral CST → 80–90 % → opposite side
  - Anterior CST → 10 % → same side
  - → spinal cord → LMN → skeletal muscle

### Brainstem tracts
- Medial motor pathways
  - 1 vestibulospinal
  - 2 reticulospinal
  - 3 tectospinal
- Lateral motor pathways
  - 4 rubrospinal
  - 5 corticospinal (anterior, lateral)
- Extrapyramidal → 1–4
- Pyramidal → 5

### Extrapyramidal tracts
- Vestibulospinal
  - Medial → controls neck musculature
  - Lateral → posture
- Reticulospinal
  - Pontine → excitatory
  - Medullary → inhibitory
- Maintain posture
  - Paravertebral muscles
  - Proximal extensors
  - = antigravity muscles
- Tectospinal
  - Origin → superior colliculus
  - Head & eye movements
- Rubrospinal
  - Only extrapyramidal tract that is lateral
  - Origin → red nucleus
  - Flexors of upper limb

### Lateral vs medial pathways
- Lateral
  - Corticospinal, rubrospinal
  - New
  - Fine, skilled, voluntary movements
  - Flexors
- Medial
  - Vestibulospinal, reticulospinal, tectospinal
  - Old
  - Maintenance of posture
  - Extensors (antigravity)

### Spinal cord
- Final common pathway → LMNs
- α motor neurons
  - Extrafusal fibres (actin, myosin)
  - Contract muscle
  - Overactivity → spasticity
- γ motor neurons
  - Intrafusal fibres of muscle spindle
  - Regulate muscle tone
  - Overactivity → ↑ muscle tone

## Disorders
### Decerebrate rigidity (extensor)
- Transection between superior & inferior colliculus
- → cortical inhibition lost
- → dorsal sensory roots activated
- → ++ reticulospinal tract (brainstem)
- → ++ γ motor neurons → ↑ extensor tone
- = decerebrate / γ rigidity
- Posture
  - Head & neck extended
  - Upper limbs extended
  - Fingers & forearm pronated
  - Lower limbs extended, toes pointing inward
- Cause → uncal herniation (space-occupying lesion)
### Decorticate rigidity (flexor)
- Decortication (cortex removed)
- → cortical inhibition lost
- Rubrospinal tract intact → ↑ flexor tone
- Posture
  - Upper limbs flexed against chest
  - Head extended
  - Lower limbs extended
- Cause → thrombosis / haemorrhage of internal capsule
### Decerebellate rigidity
- Anterior cerebellum removed
- → ++ motor neurons
- → exaggerated extensor activity
- = decerebellate / α rigidity
### UMN vs LMN lesions
- Muscle atrophy → UMN − · LMN +++
- Fasciculations → UMN − · LMN +++
- Muscle tone → UMN ↑↑ · LMN ↓
- Weakness → UMN regional · LMN segmental
- Reflexes → UMN ↑↑ (hyper) · LMN ↓↓ (sluggish)
- Babinski → UMN + (big toe dorsiflexion) · LMN − (plantar flexion)
