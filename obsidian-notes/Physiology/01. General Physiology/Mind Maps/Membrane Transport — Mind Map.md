# Membrane Transport

## ATP and the Classification of Transport
- ATP
  - Nitrogenous base + ribose sugar + 3 phosphate groups
  - High-energy bonds between phosphates
- Nobel prize named on the page
  - Boyer
  - Walker
  - Jens Skou
- ATP absent → passive transport (high → low conc.)
  - Simple diffusion
  - Facilitated diffusion (carrier protein +)
  - Non-ionic diffusion
  - Osmotic diffusion
- ATP present → active transport (low → high conc.)
  - Primary
  - Secondary
  - Vesicular → exocytosis, endocytosis, transcytosis

## Passive Transport
### Simple diffusion
- Along concentration gradient
- Without utilizing ATP
- Membrane factors → Fick's law
  - D ∝ concentration gradient
  - D ∝ surface area
  - D ∝ 1 / thickness
- Substance factors
  - D ∝ solubility (lipid soluble, O₂ across alveoli)
  - D ∝ 1 / size
- Clinical application
  - Emphysema → ↓ surface area → ↓ gas diffusion
  - Pulmonary fibrosis → ↑ thickness → ↓ gas diffusion

### Facilitated diffusion
- Through carrier protein
  - GLUT → glucose
  - Aquaporin → water
- Along concentration gradient
- Without ATP

### Non-ionic diffusion
- Non-ionised form crosses membrane
  - → becomes ionised inside → trapped
- Examples
  - Ammonia diffusion
  - Salicylate absorption

### Osmotic diffusion
- High H₂O → low H₂O, passive
  - Across semipermeable membrane
- Osmotic pressure = pressure to stop water movement
- Sodium is osmotically active → drags H₂O
- Plasma osmolarity
  - Posm = 2 Na⁺ + 0.05 Glu + 0.36 BUN
  - Normal ≈ 300 mosm/L
- Osmoles = conc. of osmotically active substance
  - Osmolarity → mosm/L (most common)
  - Osmolality → mosm/kg of solvent

## Active Transport
- ATP utilized as source of energy
- Types → primary, secondary

### Primary active transport
- Transporter has its own ATPase
- Examples
  - Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase
  - H⁺–K⁺ ATPase (proton pump)
  - Ca²⁺ ATPase
- Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase
  - Transmembrane protein
  - Heterodimer → α and β subunits
  - Coupling ratio 3:2 → 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in
  - Electrogenic pump
- Structure
  - β → glycosylation sites
  - α outer side → K⁺, ouabain (blocker)
  - α inner side → ATP, Na⁺, phosphorylation
- Functions
  - Regulates cell volume
  - RMP → contributes 4 mV (minor)
  - Indirect energy for secondary transport
- Regulation
  - ↑ → thyroid hormone, insulin, aldosterone
  - ↓ → ouabain, digitalis, dopamine (physiological)

### Secondary active transport
- Uses gradient made by primary active transport
- Symporter → same direction
  - SGLT-1 → GIT
  - SGLT-2 → PCT
  - Na⁺–K⁺–2Cl⁻ → thick ascending limb of Henle
  - Na⁺–I⁻ → thyroid gland
- Antiport (exchanger) → opposite direction
  - Cl⁻–HCO3⁻ exchanger (anion exchanger) in RBC

### Vesicular transport
- Vesicles, irrespective of gradient
- Needs Ca²⁺ and special proteins
- Exocytosis (emeiocytosis)
  - SNARE → SNAP-25, syntaxin (membrane)
  - Synaptobrevin (vesicle)
  - Synaptotagmin → Ca²⁺ sensor
  - ↑ Ca²⁺ → fusion → exocytosis
- Botulinum toxin
  - Breaks synaptobrevin → no exocytosis
  - → no ACh release → flaccid paralysis
  - Used to treat achalasia cardia
- Endocytosis
  - Phagocytosis → bacteria
  - Pinocytosis → fluid
  - Receptor mediated → LDL (clathrin, dynamin)
- Transcytosis (cytopempsis)
  - Endocytosis one side → exocytosis other side

### Saturation kinetics
- Obeyed by
  - Active transport
  - Facilitated diffusion (carrier)
- Plateau → carrier synthesis takes time on ↑ demand
- Not obeyed by simple diffusion
