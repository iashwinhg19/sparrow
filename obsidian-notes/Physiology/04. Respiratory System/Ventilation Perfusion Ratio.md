# ⚖️ Ventilation Perfusion Ratio

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The **ventilation perfusion ratio (V/Q)**[^ety-ratio] compares the air reaching an alveolus (**V**, alveolar ventilation[^ety-ventilation][^suf-ation]) with the blood flowing past it (**Q**, pulmonary perfusion[^ety-perfusion][^pre-per]). Gas exchange is best when the two are **matched (V/Q ≈ 0.8)**. Everything in this chapter answers:
> - ✅ **Where in the lung is V/Q high or low?** → **apex 3.2 (highest)**, **middle 0.8 (normal)**, **base 0.6 (lowest)**
> - ✅ **What happens at the two extremes?** → **Q = 0 → V/Q = ∞ → wasted ventilation (dead space)**; **V = 0 → V/Q = 0 → wasted perfusion (shunt)**
> - ✅ **What does each V/Q do to the alveolar gases?** → the **O₂–CO₂ diagram**: falls → PO₂ ↓ PCO₂ ↑; rises → PO₂ ↑ PCO₂ ↓

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Delivery Van and a Loading Dock

Think of each alveolus as a loading dock. **Ventilation** is the van bringing fresh O₂; **perfusion** is the line of trucks (blood) that must pick it up. If vans arrive and no trucks come, the O₂ is **wasted** (dead space). If trucks queue and no van comes, the trucks leave **empty** (shunt). Every real alveolus sits somewhere on the line between those two extremes:

```
V/Q = 0          V/Q low        V/Q 0.8          V/Q high          V/Q = ∞
SHUNT            (base 0.6)     NORMAL           (apex 3.2)        DEAD SPACE
no air           more blood     matched          more air          no blood
gas = venous     than air       gas = alveolar   than blood        gas = inspired air
PO2 40 PCO2 45   PO2 ↓ PCO2 ↑   PO2 100 PCO2 40  PO2 ↑ PCO2 ↓      PO2 150 PCO2 0
   C ◄────────────── LEFT ───────── B ──────── RIGHT ──────────────► A
(wasted perfusion)                                       (wasted ventilation)
```

## 🫁 Ventilation Perfusion Matching & Mismatch

### Ventilation perfusion matching

```latex
\text{Ventilation perfusion ratio} = \frac{\text{Alveolar ventilation } (\dot V)}{\text{Pulmonary circulation } (\dot Q)}
```

- **Ventilation perfusion ratio = Alveolar ventilation (V) ÷ Pulmonary circulation (Q).**[^1]

![[Ventilation Perfusion Ratio - apex to base gradient.png]]
*The upright lung — **large alveoli** at the apex (against gravity), **small alveoli** at the base (towards gravity); the airway and blood vessel run in parallel. Apex V/Q 3.2 (highest), middle 0.8 (normal), base 0.6 (lowest) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Region | V/Q | Compliance[^ety-compliance][^pre-com][^suf-ance] | Exchange of gases | V and Q |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Apex**[^ety-apex] | **3.2 : Highest** | **↓ compliance** | **↓ exchange of gases** | **V ↓, Q ↓↓↓ → ratio ↑** |
| **Middle** | **0.8 : Normal ratio** | — | — | — |
| **Base** | **0.6 : Lowest** | **↑ compliance** | **↑ exchange of gases** | **V ↑, Q ↑↑↑ → ratio ↓** |

🧠 *Why the gradient?* Gravity[^ety-gravity] pulls both the lung and the blood downwards. At the **apex** the alveoli are already stretched open (**large alveoli**), so they are stiff (**↓ compliance**) and take in little extra air per breath — **V ↓**. Blood barely reaches the top of a low-pressure circuit — **Q ↓↓↓**. Because blood falls off far more than air, the ratio is **highest at the apex**. At the **base** the alveoli are small and easily filled (**↑ compliance**, **V ↑**) and blood pools there (**Q ↑↑↑**); blood rises more than air, so the ratio is **lowest at the base** — but because both V and Q are large, most of the gas exchange happens at the base.[^2]

#### Applied aspect

- **Apex has highest oxygen** (**has perfusion ↓↓**) — little blood arrives to take the O₂ away, so alveolar PO₂ stays high.
  - **Significance: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis***[^ety-mycobacterium][^pre-myco][^ety-tuberculosis][^suf-osis] **infest**[^ety-infest] **apex** (**d/t ↑ O₂**).[^3]

### Ventilation perfusion mismatch

🧠 Mismatch means V and Q are no longer in proportion. The two pure extremes are an alveolus that gets air but no blood (wasted ventilation) and one that gets blood but no air (wasted perfusion).

#### 1. Wasted ventilation : (Q = 0)

- **V/Q = Infinity**[^ety-infinity][^pre-in].

**Causes:**

| Physiological | Pathological[^ety-pathological] |
|---|---|
| **Anatomical**[^ety-anatomical][^pre-ana] **dead space** — conducting airways (trachea → terminal bronchioles, generations 0–16) carry air but have no blood–gas contact | **Pulmonary**[^ety-pulmonary][^suf-ary] **embolism**[^ety-embolism][^pre-en][^suf-ism] — the clot blocks the artery, so the alveoli beyond it are ventilated but not perfused (alveolar dead space)[^4] |

![[Ventilation Perfusion Ratio - anatomical dead space airways.png]]
*Physiological wasted ventilation — the conducting zone, trachea (0) to terminal bronchioles (16), is the **anatomical dead space** (cropped from the handwritten page).*

![[Ventilation Perfusion Ratio - pulmonary embolism.png]]
*Pathological wasted ventilation — a **pulmonary embolism** plugs a pulmonary artery branch; the lung beyond it is still ventilated but receives no blood (cropped from the handwritten page).*

#### 2. Wasted perfusion : (V = 0)

- **V/Q = 0.**

**Causes:**

| Physiological | Pathological |
|---|---|
| **Alveoli with no oxygen**; **shunt**[^ety-shunt] **blood (2 % of cardiac**[^ety-cardiac] **output)**[^5] | **Foreign body obstruction**[^ety-obstruction][^pre-ob] **/ tumor**[^ety-tumor] **of airway** → **collapse**[^ety-collapse] → **no air**[^6] |

![[Ventilation Perfusion Ratio - physiological shunt.png]]
*Physiological wasted perfusion — alveoli with no oxygen and **shunt blood (2 % of cardiac output)** that bypasses ventilated alveoli (cropped from the handwritten page).*

![[Ventilation Perfusion Ratio - airway obstruction and collapse.png]]
*Pathological wasted perfusion — a **foreign body or tumour obstructing the airway**; the lung beyond it **collapses** and receives **no air**, though blood still flows through it (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| | Wasted ventilation | Wasted perfusion |
|---|---|---|
| **Defect** | **Q = 0** (air, no blood) | **V = 0** (blood, no air) |
| **V/Q** | **∞** | **0** |
| **Physiological cause** | Anatomical dead space | Shunt blood (2 % of CO) |
| **Pathological cause** | Pulmonary embolism | Foreign body / tumour of airway → collapse |
| **Alveolar gas becomes** | Like inspired air (PO₂ 150, PCO₂ 0) | Like mixed venous blood (PO₂ 40, PCO₂ 45) |

### Ventilation perfusion graph

![[Ventilation Perfusion Ratio - O2-CO2 diagram.png]]
*O₂–CO₂ diagram — the curved V/Q line runs from **C** (V/Q = 0, shunt blood, PO₂ 40 / PCO₂ ~45) through **B** (V/Q normal, alveolar air, PO₂ 100 / PCO₂ 40) to **A** (V/Q = ∞, anatomical dead space, PO₂ 150 / PCO₂ 0) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

**Left shift ← Normal → Right shift**

| Point | V/Q | What the alveolar gas looks like | PO₂ / PCO₂ (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C** | **V/Q = 0 (shunt blood)** | Mixed venous blood | ~40 / ~45 |
| **B** | **V/Q normal (alveolar air)** | Normal alveolar air | **100 / 40** |
| **A** | **V/Q = ∞ (anatomical dead space)** | Inspired air | **150 / 0** |

| Deviation[^ety-deviation][^pre-de] | Gases | V/Q |
|---|---|---|
| **Left deviation** (towards C) | **PO₂ ↓, PCO₂ ↑** | **V/Q falls** |
| **Right deviation** (towards A) | **PO₂ ↑, PCO₂ ↓** | **V/Q rises** |

🧠 *How to read it:* every alveolus must lie somewhere on this one curved line, because its gases can only be a mixture of what the air brings (point A) and what the blood brings (point C). More air → slide right towards A; more blood → slide left towards C. The apex (V/Q 3.2) sits to the right of B, the base (V/Q 0.6) to the left.[^7]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> An alveolus can only exchange what **both** air and blood bring to it, so the gas inside it is set by the **ratio** V/Q, not by V or Q alone. Gravity makes blood fall off from base to apex much faster than air, so V/Q rises from **0.6 at the base** to **3.2 at the apex**, with **0.8** as the whole-lung average. The two extremes define everything else: **V/Q = ∞** (no blood) is dead space and its gas looks like inspired air; **V/Q = 0** (no air) is shunt and its gas looks like venous blood.
>
> Every alveolus lies on the O₂–CO₂ line between those two points. Low V/Q units pull arterial PO₂ down and PCO₂ up; high V/Q units cannot make up for them, because haemoglobin is already full — which is why V/Q mismatch always causes hypoxaemia.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Whole-lung V/Q ≈ 0.8 (4.2 L alveolar ventilation ÷ 5 L cardiac output). The apex is over-ventilated for its blood (V/Q 3.2, PO₂ high), the base under-ventilated (V/Q 0.6); the anatomical dead space (V/Q ∞) and 2 % physiological shunt (V/Q 0) explain the small normal A–a gradient.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A small embolus or a partly blocked airway adds some high or low V/Q units. Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction diverts blood away from poorly ventilated alveoli and bronchoconstriction reduces air to unperfused ones, nudging V/Q back towards normal. The patient breathes faster; PaCO₂ stays normal or low, PaO₂ falls a little.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> A large pulmonary embolism (massive dead space), lobar collapse from a foreign body or tumour (shunt), pneumonia or ARDS overwhelm compensation: PaO₂ falls, the A–a gradient widens, and with a large shunt even 100 % O₂ cannot correct the hypoxaemia. Massive dead space also raises PaCO₂ when the patient tires.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal V/Q 0.8 → regional fault (embolus / obstruction) → local V/Q ∞ or 0 → vasoconstriction + tachypnoea compensate → mismatch spreads → hypoxaemia with wide A–a gradient → refractory hypoxaemia (shunt) / hypercapnia (dead space)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Whether hypoxaemia corrects with supplemental O₂ tells you the mechanism: V/Q mismatch corrects; true shunt does not. Dead space (embolism) is treated by restoring flow (anticoagulation, thrombolysis); shunt from collapse by restoring air (removing the foreign body, re-expanding the lung).

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Wasted ventilation / dead space:** "Q = 0", "V/Q = ∞", "ventilated but not perfused", "pulmonary embolism" → gas in those alveoli equals inspired air (point A).
>
> **Wasted perfusion / shunt:** "V = 0", "V/Q = 0", "perfused but not ventilated", "foreign body", "collapse", "atelectasis" → gas equals mixed venous blood (point C); hypoxaemia not corrected by 100 % O₂.
>
> **Physiological shunt:** "2 % of cardiac output", "bronchial and Thebesian veins" → why arterial PO₂ is a little below alveolar PO₂ even in health.
>
> **Apex:** "V/Q 3.2", "highest", "highest O₂", "large alveoli, ↓ compliance" → reactivation tuberculosis at the apex.
>
> **Base:** "V/Q 0.6", "lowest", "small alveoli, ↑ compliance", "most gas exchange" → where most blood and most air go.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> A curved line from (40, 45) to (150, 0) on a PO₂–PCO₂ graph = the O₂–CO₂ (V/Q) diagram; a perfusion scan defect with normal ventilation scan = mismatched defect of pulmonary embolism; an upper-lobe cavity = reactivation TB.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> The further left a unit slides (towards V/Q 0), the lower its PO₂; a shunt fraction large enough that PaO₂ does not rise on 100 % O₂ marks true shunt rather than mismatch.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "The apex gets more ventilation than the base"**
>
> **What students think:** Because V/Q is highest at the apex, the apex must be better ventilated.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Both ventilation and perfusion are **greater at the base**. The apex has the highest *ratio* only because its blood flow falls off much more than its air (V ↓, Q ↓↓↓). The base has the lowest ratio because blood rises more than air (V ↑, Q ↑↑↑).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Ratio ≠ amount. "Base wins both, but blood wins by more."
>
> **Trap 2: Mixing up V/Q = 0 and V/Q = ∞**
>
> **What students think:** Pulmonary embolism is V/Q = 0 because "nothing is flowing".
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In embolism, **Q** (blood) is zero while V continues: V/0 = **∞** → wasted ventilation (dead space). In airway obstruction, **V** is zero while Q continues: 0/Q = **0** → wasted perfusion (shunt). Ask which one is in the numerator.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**E**mbolism → **E**ndless (∞); **O**bstruction → **O** (zero)."
>
> **Trap 3: Assuming high-V/Q areas can compensate for low-V/Q areas**
>
> **What students think:** The over-ventilated apex makes up for the under-ventilated base, so arterial O₂ stays normal.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** High-V/Q blood is already ~97–100 % saturated — extra alveolar O₂ adds almost nothing (flat top of the dissociation curve). Low-V/Q blood is poorly saturated. Mixing them gives a lower PaO₂ than a perfectly matched lung. CO₂ (linear curve) can be compensated by hyperventilation; O₂ cannot.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "V/Q mismatch lowers O₂ always, CO₂ only if the patient cannot breathe harder."
>
> **Trap 4: Thinking TB goes to the apex because it is better ventilated**
>
> **What students think:** M. tuberculosis lives at the apex because more air reaches it.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The apex has the **highest alveolar PO₂** because its **perfusion is lowest** (less blood carries the O₂ away); the aerobic bacillus thrives in that O₂-rich, poorly perfused (and poorly lymph-drained) tissue.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Highest O₂ because **least blood**, not most air."
>
> **Trap 5: Reading the O₂–CO₂ diagram backwards**
>
> **What students think:** Left shift means V/Q rises.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** On the page's graph PO₂ is on the x-axis. Moving **left** means PO₂ falls and PCO₂ rises — the alveolus looks more like venous blood, so **V/Q falls** (towards C, shunt). Moving **right** means PO₂ rises and PCO₂ falls — more like inspired air, **V/Q rises** (towards A, dead space).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Anchor the ends: **C = venous (V/Q 0)** on the left, **A = air (V/Q ∞)** on the right.

[^1]: With an alveolar ventilation of about 4.2 L/min and a pulmonary blood flow (cardiac output) of about 5 L/min, the whole-lung V/Q is 4.2 / 5 ≈ 0.84 — the "0.8" of the middle zone. The dot over V and Q in textbooks means "per minute".
[^2]: Gravity makes intrapleural pressure less negative towards the base (about −10 cm H₂O at the apex vs −2.5 cm H₂O at the base), so apical alveoli start each breath already well inflated on the flat, stiff part of the pressure–volume curve while basal alveoli sit on its steep part. In West's classic figures the apex has PAO₂ ≈ 132 and PACO₂ ≈ 28 mmHg, the base PAO₂ ≈ 89 and PACO₂ ≈ 42 mmHg.
[^3]: It is **reactivation (post-primary) tuberculosis** that favours the apical and posterior segments of the upper lobes (Simon focus, Assmann focus, apical cavitation): *M. tuberculosis* is an obligate aerobe, and the apex combines the highest alveolar PO₂ with poor lymphatic drainage. Primary TB (Ghon focus) usually lies in the lower part of the upper lobe or upper part of the lower lobe, subpleurally.
[^4]: Pulmonary embolism raises alveolar dead space and the V_D/V_T ratio; the V/Q scan shows a perfusion defect with normal ventilation (a "mismatched" defect). Patients usually hyperventilate, so PaCO₂ is often low despite the dead space; reflex bronchoconstriction and loss of surfactant also follow in the affected area.
[^5]: The normal anatomical shunt: bronchial veins (nutrient blood to the airways) partly drain into the pulmonary veins, and the Thebesian (venae cordis minimae) veins drain coronary blood directly into the left heart — venous blood that never meets an alveolus, about 2 % of cardiac output. It is one reason arterial PO₂ is a few mmHg below alveolar PO₂. A true shunt does not respond to breathing 100 % O₂, because the shunted blood never meets the extra O₂.
[^6]: Collapse of a lung segment from absent air entry is called **atelectasis**: when a bronchus is blocked, the air trapped beyond it is absorbed into the blood (absorption atelectasis) and the alveoli collapse. Inhaled foreign bodies lodge more often in the right main bronchus (wider, shorter, more vertical).
[^7]: Points A and C are the end-points because the gas in any alveolus can only be a mixture of inspired air (PO₂ ~150, PCO₂ 0) and mixed venous blood (PO₂ 40, PCO₂ ~45–46). The page's graph draws point C at about PO₂ 40 and PCO₂ just above 40.
[^ety-ratio]: **ratio** — Latin *ratio*, "reckoning, calculation" (*reri*, "to reckon") → the reckoning of one quantity against another; V/Q is a ratio, so it can be high when both values are small. **Differentiate:** *ratio* (one quantity ÷ another) · *rate* (per unit time — respiratory rate) · *fraction* (part ÷ whole, e.g. V_D/V_T).
[^ety-ventilation]: **ventilation** — Latin *ventus*, "wind" → *ventilare*, "to fan" + *-ation*, the process → moving air in and out. **Differentiate:** *ventilation* (air movement, V) · *perfusion* (blood flow, Q) · *respiration* (gas exchange).
[^ety-perfusion]: **perfusion** — Latin *per-*, "through" + *fundere*, "to pour" + *-ion* → pouring blood through a capillary bed. **Differentiate:** *perfusion* (through) · *infusion* (into) · *effusion* (out into a cavity) · *diffusion* (spreading apart).
[^ety-compliance]: **compliance** — Latin *com-*, "with, together" + *plere*, "to fill" (via *complire*, "to fulfil") + *-ance* → yielding, filling readily; lung compliance = change in volume ÷ change in pressure (ΔV/ΔP). **Differentiate:** *compliance* (how easily it stretches) · *elastance* (its reciprocal — how hard it springs back) · *distensibility* (compliance per unit volume).
[^ety-apex]: **apex** — Latin *apex*, "summit, tip, point" → the top of the lung (rising about 2–3 cm above the medial third of the clavicle); plural *apices*. The opposite end is the **base** (Greek *basis*, "that on which one steps") resting on the diaphragm.
[^ety-gravity]: **gravity** — Latin *gravis*, "heavy" + *-ity*, a quality → heaviness; the weight that pulls blood and lung tissue towards the dependent base. Same root as *gravid* (heavy with child) and *grave*.
[^ety-mycobacterium]: **Mycobacterium** — Greek *mykēs*, "fungus" + *baktērion*, "a little rod" → fungus-like little rod, named (Lehmann & Neumann, 1896) for the mould-like pellicle it forms in liquid culture. *M. tuberculosis* was discovered by Robert Koch (1882).
[^ety-tuberculosis]: **tuberculosis** — Latin *tuberculum*, "a small swelling, a little knob" (diminutive of *tuber*, a lump) + *-osis*, a diseased condition → the disease of little knobs (tubercles — the granulomas seen at autopsy).
[^ety-infest]: **infest** — Latin *infestus*, "hostile, unsafe" (*in-* + *festus*, "seized") → to overrun as an enemy. **Differentiate:** *infest* (parasites living on or in the host — strictly for lice, worms) · *infect* (*in-* + *facere* — microbes invading and multiplying in tissue). TB is properly an *infection*; the page's "infest" is used loosely.
[^ety-infinity]: **infinity** — Latin *in-*, "not" + *finis*, "end, boundary" + *-ity* → without end; V/Q = V/0 = ∞ in an unperfused alveolus.
[^ety-pathological]: **pathological** — Greek *pathos*, "suffering, disease" + *logos*, "study" + *-ical* → relating to disease. **Differentiate:** *pathological* (caused by disease — embolism, obstruction) · *physiological* (present in health — dead space, 2 % shunt).
[^ety-anatomical]: **anatomical** — Greek *ana-*, "up" + *tomē*, "a cutting" → cutting up, dissection → relating to structure. Anatomical dead space = set by the structure of the airways.
[^ety-pulmonary]: **pulmonary** — Latin *pulmo*, "lung" + *-ary*, "relating to" → of the lungs; Greek equivalent *pneumōn* (pneumonia).
[^ety-embolism]: **embolism / embolus** — Greek *en-*, "in" + *ballein*, "to throw" → *embolos*, "a plug, a wedge thrown in" + *-ism*, a condition → a plug thrown into a vessel. **Differentiate:** *thrombus* (a clot formed in place) · *embolus* (any mass carried in the blood that lodges downstream — clot, fat, air, amniotic fluid) · *infarct* (the tissue death that may follow).
[^ety-shunt]: **shunt** — Middle English *shunten*, "to shy away, to turn aside" → blood turned aside past the alveoli without exchanging gas. **Differentiate:** *anatomical shunt* (bronchial and Thebesian veins — a real bypass) · *physiological / capillary shunt* (blood passing unventilated alveoli, V/Q = 0) · *venous admixture* (the total effect of shunt plus low-V/Q units).
[^ety-cardiac]: **cardiac** — Greek *kardia*, "heart" + *-ac*, "relating to" → of the heart; *cardiac output* = the blood pumped per minute by one ventricle (~5 L/min), equal to pulmonary blood flow Q.
[^ety-obstruction]: **obstruction** — Latin *ob-*, "against, in the way" + *struere*, "to build, pile up" + *-ion* → something built in the way. **Differentiate:** *obstruction* (lumen blocked from inside — foreign body, tumour) · *compression* (squeezed from outside) · *constriction* (narrowed by the wall's own muscle).
[^ety-tumor]: **tumour / tumor** — Latin *tumere*, "to swell" + *-or*, a state → a swelling; now used for a neoplasm. **Differentiate:** *tumour* (a mass) · *neoplasm* (*neo-* + *plasma*, "new formation" — autonomous growth) · *cancer* (a malignant neoplasm).
[^ety-collapse]: **collapse** — Latin *com-*, "together" + *labi*, "to slip, fall" → *collapsus*, "fallen together"; a lung segment whose alveoli have emptied and fallen in. The medical synonym is **atelectasis** (Greek *atelēs*, "incomplete" + *ektasis*, "expansion").
[^ety-deviation]: **deviation** — Latin *de-*, "away from" + *via*, "the road" + *-ation* → going off the road; on the O₂–CO₂ diagram, moving off the normal point B to the left or right. **Differentiate:** *deviation* (moving from a reference) · *shift* (the whole curve moving, as in the O₂–Hb dissociation curve).
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -ion** — the process or result of an action: ventilation, perfusion, obstruction, deviation, circulation. Don't confuse with **-osis** a diseased condition (tuberculosis) · **-ism** a state (embolism).
[^pre-per]: **per-** — through, thoroughly: perfusion, permeable, percutaneous. Don't confuse with **peri-** around (pericardium) · **pre-** before · **para-** beside.
[^pre-com]: **com- / con- / col- / co-** — together, with, thoroughly (the *m* turns into the next consonant): compliance, collapse, compression, constriction. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** against (countercurrent).
[^suf-ance]: **-ance / -ence** — the quality or state of: compliance, resistance, elastance, conductance. Don't confuse with **-ancy / -ency** a state that lasts (pregnancy, deficiency) · **-ant / -ent** the agent that does it (surfactant, afferent).
[^pre-myco]: **myco- / myc-** — fungus: Mycobacterium, mycosis, mycology. Don't confuse with **myo-** muscle (myocardium, myoglobin) · **myelo-** marrow, spinal cord (myeloma) · **myx-** mucus (myxoedema).
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a condition, usually diseased or abnormal: tuberculosis, fibrosis, thrombosis, acidosis. Don't confuse with **-iasis** a disease from an agent (amoebiasis) · **-itis** inflammation (bronchitis) · **-ation** a process.
[^pre-in]: **in- (im-, il-, ir-)** — not: infinity, insoluble, incompetent, irreversible. Don't confuse with the other **in-** "into" (inspiration, infusion) · **un-** not (Germanic, unconscious).
[^pre-ana]: **ana-** — up, throughout, again: anatomical, anabolism, anaphylaxis. Don't confuse with **an- / a-** without (anoxia) · **ante-** before · **anti-** against.
[^suf-ary]: **-ary** — relating to: pulmonary, coronary, capillary. Don't confuse with **-ory** relating to an action (respiratory) · **-arium** a place (sanatorium).
[^pre-en]: **en- / em- (Greek)** — in, into: embolism (thrown in), encephalon (in the head), empyema (pus within). Don't confuse with **endo-** within (endothelium) · **ex- / ec-** out of (ectopic) · Latin **in-** into.
[^suf-ism]: **-ism** — a condition, state or process: embolism, metabolism, hyperthyroidism. Don't confuse with **-ist** a person who practises (internist) · **-osis** a diseased condition.
[^pre-ob]: **ob- (oc-, of-, op-)** — against, in the way, towards: obstruction, occlusion, oblique, opposite. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from (abduction) · **ad-** towards (adduction).
[^pre-de]: **de-** — away from, down, removal: deviation, deoxygenated, depolarisation. Don't confuse with **di- / dis-** apart · **dia-** through (diaphragm).
