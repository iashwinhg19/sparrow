# Membrane Potentials

## Introduction — The Voltmeter and the Types
- Membrane connected to voltmeter
  - Recording electrode inside, reference outside
- Types
  - Resting membrane potential (RMP)
  - Equilibrium potential (EP)

## Resting Membrane Potential
- AKA diffusion potential → d/t diffusion of K⁺
### Factors determining negative RMP
- Charge → positive K⁺ moves out of cell
- Concentration gradient of K⁺
  - Intracellular → high
  - Extracellular → low
- Permeability to K⁺ at rest
  - Passive K⁺ diffusion → ↑ intracellular negative charge

### Types
- Stable
  - Neuron → −70 mV
  - Skeletal muscle → −90 mV
  - Heart ventricles → −90 mV
- Unstable / oscillating (pacemakers) → −60 to −40 mV
  - Heart → SA node
  - GIT → Cajal cells
  - Respiratory → pre-Bötzinger complex (medulla)

### Contributing factors
- Diffusion of K⁺ → −86 mV
- Na⁺–K⁺ pump → −4 mV
- Diffusion of Na⁺ → very less

### Clinical significance
- Hyperkalemia (eg rhabdomyolysis)
  - ↑ ECF K⁺ → passive K⁺ diffusion inhibited
  - → ↑ intracellular K⁺ (↑ positive charge)
  - → depolarized cell → more excitable
- Hypokalemia
  - ↓ ECF K⁺ → ↑ passive K⁺ diffusion
  - → ↓ intracellular K⁺ (↑ negative charge)
  - → hyperpolarized cell → less excitable

## Equilibrium Potential (E)
- AKA isoelectric potential → no ion movement
- Concentration gradient = electrical gradient
### Determining factors
- Ion movement from high → low concentration
- Charge inside cell at equilibrium
### Values
- Na⁺ → +60 mV
- K⁺ → −90 mV
- Cl⁻ → −70 mV (same as neuron RMP)
- Ca²⁺ → +130 mV (highest)
### Equations
- Nernst equation
  - EP of a single ion
  - E = ±61 log Co/Ci
  - Limitation → single ion only
- Goldman's equation
  - Multiple ions → concentration + permeability
  - Rapid permeability changes → action potential

## Driving Force (DF)
- DF = RMP − EP
  - Na⁺ → −70 − (+60) = −130 mV
- Negative (intracellular) DF
  - Cation (Na⁺) → enters cells
  - Anion (Cl⁻) → moves out of cells
### Gibbs Donnan effect
- Non-diffusible ions (proteins) alter diffusible ion distribution
  - K⁺, Cl⁻ → diffusible
  - Proteins (anions) inside → non-diffusible
- In neuron → RMP negative d/t non-diffusible protein
- In blood vessel
  - Plasma proteins → colloid oncotic pressure → reabsorb fluid
  - Kidney disease → loss of πc → interstitial fluid → edema
