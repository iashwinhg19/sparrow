# Environmental Physiology

## On Level Lands
- Atmospheric pressure → 760 mmHg
- O2 content → 21 %
- PO2 = 21 % of 760 = 160 mmHg

## High Altitude Physiology
- Atmospheric pressure ↓ → ↓ PO2 → hypoxia

### Acclimatisation (compensatory changes)
- ↑ Ventilation → earliest change
  - D/t activation of peripheral chemoreceptors
  - → respiratory alkalosis (CO2 washout → ↓ PCO2, ↓ H+)
  - → hypocalcaemia → tetany (↓ free Ca2+)
- Erythropoietin stimulation → polycythaemia (↑ RBC count)
- ↑ VEGF
  - → ↑ vascularity (↑ angiogenesis)
  - → ↑ O2 diffusion
- Cellular acclimatisation (↑ cytochrome oxidase) → ↑ O2 utilisation
- Release of O2 → right shift of O2 dissociation curve

### Clinical aspect
- Normal mechanism
  - Respiratory alkalosis → ↑ HCO3−, ↓ PCO2, ↓ H+ (page wording)
  - ↓ H+ → ↑ Ca2+–albumin binding → ↓ free Ca2+ → tetany
  - H+ & Ca2+ compete for binding with albumin
- Renal compensation
  - HCO3− excreted via kidney → ↓ HCO3−
  - → prevents respiratory alkalosis → prevents tetany

### High altitude illness
- Acute mountain sickness
  - 6–12 hrs after ascent
  - C/f → nausea, vomiting, confusion
  - Complication → HAPE (high altitude pulmonary oedema)
  - Complication → HACE (high altitude cerebral oedema)
  - Rx → immediate descent, O2 therapy
  - Rx → Ca2+ channel blocker (nifedipine), dexamethasone
- Chronic mountain sickness / Monge's disease
  - Prolonged hypoxia (↑ erythrocytosis)
  - → pulmonary hypertension → cor pulmonale
  - Rx → venesection, acetazolamide

## Deep Sea Physiology
### Etiology
- Divers
- Military operation
- Caisson workers
- Recreation activities

### Mechanism
- Sea level → pressure 1 atm
- Every 10 m → pressure ↑ by 1 atm
- High barometric pressure → gases compressed

### Decompression sickness
- AKA Caisson's disease / diver's disease
- MOA → high pressure → quick ascent → low pressure
  - → compressed N2 released as bubbles
- N2 bubbles
  - Crossing BBB → euphoria (N2 narcosis)
  - Lungs → chokes
  - Joints → bends
  - Blood vessel → obstruction → air embolism → death
- Rx → hyperbaric chamber → slow ascent → slow decompression
  - Less bubble formed
- Also seen in pilots & mountaineers

## Space Physiology
### Effects of microgravity
- Loss of Ca2+ & PO4 from bones → prone to fractures
- Loss of muscle mass
  - Bone + muscle loss → ↓ performance of activity
- ↓↓ RBC count

### Positive G
- Gravitational force → head to foot
- ↓ venous return (venous pooling)
- → ↓ cardiac output → ↓ cerebral perfusion
- → unconsciousness (black out)

### Negative G
- Gravitational force → foot to head
- ↑ venous return → ↑ cardiac output
- → ↑ cerebral perfusion
- → congestion in eyes (red out)
