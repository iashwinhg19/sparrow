# 🌀 Limbic System and Hypothalamus

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The **limbic system**[^ety-limbic] is the old rim of cortex and deep nuclei around the brainstem that handles **emotion, smell and memory**; the **hypothalamus**[^ety-hypothalamus][^pre-hypo] below the thalamus is its output — the **head ganglion of the autonomic nervous system** and the master of the pituitary. The chapter answers:
> - ✅ Which structure does what — **amygdala** (seat of emotions), **hippocampus–Papez circuit** (memory & emotion), **hypothalamic nuclei** (clock, hunger, temperature, sleep, memory, hormones)?
> - ✅ What happens when they fail — **Klüver–Bucy syndrome**, **mesial temporal lobe epilepsy**, **Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome**, **sham rage**?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — The Feeling Brain and Its Control Room

Picture the **limbic system as an emotional newsroom** wrapped around the brainstem: the **amygdala** is the alarm desk (fear, anger), the **hippocampus** is the archive (new memories), the **olfactory** inputs are the oldest wire service, and the **frontal cortex** is the editor who tones the headlines down. The **hypothalamus is the control room** downstairs that turns every headline into body action — heart rate, sweating, hunger, sleep and hormones.

```
LIMBIC CORTEX   emotions · smell · memory      (rim of old cortex = rhinencephalon)
AMYGDALA        genesis / seat of emotions      → regulated by frontal cortex
PAPEZ CIRCUIT   cingulate → entorhinal → hippocampus → fornix → mammillary → MTT → anterior thalamus → cingulate
HYPOTHALAMUS    SCN = clock · arcuate → VMN (stop eating) / lateral (eat) · anterior = cool · posterior = warm
                lateral (orexin) & posterior (histamine) = awake · VLPO = sleep · mammillary = memory
                PVN (oxytocin) · SON (ADH) → posterior pituitary · releasing hormones & dopamine → anterior pituitary
CORTEX ⊖ DMN    remove the cortex → SHAM RAGE
```

## 🧩 Limbic System

### Structure

#### Limbic cortex
- **AKA smell brain / rhinencephalon**[^ety-rhinencephalon][^pre-rhino][^pre-en].
- **Rim of cortical tissue** (the Latin *limbus* = rim) around the brainstem and corpus callosum.
- **Oldest part of the human body in terms of evolution**[^ety-evolution].[^1]
- **Functions:** **Emotions, smell & memory.**

#### Amygdala[^ety-amygdala]
- **Window of the limbic system** — it receives information from every sense and looks out on the world through them.
- **Function:** **Genesis of emotions / seat of emotions**[^ety-emotion][^pre-e].

> [!note] Note
> **Emotions are regulated by the frontal cortex.**[^2]

### Clinical aspect

```
                                Lesion at
               ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
    Bitemporal region, amygdala               Medial temporal lobe
               ↓                                        ↓
     KLÜVER–BUCY SYNDROME                 MESIAL TEMPORAL LOBE EPILEPSY
```

| **Klüver–Bucy syndrome**[^ety-kluver-bucy] (bitemporal[^pre-bi] / amygdala lesion) | **Mesial**[^ety-mesial] **temporal**[^ety-temporal] **lobe epilepsy**[^ety-epilepsy] (medial temporal lobe) |
|---|---|
| **Visual agnosia**[^ety-agnosia][^pre-a] — objects not recognised on sight | **Complex partial seizure**[^ety-seizure] |
| **Hypermetamorphosis**[^ety-hypermetamorphosis][^pre-hyper][^pre-meta] — compulsion to explore the environment | **Uncinate**[^ety-uncinate] **fits** — smell / taste hallucination[^ety-hallucination] |
| **Placidity**[^ety-placidity] — no fears / anger | **Déjà vu**[^ety-deja-vu] — unfamiliar environment seems familiar |
| **Hyperorality / hyperphagia**[^ety-hyperphagia][^suf-phagia] — indiscriminate habit of eating (and mouthing objects) | **Jamais vu** — familiar environment seems unfamiliar |
| **Hypersexuality** | — |

- 🧠 *Why these signs:* without the amygdala, things lose their emotional "tag" — nothing is frightening (placidity), everything must be examined and put in the mouth (hypermetamorphosis, hyperorality), and objects are seen but not recognised for what they mean (visual agnosia).[^3]
- 🧠 *Why déjà vu:* the medial temporal lobe (hippocampus, entorhinal cortex) generates the feeling of familiarity; a seizure there produces that feeling falsely.[^4]

### Papez circuit[^ety-papez]

```
             ┌──────────── Cingulate cortex ◄────────────┐
             │ Cingulum                                   │
             ▼                                            │
      Entorhinal cortex                       Anterior nucleus of thalamus
             │                                            ▲
             ▼                                            │ Mammillothalamic tract
        Hippocampus ────────── Fornix ──────────► Mamillary bodies
```

- **Cingulate**[^ety-cingulate] **cortex** →(**cingulum**)→ **entorhinal**[^ety-entorhinal][^pre-ento] **cortex** → **hippocampus**[^ety-hippocampus] →(**fornix**[^ety-fornix])→ **mamillary bodies**[^ety-mammillary] →(**mammillothalamic tract**)→ **anterior nucleus of thalamus**[^ety-thalamus] → back to the cingulate cortex.
- **Connects the major functions (memory & emotion) of the limbic system.**[^5]

## 🎛 Hypothalamus

**Head ganglion**[^ety-ganglion] **of the autonomic**[^ety-autonomic][^pre-auto] **nervous system.**[^6]

![[Limbic System and Hypothalamus - hypothalamic nuclei.png]]
*Hypothalamic nuclei — posterior hypothalamic nucleus, dorsomedial, ventromedial, paraventricular, anterior hypothalamic, medial and lateral pre-optic, suprachiasmatic, supraoptic and arcuate nuclei, and the mamillary body (cropped from the handwritten page).*

### Functions

#### 1. Circadian rhythm[^ety-circadian][^pre-circa]
- **Dominant pacemaker AKA master clock: Suprachiasmatic**[^ety-suprachiasmatic][^pre-supra] **nucleus (SCN).**
- **Function:** **Synchronize**[^ety-synchronize][^pre-syn] **24 hr bodily functions** (sleep–wake, temperature, cortisol, melatonin).[^7]

#### 2. Regulation of food intake

```
                         ARCUATE NUCLEUS (integration center)
                  ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
     α-MSH (Alpha melanocyte stimulating hormone)   Neuropeptide Y (NPY)
     CART (Cocaine amphetamine regulated transcript) Agouti related peptide (AgRP)
                  ↓                                          ↓
     VENTROMEDIAL NUCLEUS (satiety center)            LATERAL NUCLEUS
                  ↓                                          ↓  Orexin neurotransmitter
            ↓ FOOD INTAKE                              ↑ FOOD INTAKE
```

- **Arcuate**[^ety-arcuate] **nucleus (integration centre)** has two sets of neurons:
  - **α-MSH**[^ety-melanocyte] **+ CART** ("CAR" on the page) → **ventromedial**[^ety-ventromedial] **nucleus (satiety**[^ety-satiety] **centre)** → **↓ food intake**.
  - **Neuropeptide**[^pre-neuro] **Y (NPY) + Agouti**[^ety-agouti] **related peptide (AgRP)** → **lateral nucleus** → **orexin**[^ety-orexin] **neurotransmitter** → **↑ food intake**.[^8]

#### 3. Regulation of body temperature

| **Hot temperature** | **Cold temperature** |
|---|---|
| ↓ **Anterior nucleus** | ↓ **Posterior nucleus** |
| **Sweating** | **Shivering** |
| **Vasodilation**[^ety-vasodilation][^pre-vaso] | **Vasoconstriction**[^ety-vasoconstriction] |
| **Thirst (osmoreceptor**[^ety-osmoreceptor][^pre-osmo]**)** | **↑ Urinary frequency** |

- 🧠 *Memory hook:* the **anterior** hypothalamus is the **heat-loss** (cooling) centre, the **posterior** hypothalamus the **heat-gain** (warming) centre.[^9]

#### 4. Regulation of sleep & wakefulness
- **Awake / arousal state:**
  - **Lateral nucleus: Orexin.**
  - **Posterior nucleus: Histamine**[^ety-histamine].
- **Sleep centre: Ventrolateral pre-optic**[^pre-pre] **nucleus (VLPO).**[^10]

#### 5. Role in memory
- **Mammillary body.**

**Applied aspect:** **Chronic alcoholism → memory impaired** (D/t **degeneration**[^ety-degeneration][^pre-de] **of mammillary bodies**).

**Presentation:**

```
      CONFUSION ─────────── ATAXIA
              \           /
               \         /
                \       /
                 \     /
           CONFABULATION (honest lying)
                  ║
      WERNICKE–KORSAKOFF SYNDROME
```

- **Confusion**[^ety-confusion][^pre-con] · **Ataxia**[^ety-ataxia] · **Confabulation**[^ety-confabulation] **(honest lying)** → **Wernicke–Korsakoff**[^ety-wernicke-korsakoff] **syndrome**[^ety-syndrome].[^11]

#### 6. Endocrine functions

| Output | Nucleus / hormone | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Releasing hormones** | e.g. **CRH** → anterior pituitary → **ACTH** → adrenal cortex → **cortisol** (negative feedback on both) | **Hypothalamo-pituitary axis** |
| **Inhibiting hormone** | **Dopamine (Prolactin**[^ety-prolactin][^pre-pro] **inhibiting factor)** | **Inhibits prolactin release from anterior pituitary** |
| **Paraventricular**[^ety-paraventricular][^pre-para] **nucleus** | **Major: Oxytocin**[^ety-oxytocin]**. Minor: ADH** | Both **stored & released in posterior pituitary**[^ety-pituitary] |
| **Supraoptic**[^ety-supraoptic] **nucleus** | **Major: Vasopressin**[^ety-vasopressin] **(ADH). Minor: Oxytocin** | ″ |

![[Limbic System and Hypothalamus - hypothalamo-pituitary axis.png]]
*Hypothalamo-pituitary axis — releasing hormones: hypothalamus → CRH → anterior pituitary → ACTH → adrenal cortex → cortisol (CORT), with negative feedback arcs back to the pituitary and hypothalamus (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- 🧠 *Two routes out:* releasing / inhibiting hormones go to the **anterior** pituitary through the portal blood; oxytocin and ADH are made in the PVN and SON and travel **down the axons** to the **posterior** pituitary, where they are stored and released.[^12]

### Clinical aspect — Sham rage

```
In a healthy individual:   Cerebral cortex ──⊖──► Hypothalamus (Dorsomedial nucleus) → Sham rage NOT seen
On decortication
(removal of cerebral cortex):   inhibition lost ──► SHAM RAGE
```

- **Sham rage**[^ety-sham-rage] — on a **mild touch** the decorticate animal shows **arching of the back, hissing, biting** — full-blown rage with no real cause ("sham").
- **Centre: dorsomedial**[^ety-dorsomedial] **nucleus**; **removal of the hypothalamus abolishes sham rage.**[^13]
- **Decortication**[^ety-decortication] = removal of the cerebral cortex.

![[Limbic System and Hypothalamus - sham rage.png]]
*Sham rage in a decorticate cat — a mild touch provokes arching of the back, hissing and biting; centre: dorsomedial nucleus; removal of the hypothalamus abolishes sham rage (cropped from the handwritten page).*

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> The limbic system **gives meaning** to what the senses report — emotional colour from the **amygdala**, a place in memory from the **hippocampus–Papez circuit**, and the most ancient sense, smell, wired straight into both. The **hypothalamus turns that meaning into body action**: autonomic tone, hormones, hunger, temperature, sleep and the outward show of emotion. The **cerebral cortex sits above both as a brake** — frontal cortex regulates emotion, and removing the cortex releases sham rage.
>
> Almost every exam question in this chapter is "which nucleus?": SCN = clock, arcuate = integration of hunger, VMN = satiety, lateral = hunger and wakefulness (orexin), anterior = heat loss, posterior = heat gain and wakefulness (histamine), VLPO = sleep, mammillary = memory, PVN = oxytocin, SON = ADH, dorsomedial = rage.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> The amygdala tags stimuli with emotion under frontal cortical control; the Papez loop links emotion to memory; hypothalamic nuclei keep body weight, temperature, sleep–wake timing and hormone levels within their set ranges, and the cortex inhibits the dorsomedial rage centre.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> In chronic alcoholism, thiamine deficiency first damages the mammillary bodies and periventricular grey silently; early signs are subtle forgetfulness and unsteadiness. In mesial temporal sclerosis, brief auras (déjà vu, rising epigastric feeling, odd smells — uncinate fits) precede overt seizures. Unilateral temporal damage is compensated by the other side.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Wernicke–Korsakoff: confusion, ataxia, eye signs, then permanent amnesia with confabulation. Bilateral temporal/amygdala destruction (herpes encephalitis, trauma): Klüver–Bucy syndrome — placidity, hyperorality, hypersexuality, hypermetamorphosis, visual agnosia. Hypothalamic lesions: obesity (VMN), wasting (lateral), hyperthermia (anterior), poikilothermia (posterior), diabetes insipidus (SON/PVN), hyperprolactinaemia (loss of dopamine), narcolepsy (orexin loss).
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → injury (thiamine deficiency / bilateral temporal damage / hypothalamic tumour) → silent nuclear loss → subtle signs (forgetfulness, auras) → overt syndrome (Wernicke–Korsakoff, Klüver–Bucy, DI, obesity) → permanent deficit
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Wernicke's encephalopathy is reversible if thiamine is given before glucose and before Korsakoff amnesia sets in; recognising which hypothalamic nucleus is involved predicts the endocrine and autonomic picture after pituitary surgery or with craniopharyngioma.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Klüver–Bucy syndrome:** "Docile, puts everything in the mouth, hypersexual, after herpes simplex encephalitis" → bilateral amygdala / anterior temporal lesion.
>
> **Mesial temporal lobe epilepsy:** "Uncinate fit — smells burning rubber", "déjà vu / jamais vu", "complex partial seizure with lip-smacking" → hippocampal sclerosis.
>
> **Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome:** "Chronic alcoholic, confused, ataxic, makes up stories (confabulation)" → mammillary body degeneration, thiamine deficiency.
>
> **Sham rage:** "Decorticate cat, mild touch → hissing, arching" → loss of cortical inhibition of the dorsomedial hypothalamus.
>
> **Circadian disorder:** "Jet lag, shift-work", "SCN" → master clock.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Atrophic mammillary bodies on MRI = Wernicke–Korsakoff; hippocampal atrophy with T2 hyperintensity = mesial temporal sclerosis; bilateral temporal T2 lesions = herpes encephalitis (→ Klüver–Bucy).
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Lateral hypothalamic lesion → refusal to eat (can be fatal); posterior lesion → loss of temperature control (poikilothermia); bilateral SON/PVN loss → central diabetes insipidus.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Swapping the hunger and satiety centres**
>
> **What students think:** The ventromedial nucleus makes you eat.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The **ventromedial** nucleus is the **satiety** centre (α-MSH/CART input) — its lesion causes hyperphagia and obesity. The **lateral** nucleus is the **feeding** centre (NPY/AgRP → orexin) — its lesion causes aphagia and starvation. The arcuate nucleus integrates both.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**L**ateral = **L**unch; **V**entro**M**edial = **V**ery **M**uch full."
>
> **Trap 2: Swapping the temperature centres**
>
> **What students think:** The anterior hypothalamus warms the body.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Hot temperature acts on the **anterior** nucleus, which triggers **sweating, vasodilation** (heat loss); cold acts on the **posterior** nucleus, which triggers **shivering, vasoconstriction** (heat gain). An anterior lesion gives hyperthermia; a posterior lesion gives poikilothermia.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**A**nterior = **A**ir-conditioner (cooling); **P**osterior = **P**ullover (warming)."
>
> **Trap 3: Swapping oxytocin and ADH nuclei**
>
> **What students think:** The supraoptic nucleus makes oxytocin.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Both nuclei make both hormones, but the **paraventricular** nucleus makes **mainly oxytocin** and the **supraoptic** nucleus **mainly ADH (vasopressin)**; both release them from the **posterior** pituitary.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**S**upra**O**ptic = **S**alt and **O**smolality (ADH); **P**ara**V**entricular = **P**arturition (oxytocin)."
>
> **Trap 4: Thinking the hypothalamus causes sham rage only when it is overactive**
>
> **What students think:** Sham rage needs a lesion of the hypothalamus.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Sham rage appears after **decortication** — the cortex normally inhibits the dorsomedial hypothalamus; remove the cortex and the rage pattern is released. Removing the **hypothalamus** itself **abolishes** sham rage.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Cortex off → rage on; hypothalamus off → rage gone."
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing the Wernicke triad with the page's triangle**
>
> **What students think:** Confabulation is part of Wernicke's encephalopathy.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The classic Wernicke triad is confusion, ataxia and ophthalmoplegia (nystagmus); confabulation and amnesia belong to Korsakoff psychosis. The page combines them as Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome — confusion, ataxia, confabulation.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Wernicke = acute (eyes, gait, confusion) · Korsakoff = chronic (memory, confabulation)."

>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Learning and Memory]]**

>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Pituitary Gland]]**
> Releasing and inhibiting hormones of the anterior pituitary, the portal system, and the PVN/SON origin of oxytocin and ADH continue there.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Regulation of Body Temperature]]**
> The anterior (heat-loss) and posterior (heat-gain) hypothalamic centres, the set point and fever are covered in full.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[EEG and Sleep]]**
> VLPO (sleep), orexin (lateral) and histamine (posterior) are the hypothalamic switches of sleep and wakefulness; orexin loss causes narcolepsy.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Hearing, Smell and Taste]]**
> Smell is the only sense that reaches the limbic cortex without a thalamic relay — the reason odours trigger emotion and memory, and why uncinate fits are olfactory.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[CSF, CBF and BBB]]**
> The OVLT and subfornical organ — circumventricular organs without a BBB — let the hypothalamus sense plasma osmolality and angiotensin II for thirst.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Alcoholic patients get thiamine before glucose; temporal lobe epilepsy and herpes encephalitis are recognised by their limbic signs; craniopharyngioma and pituitary surgery produce hypothalamic syndromes (DI, obesity, temperature instability).
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Expect: window/seat of emotions (amygdala), master clock (SCN), satiety vs feeding centre, heat-loss vs heat-gain centre, sleep centre (VLPO), major hormone of PVN and SON, Papez circuit order, Klüver–Bucy features, sham rage, and the mammillary bodies in Wernicke–Korsakoff.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Limbic cortex = **smell brain / rhinencephalon**, a **rim** of old cortex; functions **emotions, smell & memory**.
> 2. **Amygdala** = **window of the limbic system**, **seat (genesis) of emotions**; emotions regulated by **frontal cortex**.
> 3. **Bitemporal / amygdala lesion → Klüver–Bucy**: visual agnosia, hypermetamorphosis, placidity, hyperorality/hyperphagia, hypersexuality.
> 4. **Medial temporal lobe → mesial temporal lobe epilepsy**: complex partial seizure, **uncinate fits** (smell/taste hallucination), **déjà vu**, **jamais vu**.
> 5. **Papez circuit**: cingulate → (cingulum) → entorhinal → hippocampus → (**fornix**) → mamillary bodies → (**mammillothalamic tract**) → anterior thalamic nucleus → cingulate; links **memory & emotion**.
> 6. Hypothalamus = **head ganglion of the ANS**.
> 7. **SCN = master clock** (circadian rhythm).
> 8. **Arcuate** = integration centre: **α-MSH, CART → VMN (satiety) → ↓ intake**; **NPY, AgRP → lateral nucleus (orexin) → ↑ intake**.
> 9. **Anterior** = hot → sweating, vasodilation, thirst; **posterior** = cold → shivering, vasoconstriction, ↑ urinary frequency.
> 10. Awake: **lateral (orexin)**, **posterior (histamine)**; sleep: **VLPO**.
> 11. **Mammillary body** = memory; chronic alcoholism → **Wernicke–Korsakoff** (confusion, ataxia, **confabulation = honest lying**).
> 12. **PVN → mainly oxytocin; SON → mainly ADH** — stored & released in **posterior pituitary**; **dopamine = prolactin-inhibiting factor**.
> 13. **Sham rage** after **decortication**; centre **dorsomedial nucleus**; abolished by removal of the hypothalamus.

[^1]: The page says "oldest part of human body"; it means the oldest part of the **cerebral cortex**. Limbic cortex is phylogenetically old **allocortex** (three-layered: archicortex of the hippocampus, paleocortex of the olfactory areas) as opposed to the six-layered neocortex. Paul Broca named the *grand lobe limbique* in 1878.
[^2]: The orbitofrontal and ventromedial prefrontal cortex damp amygdala output; damage there (e.g. Phineas Gage, 1848) produces disinhibited, impulsive emotional behaviour.
[^3]: Heinrich Klüver and Paul Bucy described the syndrome in 1937–1939 in rhesus monkeys after bilateral temporal lobectomy; Klüver called the visual agnosia "psychic blindness". In humans it follows herpes simplex encephalitis, trauma or frontotemporal dementia.
[^4]: Mesial temporal lobe epilepsy, usually from hippocampal sclerosis, is the commonest drug-resistant focal epilepsy in adults. "Complex partial seizure" is now called a focal impaired-awareness seizure (ILAE 2017). Uncinate fits arise from the uncus, the hook at the front of the parahippocampal gyrus near the primary olfactory cortex.
[^5]: James Papez proposed the circuit in 1937 as the anatomical basis of emotion; today it is regarded mainly as a memory circuit, with the amygdala as the key emotional structure. Textbooks usually route cingulate → parahippocampal/entorhinal cortex → hippocampus; the mammillothalamic tract is also called the tract of Vicq d'Azyr.
[^6]: The hypothalamus weighs only about 4 g (< 1 % of the brain). Classically its anterior part drives parasympathetic and its posterior part sympathetic responses.
[^7]: The SCN receives light information directly from melanopsin-containing retinal ganglion cells through the retinohypothalamic tract, which resets the clock each day; it drives melatonin secretion from the pineal gland at night through a sympathetic relay.
[^8]: The arcuate α-MSH/CART neurons are the POMC neurons — α-MSH acts on melanocortin-4 receptors (MC4R) to reduce eating; AgRP blocks the same receptors. Leptin (from fat) stimulates POMC/CART and inhibits NPY/AgRP; ghrelin (from the stomach) does the reverse. The page writes the abbreviation "CAR"; the standard abbreviation is **CART** (cocaine- and amphetamine-regulated transcript). Orexin is also called hypocretin. Classic lesion experiments: VMN lesion → hyperphagia and obesity; lateral hypothalamic lesion → aphagia.
[^9]: The thermostatic set point lies in the preoptic–anterior hypothalamus. Cold-induced diuresis (the page's "↑ urinary frequency") follows peripheral vasoconstriction, which shifts blood centrally, raises central volume and suppresses ADH. Thirst under heat follows sweat loss and rising plasma osmolality, sensed by osmoreceptors in the anterior hypothalamus (OVLT).
[^10]: The VLPO uses GABA and galanin to switch off the arousal centres (tuberomammillary histamine neurons in the posterior hypothalamus, locus coeruleus, raphe, lateral hypothalamic orexin) — the sleep "flip-flop switch". Loss of orexin neurons causes narcolepsy; blocking histamine H₁ receptors is why older antihistamines make people drowsy.
[^11]: Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome is caused by **thiamine (vitamin B₁) deficiency**. The classic **Wernicke triad** is confusion, ataxia and ophthalmoplegia (with nystagmus); **Korsakoff psychosis** adds anterograde and retrograde amnesia with confabulation. Thiamine must be given before glucose in alcoholic patients.
[^12]: The anterior pituitary is reached by the hypothalamo-hypophyseal portal system (releasing hormones TRH, CRH, GnRH, GHRH; inhibiting hormones somatostatin and dopamine). Oxytocin and ADH are made in magnocellular neurons, carried down the hypothalamo-hypophyseal tract bound to neurophysins, and stored in the posterior pituitary. Cutting the stalk raises prolactin (dopamine can no longer reach the lactotrophs) while other anterior pituitary hormones fall.
[^13]: Cannon and Britton (1925) described sham rage in decorticate cats; Philip Bard (1928) showed it depends on the posterior hypothalamus. The page assigns the centre to the dorsomedial nucleus; most modern texts place the defence/rage area in the perifornical and dorsomedial hypothalamus.

[^ety-arcuate]: **arcuate** — Latin *arcuatus*, "bow-shaped, curved" (*arcus*, bow) → the arcuate (infundibular) nucleus curves around the base of the third ventricle; same root as *arcuate fasciculus* and *arc*.
