# 😴 EEG and Sleep

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> **Electro encephalography (EEG)**[^ety-electroencephalography][^pre-electro][^pre-en][^suf-graphy] **records brain waves** — the **summation of local potentials** of the cortex, i.e. the overall electrical activity of the brain. The faster the brain works, the faster the waves (**frequency ∝ brain activity**). Sleep is read off the same waves, and everything in this chapter answers three questions:
> - ✅ **Which wave goes with which state?** (α relaxed · β attention / REM · θ stage 1 · spindle + K-complex stage 2 · δ deep sleep)
> - ✅ **How do NREM and REM sleep differ** — in EEG, body, hormones, dreams and disorders?
> - ✅ **Which brain areas switch us awake (RAS, raphe, hypothalamus) and asleep (VLPO, cholinergic REM-on neurons, pineal)?**

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Stadium Crowd Seen from Above

The EEG electrode is a microphone hung above a stadium of cortical neurons. When everyone chats independently (alert, thinking) you hear a **fast, low hum** (β). When the crowd relaxes with eyes shut they sway together gently (α). As sleep deepens they all do one **slow, huge Mexican wave** together (δ). In REM the crowd is noisy again (β, "paradoxical") — but the players on the field are **tied to their seats** (atonia), and only their eyes and breathing still move.

```
STATE                     WAVE    Hz        WHERE / FEATURE
Awake, alert, thinking    β       13–30     prefrontal cortex          → attention
Awake, relaxed, eyes shut α        8–12     occipital + parietal        → inattention (α block: α → β)
NREM stage 1  (N1)        θ        4–7      (θ also hippocampus: memory)
NREM stage 2  (N2)        spindle + K-complex  (8–12 on the page)   45 % of the night
NREM stage 3+4 (N3)       δ        1–3      slowest — deep sleep, GH ↑, sleepwalking
REM                       β        —        EEG awake, body paralysed (atonia), dreams, prolactin ↑
Focused attention         γ       > 60      fastest
```

## ⚡ Electro Encephalography (EEG)

- **Records brain waves.**
- Represents **summation**[^ety-summation][^suf-ation] **of local potentials**[^ety-potential] (**overall electrical activity of brain**).[^1]

### Characteristics of EEG waves

- **Frequency**[^ety-frequency]:
  - Number of waves / second.
  - **Frequency ∝ brain activity.**
- **Amplitude**[^ety-amplitude]:
  - Height of wave.

### Types

| Features | **α wave**[^ety-greek-waves] | **β wave** | **θ wave** | **δ wave** | **γ wave** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Waveform** | regular rhythm (trace below) | fast, low, irregular (trace below) | slower (trace below) | slow, large (trace below) | — |
| **Significance** | Wave of **inattention**[^ety-attention][^pre-in] (**relaxed state**) | Wave of **attention / wakefulness** | • **Memory** • **Sleep** | **Deep sleep** | **Focused attention** |
| **Frequency** | **8–12 Hz** | **13–30 Hz** | **4–7 Hz** | **1–3 Hz (slowest)** | **> 60 Hz (max)**[^2] |
| **Source** | **Occipital & parietal**[^ety-occipital] **cortex**[^ety-cortex] | **Prefrontal cortex**[^pre-pre] | **Hippocampus**[^ety-hippocampus] (short term → long term memory) | — | — |

## 🌙 Sleep

### Types

#### 1. Non-rapid eye movement (NREM) sleep

- **Deep sleep.**
- **↑ delta activity.**

**Stages:**

| Features | **1** | **2** | **3** | **4** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | **4–7 Hz** | **8–12 Hz** | — | — |
| **Wave** | **Theta** | **Sleep spindle**[^ety-spindle] on a **sinusoidal**[^ety-sinusoidal][^suf-oid] wave + **K-complex**[^ety-k-complex] (**biphasic**[^ety-biphasic][^pre-bi] **wave**) | **Delta** — appears in stage 3 | **Delta** — max activity in stage 4 |
| **New nomenclature**[^ety-nomenclature] | **N1** | **N2** | **N3 (deep sleep)** | **N3 (deep sleep)** |

![[EEG and Sleep - NREM stage 2 sleep spindle and K-complex.png]]
*Stage 2 (N2) NREM sleep as drawn on the page — a waxing–waning **sleep spindle** riding on a sinusoidal wave, then the large **biphasic K-complex** (cropped from the handwritten page).*[^4]

**Physiological changes (NREM):**

| # | Parameter | Change |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **RR** | **↓** |
| 2 | **BP** | **↓** |
| 3 | **Temperature**[^ety-temperature] | **↓↓** |
| 4 | **Respiration**[^ety-respiration][^pre-re] | **Slow & deep** |

> [!tip] Why NREM is the "rest-and-repair" sleep
> Parasympathetic tone rises, so heart rate, BP and breathing rate fall, the thermostat is set lower, and growth hormone surges (see hormonal changes) — the body idles while the cortex synchronises into slow δ waves.[^5]

#### 2. Rapid eye movement (REM) sleep[^ety-rem]

- **EEG: β wave.**
- AKA **paradoxical**[^ety-paradoxical][^pre-para] **sleep** (**EEG resembles wakefulness**).

**Eyeball movement — mechanism: Ponto-geniculo-occipital (PGO) spikes**[^ety-pgo]

The burst starts in the pons, excites the **lateral geniculate body** — activating its **magnocellular**[^ety-magnocellular][^pre-magno] pathway — and reaches the occipital lobe; the result is the darting eye movement that gives REM its name:

```
          Pons
            ↓ (+)
Lateral geniculate body — activation of magnocellular pathway
            ↓ (+)
      Occipital lobe
            ↓
     Eyeball movement
```

**Physiological changes (REM):**

1. **Muscle atonia**[^ety-atonia][^pre-a][^suf-ia] → **prevents acting out of dreams.**
   - **Exceptions:**
     - **Diaphragm**[^ety-diaphragm][^pre-dia]: for respiration.
     - **Extra ocular muscles**[^ety-extraocular][^pre-extra]: for eyeball movement.
2. **Genital organ (penis / clitoris) enlargement.**[^ety-genital][^6]

![[EEG and Sleep - REM sleep polysomnogram EOG and EMG.png]]

### Hormonal changes[^ety-hormone]

| # | Hormone | Change in sleep |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Growth hormone** | **↑ in stages 3 & 4 of NREM sleep** |
| 2 | **Prolactin**[^ety-prolactin][^pre-pro][^suf-in] | **↑ in REM sleep**[^7] |
| 3 | **Luteinizing hormone**[^ety-luteinizing] | **Nocturnal**[^pre-noct] **LH surge in puberty**[^ety-puberty] |
| 4 | **Melatonin**[^ety-melatonin] | **Sleep-inducing hormone secreted**[^ety-secretion] by **pineal**[^ety-pineal] **gland** |

### Dreams

| Features | **NREM sleep** | **REM sleep** |
|---|---|---|
| **Duration** | Shorter | **Longer (early morning)** |
| **Type of dream** | Conceptual | **Emotional** |
| **Content of dream** | Related to day to day activities | **Unrelated** to day to day activities |

> [!note] Why REM dreams are long, emotional and strange
> REM periods lengthen towards morning (see the hypnogram), the limbic system is highly active while the prefrontal "logic" cortex is relatively quiet — so the morning dream is long, vivid, emotional and unrelated to the day's events. NREM mentation is short, thought-like and replays the day.[^8]

| Group | NREM | REM |
|---|---|---|

| **Neonate**[^ety-neonate][^pre-neo] | — | **50 %** |
| **Elders** | **Less deep sleep** | — |

### Regulation of sleep[^ety-regulation]

**Areas promoting wakefulness:**

| Areas | Neurotransmitter |
|---|---|
| 1. **Reticular activating system (RAS)**[^ety-reticular][^ety-activation] | **Norepinephrine**[^ety-norepinephrine] → **widespread brain activation** |
| 2. **Raphe nuclei**[^ety-raphe] | **Serotonin**[^ety-serotonin] |
| 3. **Hypothalamus**[^ety-hypothalamus][^pre-hypo] — **lateral** | **Orexin**[^ety-orexin] |
| — **posterior** | **Histamine**[^ety-histamine] |

- **EEG alerting response:** **high frequency stimulation**[^ety-stimulation] **of RAS → awakens person.**[^10]

**Areas promoting sleep:**

1. **Hypothalamus: ventrolateral pre optic nucleus (VLPON)**[^ety-preoptic][^pre-ventro].
2. **Cholinergic**[^ety-cholinergic][^suf-ergic] **neurons → acetylcholine (facilitates**[^ety-facilitation] **REM sleep).**
3. **Pineal gland → melatonin.**

**Mechanism:**

- **NREM-on neurons** release **GABA**[^ety-gaba], which acts on the **posterior hypothalamus**: its **histamine release is inhibited**[^ety-inhibition] → **NREM sleep**.
- **REM-on neurons** release **acetylcholine** → **REM sleep**.

```
   NREM-on neurons                     REM-on neurons
         ↓                                   ↓
       GABA                            ACETYLCHOLINE
         ↓                                   ↓
 Posterior hypothalamus                  REM sleep
         ✖  ← release inhibited
     Histamine
         ↓
     NREM sleep
```

### 🌀 Parasomnias[^ety-parasomnia][^pre-somno]

**NREM parasomnias:**

1. **Sleep walking / somnambulism**[^ety-somnambulism][^suf-ism]: in **stages 3 & 4 (deep sleep)**.
2. **Sleep talking.**
3. **Bruxism**[^ety-bruxism] / **teeth grinding**: in **stage 2**.
   - Seen in **emotionally neglected children**.
4. **Nocturnal enuresis**[^ety-enuresis][^suf-esis] / **bed wetting**.
5. **Night terrors / pavor nocturnus**[^ety-pavor].

**REM parasomnias:**

1. **Narcolepsy**[^ety-narcolepsy][^suf-lepsy]: **↑ day time sleepiness.**[^11]
2. **Nightmares**[^ety-nightmare]: **terror dreams in REM.**

> [!warning] Night terror vs nightmare — the classic pair
> **Night terror (pavor nocturnus)** = NREM stage 3/4, first third of the night, the child screams but **cannot be woken and remembers nothing**. **Nightmare** = REM, last third of the night, the dreamer **wakes fully and remembers the dream**. Same logic for sleepwalking (deep NREM — no memory) vs acting out dreams (REM — only when atonia fails).[^12]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> **The EEG is a readout of how synchronised the cortex is.** When neurons work independently (attention) their tiny potentials cancel into a fast, low β; as the thalamus and brainstem withdraw their drive, the cortex falls into step and the waves become slower and bigger — α (relaxed), θ (N1), spindles and K-complexes (N2), δ (N3). Frequency therefore tracks brain activity, and amplitude tracks synchrony.
>
> REM breaks the rule on purpose: the cortex is driven again by pontine cholinergic REM-on neurons (β EEG, PGO spikes, eye movements, dreams), while the brainstem paralyses every skeletal muscle except the diaphragm and the extra-ocular muscles so that the dream is not acted out. Every NREM-vs-REM question — EEG, body, hormones, dreams, parasomnias — follows from "NREM = synchronised and resting; REM = awake brain in a paralysed body".

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Wakefulness is held up by the RAS (norepinephrine), raphe (serotonin), lateral hypothalamus (orexin) and posterior hypothalamus (histamine). At night the VLPO's GABA switches these off and the sleeper descends N1 (θ) → N2 (spindles, K-complexes) → N3 (δ), then rises into REM — a 90-minute ultradian cycle repeated 4–6 times, with N3 front-loaded and REM back-loaded (adult: NREM 75 %, stage 2 45 %, REM 25 %).
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Sleep loss or a shifted clock is first compensated: after deprivation the next night shows "rebound" N3 and REM, and the day is carried by extra arousal-system drive and caffeine. Ageing shortens N3 (elders: less deep sleep); in children the deep N3 of the first cycles is where partial arousals happen — sleepwalking, night terrors, bed wetting — usually outgrown.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> When a switch fails the stages leak into each other. Loss of orexin neurons (narcolepsy) lets REM intrude into the day — sleep attacks, cataplexy, sleep paralysis, hypnagogic hallucinations — and REM appears within minutes of sleep onset. Failure of REM atonia makes patients act out their dreams (REM behaviour disorder). Obstructive sleep apnoea fragments sleep so N3 and REM never consolidate, causing daytime sleepiness and hypertension.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal sleep architecture → trigger (deprivation, ageing, orexin loss, airway collapse) → fragmented or lightened sleep → compensation (rebound sleep, extra arousal drive) → decompensation (daytime sleepiness, REM intrusion) → full sleep disorder
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> The stage in which a symptom occurs names the disorder and its treatment: deep-NREM partial arousals in children need reassurance and safety, REM-intrusion disorders need stimulants or sodium oxybate, and a polysomnogram (EEG + EOG + EMG) is the test that proves which stage is involved.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Relaxed wakefulness:** "Eyes closed, 8–12 Hz over the occiput" → α rhythm from the occipital and parietal cortex; it disappears on eye opening (α block → β).
>
> **NREM stage 2 (N2):** "Sleep spindles", "K-complex", "biphasic wave" → the commonest stage (45 % of sleep); also the stage of bruxism.
>
> **Deep sleep (N3):** "δ waves, 1–3 Hz, slowest", "growth hormone peak", "sleepwalking", "night terrors", "bed wetting" → stages 3 + 4 of NREM, front-loaded in the first third of the night.
>
> **REM sleep:** "Paradoxical sleep", "EEG looks awake", "PGO spikes", "muscle atonia except diaphragm and extra-ocular muscles", "penile tumescence", "vivid emotional dreams in the early morning", "prolactin ↑" → REM.
>
> **Narcolepsy:** "Excessive daytime sleepiness + sudden loss of tone with laughter (cataplexy) + sleep-onset REM" → orexin (hypocretin) neuron loss in the lateral hypothalamus.
>
> **Sedating antihistamines:** "Drowsy after an H1 blocker" → posterior hypothalamic histamine is a wake signal.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Hypnogram: staircase down to stage 4 in the first cycles, REM blocks lengthening towards morning; polysomnogram: boxed eye-movement bursts on the EOG with a flat chin EMG = REM.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Neonates spend 50 % of sleep in REM, adults 25 %; elders lose deep sleep. A REM onset within 15 minutes of sleep (sleep-onset REM) is pathological and points to narcolepsy.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "Deep sleep = REM"**
>
> **What students think:** REM is the deepest sleep because dreams happen there.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The page calls **NREM** the deep sleep, with ↑ δ activity, and stage 3 + 4 (N3) as "deep sleep". REM has a β (awake-like) EEG — hence "paradoxical". REM is "deep" only in the sense that muscle tone is lowest and the sleeper can be hard to arouse.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** δ = deep = NREM N3; β = REM = "awake brain, paralysed body".
>
> **Trap 2: Mixing up α and β significance**
>
> **What students think:** α, being the first letter, must be the alert rhythm.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** α (8–12 Hz, occipital–parietal) is the wave of **inattention** — relaxed, eyes closed. β (13–30 Hz, prefrontal) is the wave of attention/wakefulness and also the REM EEG. The α block is α being replaced by β when attention is engaged.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**A**lpha = **A**t ease; **B**eta = **B**usy."
>
> **Trap 3: Total atonia in REM**
>
> **What students think:** Every muscle is paralysed in REM.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Two exceptions are on the page: the **diaphragm** (otherwise we would stop breathing every 90 minutes) and the **extra-ocular muscles** (the rapid eye movements themselves). Atonia exists to prevent acting out dreams.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "REM spares the muscles that Breathe and Look."
>
> **Trap 4: Placing parasomnias in the wrong sleep type**
>
> **What students think:** All sleep disturbances with frightening content are REM events.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Night terrors, sleepwalking and bed wetting are NREM (stage 3/4) arousal disorders; bruxism is stage 2. Only nightmares (and narcolepsy, which is REM intruding into wakefulness) belong to REM. The giveaway is memory: NREM events are not remembered, REM dreams are.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Screams without a story = NREM; story without a scream = REM."
>
> **Trap 5: Wrong hormone for the stage**
>
> **What students think:** All sleep hormones peak in REM.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Growth hormone peaks in stages 3 & 4 of NREM (slow-wave sleep), prolactin rises with REM per the page, LH shows a nocturnal surge at puberty, and melatonin from the pineal induces sleep.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "GH grows in the deep (δ); prolactin plays in REM."

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Limbic System and Hypothalamus]]**
> The hypothalamus holds both switches of this chapter — the VLPO (sleep) and the lateral (orexin) and posterior (histamine) wake centres — and the hippocampus of the limbic system generates θ and turns short-term into long-term memory.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Synaptic Transmission and Neurotransmitters]]**
> Norepinephrine, serotonin, histamine, orexin, acetylcholine and GABA — the transmitters of the sleep–wake switch — and the postsynaptic potentials whose summation the EEG records.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Vision]]**
> The lateral geniculate body and its magnocellular layers relay PGO spikes to the occipital lobe; the same pathway carries motion vision when awake.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Pituitary Gland]]**
> Growth hormone peaks in N3 and prolactin rises in sleep — the reason GH stimulation testing and prolactin sampling must respect the sleep–wake cycle.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Male Reproduction]]** and **[[Female Reproduction]]**
> The nocturnal LH surge in puberty is the first hormonal sign of awakening of the GnRH pulse generator; REM erection (nocturnal penile tumescence) separates organic from psychogenic erectile dysfunction.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Neural Regulation of Respiration]]**
> In NREM respiration becomes slow and deep under metabolic control; in REM only the diaphragm keeps breathing — the background to sleep apnoea and sleep hypoventilation.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Epilepsy diagnosis (spikes on EEG), brain-death assessment (electrocerebral silence), anaesthesia depth monitoring, narcolepsy (multiple sleep latency test), obstructive sleep apnoea and childhood parasomnias all use this chapter's waves and stages.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Expect one-liners: frequency of each wave and its source, the α block, spindle and K-complex = stage 2, δ = N3, paradoxical sleep = REM, PGO spikes, the two muscles spared in REM, GH vs prolactin, NREM 75 % / REM 25 % / neonate 50 %, VLPO and orexin, and NREM vs REM parasomnias.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **EEG** = summation of local potentials; **frequency ∝ brain activity**, amplitude = height of the wave.
> 2. **α 8–12 Hz** — inattention / relaxed — **occipital & parietal**; **β 13–30 Hz** — attention — **prefrontal cortex**; **θ 4–7 Hz** — memory, sleep — **hippocampus**; **δ 1–3 Hz** — deep sleep (slowest); **γ > 60 Hz** — focused attention (max).
> 3. **α block** = replacement of α by β.
> 4. NREM = **deep sleep, ↑ δ**; stage 1 **θ** (N1) · stage 2 **sleep spindle + K-complex (biphasic)** (N2) · stages 3–4 **δ** (N3).
> 5. NREM body: **RR ↓, BP ↓, temperature ↓↓, respiration slow & deep**.
> 6. REM = **β EEG** = **paradoxical sleep**; eye movements by **PGO spikes** (pons → LGB magnocellular → occipital lobe).
> 7. REM: **muscle atonia** (prevents acting out dreams) — **except diaphragm and extra-ocular muscles**; **penile/clitoral enlargement**.
> 8. Hormones: **GH ↑ in stages 3 & 4 NREM**, **prolactin ↑ in REM**, **nocturnal LH surge in puberty**, **melatonin** (pineal) induces sleep.
> 9. Dreams: REM = **longer (early morning), emotional, unrelated** to the day; NREM = shorter, conceptual, related.
> 10. One cycle = **90 min (ultradian)** = 1 NREM + 1 REM; **towards morning sleep lightens** (less stages 3 & 4).
> 11. Adults **NREM 75 % (stage 2 = 45 %), REM 25 %**; **neonate REM 50 %**; **elders less deep sleep**.
> 12. Wake: **RAS (NE), raphe (serotonin), lateral hypothalamus (orexin), posterior hypothalamus (histamine)**; sleep: **VLPON**, **cholinergic → ACh → REM**, **pineal → melatonin**; NREM-on → GABA → ⊣ histamine.
> 13. NREM parasomnias: **sleepwalking (stage 3–4), sleep talking, bruxism (stage 2, emotionally neglected children), enuresis, night terrors**; REM: **narcolepsy, nightmares**.

[^1]: The EEG is generated mainly by the summed excitatory and inhibitory **postsynaptic potentials** of the vertically aligned dendrites of cortical pyramidal cells, not by action potentials — which is why it is called a summation of *local* (graded) potentials. Thalamocortical circuits pace its rhythms.
[^2]: Textbook bands vary slightly: δ 0.5–4 Hz, θ 4–8 Hz, α 8–13 Hz, β 13–30 Hz, γ > 30 Hz (often quoted 30–100 Hz). The page's values (δ 1–3 Hz, γ > 60 Hz) are kept in the body.
[^3]: The α block is also called *desynchronisation* or the **Berger effect**, after Hans Berger, who recorded the first human EEG (1924, published 1929) and saw the α rhythm vanish when the subject opened the eyes. Desynchronised = low amplitude, high frequency.
[^4]: In the AASM scoring rules (2007) stages 3 and 4 were merged into **N3** (slow-wave sleep, δ ≥ 20 % of the epoch) — hence the page's "new nomenclature". Sleep spindles are usually quoted at **11–16 Hz** (sigma band) and the K-complex as a sharp negative wave followed by a slower positive one lasting ≥ 0.5 s; the page gives 8–12 Hz for stage 2, which is kept in the body.
[^5]: In NREM sympathetic tone falls and parasympathetic tone rises, so heart rate also falls; the hypothalamic temperature set-point is lowered. Breathing is regular and under metabolic (chemical) control.
[^6]: REM also brings **irregular** heart rate, BP and breathing, loss of temperature regulation (poikilothermia), and ↑ brain O₂ use. Atonia is produced by medullary glycine/GABA neurons that inhibit spinal motor neurons; when it fails, patients act out dreams (REM sleep behaviour disorder, an early sign of synucleinopathies). REM erection = **nocturnal penile tumescence**, used to tell organic from psychogenic erectile dysfunction.
[^7]: Prolactin rises soon after sleep onset and peaks in the early morning, regardless of stage in most textbooks; the page ties its rise to REM sleep, which is kept in the body. Cortisol, by contrast, is lowest at sleep onset and peaks just before waking.
[^8]: When subjects are woken from REM, most report vivid narrative dreams; woken from NREM, fewer report anything, and what they report is thought-like. Functional imaging in REM shows active limbic and visual association cortex with a relatively quiet dorsolateral prefrontal cortex.
[^9]: A night has **4–6 cycles**. N3 dominates the first third of the night; REM periods lengthen with each cycle, so the last third is REM-rich — the basis of "towards morning sleep lightens" and of the early-morning dreams in the dreams table. A hypnogram is the summary graph of a **polysomnogram** (EEG + EOG + EMG + respiration + oxygen saturation).
[^10]: The RAS's norepinephrine comes mainly from the **locus coeruleus**; acetylcholine from the pedunculopontine and laterodorsal tegmental nuclei also activates the cortex. Lesions of the RAS (or bilateral thalamus) cause coma. The "EEG alerting / arousal response" = α replaced by β on stimulation.
[^11]: Narcolepsy is classed as a disorder of hypersomnolence with **REM intrusion into wakefulness**; the tetrad is excessive daytime sleepiness, **cataplexy** (sudden loss of tone with emotion), **sleep paralysis** and **hypnagogic hallucinations**, with sleep-onset REM periods. Type 1 is caused by loss of **orexin (hypocretin)** neurons in the lateral hypothalamus.
[^12]: Night terrors and sleepwalking occur on partial arousal from N3, mostly in the first third of the night in children; nightmares occur in REM, mostly in the last third. Nocturnal enuresis is also mostly an N3 arousal event. Sleep talking (somniloquy) can occur in any stage.
[^ety-electroencephalography]: **electroencephalography** — Greek *ēlektron*, "amber" (rubbed amber gives a static charge) + *en-*, "in" + *kephalē*, "head" + *-graphia*, "writing, recording" → recording the electricity inside the head; first human EEG by Hans Berger (German psychiatrist, 1873–1941). **Differentiate the four sleep-study channels:** EEG (brain) · EOG (eyes) · EMG (muscle) · ECG (heart).
[^ety-summation]: **summation** — Latin *summa*, "the total, the highest" + *-ation*, the process → the process of adding up. **Differentiate:** *spatial* summation (many synapses at once — the EEG) vs *temporal* summation (one synapse firing repeatedly).
[^ety-potential]: **potential** — Latin *potentia*, "power, capacity" (*posse*, to be able) → a stored ability to do electrical work, measured as voltage. **Differentiate:** *local (graded) potential* — EPSP/IPSP, decremental, summable vs *action potential* — all-or-none, propagated, not summable.
[^ety-frequency]: **frequency** — Latin *frequens*, "crowded, repeated often" + *-ency*, the quality → how often a wave repeats each second, in hertz (Hz), after Heinrich Hertz (German physicist, 1857–1894).
[^ety-amplitude]: **amplitude** — Latin *amplus*, "large, wide" + *-itude*, the quality → the height of a wave (µV in EEG). **Differentiate:** frequency = how often, amplitude = how big; as arousal rises, frequency rises and amplitude falls.
[^ety-greek-waves]: **α, β, θ, δ, γ waves** — Greek letters used as labels: Hans Berger named *alpha* (the first rhythm he saw, eyes closed) and *beta* (the faster one that replaced it); *delta* and *theta* were named later by W. Grey Walter, and *gamma* later still. The letters record the order of naming, not of frequency.
[^ety-attention]: **attention / inattention** — Latin *ad-*, "towards" + *tendere*, "to stretch" → stretching the mind towards something; *in-*, "not" → inattention. α = relaxed inattention, β = attention, γ = focused attention.
[^ety-occipital]: **occipital / parietal** — Latin *occiput*, "back of the head" (*ob-*, "against" + *caput*, "head"); *paries, parietis*, "wall" → the side walls of the skull. The visual cortex lies in the occipital lobe, so α (eyes closed) is recorded best over the back of the head.
[^ety-cortex]: **cortex** — Latin, "bark, rind" → the thin outer grey layer of the brain that generates the EEG. Same word in *renal* and *adrenal cortex* (outer layer); vs *medulla* (inner pith).
[^ety-hippocampus]: **hippocampus** — Greek *hippos*, "horse" + *kampos*, "sea monster" → seahorse, for its curled shape (named by Giulio Cesare Aranzi, 1587). It consolidates short-term into long-term memory — the source of θ on the page.
[^ety-spindle]: **sleep spindle** — Old English *spinel*, the tapering rod used in spinning → a short burst that waxes and wanes, shaped like wool wound on a spindle. **Differentiate:** spindle (a 0.5–2 s burst of fast waves, thalamic) vs K-complex (one large, slow biphasic wave).
[^ety-sinusoidal]: **sinusoidal** — Latin *sinus*, "curve, fold, bay" + Greek *-oeidēs*, "like" → shaped like a sine curve, smooth and regular. The same *sinus* gives the paranasal and carotid sinuses (a curved pocket).
[^ety-k-complex]: **K-complex** — described by Alfred Loomis and colleagues (1937–38); the letter is often linked to the *knock* that could evoke it in a sleeper — origin uncertain. *Complex*, Latin *complexus*, "woven together" → a wave made of several parts (sharp negative + slower positive).
[^ety-biphasic]: **biphasic** — Latin *bi-*, "two" + Greek *phasis*, "appearance" + *-ic* → having two phases (one up, one down). **Differentiate:** monophasic · biphasic (K-complex) · triphasic waves (hepatic encephalopathy).
[^ety-nomenclature]: **nomenclature** — Latin *nomen*, "name" + *calare*, "to call" → a system of naming. The 2007 AASM nomenclature renamed stages 1 / 2 / 3 + 4 / REM as N1 / N2 / N3 / R.
[^ety-temperature]: **temperature** — Latin *temperare*, "to mix in due proportion, to moderate" → the "tempered" state of the body. Falls in NREM (set-point lowered); is not regulated in REM.
[^ety-respiration]: **respiration** — Latin *re-*, "again" + *spirare*, "to breathe" + *-ation*, the process → breathing again and again. **Differentiate:** *respiration* (gas exchange, cellular O₂ use) vs *ventilation* (moving air in and out) vs *inspiration / expiration* (*in-* "in", *ex-* "out").
[^ety-rem]: **rapid eye movement (REM)** — Latin *rapidus*, "snatching, swift" (*rapere*, to seize) → the darting eye movements under closed lids, described by Eugene Aserinsky and Nathaniel Kleitman (Chicago, 1953).
[^ety-paradoxical]: **paradoxical** — Greek *para-*, "contrary to, beside" + *doxa*, "opinion, expectation" + *-ical* → contrary to expectation: the sleeper is hard to wake and atonic, yet the EEG looks awake. Term popularised by Michel Jouvet (*sommeil paradoxal*, 1959).
[^ety-pgo]: **ponto-geniculo-occipital** — Latin *pons*, "bridge" + *geniculum*, "little knee" (the lateral geniculate body is bent like a knee) + *occipital* → spikes that start in the pons, pass the LGB and reach the occipital cortex; they herald each REM eye movement.
[^ety-magnocellular]: **magnocellular** — Latin *magnus*, "large" + *cellula*, "small room, cell" + *-ar*, "pertaining to" → the large-celled layers (1–2) of the LGB carrying motion and contrast. **Differentiate:** *parvocellular* (*parvus*, small — layers 3–6, colour and detail) · *koniocellular* (Greek *konis*, dust — tiny cells between layers).
[^ety-atonia]: **atonia** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *tonos*, "tension, stretching" + *-ia*, condition → loss of muscle tone. **Differentiate:** *atonia* (no tone — REM) · *hypotonia* (reduced — cerebellar lesion) · *hypertonia* (spasticity, rigidity) · *atony* of the uterus (fails to contract after delivery).
[^ety-diaphragm]: **diaphragm** — Greek *dia-*, "across, through" + *phragma*, "fence, partition" → the partition across the trunk; it escapes REM atonia, so breathing continues.
[^ety-extraocular]: **extraocular** — Latin *extra-*, "outside" + *oculus*, "eye" + *-ar* → the muscles outside the eyeball that move it (four recti, two obliques); vs *intraocular* muscles (ciliary muscle, iris sphincter and dilator).
[^ety-genital]: **genital / clitoris** — Latin *genitalis*, "of begetting" (*gignere*, to beget); *clitoris* from Greek *kleitoris* — origin uncertain. *Tumescence* (Latin *tumescere*, "to begin to swell") is the REM enlargement.
[^ety-eog]: **electro-oculogram / electromyograph** — *electro-* + Latin *oculus*, "eye" / Greek *mys*, "muscle" (literally "mouse" — a flexing muscle looks like a mouse under the skin) + *-gram*, "record" / *-graph*, "recording instrument". **Differentiate:** *-gram* (the record) · *-graph* (the instrument) · *-graphy* (the procedure).
[^ety-hormone]: **hormone** — Greek *hormaō*, "I set in motion, I excite" → a chemical messenger that sets distant cells in motion; coined by Ernest Starling (1905).
[^ety-prolactin]: **prolactin** — Latin *pro-*, "for" + *lac, lactis*, "milk" + *-in*, a chemical substance → the hormone "for milk", from anterior pituitary lactotrophs.
[^ety-luteinizing]: **luteinizing hormone (LH)** — Latin *luteus*, "yellow" + *-ize*, "to make into" → makes the follicle into the *corpus luteum* ("yellow body"); in males it drives Leydig cell testosterone.
[^ety-puberty]: **puberty** — Latin *pubertas*, "adulthood", from *pubes*, "grown-up; the hair of the groin" → the age when pubic hair appears; nocturnal (sleep-entrained) LH pulses are its first hormonal sign.
[^ety-melatonin]: **melatonin** — Greek *melas*, "black" (it acts on *melan*ophores, lightening frog skin) + *-tonin* from serotonin, its precursor; isolated by Aaron Lerner (1958). Secreted in darkness — the "hormone of darkness".
[^ety-secretion]: **secretion** — Latin *se-*, "apart" + *cernere*, "to sift, separate" + *-ion*, the process → separating out a useful substance. **Differentiate:** *secretion* (useful product) · *excretion* (*ex-*, out — removal of waste) · *incretion* (old word for internal, i.e. endocrine, secretion).
[^ety-pineal]: **pineal** — Latin *pinea*, "pine cone" + *-al* → the pine-cone-shaped gland on the roof of the third ventricle; Descartes called it "the seat of the soul".
[^ety-hypnogram]: **hypnogram** — Greek *hypnos*, "sleep" + *gramma*, "something written" → the written graph of sleep stages across the night; Hypnos was the Greek god of sleep.
[^ety-ultradian]: **ultradian** — Latin *ultra*, "beyond" + *dies*, "day" → a rhythm repeating more often than once a day (period < 24 h — the 90-min sleep cycle). **Differentiate:** *circadian* (*circa*, about — about one day: sleep–wake, cortisol) · *infradian* (longer than a day: menstrual cycle).
[^ety-neonate]: **neonate** — Greek *neos*, "new" + Latin *natus*, "born" → a newborn (first 28 days of life), who spends about half of sleep in REM.
[^ety-regulation]: **regulation** — Latin *regula*, "rule, straight stick" + *-ation*, the process → keeping something to a rule. **Differentiate:** *regulation* (setting the level) · *modulation* (fine-tuning it) · *control* (the whole system).
[^ety-reticular]: **reticular activating system** — Latin *reticulum*, "little net" (*rete*, net) → the net-like core of the brainstem whose ascending fibres keep the cortex awake (Giuseppe Moruzzi and Horace Magoun, 1949).
[^ety-activation]: **activation** — Latin *activus* (*agere*, "to drive, to do") + *-ation*, the process → switching on. **Differentiate:** *activation* (switching on) · *stimulation* (the goad that causes it) · *facilitation* (making it easier) · *inhibition* (holding it back).
[^ety-norepinephrine]: **norepinephrine / noradrenaline** — *nor-* (marks the form lacking a methyl group; origin of the abbreviation debated) + Greek *epi-*, "upon" + *nephros*, "kidney" → the adrenal ("upon the kidney") amine without its methyl group; *noradrenaline* is the Latin twin (*ad-* "near" + *renes* "kidneys").
[^ety-raphe]: **raphe** — Greek *rhaphē*, "seam, suture" → the midline seam of the brainstem where the serotonergic nuclei lie; the same word as the *raphe* of the scrotum and the palate.
[^ety-serotonin]: **serotonin** — Latin *serum*, "whey; the fluid of blood" + Greek *tonos*, "tension" + *-in* → the serum factor that tones (constricts) vessels; chemically 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT).
[^ety-hypothalamus]: **hypothalamus** — Greek *hypo-*, "below" + *thalamos*, "inner chamber" → the region below the thalamus; it holds both the sleep (VLPO) and the wake (orexin, histamine) switches.
[^ety-orexin]: **orexin (hypocretin)** — Greek *orexis*, "appetite" → first found (1998) as an appetite peptide of the lateral hypothalamus; *hypocretin* = **hypo**thalamic + se**cretin**-like. Loss of orexin neurons = narcolepsy type 1.
[^ety-histamine]: **histamine** — Greek *histos*, "tissue" + *amine* → the "tissue amine"; brain histamine comes from the tuberomammillary nucleus of the posterior hypothalamus — why sedating H1-antihistamines cause drowsiness.
[^ety-stimulation]: **stimulation** — Latin *stimulus*, "a goad for driving cattle" + *-ation*, the process → prodding a structure into action. **Differentiate:** stimulation (the input) vs activation (the result).
[^ety-preoptic]: **ventrolateral preoptic nucleus (VLPO)** — Latin *venter*, "belly" (the under side) + *latus*, "side" + *prae-*, "in front of" + Greek *optikos*, "of sight" (the optic chiasma) → the cell group in front of the optic chiasma, low and to the side; its GABA neurons switch off the arousal centres — the "sleep switch".
[^ety-cholinergic]: **cholinergic** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" (choline was first isolated from bile) + *ergon*, "work" + *-ic* → working through acetylcholine. **Differentiate:** cholinergic (ACh) · adrenergic (noradrenaline/adrenaline) · GABAergic · serotonergic.
[^ety-facilitation]: **facilitation** — Latin *facilis*, "easy" + *-ation*, the process → making a response easier to trigger (ACh facilitates REM); vs *stimulation* (directly triggering it) and *inhibition* (making it harder).
[^ety-gaba]: **GABA (γ-aminobutyric acid)** — *butyric*, Latin *butyrum*, "butter" (butyric acid smells of rancid butter); *gamma* — the amino group sits on the third (γ) carbon. The main inhibitory transmitter of the brain.
[^ety-inhibition]: **inhibition** — Latin *in-*, "in, on" + *habere*, "to hold" + *-ition*, the process → holding in, restraining. **Differentiate:** *inhibition* (an active process held back) · *blockade* (a pathway physically blocked) · *antagonism* (one agent opposing another at a receptor).
[^ety-parasomnia]: **parasomnia** — Greek *para-*, "beside, abnormal" + Latin *somnus*, "sleep" + *-ia*, condition → abnormal events beside (during) sleep. **Differentiate:** *parasomnia* (abnormal behaviour in sleep) vs *dyssomnia* (wrong amount or timing of sleep — insomnia, hypersomnia).
[^ety-somnambulism]: **somnambulism** — Latin *somnus*, "sleep" + *ambulare*, "to walk" + *-ism*, a condition → sleepwalking; it arises from deep NREM (stages 3–4), so the walker is hard to wake and has no memory of it.
[^ety-bruxism]: **bruxism** — Greek *brychein*, "to gnash the teeth" + *-ism* → teeth grinding, mostly in stage 2 sleep.
[^ety-enuresis]: **enuresis** — Greek *en-*, "in" + *ourein*, "to urinate" + *-esis*, a condition → involuntary passing of urine; *nocturnal* enuresis = bed wetting, an NREM arousal disorder of childhood.
[^ety-pavor]: **pavor nocturnus** — Latin *pavor*, "terror, trembling" + *nocturnus*, "of the night" → night terror. **Differentiate:** night terror (NREM 3–4, inconsolable, no memory) vs nightmare (REM, wakes fully, vivid recall).
[^ety-narcolepsy]: **narcolepsy** — Greek *narkē*, "numbness, stupor" + *lēpsis*, "a seizing, an attack" → attacks of sleep; named by Jean-Baptiste Gélineau (1880). Same *narkē* as *narcotic*; same *-lepsy* as *epilepsy*.
[^ety-nightmare]: **nightmare** — Old English *niht*, "night" + *mare*, "a goblin believed to sit on the sleeper's chest" → a terrifying REM dream that wakes the dreamer, who remembers it.
[^pre-electro]: **electro-** — electricity: electroencephalography, electro-oculogram, electromyograph, electrocardiogram. Don't confuse with **electrolyte** (a salt that conducts in solution) · **electron** (the particle; same root *ēlektron*, amber).
[^pre-en]: **en- / em- / endo-** — in, within: encephalon (in the head), enuresis, embolus. Don't confuse with **an- / a-** without (anuria, atonia) · **ex- / ecto-** out · **endo-** within a structure (endocrine).
[^suf-graphy]: **-graphy / -gram / -graph** — *-graphy* the procedure of recording (electroencephalography, polysomnography) · *-gram* the record itself (hypnogram, electro-oculogram) · *-graph* the recording instrument (electromyograph). Don't confuse with **-scopy** looking (endoscopy) · **-metry** measuring (spirometry).
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -ition / -ion** — the process or result of an action: summation, activation, regulation, stimulation, secretion, inhibition. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition (narcosis) · **-ism** a state (bruxism).
[^pre-in]: **in- (negative)** — not: inattention, involuntary, insomnia. Don't confuse with **in- (directional)** in, into (inspiration, inhibition) · **un-** (English: unrelated) · **dis-** apart, absence.
[^pre-pre]: **pre- / prae-** — before, in front of: prefrontal, preoptic, preterm. Don't confuse with **pro-** for, forward (prolactin) · **post-** after · **para-** beside.
[^suf-oid]: **-oid / -oidal** — like, resembling: sinusoidal (sine-like), thyroid (shield-like), adenoid. Don't confuse with **-ode** a path or way (electrode, cathode) · **-osis** a condition.
[^pre-bi]: **bi-** — two, twice: biphasic, bilateral, biconcave. Don't confuse with **di-** two (Greek: diphasic, dioxide) · **bio-** life (biopsy) · **semi- / hemi-** half.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: respiration (breathing again and again), reflex, recoil, reabsorption. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards, behind (retroperitoneal) · **reti- / rete** net (reticular — a root, not the prefix).
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, contrary to, abnormal: paradoxical, parasomnia, parasympathetic. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before · **pan-** all.
[^pre-magno]: **magno- / macro-** — large: magnocellular, macrophage. Don't confuse with **parvo- / micro-** small (parvocellular, microcirculation) · **mega- / megalo-** very large, enlarged (megaloblast, splenomegaly).
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — without, not: atonia, apnoea, anaemia, anuria. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from (abduction) · **ad- / a-** towards (Latin, ascend) · **ana-** up, again (anabolism).
[^suf-ia]: **-ia** — a condition, a state: atonia, parasomnia, anaemia, pneumonia. Don't confuse with **-iasis** a disease process or infestation (lithiasis, ascariasis) · **-ism** a state or practice (bruxism).
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, across, apart: diaphragm, diagnosis, diarrhoea, dialysis. Don't confuse with **di-** two (dioxide) · **dys-** bad, difficult (dyspnoea).
[^pre-extra]: **extra-** — outside, beyond: extraocular, extracellular, extrathoracic. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek: exocrine) · **intra-** within · **inter-** between.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — for, before, forward: prolactin (for milk), prophase, progesterone (for gestation). Don't confuse with **pre-** before (in time or place) · **post-** after · **para-** beside.
[^suf-in]: **-in / -ine** — a chemical substance (protein, amine, alkaloid): prolactin, melatonin, serotonin, orexin, histamine. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation · **-ase** an enzyme (amylase) · **-ose** a sugar (glucose).
[^pre-noct]: **noct- / nocti- / nyct-** — night: nocturnal, nocturia, nyctalopia. Don't confuse with **noci-** harm, pain (nociceptor) · **necro-** death.
[^pre-hypno]: **hypno-** — sleep: hypnogram, hypnotic, hypnagogic. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under, deficient (hypothalamus) · **hyper-** over.
[^suf-gram]: **-gram** — a written record or picture: hypnogram, electro-oculogram, spirogram, angiogram. Don't confuse with **-graph** the instrument · **-graphy** the procedure · **gram** the unit of weight (Greek *gramma*, a small weight, same root).
[^pre-ultra]: **ultra-** — beyond, excessive: ultradian, ultrasound, ultrafiltration. Don't confuse with **infra-** below (infradian, infrared) · **supra- / super-** above · **circa-** about (circadian).
[^pre-neo]: **neo-** — new: neonate, neoplasm, neocortex. Don't confuse with **nephro-** kidney · **necro-** death · **paleo- / archi-** old (paleocortex, archicerebellum).
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypothalamus, hypotonia, hypocretin. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep · **sub- / infra-** below (Latin equivalents).
[^pre-ventro]: **ventro- / ventral** — belly side, front (in man) / under side: ventrolateral, ventromedial. Don't confuse with **dorso- / dorsal** back side · **ventricle** (Latin *ventriculus*, "little belly" — a chamber).
[^suf-ergic]: **-ergic** — working through, activated by: cholinergic, adrenergic, GABAergic, dopaminergic. Don't confuse with **-ergy** work, energy (synergy, allergy) · **-genic** produced by, causing (neurogenic).
[^pre-somno]: **somn- / somno- / somni-** — sleep (Latin *somnus*): parasomnia, somnambulism, insomnia, somnolence. Don't confuse with **soma- / somato-** body (somatosensory) · **hypno-** sleep (Greek equivalent).
[^suf-ism]: **-ism** — a condition, state or practice: somnambulism, bruxism, metabolism, hyperthyroidism. Don't confuse with **-osis** a disease condition · **-ist** a person who practises (dentist).
[^suf-esis]: **-esis / -sis** — a process or condition: enuresis, diuresis, emesis, mitosis. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation · **-iasis** a disease state (lithiasis).
[^suf-lepsy]: **-lepsy / -leptic** — a seizure, an attack, taking hold: narcolepsy, epilepsy, catalepsy, neuroleptic. Don't confuse with **-plegia** paralysis (hemiplegia) · **-lysis** breaking down.
