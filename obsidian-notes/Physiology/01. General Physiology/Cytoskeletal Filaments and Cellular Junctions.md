# 🦴 Cytoskeletal Filaments and Cellular Junctions

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The **cytoskeleton**[^ety-cytoskeleton][^pre-cyto] is the cell's internal support system of protein **filaments**[^ety-filament] — it gives strength and moves the cell and its cargo; **cellular junctions**[^ety-junction] are the protein contacts that seal, rivet and wire cells to each other and to the basal lamina. Every question in this chapter answers:
> - ✅ **Which filament, which protein, which motor?** (microtubules — tubulin, kinesin, dynein · microfilaments — actin, myosin · intermediate filaments — cell-specific tumour markers)
> - ✅ **Which junction, which protein, which disease?** (tight — claudin-16 · desmosome — desmoglein/pemphigus · gap — connexin-32/40)

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — Scaffolding, Railways and Rivets

Think of a building site. **Intermediate filaments** are the steel cables that give strength; **microtubules** are railway lines on which motor "trains" run — **kinesin** outward, **dynein** back home; **microfilaments** are ropes and winches (actin pulled by myosin). Between the bricks, **tight junctions** are the sealant, **adherens junctions and desmosomes** are rivets, **gap junctions** are intercom doorways, and **hemidesmosomes / focal adhesions** bolt the bricks to the floor.

```
CYTOSKELETON (by diameter)
  MICROTUBULES   (big)    tubulin + motors kinesin (→ synapse) / dynein (→ cell body)
                          axonal transport · cilia 9+2 · mitotic spindle (MTOC) · vinca, taxanes, colchicine
  MICROFILAMENTS (small)  actin + myosin · sliding filament · actin polymerisation (Listeria comet tail)
  INTERMEDIATE   (most abundant) cell-specific → tumour markers: keratin · desmin · vimentin · GFAP · lamin

CELL JUNCTIONS
  CELL–CELL          tight (occludin, claudin) · adhesive: zonula adherens (E-cadherin–β-catenin–actin)
                                                         desmosome (desmoglein/collin–desmoplakin–tonofilament)
                     gap (connexon = connexins; 3–4 nm)
  CELL–BASAL LAMINA  hemidesmosome (→ intermediate filaments) · focal adhesion (→ actin)
```

### 1. MICROTUBULES

**Proteins:**
1. **Kinesin**[^ety-kinesin].
2. **Dynein**[^ety-dynein].
3. **Tubulin**[^ety-tubulin].[^2]

🧠 **Tubulin** is the brick that builds the tube; **kinesin** and **dynein** are the motors that walk along it carrying cargo — kinesin towards the axon terminal, dynein back towards the cell body.

**Functions:**

**1. Axonal**[^ety-axon] **transport (AT):**

| | **Forward AT** | **Reverse AT** |
|---|---|---|
| **Transport direction** | **Cell body → synapse**[^ety-synapse][^pre-syn] (**anterograde**[^ety-anterograde][^pre-antero] / antegrade) | **Synapse → cell body** (retrograde[^pre-retro]) |
| **Protein** | **Kinesin** | **Dynein** |
| **Speed of transport** | **Fast: 400 mm/day** · **Slow: 0.5–10 mm/day** | **Fast: 200 mm/day**[^3] |
| **Clinical significance** | — | **Invasion of microorganisms:** • **Rabies**[^ety-rabies] **virus** • **Tetanus**[^ety-tetanus] **toxin: tetanospasmin**[^ety-tetanospasmin][^4] |

### 2. MICROFILAMENTS

**Proteins:**
1. **Actin**[^ety-actin].
2. **Myosin**[^ety-myosin][^pre-myo].[^10]

![[Cytoskeletal Filaments and Cellular Junctions - actin and myosin.png]]
*Skeletal muscle — thin actin filaments (beaded double strand) and a thick myosin filament with protruding heads that pull on the actin (cropped from the handwritten page).*

**Functions:**
1. **Skeletal muscle contraction & relaxation — sliding filament theory.**[^11]
2. **Cell motility**[^ety-motility] — **actin polymerization**[^ety-polymerization][^pre-poly][^suf-ation] **→ enhances motility.**

**Clinical significance — action of *Listeria***[^ety-listeria]**:**

```
Listeria ──(Internalins A/B)──► Enters into cell
                                      ↓
              Engulfed into vacuole (to contain its spread)
                                      ↓  Listeriolysin-O (LLO)
                              Breakdown of vacuole
                                      ↓  Act-A
                              Actin polymerization
                                      ↓
              Comet tail pattern (polymerized actin)
                                      ↓
                               Tumbling motility
                                      ↓
                             Infect nearby cells
```
🧠 *Listeria* hijacks the host's own actin: **internalins**[^ety-internalin] let it in, **listeriolysin**[^ety-listeriolysin][^suf-lysin]-O dissolves the vacuole that was meant to trap it, and **ActA** builds an actin **comet**[^ety-comet] tail that shoves the bacterium into the neighbouring cell — so it spreads without ever leaving the cytoplasm.[^12]

- **AKA zona occludens**[^ety-zona-occludens].

**Proteins:**
1. **Occludin.**
2. **Claudin**[^ety-claudin].

**Location:**[^21]
- **Blood–brain barrier.**
- **GIT.**
- **Kidneys.**

![[Cytoskeletal Filaments and Cellular Junctions - tight junction strands.png]]

**Clinical significance:**
- **Claudin-16 mutation (in kidneys): familial hypomagnesemia**[^ety-hypomagnesemia][^pre-hypo][^suf-emia] **hypercalciuria**[^ety-hypercalciuria][^pre-hyper][^suf-uria] (↑ Ca²⁺ excretion → renal stones).[^22]

🧠 Substances cross an epithelium by two roads: **through** the cells (**transcellular**[^pre-trans] — across apical and basal membranes) or **between** them (**paracellular** — through the tight junction). Claudins decide which ions may take the paracellular road.

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Structure in this chapter is always built from the **same three parts — a track or bridge, a linker, and a motor or anchor** — and every disease is one broken part. Microtubules are tracks (tubulin) with motors (kinesin forward, dynein back): break dynein and cilia, sperm and embryonic rotation stop (Kartagener); poison tubulin and mitosis stops (vinca, taxanes) or neutrophils stop (colchicine). Microfilaments are actin pulled by myosin: *Listeria* steals the polymerisation. Junctions are primary protein + linker + filament: attack desmoglein and the skin splits (pemphigus), mutate claudin-16 and Mg²⁺/Ca²⁺ leak away, mutate connexins and the nerves (CMT) or atria (AF) lose their wiring.
>
> Intermediate filaments are the exception that proves useful: they are **cell-specific**, so finding keratin, desmin, vimentin, GFAP or lamin tells you which cell a tumour or deposit came from.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Kinesin carries cargo from the cell body to the synapse (fast 400 mm/day) and dynein brings it back (200 mm/day); motile cilia (9 + 2, dynein arms) sweep sputum out of the lungs and move sperm and ova; tight junctions seal epithelia and regulate the paracellular road; desmosomes resist mechanical stress; gap junctions let heart and nerve cells talk in 3–4 nm channels.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A partial defect is masked: in primary ciliary dyskinesia, coughing partly replaces the mucociliary escalator, so the child has only recurrent chest and sinus infections; a few desmoglein antibodies cause only oral erosions; dynein's retrograde route quietly carries rabies virus or tetanus toxin towards the CNS during the incubation period.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Sputum pools and infects until the bronchi dilate (bronchiectasis), with infertility and situs inversus completing Kartagener syndrome; widespread acantholysis produces flaccid skin blisters (pemphigus); rabies and tetanus reach the CNS and cause encephalitis or spastic paralysis; claudin-16 loss gives hypomagnesaemia with hypercalciuria and stones; connexin mutations give neuropathy or atrial fibrillation; tumours lose their basal-lamina anchors and metastasise.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal track/linker/anchor → one protein mutated, poisoned or attacked → partial compensation (cough, local lesions, incubation) → failure of the tissue's function (clearance, cohesion, sealing, conduction) → overt syndrome (bronchiectasis, blistering, paralysis, stones, arrhythmia, metastasis)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Knowing the broken part predicts the treatment: physiotherapy and antibiotics for immotile cilia, immunosuppression against desmoglein antibodies, early post-exposure prophylaxis before rabies or tetanus completes its dynein journey, and microtubule poisons chosen for their target (vincristine — neuropathy because axonal transport also needs microtubules).

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Swapping kinesin and dynein**
>
> **What students think:** Dynein carries cargo forward to the synapse.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Kinesin** drives **forward (anterograde)** transport — cell body → synapse, fast 400 mm/day; **dynein** drives **reverse (retrograde)** transport — synapse → cell body, 200 mm/day. That is why rabies virus and tetanus toxin, which enter at the nerve ending, use dynein. Dynein is also the arm motor of cilia and flagella.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**K**inesin **K**icks it out (to the terminal); **D**ynein **D**rags it home."
>
> **Trap 2: Thinking all microtubule drugs act the same way**
>
> **What students think:** Vinca alkaloids, taxanes and colchicine all "break" microtubules.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Vinca alkaloids and colchicine bind tubulin and **prevent polymerisation**; taxanes (paclitaxel) do the opposite — they **stabilise** microtubules and prevent depolymerisation. Both extremes freeze the mitotic spindle. Colchicine is used in gout not to stop mitosis but to stop neutrophil migration into the joint.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Vinca and colchicine — no building; taxanes — no dismantling."
>
> **Trap 3: Mixing up zonula adherens and desmosome proteins**
>
> **What students think:** Desmosomes use E-cadherin and actin.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Zonula adherens = E-cadherin → β-catenin → actin.** **Desmosome = desmoglein / desmocolin → desmoplakin → tonofilaments (intermediate filaments).** The same split continues at the base: **hemidesmosome → intermediate filaments**, **focal adhesion → actin**.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Desmo- with desmo- (desmoglein, desmoplakin) and the strong cables (intermediate filaments); adherens and focal with actin."
>
> **Trap 4: Calling the tight junction a cell adhesive junction**
>
> **What students think:** Tight junctions hold cells together mechanically.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Tight junctions (zona occludens — occludin, claudin) **seal** the space between cells and **regulate paracellular movement**; mechanical adhesion is the job of zonula adherens and desmosomes, and communication is the job of gap junctions. Each cell–cell junction has one job: seal, stick or talk.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Tight = seal (occlude); adherens/desmosome = stick; gap = talk."
>
> **Trap 5: Matching the wrong intermediate filament to a tumour**
>
> **What students think:** Vimentin marks muscle tumours and desmin marks all mesenchymal tumours.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Desmin = muscle** (rhabdomyosarcoma); **vimentin = connective tissue / fibroblast** (tumours of mesenchymal origin); **keratin = epithelium** (squamous cell carcinoma, Mallory–Denk bodies); **GFAP = astrocytes** (glioma); **lamin = nucleus** (progeria).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Say the location first, then the tumour: "epithelium–keratin–carcinoma, muscle–desmin–rhabdo, fibroblast–vimentin–mesenchymal, astrocyte–GFAP–glioma, nucleus–lamin–progeria."

> **Related Topic 3: [[Cardiac and Smooth Muscles]]**
> Gap junctions in the intercalated discs make cardiac muscle a functional syncytium; desmosomes in the same discs resist stress — and connexin-40 mutation explains idiopathic atrial fibrillation.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[CSF, CBF and BBB]]**
> Tight junctions between brain capillary endothelial cells are the anatomical basis of the blood–brain barrier.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule]]**
> Claudin-16 lines the paracellular pathway of the thick ascending limb, where Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ are reabsorbed — its mutation gives familial hypomagnesaemia hypercalciuria.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Cellular Physiology]]**
> Transmembrane and peripheral proteins (dystrophin, spectrin, ankyrin) link the membrane to actin — the bridge between the membrane chapter and this one.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Recurrent chest infections with dextrocardia, blistering skin disease, rabies and tetanus prophylaxis, vincristine neuropathy, tumour typing by immunohistochemistry (keratin, desmin, vimentin, GFAP) and inherited neuropathies and arrhythmias all draw on this chapter.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Cytoskeleton (cyto = cell, skeleton = bone) = support system, movement and strength; by diameter: **microtubules big**, **microfilaments small**, **intermediate filaments most abundant**.
> 2. Microtubule proteins: **kinesin, dynein, tubulin**.
> 3. **Forward (anterograde) AT = kinesin**, cell body → synapse, **fast 400 mm/day, slow 0.5–10 mm/day**; **reverse AT = dynein**, synapse → cell body, **fast 200 mm/day** — route of **rabies virus** and **tetanospasmin**.
> 4. Cilia: **9 + 2** arrangement; **dynein arms** power cilia, sperm and fallopian tube; normal role — clearing sputum and rotation of organs in the embryo.
> 5. **Kartagener (immotile cilia) syndrome = absent dynein** → **bronchiectasis, infertility, situs inversus**.
> 6. Mitosis: **tubulin in the MTOC**; inhibitors — **vinca alkaloids (vincristine, vinblastine)**, **taxanes (paclitaxel)** (anticancer), **colchicine** (gout).
> 7. Microfilaments: **actin + myosin** — **sliding filament theory**; **actin polymerisation → motility**.
> 8. **Listeria:** internalins → entry → **LLO** breaks vacuole → **ActA** → actin polymerisation → **comet tail** → tumbling motility → infects nearby cells.
> 9. Intermediate filaments (tumour markers): **keratin** — epithelium, **squamous cell carcinoma**, **Mallory–Denk bodies**; **desmin** — muscle, **rhabdomyosarcoma**; **vimentin** — fibroblast, **mesenchymal tumours**; **GFAP** — astrocytes, **glioma**; **lamin** — nucleus, **progeria**.
> 10. Junctions: **cell–cell** (tight, adhesive — zonula adherens & desmosomes, gap) and **cell–basal lamina** (hemidesmosomes, focal adhesions); CAMs — **cadherins, integrin, selectin**; structure — primary protein, linker, cytoskeletal filament.
> 11. **Zonula adherens: E-cadherin – β-catenin – actin** (Listeria internalins hijack E-cadherin); **desmosome: desmoglein/desmocolin – desmoplakin – tonofilaments**; desmosomes abundant in **heart, skin, uterine cervix**; **pemphigus = autoantibodies against desmoglein**.
> 12. **Tight junction = zona occludens**: **occludin, claudin**; BBB, GIT, kidneys; **claudin-16 → familial hypomagnesaemia hypercalciuria** (stones); regulates **paracellular** movement.
> 13. **Gap junction**: rapid communication, **3–4 nm**, heart (abundant) and nerves; **connexon** of **connexin** subunits; **connexin-32 → Charcot–Marie–Tooth**, **connexin-40 → idiopathic AF**. **Hemidesmosome → intermediate filaments**, **focal adhesion → actin**; disrupted in **metastasis**.

[^1]: Typical diameters: microfilaments (actin) about 7 nm, intermediate filaments about 10 nm, microtubules about 25 nm — "intermediate" because their width lies between the other two. The page's "most abundant" refers to how plentiful they are in cells under mechanical stress (keratin in skin, neurofilaments in axons).
[^2]: Tubulin is the structural subunit: α- and β-tubulin dimers line up in 13 protofilaments that form the hollow tube, which grows at its plus end. Kinesin and dynein are not part of the tube; they are motor proteins (ATPases) that walk on it — kinesin towards the plus end (periphery), dynein towards the minus end (centrosome, cell body).
[^3]: Ganong gives the same figures: fast anterograde transport about 400 mm/day (vesicles, mitochondria), slow anterograde 0.5–10 mm/day (cytoskeletal proteins), and retrograde about 200 mm/day (used vesicles, growth factors such as NGF).
[^4]: Rabies virus (also herpes simplex, varicella-zoster and polio) and tetanus toxin enter at nerve terminals and ride dynein back to the CNS; the distance from the wound to the CNS sets the incubation period. Tetanospasmin then blocks release of the inhibitory transmitters glycine and GABA from spinal interneurons → spastic paralysis, lockjaw.
[^5]: Cilia lining the airway form the **mucociliary escalator**, moving mucus up towards the pharynx. In the embryo, rotating cilia of the primitive node sweep fluid leftward; this flow sets the body's left–right asymmetry, which is why ciliary failure leaves organ position to chance (situs inversus in about half of patients).
[^6]: The 9 + 2 axoneme = nine outer microtubule doublets around a central pair; dynein arms on each doublet grip the next and slide them, which bends the cilium. Non-motile primary cilia are 9 + 0. The tail of a sperm (a flagellum) has the same 9 + 2 core.
[^7]: Kartagener syndrome is the subset of primary ciliary dyskinesia (autosomal recessive, often DNAH5/DNAI1 dynein genes) with the triad **situs inversus + chronic sinusitis + bronchiectasis**; men are infertile (immotile sperm), women have reduced fertility and a higher risk of ectopic pregnancy.
[^8]: The MTOC of animal cells is the **centrosome** (a pair of centrioles, each nine microtubule triplets, in pericentriolar material). Kinetochore microtubules attach to the kinetochore at each chromosome's centromere; polar microtubules push the poles apart; astral microtubules anchor the spindle to the cell cortex.
[^9]: Vinca alkaloids (from the Madagascar periwinkle, *Catharanthus roseus*, formerly *Vinca rosea*) and colchicine bind tubulin and **prevent polymerisation**; taxanes (from the Pacific yew, *Taxus brevifolia*) **stabilise** microtubules and prevent depolymerisation — both arrest cells in metaphase. Colchicine in gout works by stopping neutrophil migration and inflammasome activation, not by stopping mitosis. Vincristine's dose-limiting neuropathy comes from blocking axonal transport.
[^10]: Actin exists as G-actin (globular monomers) that polymerise into F-actin (filamentous, a double helix). Myosin II is the motor of muscle; in non-muscle cells actin–myosin drive cytokinesis, cell crawling and the contraction of the terminal web.
[^11]: The sliding filament theory was proposed in 1954 by two pairs working independently — Andrew Huxley and Rolf Niedergerke, and Hugh Huxley and Jean Hanson: the filaments do not shorten, they slide past each other — see [[Skeletal Muscles]].
[^12]: Internalin A binds E-cadherin (internalin B binds the Met receptor); listeriolysin-O is a pore-forming toxin; ActA recruits the host Arp2/3 complex to build the actin "comet" or "rocket" tail. In the laboratory *Listeria* shows **tumbling motility** in broth at 25 °C, which is flagellar; the page places "tumbling motility" in the actin sequence — keep both facts in mind.
[^13]: Mallory–Denk bodies are eosinophilic clumps of misfolded, ubiquitinated keratin 8/18 in ballooned hepatocytes; typical of alcoholic hepatitis but also seen in non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, Wilson disease and chronic cholestasis.
[^14]: Desmin also marks leiomyosarcoma (smooth muscle); for rhabdomyosarcoma the nuclear markers myogenin and MyoD1 are more specific.
[^15]: Vimentin is the intermediate filament of fibroblasts, endothelium and other mesenchymal cells (sarcomas, melanoma), but it is also co-expressed by some carcinomas (renal cell carcinoma), so it is a sensitive rather than specific marker.
[^16]: GFAP is expressed by astrocytes and by tumours derived from them (astrocytoma, glioblastoma — WHO grade 4) and by ependymomas; it is the classic immunohistochemical marker of glial origin.
[^17]: Lamins A/C (LMNA gene) build the nuclear lamina. **Hutchinson–Gilford progeria** (childhood) is the lamin disease (a toxic "progerin"); **Werner syndrome** ("adult progeria") is caused by mutation of WRN, a DNA helicase, not lamin — the page groups both under premature ageing.
[^18]: Cadherins are Ca²⁺-dependent cell-to-cell adhesion proteins; integrins bind the extracellular matrix (fibronectin, laminin) and link it to the cytoskeleton (they are the primary proteins of hemidesmosomes and focal adhesions); selectins bind carbohydrates and mediate the rolling of leucocytes on endothelium.
[^19]: The desmosome is also called **macula adherens** ("adhering spot"), as opposed to the **zonula adherens** ("adhering belt") that runs all round the cell. The page writes "desmocolin"; the standard spelling is **desmocollin**.
[^20]: In pemphigus vulgaris IgG autoantibodies target **desmoglein-3** (± desmoglein-1) → keratinocytes separate (acantholysis) above the basal layer → flaccid intraepidermal blisters and a positive Nikolsky sign; in pemphigus foliaceus the target is desmoglein-1. Contrast **bullous pemphigoid**, where autoantibodies target the **hemidesmosome** (BP180/BP230) → tense subepidermal blisters.
[^21]: Tight junctions form the barrier between brain capillary endothelial cells (blood–brain barrier — see [[CSF, CBF and BBB]]), between intestinal epithelial cells and along the renal tubule. Occludin is regulatory; the claudins (about 27 types) decide which ions can pass — "leaky" in the proximal tubule, "tight" in the collecting duct and distal colon.
[^22]: Claudin-16 (paracellin-1) and claudin-19 form the cation-selective paracellular channel of the thick ascending limb, where most filtered Mg²⁺ and a large share of Ca²⁺ are reabsorbed. Their loss causes **familial hypomagnesaemia with hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis (FHHNC)**, which often progresses to renal failure — see [[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule]].
[^23]: Tight junctions act as a **gate** (controlling the paracellular passage of ions and water) and as a **fence** (stopping apical and basolateral membrane proteins mixing, so the cell keeps its polarity).
[^24]: Textbooks give the gap between the two membranes as about 2–4 nm (Guyton 2–3 nm). Each connexon is a ring of six connexins; two connexons, one from each cell, dock to form a channel of about 1.5 nm that passes ions and small molecules (< 1 kDa) — the basis of electrical synapses and of the functional syncytium of heart and single-unit smooth muscle.
[^25]: X-linked Charcot–Marie–Tooth (CMTX1) is caused by mutations of GJB1, which codes connexin-32 in myelinating Schwann cells; clinically it is a motor *and* sensory neuropathy — distal wasting ("stork legs"), pes cavus and sensory loss. The page records the sensory part.
[^26]: Connexin-40 (GJA5) is expressed mainly in the atria and the His–Purkinje system; somatic and germline mutations have been found in idiopathic (lone) atrial fibrillation.
[^27]: Hemidesmosomes use integrin α6β4 and BP180 (collagen XVII), linked through plectin and BP230 to keratin intermediate filaments. Focal adhesions use integrins linked through talin, vinculin and α-actinin to actin stress fibres and also signal (focal adhesion kinase).
[^28]: To metastasise, carcinoma cells lose E-cadherin (epithelial–mesenchymal transition), loosen their cell–cell junctions, change their integrins and digest the basement membrane with matrix metalloproteinases before entering vessels.

[^ety-vinca]: **vinca alkaloids** — Latin *vinca* (*pervinca*), "periwinkle" → drugs (vincristine, vinblastine) from the Madagascar periwinkle; *alkaloid* = *alkali* (Arabic *al-qaly*, plant ashes) + *-oid*, "alkali-like" nitrogenous plant bases.

[^ety-sarcoma]: **sarcoma** — Greek *sarx, sarkos*, "flesh" + *-ōma*, tumour → a "fleshy" tumour; malignant tumour of **mesenchymal** (connective) tissue — bone, muscle, fat, fibrous tissue.
[^ety-rhabdomyosarcoma]: **rhabdomyosarcoma** — Greek *rhabdos*, "rod, stripe" + *mys*, "muscle" + *sarx*, "flesh" + *-ōma* → a malignant tumour of striated (striped) skeletal muscle. **Differentiate:** *rhabdomyo-* (skeletal/striated) vs *leiomyo-* (*leios*, smooth — smooth muscle: leiomyoma, leiomyosarcoma).
[^ety-vimentin]: **vimentin** — Latin *vimentum*, "wickerwork, osier-work" (*vimen*, a flexible twig) + *-in* → the flexible, basket-like intermediate filament of fibroblasts and other mesenchymal cells.
[^ety-mesenchymal]: **mesenchymal** — Greek *mesos*, "middle" + *enchyma*, "infusion, something poured in" (*en-*, in + *chein*, to pour) → the loose middle (mesodermal) embryonic tissue that gives rise to connective tissue, muscle, bone and blood vessels.
[^ety-glia]: **glia / glial** — Greek *glia*, "glue" → Rudolf Virchow's (1856) *neuroglia*, "nerve glue", the supporting cells he thought held neurons together; GFAP = **g**lial **f**ibrillary (Latin *fibrilla*, little fibre) **a**cidic **p**rotein.
[^ety-astrocyte]: **astrocyte** — Greek *astron*, "star" + *kytos*, "cell" → star-shaped glial cell with many radiating processes, as the page glosses it.
[^ety-glioblastoma]: **glioblastoma multiforme** — Greek *glia*, "glue" + *blastos*, "germ, bud" + *-ōma*, tumour + Latin *multiformis*, "many-shaped" → a highly malignant glial tumour made of primitive-looking cells of many shapes (necrosis, haemorrhage). **Differentiate:** *glioma* (any glial tumour) · *astrocytoma* (astrocytic, graded 1–4) · *glioblastoma* (grade 4).
[^ety-lamin]: **lamin** — Latin *lamina*, "thin plate, layer" + *-in* → the intermediate filament that forms the nuclear lamina, a mesh lining the inside of the nuclear envelope.
[^ety-progeria]: **progeria** — Greek *pro-*, "before" + *gēras*, "old age" + *-ia*, condition → old age coming too early; named by Hastings Gilford (1904) — Hutchinson–Gilford progeria.
[^ety-werner]: **Werner syndrome** — Otto Werner (1879–1936), German physician, who described in his 1904 doctoral thesis siblings with cataracts, scleroderma-like skin and premature ageing ("adult progeria").
[^ety-zonula-adherens]: **zonula adherens** — Latin *zonula*, "little belt" (diminutive of *zona*, girdle) + *adhaerens*, "sticking to" (*ad-* + *haerere*, to cling) → a sticking belt that runs all round the cell just below the tight junction. **Differentiate:** *zonula* (a belt) vs *macula* (a spot — *macula adherens* = desmosome).
[^ety-desmosome]: **desmosome** — Greek *desmos*, "bond" + *sōma*, "body" → a "binding body", a spot weld between cells anchored to intermediate filaments (term from Josef Schaffer, 1920).
[^ety-basal-lamina]: **basal lamina** — Greek *basis*, "base, step" + Latin *lamina*, "thin plate" → the thin sheet of matrix (type IV collagen, laminin) under every epithelium. **Differentiate:** *basal lamina* (seen on electron microscopy) vs *basement membrane* (basal lamina + reticular lamina, seen on light microscopy).
[^ety-hemidesmosome]: **hemidesmosome** — Greek *hēmi-*, "half" + *desmos*, "bond" + *sōma*, "body" → half a desmosome: the cell supplies only one side and binds the basal lamina, not another cell.
[^ety-focal]: **focal adhesion** — Latin *focus*, "hearth, fireplace" → a central point + *adhaesio*, "a sticking" → discrete points where actin stress fibres attach through integrins to the matrix.
[^ety-cadherin]: **cadherin** — coined (Takeichi, 1980s) from **ca**lcium-dependent **adher**ence prote**in** → adhesion proteins that need Ca²⁺ and bind like to like (E-cadherin = epithelial).
[^ety-integrin]: **integrin** — named (1987) because it **integrates** the extracellular matrix with the intracellular cytoskeleton (Latin *integrare*, to make whole) + *-in*.
[^ety-selectin]: **selectin** — named for its **lectin** domain (sugar-binding; Latin *legere*, "to pick, choose", as in *select*) + *-in* → adhesion molecules that pick out carbohydrate ligands, used by leucocytes to roll on endothelium (E-, P-, L-selectin).
[^ety-desmoglein]: **desmoglein** — Greek *desmos*, "bond" + *gloios*, "glue, sticky substance" + *-in* → the "bond-glue" cadherin of desmosomes; the pemphigus antigen.
[^ety-desmocollin]: **desmocollin** — Greek *desmos*, "bond" + *kolla*, "glue" (as in *collagen*, *colloid*) + *-in* → the second desmosomal cadherin, partner of desmoglein.
[^ety-catenin]: **catenin** — Latin *catena*, "chain" + *-in* → the linker that chains cadherin to actin (β-catenin); same root as *concatenate*.
[^ety-desmoplakin]: **desmoplakin** — Greek *desmos*, "bond" + *plax, plakos*, "flat plate" + *-in* → the protein of the desmosome's dense inner plaque that links the cadherins to keratin filaments.
[^ety-tonofilament]: **tonofilament** — Greek *tonos*, "tension, stretching" + Latin *filamentum*, "thread" → the bundles of keratin intermediate filaments that take the strain at desmosomes.
[^ety-pemphigus]: **pemphigus** — Greek *pemphix, pemphigos*, "bubble, blister" → a group of autoimmune blistering diseases. **Differentiate:** *pemphigus* (antibodies to desmoglein — intraepidermal, flaccid) vs *pemphigoid* (*-oid*, like — antibodies to hemidesmosome — subepidermal, tense).
[^ety-zona-occludens]: **zona occludens** — Latin *zona*, "belt, girdle" + *occludens*, "shutting" (*ob-*, against + *claudere*, to close) → the sealing belt around the apex of epithelial cells: the tight junction.
[^ety-claudin]: **claudin / occludin** — Latin *claudere*, "to close, shut" + *-in*; *occludere* (*ob-* + *claudere*), "to shut up" + *-in* → the closing proteins of the tight junction (claudins discovered in 1998). Same root as *claustrophobia* and *occlusion*.
[^ety-hypomagnesemia]: **hypomagnesemia** — Greek *hypo-*, "under" + *magnesium* (from *Magnesia*, a district of Thessaly where magnesium minerals were mined) + *-aemia*, "blood condition" → low blood magnesium.
[^ety-hypercalciuria]: **hypercalciuria** — Greek *hyper-*, "over" + Latin *calx, calcis*, "lime, limestone" + Greek *ouron*, "urine" + *-ia* → too much calcium in the urine (→ renal stones, nephrocalcinosis).
[^ety-paracellular]: **paracellular / transcellular** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + Latin *cellula*, "small room" → passing beside (between) the cells through the tight junction; Latin *trans-*, "across" → passing across (through) the cell's two membranes.
[^ety-connexon]: **connexon / connexin** — Latin *connexus*, "joined together" (*con-*, together + *nectere*, to bind) → *connexin*, the subunit protein; *connexon* (+ *-on*, a unit), the hexamer of six connexins that forms half of a gap-junction channel.
[^ety-cmt]: **Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease** — Jean-Martin Charcot (1825–1893) and Pierre Marie (1853–1940), French neurologists, and Howard Henry Tooth (1856–1925), British physician, who independently described the "peroneal muscular atrophy" in 1886.
[^ety-neuropathy]: **neuropathy** — Greek *neuron*, "sinew, nerve" + *pathos*, "suffering, disease" → disease of the peripheral nerves. **Differentiate:** *neuropathy* (nerve disease) · *neuritis* (inflammation) · *neuralgia* (*algos*, pain — nerve pain) · *myopathy* (muscle disease).
[^ety-idiopathic]: **idiopathic** — Greek *idios*, "one's own, peculiar" + *pathos*, "disease" → a disease of its own kind, i.e. of unknown cause. Same root as *idiosyncrasy*.
[^ety-atrial]: **atrial** — Latin *atrium*, "entrance hall" (the open central hall of a Roman house) → the receiving chambers of the heart. Older name *auricle* (Latin *auricula*, little ear) for the atrial appendage.
[^ety-fibrillation]: **fibrillation** — Latin *fibrilla*, "small fibre" + *-ation*, process → fine, uncoordinated twitching of individual muscle fibres. **Differentiate:** *fibrillation* (chaotic — no effective contraction: AF, VF) · *flutter* (rapid but organised) · *fasciculation* (visible twitching of a whole motor unit).
[^ety-metastasis]: **metastasis / metastatic** — Greek *meta-*, "change" + *stasis*, "standing, place" → a change of place: spread of a tumour from its primary site to a distant one. **Differentiate:** *metastasis* (spread to a distant site) · *invasion* (local spread into neighbouring tissue) · *metaplasia* (change of one cell type into another).
[^pre-cyto]: **cyto- / -cyte** — cell: cytoskeleton, cytokeratin, astrocyte, fibrocyte. Don't confuse with **cysto-** bladder, sac (cystitis) · **-cytosis** a process or excess of cells.
[^pre-micro]: **micro-** — small: microtubule, microfilament, microorganism, microvilli. Don't confuse with **macro-** large · **mega-** very large · **mio- / meio-** less (meiosis, miosis).
[^suf-ule]: **-ule / -ulus / -ula / -ole** — small (diminutive): microtubule (*tubulus*), zonula (little belt), molecule, arteriole. Don't confuse with **-oma** a tumour · **-ium** a structure.
[^pre-inter]: **inter-** — between: intermediate, intercellular, intercalated disc. Don't confuse with **intra-** within (intracellular) — the commonest exam slip · **infra-** below.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together, with: synapse, syndrome, syncytium, symphysis. Don't confuse with **semi-** half · **sin-** left (Latin *sinister*).
[^pre-antero]: **antero- / ante-** — in front, before, forward: anterograde, antegrade, anterior, antenatal. Don't confuse with **anti-** against (antibody) · **retro-** backward · **post-** after.
[^pre-retro]: **retro-** — backward, behind: retrograde, retroperitoneal, retroversion. Don't confuse with **re-** back, again (receptor) · **antero-** forward · **para-** beside.
[^suf-ectasis]: **-ectasis / -ectasia** — dilatation, stretching out: bronchiectasis, telangiectasia, ductal ectasia. Don't confuse with **-ectomy** surgical removal · **-ectopia** displacement (ectopic pregnancy) · **atelectasis** (incomplete expansion — the opposite idea).
[^pre-in]: **in- / im- / il- / ir-** — not (Latin, before adjectives): infertility, immotile, insoluble, irreversible. Don't confuse with **in-** into (Latin, before verbs: inspiration, invasion) · **en-** in (Greek) · **un-** not (English).
[^pre-myo]: **myo- / my-** — muscle: myosin, myopathy, myocardium, rhabdomyosarcoma. Don't confuse with **myelo-** marrow, spinal cord (myelin, myeloma) · **myco-** fungus (mycosis) · **myxo-** mucus (myxoedema).
[^pre-poly]: **poly-** — many: polymerization, polymer, polyunsaturated, polyuria. Don't confuse with **multi-** many (Latin) · **pan-** all · **oligo-** few.
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -ization / -ion** — the process or result of an action: polymerization, fibrillation, rotation, communication. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition · **-ism** a state.
[^suf-lysin]: **-lysin / -lysis / -lytic** — dissolving, loosening, breaking apart: listeriolysin, haemolysin, streptolysin, autolysis. Don't confuse with **-lysine** (the amino acid, same root) · **-ptosis** falling · **-stasis** stopping.
[^suf-oma]: **-oma** — tumour, swelling: carcinoma, sarcoma, glioma, astrocytoma, glioblastoma. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition · **stoma** mouth, opening · **haematoma / granuloma** (swellings that are not neoplasms).
[^pre-meso]: **meso- / mes-** — middle: mesenchymal, mesoderm, mesentery. Don't confuse with **meta-** change, after · **myo-** muscle · **mio-** less.
[^suf-blast]: **-blast** — germ, bud, an immature precursor cell: glioblastoma, fibroblast, osteoblast, neuroblastoma. Don't confuse with **-cyte** a mature cell (fibrocyte, osteocyte) · **-clast** a cell that breaks down (osteoclast).
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, forward, in front: progeria, prophase, prodrome, prokaryote. Don't confuse with **post-** after · **proto-** first · **pre-** before (Latin).
[^pre-hemi]: **hemi-** — half (Greek): hemidesmosome, hemiplegia, hemisphere. Don't confuse with **haemo- / hemo-** blood · **semi-** half (Latin equivalent) · **demi-** half (French).
[^pre-auto]: **auto-** — self: autoantibodies, autoimmune, autonomic, autosome. Don't confuse with **allo-** other (alloantibody — against another person's antigen) · **xeno-** foreign.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypomagnesemia, hypotension, hypothalamus. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep · **sub- / infra-** below (Latin equivalents).
[^suf-emia]: **-emia / -aemia** — a condition of the blood: hypomagnesemia, anaemia, hypercalcaemia, uraemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** a condition of the urine · **-oemia** spelling variants are the same suffix.
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, above, excessive: hypercalciuria, hypertension, hypertrophy. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra- / super-** above (Latin equivalents).
[^suf-uria]: **-uria** — a condition of the urine: hypercalciuria, haematuria, proteinuria, polyuria. Don't confuse with **-uresis** passage of urine (diuresis, natriuresis) · **-aemia** blood.
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, alongside, abnormal: paracellular, paracrine, paralysis, parathyroid. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **per-** through · **trans-** across.
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: transcellular, transmembrane, transport, transplant. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **para-** beside (paracellular — the other road across an epithelium).
[^suf-pathy]: **-pathy** — disease, suffering, feeling: neuropathy, myopathy, idiopathic, cardiomyopathy. Don't confuse with **-path** a practitioner (osteopath) · **-pathic** in *sympathetic* (feeling together).
[^pre-meta]: **meta-** — change, after, beyond: metastasis, metaplasia, metabolism, metaphase. Don't confuse with **meso-** middle · **para-** beside · **epi-** upon.
