# Mechanics of Breathing

## Pressures in the Lung
### Layout
- Parietal pleura → lines chest wall
- Visceral pleura → covers lung
- Pleural cavity → thin fluid film
- Pressures labelled
  - Transpleural pressure
  - Intrapleural pressure (IPP)
  - Alveolar pressure
- Boyle's law → pressure ∝ 1 / volume (tidal volume)
### One quiet breath (graph)
- Alveolar pressure
  - Rest → 0
  - Inspiration → −1 mm Hg
  - Expiration → +1 mm Hg
- Intrapleural pressure
  - Rest → about −2.5 mm Hg
  - Inspiration → −6 mm Hg
- Volume of breath → about 0.5 L over 4 s
### Types of pressure
- Intrapleural pressure (IPP)
  - Rest → negative (vacuum by pleural stretch)
  - Inspiration → more negative
  - Expiration → same as rest
  - Forceful expiration → positive
- Alveolar pressure (AP)
  - Rest → zero
  - Inspiration → negative
  - Expiration → positive
- Transpulmonary pressure = AP − IPP
  - Rest → positive
  - Keeps alveoli distended
### Note
- 1 mm Hg = 1.36 cm H₂O
- Muscles of inspiration
  - Primary → diaphragm
  - Accessory → external intercostal
  - Accessory → scalene
  - Accessory → sternocleidomastoid
- Muscles of expiration
  - Normal → passive process
  - Forceful → active process
    - Rectus abdominis
    - Transverse abdominis
    - Internal intercostal

## Lung compliance
### Definition
- Compliance = change in volume / change in pressure
  - C = ΔV/ΔP = 200 ml/cm H₂O (normal)
- Compliance ∝ 1 / elastance
- Opposing forces on inspiration
  - Surface tension forces
  - Lung elasticity → elastic recoil (elastance)
  - Action → distension (compliance)
### Hysteresis
- Difference b/w inspiratory & expiratory compliance
  - Compliance greatest during expiration
- Due to surface tension forces
  - Saline filled → no air fluid interface
  - Air filled → air fluid interface → ↑ surface tension
  - → ↑ hysteresis / ↑ compliance (page wording)
- Due to surfactant
  - Inspiration → widely placed surfactant
    - → less reduction in surface tension → less compliant
  - Expiration → closely placed surfactant
    - → more reduction in surface tension → more compliant
### Compliance curves
- CCW → compliance of chest wall → expanding force
- CL → compliance of lung → collapsing force
- CT → total compliance = CL + CCW (page)
- FRC → equilibrium between expanding & collapsing forces
- Minimal volume → point of complete collapse
  - Applied → minimum volume seen in pneumothorax
### Types of compliance
- Static → during breath holding
- Dynamic → during normal breathing
  - D/t impedance (resistance to air flow)
- Specific → compliance / FRC
### Applied aspect
- Emphysema
  - ↑ compliance
  - D/t loss of elastance
  - X-ray → hyperinflation of lung
- Pulmonary fibrosis / ILD
  - ↓ compliance
  - D/t stiff lung

## Work of breathing
- Work = force × displacement
  - Force → pressure changes
  - Displacement → volume changes
  - = 0.5 kg/m/min (by lung)
- 65 % work → elastic forces
  - 2/3rd → surface tension forces
  - 1/3rd → tissue elasticity
- 35 % work → resistance
  - 28 % → airway resistance
  - 7 % → lung tissue resistance
