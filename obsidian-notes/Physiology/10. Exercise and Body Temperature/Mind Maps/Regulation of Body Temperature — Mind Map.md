# Regulation of Body Temperature

## Introduction
### Types of animals
- Poikilotherm → depends on environment (frog)
- Homeotherm → constant irrespective of environment (humans)
### Normal body temperature
- 36.8 ± 0.4 °C
- 98.2 ± 0.7 °F
- Circadian rhythm
  - Lowest → 6 AM
  - Highest → 4–6 PM
### Temperature sensors — TRP channels
- Heat
  - TRPV1 (vanilloid)
  - TRPA1
  - TRPM2
  - TRPM3
- Cold
  - TRPM8 (menthol)
### Heat unit
- Calorie
  - Heat to raise 1 kg water by 1 °C
### Pathology
- Excessive heat → protein denaturation → loss of enzyme function
- Excessive cold → mechanical damage by ice crystals

## Sources of heat generation
- Metabolism
  - Breakdown of food with O2 → heat
  - Measured by indirect calorimetry
- RQ = CO2 produced ÷ O2 consumed
  - Carbohydrates → 1
  - Proteins → 0.8
  - Fats → 0.7
  - Mixed diet → 0.82
- RER = RQ at steady state
  - All CO2 produced expelled by lungs

## Mechanism of heat loss
- Evaporation → sweating
- Conduction → direct transfer to colder objects
- Convection → loss to air currents
- Radiation → electromagnetic waves

## Regulation of body temperature
### Anterior hypothalamic nucleus
- Responds to heat
- Sweating
- Vasodilation
- ↑ Thirst (osmoreceptors)
- → Heat dissipating mechanism
### Posterior hypothalamic nucleus
- Responds to cold
- Shivering
- Vasoconstriction
- ↑ Micturition frequency
- → Heat generating mechanism
### Set point curve
- A → original set point
- B → heat generating (shivering)
- C → new set point
- D → heat loss (sweating)
### Feedforward / anticipatory control
- Skin → shell temperature
- Internal organs → core temperature
- Shell ↓ → anticipation of core ↓
  - → premature ↑ of core temperature

## Fever
- Body's defence enhanced by ↑ temperature
- Pyrogens alter hypothalamic set point
### Exogenous pyrogens
- Endotoxins of gram −ve bacteria
### Endogenous pyrogens
- IL-1
- IL-6
- TNF
- INF-α (used in Rx of hepatitis)
### Mechanism
- Pyrogens cannot cross BBB
- Act on OVLT (outside BBB)
  - Close to hypothalamic temperature area
- PGE2 → EP3 receptor → ↑ cAMP
  - → Fever, arthritis, myalgia
- Rx → antipyretics (↓ PGE2)

## Heat disorders
### Heat cramps
- Very mild
- Hot environment
- Profuse sweating → loss of electrolytes
- Muscle pain, muscle cramps
- Thermoregulation intact
- Rx → electrolyte replacement fluid
### Heat syncope
- Mild
- Prolonged standing in hot environment
- Vasodilation → venous pooling
  - → ↓ CO, ↓ brain blood flow
- Syncope, loss of consciousness
- Thermoregulation intact
- Rx → spontaneous recovery on lying down
### Heat exhaustion / collapse
- Classic → old age
- Exertional → young athletes, soldiers
- Extensive vasodilation → ↓ diastolic filling
- Loss of consciousness
- Thermoregulation intact
- Rx → lying down + fluid replacement
### Heat stroke
- Medical emergency
- Classic → old age; exertional → athletes, soldiers
- Sweating is absent
- Triad
  - Hot dry skin
  - Coma
  - Rectal temp > 106 °F
- Thermoregulation not intact
- Rx → active cooling + fluid replacement
### Malignant hyperthermia
- Most severe
- Triggers → succinylcholine, halothane
- Ryanodine receptor overactivity → ↑↑ calcium
- Sustained contraction → ↑ heat → ↑ temperature
- Rx → dantrolene sodium (RyR blocker)

## Cold disorders
### Accidental hypothermia
- Cold environment
- Drugs → barbiturates
### Therapeutic hypothermia
- 1 °C ↓ temperature → CBF ↓ 7 %
- Minimize blood loss in neurosurgery
- Cardiomyocyte cooling
### Cardiomyocyte cooling
- MI → ROSC → oxidative stress → death
- Cooling by 5 °C → activates RISK
- RISK → survival kinase
  - → Akt phosphorylation
  - → mitochondrial protection → ↑ survival
- RISK → heat shock protein HSP27
  - → positive regulator of Akt
