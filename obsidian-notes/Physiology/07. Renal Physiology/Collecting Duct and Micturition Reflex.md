# 🚰 Collecting Duct and Micturition Reflex

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The collecting duct[^ety-duct] is where the kidney makes its **final, hormone-controlled decisions**: **aldosterone**[^ety-aldosterone] (a steroid acting on principal cells) trades Na⁺ for K⁺ and H⁺, **ADH**[^ety-adh][^pre-anti][^pre-dia] (a peptide acting through V2 → cAMP → AQP-2) decides how much water is reabsorbed, and **natriuretic**[^ety-natriuretic] **peptides** from the heart push the other way. The urine then leaves through the ureters to the bladder, which empties by the **micturition**[^ety-micturition] **reflex** — a spinal parasympathetic reflex (S2–S4) switched on or off by the pons, midbrain and paracentral lobule. Everything here answers:
> - ✅ **How do aldosterone and ADH act, and which drugs block them?** — MR → ENaC / ROMK (spironolactone, amiloride); V2 → AQP-2 (vaptans)
> - ✅ **What sets ADH and ANP levels, and what does free-water clearance tell you?** — osmolality, ECF volume; −ve in SIADH, +ve in diabetes insipidus
> - ✅ **How does the bladder fill quietly and empty completely?** — Laplace's law keeps pressure flat; stretch → parasympathetic → detrusor contracts, sphincters relax

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Customs Office and a Water Tank

Think of the collecting duct as the **customs office at the border**: by now ~90 % of salt and water has already been waved through, and two officers make the final call. **Aldosterone** is the salt officer — he lets Na⁺ back in and charges a fee in K⁺ and H⁺. **ADH** is the water officer — he opens water gates (AQP-2) only when the body is short of water. **ANP** is the heart's complaint letter — "too much volume, let salt go". Downstream, the **bladder is a stretchy water tank** with a valve operated by a spinal reflex, overruled from the brain.

```
ALDOSTERONE (steroid, crosses membrane) → MR in PRINCIPAL cell → nucleus → ↑ENaC (Na⁺ + H₂O in) · ↑Na⁺K⁺ATPase → K⁺ out (ROMK)
                                          → TYPE A INTERCALATED cell → ↑H⁺K⁺ATPase → H⁺ out
ADH (peptide, 1st messenger) → V2 (Gs) → AC → cAMP → PKA → AQP-2 into lumen membrane → facultative H₂O in
ANP (atria) / BNP (ventricles) / CNP (endothelium) → afferent dilatation → ↑GFR → natriuresis · aldosterone escape
BLADDER: fill (Laplace: ↑T ↑r → P flat) → 150 mL first urge → 400 mL fullness → stretch → S2–S4 parasympathetic
         → detrusor contracts → internal then external sphincter relax → micturition (pons facilitates, midbrain inhibits)
```

### ADH / VASOPRESSIN

**Site of action in collecting duct:**
1. **P cells** (principal cells).
2. **Medullary collecting duct.**

**Mechanism of action — major action: solute-free water reabsorption by facilitated diffusion**[^ety-diffusion]**.**

![[Collecting Duct and Micturition Reflex - ADH action via V2 receptor and aquaporin-2.png]]
*ADH on the collecting-duct cell — ADH binds the V2 receptor (a GPCR) on the interstitial side → Gs → adenylyl cyclase (AC) → ATP → cAMP → protein kinase A → protein phosphorylation → aquaporin-2 (AQP-2) inserted in the luminal membrane → hormone-dependent facultative H₂O reabsorption; water leaves through AQP-3 and AQP-4 on the blood side; conivaptan blocks V2 (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
ADH ──► V2 receptor (GPCR) ──► Gs ──► Adenylyl cyclase (AC): ATP → cAMP ──► Protein kinase A
                                                                                  ↓
                                                                     Protein phosphorylation
                                                                                  ↓
                                              Aquaporin-2 (AQP-2) vesicles → inserted in luminal membrane
                                                                                  ↓
                                     H₂O enters (hormone-dependent facultative H₂O reabsorption)
                                                                                  ↓
                                               leaves to interstitium through AQP-3 / AQP-4
Conivaptan ⊣ V2
```

- **ADH → V2**[^4] **(GPCR**[^ety-gpcr]**)** → **Gs** → **AC** → **cAMP**[^ety-camp] → **protein kinase**[^ety-kinase] **A** → **protein phosphorylation**[^ety-phosphorylation] → **aquaporin**[^ety-aquaporin][^pre-aqua]**-2 (AQP-2)** → **hormone dependent (ADH) facultative**[^ety-facultative] **H₂O reabsorption.**
- Blood side: **AQP-3, AQP-4.**
- **Conivaptan** ⊣ V2.

> [!info] **ADH:**
> - **Protein hormone.**
> - **Can't cross cell membrane.**
> - **1st messenger** (cAMP is the 2nd messenger).
>
> 🧠 Contrast with aldosterone: a steroid slips through the membrane to a nuclear receptor (slow, needs new protein); a peptide must knock on a surface receptor and use a second messenger (fast — AQP-2 vesicles already wait inside the cell).

**Applied aspect:**

**1. Aquaporins:**
- **Vaptans**[^ety-vaptan] **(conivaptan): V2 receptor blockers.**
  - **↑ pure water excretion in urine.**

**2. Vasopressin in cancer: SIADH**[^ety-siadh].
- **Kidney escapes the effect of ↑↑ ADH by downregulation**[^pre-de] **of AqP-2.**[^5]

### Free water clearance

| Free water clearance[^ety-clearance] | Concentration of urine | Level of ADH | Seen in |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Negative** | **High** | **High** | **Syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion (SIADH)** |
| **Positive** | **Low** | **Low / absent** | **Diabetes insipidus**[^ety-diabetes-insipidus] |

- **Free water clearance is zero → kidney has lost the ability to dilute or concentrate urine.**
- **Max water reabsorption: PCT (67 %).**

🧠 Free-water clearance = urine flow − osmolar clearance. Positive means the kidney is excreting water without solute (dilute urine — no ADH); negative means it is holding water back (concentrated urine — ADH acting); zero means urine is isotonic, as when the medullary gradient or the diluting segment is lost.[^8]

**Types:**

| | **A type (ANP)** | **B type (BNP)** | **C type (CNP)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Site** | **Atrium**[^ety-atrium] | **Cardiac ventricles**[^ety-ventricle] | **Vascular endothelium** |

**Site (of action): terminal DCT and CD.**

**Action:**

**1. Natriuresis**[^suf-uresis]**: ↑ Na⁺ excretion.**

```
Dilatation of afferent arteriole → ↑ GFR → ↑ Na⁺ filtration & excretion
```

**2. Physiological response regulating ANP:**
- **Fluid overload, ↑ ECF (excess Na⁺ & H₂O) → ↑ ANP.**
- **Hypovolemia**[^ety-hypovolaemia][^pre-hypo]**, → ↓ central nervous pressure (low Na⁺ & H₂O) → ↓ ANP.**[^9]

**3. Physiological antagonism**[^ety-antagonism] **→ maintains homeostasis**[^ety-homeostasis]**.**
- **↑ Na⁺: activates natriuretic peptide.**
- **↓ Na⁺: activates Renin-Angiotensin system (RAAS).**

**4. Aldosterone escape:**
- **Kidneys escape the effect of aldosterone in hyperaldosteronism**[^ety-hyperaldosteronism]**.**

```
↑↑ Aldosterone → ↑ Na⁺ & H₂O reabsorption → ↑ BP, no edema. Mechanism:
     a. ↑ BP → pressure diuresis
     b. Atrial natriuretic peptide (aldosterone antagonist) → causes natriuresis
```

- **↑↑ aldosterone → ↑ Na⁺ & H₂O reabsorption → ↑ BP, no edema**[^ety-oedema] **— mechanism:**
  - **a. ↑ BP → pressure diuresis**[^ety-diuresis]**.**
  - **b. Atrial natriuretic peptide (aldosterone antagonist): causes natriuresis.**[^10]


🧠 Laplace's law: as the bladder fills, the wall tension (T) rises but so does the radius (r), so pressure (P) barely changes — the bladder can store 400 mL without leaking.[^14]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> The collecting duct carries only ~10 % of the filtrate, but it is the only segment where **hormones decide the final composition** of urine. **Aldosterone** (steroid → nuclear MR) buys Na⁺ with K⁺ and H⁺ through ENaC, ROMK and H⁺–K⁺ ATPase; **ADH** (peptide → V2 → cAMP) opens AQP-2 so water flows into the hyperosmotic medulla; **natriuretic peptides** oppose both, which is why aldosterone excess raises BP without oedema (aldosterone escape). Every drug in the chapter blocks one of these doors: spironolactone (MR), amiloride (ENaC), vaptans (V2); Liddle's syndrome is the door stuck open.
>
> The bladder obeys **Laplace's law** while filling (pressure flat) and a **sacral parasympathetic reflex** while emptying (detrusor contracts, sphincters relax), with the pons (Barrington) facilitating, the midbrain inhibiting and the paracentral lobule giving voluntary command over the external sphincter through Onuf's nucleus.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Aldosterone (driven by hyperkalaemia and angiotensin II) and ADH (driven by plasma osmolality > ~280 mOsm/kg and low ECF volume) fine-tune Na⁺, K⁺, H⁺ and water; ANP rises with atrial stretch. The bladder stores up to ~400 mL at low pressure, first urge at ~150 mL, and empties completely under pontine coordination.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> In primary hyperaldosteronism, Na⁺ retention raises BP, but pressure diuresis and ANP produce **aldosterone escape**, so oedema does not develop — only hypertension with hypokalaemia and alkalosis. In SIADH the kidney partly escapes by down-regulating AQP-2, limiting hyponatraemia. Early bladder outlet obstruction is compensated by detrusor hypertrophy.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Liddle's syndrome (ENaC stuck open) and hyperaldosteronism cause resistant hypertension with hypokalaemia; aldosterone deficiency (Addison's, spironolactone, amiloride) causes hyperkalaemia and salt wasting. Lack of ADH or of its renal action (diabetes insipidus) gives positive free-water clearance and polyuria; SIADH gives negative free-water clearance and dilutional hyponatraemia. Lesions of the paracentral lobule, the pons–sacral pathway or Onuf's nucleus produce urinary incontinence or retention.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → hormone excess / deficiency / receptor defect / spinal or cortical lesion → early compensation (escape, AQP-2 down-regulation, detrusor hypertrophy) → overt K⁺, Na⁺, water and BP disturbances or incontinence / retention
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Recognising which door is affected predicts the drug: amiloride for Liddle's, spironolactone for hyperaldosteronism and heart failure, vaptans for euvolaemic hyponatraemia (SIADH), desmopressin for central DI and von Willebrand disease, terlipressin for variceal bleeding and hepatorenal syndrome.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Liddle's syndrome:** "Young hypertensive with hypokalaemia and metabolic alkalosis but **low renin and low aldosterone**", "responds to amiloride, not spironolactone" → gain-of-function ENaC.
>
> **Primary hyperaldosteronism:** "Hypertension, hypokalaemia, **no oedema**", "high aldosterone, low renin" → aldosterone escape via pressure natriuresis and ANP.
>
> **SIADH:** "Small-cell lung cancer", "hyponatraemia with concentrated urine, euvolaemic", "negative free-water clearance" → ectopic or excess ADH; treat with fluid restriction, vaptans.
>
> **Diabetes insipidus:** "Polyuria with dilute urine", "positive free-water clearance", "low / absent ADH" (central) → desmopressin.
>
> **Bladder control:** "Frontal / paracentral lesion (ACA stroke) → incontinence"; "Onuf's nucleus spared in ALS" → continence preserved; "cold weather → frequent urination" → posterior hypothalamus.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> A flat cystometrogram until ~400 mL then a vertical spike = normal bladder (Laplace); a principal cell with ENaC and ROMK on the lumen side = aldosterone target; a cell with V2 → cAMP → AQP-2 = ADH target.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> First urge to void ~150 mL; marked fullness ~400 mL; free-water clearance negative (SIADH) vs positive (DI) vs zero (isosthenuria); PCT reabsorbs 67 % of water, the collecting duct the facultative remainder.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Spironolactone and amiloride work in Liddle's syndrome equally**
>
> **What students think:** Any K⁺-sparing diuretic treats Liddle's.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In Liddle's the ENaC channel itself is over-active independent of aldosterone (aldosterone is actually suppressed). Blocking the mineralocorticoid receptor (spironolactone) does nothing; blocking the channel (amiloride, triamterene) works.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Spironolactone blocks the **receptor**, amiloride blocks the **channel** — Liddle's is a **channel** disease.
>
> **Trap 2: Thinking ADH crosses the membrane like aldosterone**
>
> **What students think:** Both kidney hormones act on nuclear receptors.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Aldosterone is a steroid (lipid-soluble) and binds an intracellular MR; ADH is a protein hormone that cannot cross the membrane, so it acts as a **first messenger** on the V2 GPCR and uses cAMP as the second messenger to insert pre-formed AQP-2.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Steroid = in the nucleus, slow; peptide = on the surface, fast.
>
> **Trap 3: Mixing up the vasopressin receptors**
>
> **What students think:** V2 is the vascular receptor because terlipressin and desmopressin are both "vasopressins".
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **V1** = blood vessels (vasoconstriction — terlipressin for varices); **V2** = collecting duct and endothelium (water reabsorption and vWF release — desmopressin for DI and von Willebrand disease); **V3 (V1b)** = anterior pituitary (ACTH).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "V1 = Vessels, V2 = water (two kidneys), V3 = the pituitary above."
>
> **Trap 4: Reading free-water clearance backwards**
>
> **What students think:** Positive free-water clearance means the kidney is conserving water.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Positive C(H₂O) means solute-free water is being **excreted** (dilute urine, low ADH — diabetes insipidus); negative means water is being **retained** (concentrated urine, high ADH — SIADH).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** The sign tells you what happens to **water in the urine**: + = more water out.
>
> **Trap 5: Sympathetic = emptying**
>
> **What students think:** Micturition is a sympathetic "fight or flight" discharge.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Parasympathetic (S2–S4)** contracts the detrusor and relaxes the internal sphincter — it facilitates micturition. **Sympathetic (L1–L3)** supports storage (and closes the bladder neck during ejaculation, preventing retrograde ejaculation). The external sphincter is **somatic** (pudendal, Onuf's nucleus).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**P**arasympathetic = **P**ee; **S**ympathetic = **S**tore; **S**omatic = **S**ay when."

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Collecting duct hormones: **aldosterone** and **ADH / vasopressin**; parts: **cortical (CCD)** and **medullary (MCD)**.
> 2. Aldosterone: **steroid from adrenal cortex, crosses membrane**; **hyperkalaemia = major stimulus**; acts on **principal cell** via **MR → nucleus → ↑ ENaC (Na⁺, H₂O in) + ↑ Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase → K⁺ out (ROMK)**.
> 3. Type A intercalated cell: **H⁺ ATPase + H⁺–K⁺ ATPase (↑ by aldosterone) → H⁺ excretion; Cl⁻–HCO₃⁻ exchanger** on blood side.
> 4. **Amiloride blocks ENaC** (diuretic); **spironolactone blocks MR** (K⁺-sparing); **Liddle's = overactive ENaC → hypertension**.
> 5. ADH: **protein, cannot cross membrane, 1st messenger** → **V2 (GPCR) → Gs → AC → cAMP → PKA → AQP-2** → facultative water reabsorption; **AQP-3, AQP-4** basolateral; acts on **P cells** and **medullary CD**.
> 6. **Vaptans (conivaptan) = V2 blockers → ↑ pure water excretion**; in **SIADH** the kidney escapes by **↓ AQP-2**.
> 7. **↑ ADH**: ↑ osmotic pressure, ↓ ECF, pain, nausea & vomiting; **↓ ADH**: ↓ osmotic pressure, ↑ ECF, **alcohol**.
> 8. **V1** vessels → vasoconstriction → **terlipressin (varices)**; **V2** CD + endothelium → vWF → **desmopressin, intranasal (von Willebrand)**; **V3** anterior pituitary → ACTH.
> 9. Free-water clearance: **negative → SIADH** (high ADH, concentrated urine); **positive → diabetes insipidus**; **zero → cannot dilute or concentrate**; **max water reabsorption = PCT (67 %)**.
> 10. **ANP — atrium; BNP — ventricles; CNP — vascular endothelium**; act on **terminal DCT and CD**; **afferent dilatation → ↑ GFR → natriuresis**; **↑ Na⁺ → ANP, ↓ Na⁺ → RAAS**.
> 11. **Aldosterone escape**: ↑ aldosterone → ↑ BP, **no oedema** — **pressure diuresis + ANP**.
> 12. Bladder: **sympathetic L1–L3** (pain, prevents retrograde ejaculation), **parasympathetic S2–S4** (facilitates micturition), **somatic S2–S4, Onuf's nucleus** (voluntary).
> 13. **Pons = Barrington centre** (facilitates); **posterior hypothalamus — cold → ↑ frequency**; **midbrain inhibits**; **paracentral lobule (ACA) = highest control → lesion: incontinence**.
> 14. Reflex: **stretch → afferents → parasympathetic → detrusor contracts, internal then external sphincter relax**; CMG: **150 mL first urge, 400 mL marked fullness, flat Ib by Laplace (↑T, ↑r)**.

[^ety-facultative]: **facultative** — Latin *facultas*, "ability, opportunity" (*facilis*, doable) + *-ive* → optional; water reabsorbed only when ADH allows. **Differentiate:** *facultative* (CD, hormone-dependent) vs *obligatory* (PCT, follows Na⁺).
[^ety-vaptan]: **vaptan (conivaptan, tolvaptan)** — the drug stem **-vaptan** = *va(sopressin)* + *-ptan* (antagonist) → vasopressin-receptor antagonists; *conivaptan* blocks V1a and V2 (IV), *tolvaptan* is V2-selective (oral). They cause aquaresis — free water loss without salt.
[^ety-siadh]: **SIADH** — Syndrome of Inappropriate (Latin *in-*, not + *appropriare*, to make one's own → not suited) AntiDiuretic Hormone secretion → ADH released although plasma is dilute; described by Schwartz and Bartter (1957) in lung cancer.
[^ety-osmotic]: **osmotic** — Greek *ōsmos*, "push, thrust" + *-tic* → relating to the push of water across a membrane; *osmotic pressure* of plasma (~285 mOsm/kg) is the main ADH stimulus. Do not confuse with *osmo-* "smell" (*osmē*).
[^ety-alcohol]: **alcohol** — Arabic *al-kuḥl*, "the kohl" (a fine antimony powder) → any fine essence → the "essence" of wine (16th c.). Ethanol inhibits ADH release from the posterior pituitary → diuresis after drinking.
[^ety-nausea]: **nausea** — Greek *nausia*, "seasickness" (*naus*, ship) → the queasy feeling before vomiting; one of the strongest ADH stimuli (up to 100-fold). Same root as *nautical*, *navy*.
[^ety-vasoconstriction]: **vasoconstriction** — Latin *vas*, "vessel" + *con-*, "together" + *stringere*, "to draw tight" + *-ion* → drawing a vessel tight; V1 action. **Differentiate:** *vasoconstriction* (narrowing) vs *vasodilatation* (widening) vs *vasospasm* (sudden, excessive constriction).
[^ety-terlipressin]: **terlipressin** — *tri-* (*ter-*, Latin "three times") + *glycyl* + *lysine vasopressin* → **tri**glycyl-**l**ysine-vaso**pressin**: lysine vasopressin with three glycine residues that are slowly cleaved off (a prodrug), giving long V1 action.
[^ety-varices]: **varices (oesophageal)** — Latin *varix, varicis*, "a dilated vein" → dilated, tortuous veins; oesophageal varices form in portal hypertension. Terlipressin's splanchnic V1 vasoconstriction lowers portal pressure and stops the bleeding.
[^ety-endothelium]: **endothelium** — Greek *endon*, "within" + *thēlē*, "nipple" + *-ium* → the inner lining of vessels; its Weibel–Palade bodies store von Willebrand factor, released by V2 stimulation.
[^ety-desmopressin]: **desmopressin (dDAVP)** — *des-amino* + *D-arginine* + *vasopressin* → **d**esamino-**D**-**a**rginine **v**aso**p**ressin: removing the amino group and using D-arginine makes it long-acting and V2-selective. Used for central DI, nocturnal enuresis, haemophilia A (mild) and von Willebrand disease type 1.
[^ety-von-willebrand]: **von Willebrand disease** — Erik Adolf von Willebrand (1870–1949), Finnish physician, who in 1926 described a bleeding disorder in families of the Åland Islands. vWF carries factor VIII and glues platelets to injured vessels.
[^ety-pituitary]: **pituitary** — Latin *pituita*, "phlegm, slime" + *-ary* → the gland once thought (Galen, Vesalius) to drain phlegm from the brain into the nose. *Anterior* pituitary (adenohypophysis) has V3 (V1b) receptors on corticotrophs.
[^ety-acth]: **ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone)** — Latin *ad-* + *ren*, kidney (→ adrenal) + *cortex* + Greek *tropos*, "turning toward" + *-ic* → the hormone that turns toward (stimulates) the adrenal cortex. ADH (via V3) potentiates CRH in releasing it.
[^ety-clearance]: **clearance** — Latin *clarus*, "clear" + *-ance* → the volume of plasma cleared per minute; *free-water clearance* = the volume of solute-free water excreted (+) or retained (−) per minute.
[^ety-diabetes-insipidus]: **diabetes insipidus** — Greek *diabētēs*, "siphon, passing through" (*dia-* + *bainein*, to go) + Latin *insipidus*, "tasteless" (*in-*, not + *sapidus*, tasty) → a tasteless siphoning of urine, contrasted with the sweet urine of *diabetes mellitus* (*mel*, honey). **Differentiate:** *central* DI (no ADH — responds to desmopressin) vs *nephrogenic* DI (kidney ignores ADH — V2 or AQP-2 defect, lithium).
[^ety-atrium]: **atrium** — Latin *atrium*, "entrance hall" (of a Roman house) → the receiving chamber of the heart; its stretch releases ANP. Plural *atria*. **Differentiate:** *atrium* (the chamber) vs *auricle* (Latin *auricula*, little ear — the ear-shaped appendage).
[^ety-ventricle]: **ventricle** — Latin *ventriculus*, "little belly" (*venter*, belly + *-culus*, little) → the muscular pumping chamber; stretched ventricles release BNP (a heart-failure marker). Same word for the brain's ventricles.
[^ety-hypovolaemia]: **hypovolaemia** — Greek *hypo-*, "under" + Latin *volumen*, "a roll, volume" + Greek *haima*, "blood" + *-ia* → too little blood volume; it lowers atrial stretch and ANP. **Differentiate:** *hypovolaemia* (low volume) vs *hyponatraemia* (low Na⁺ concentration) vs *dehydration* (water loss).
[^ety-antagonism]: **antagonism** — Greek *anti-*, "against" + *agōnizesthai*, "to struggle" (*agōn*, contest) + *-ism* → struggling against; *physiological antagonism* = two systems with opposite effects through different receptors (ANP vs RAAS). **Differentiate:** *physiological* antagonism (different receptors, opposite effects) vs *pharmacological / competitive* antagonism (same receptor — spironolactone at MR).
[^ety-homeostasis]: **homeostasis** — Greek *homoios*, "similar" + *stasis*, "standing" → staying similar; Walter Cannon (1926). See [[Homeostasis]].
[^ety-hyperaldosteronism]: **hyperaldosteronism** — Greek *hyper-*, "over" + *aldosterone* + *-ism*, a state → a state of excess aldosterone. *Primary* (Conn's syndrome — adrenal adenoma, low renin) vs *secondary* (high renin — renal artery stenosis, heart failure, cirrhosis).
[^ety-oedema]: **oedema (edema)** — Greek *oidēma*, "a swelling" (*oidein*, to swell) → excess interstitial fluid. Its absence in primary hyperaldosteronism is the clue to aldosterone escape.
[^ety-diuresis]: **diuresis** — Greek *dia-*, "through" + *ourēsis*, "urination" → increased urine flow; *pressure diuresis* = ↑ urine output when arterial pressure rises (Guyton's renal–body fluid mechanism). **Differentiate:** *diuresis* (↑ urine volume) vs *natriuresis* (↑ Na⁺ excretion) vs *aquaresis* (↑ free water).
[^ety-ureter]: **ureter** — Greek *ourētēr*, "the urinary passage" (*ourein*, to urinate) → the tube from kidney to bladder. **Differentiate:** *ureter* (kidney → bladder, two of them) vs *urethra* (bladder → outside, one).
[^ety-peristalsis]: **peristalsis** — Greek *peri-*, "around" + *stellein*, "to place, compress" (*stalsis*, constriction) → constriction all around; a ring of contraction moving along a tube. **Differentiate:** *peristalsis* (propulsive wave) vs *segmentation* (mixing contractions) vs *antiperistalsis* (reverse wave).
[^ety-sympathetic]: **sympathetic / parasympathetic** — Greek *syn-* (*sym-*), "together" + *pathos*, "feeling" → feeling together (Galen); *para-*, "beside" → the system beside it (craniosacral outflow — S2–S4 for the bladder).
[^ety-hypogastric]: **hypogastric** — Greek *hypo-*, "below" + *gastēr*, "belly, stomach" + *-ic* → below the stomach (the lower abdomen); the hypogastric nerves carry sympathetic fibres from L1–L2 to the bladder.
[^ety-ganglion]: **ganglion** — Greek *ganglion*, "a swelling, a knot under the skin" (Galen used it for nerve knots) → a cluster of nerve-cell bodies outside the CNS; the inferior mesenteric ganglion relays bladder sympathetic fibres. Plural *ganglia*.
[^ety-retrograde]: **retrograde** — Latin *retro-*, "backwards" + *gradi*, "to step, walk" → stepping backwards; *retrograde ejaculation* = semen goes back into the bladder when the bladder neck fails to close (sympathetic loss, α-blockers, TURP). **Differentiate:** *retrograde* (backward) vs *anterograde* (forward).
[^ety-ejaculation]: **ejaculation** — Latin *e-* (*ex-*), "out" + *jaculari*, "to throw a javelin" (*jaculum*, javelin) + *-ation* → throwing out; emission is sympathetic, expulsion somatic.
[^ety-somatic]: **somatic** — Greek *sōma, sōmatos*, "body" + *-tic* → of the body (wall), i.e. voluntary skeletal-muscle supply as opposed to visceral (autonomic). The pudendal nerve is the somatic supply to the external urethral sphincter.
[^ety-pudendal]: **pudendal** — Latin *pudenda*, "the parts to be ashamed of" (*pudere*, to be ashamed) + *-al* → of the external genitalia; the pudendal nerve (S2–S4) supplies the external sphincters and perineum.
[^ety-onuf]: **Onuf's nucleus** — Bronisław Onufrowicz (1863–1928), Polish-born neurologist in New York, who described the nucleus in the sacral anterior horn in 1899 (published as "Onuf"). It is spared in ALS but affected in multiple system atrophy.
[^ety-pons]: **pons** — Latin *pons*, "bridge" → the part of the brainstem that bridges the two cerebellar hemispheres (Varolius, 16th c. — *pons Varolii*). The pontine micturition centre coordinates detrusor contraction with sphincter relaxation.
[^ety-barrington]: **Barrington's centre** — Frederick J. F. Barrington (1884–1956), English urologist at University College Hospital, London, who located the pontine micturition centre in cats in 1925.
[^ety-hypothalamus]: **hypothalamus** — Greek *hypo-*, "below" + *thalamos*, "inner chamber" → the region below the thalamus; its posterior part (heat conservation) explains why cold increases urinary frequency.
[^ety-paracentral]: **paracentral lobule** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + Latin *centrum*, "centre" + *-al* → beside the central sulcus; *lobule* = Latin *lobulus*, small lobe (Greek *lobos*, lobe + *-ulus*, small). It holds the leg and perineal areas of the motor and sensory cortex.
[^ety-incontinence]: **incontinence** — Latin *in-*, "not" + *continere*, "to hold together, contain" (*con-* + *tenere*, to hold) + *-ence* → not holding in; involuntary leakage of urine. **Differentiate:** *incontinence* (cannot hold) vs *retention* (cannot empty) vs *enuresis* (involuntary voiding, usually at night in children).
[^ety-afferent]: **afferent** — Latin *ad-* (→ *af-*), "towards" + *ferre*, "to carry" → carrying toward the spinal cord (stretch signals from the bladder wall travel in the pelvic nerves).
[^ety-detrusor]: **detrusor** — Latin *de-*, "down, away" + *trudere*, "to thrust, push" + *-or*, agent → "the one that thrusts down/away" — the smooth muscle of the bladder wall that expels urine. Same root as *intrude*, *protrude*.
[^ety-sphincter]: **sphincter** — Greek *sphinktēr*, "a band, that which binds tight" (*sphingein*, to bind; same root as *Sphinx*, "the strangler") → a ring muscle that closes an opening. **Differentiate:** *internal* urethral sphincter (smooth, involuntary, sympathetic α1) vs *external* (skeletal, voluntary, pudendal).
[^ety-urethra]: **urethra** — Greek *ourēthra*, from *ourein*, "to urinate" → the channel through which urine leaves the body. **Differentiate:** *urethra* (one, out of the bladder) vs *ureter* (two, into it).
[^ety-trigone]: **trigone** — Greek *tri-*, "three" + *gōnia*, "angle, corner" → a three-cornered area: the smooth triangle of the bladder base between the two ureteric orifices and the internal urethral orifice. Same root as *trigonometry*.
[^ety-prostate]: **prostate** — Greek *pro-*, "before, in front" + *histanai*, "to stand" → *prostatēs*, "one who stands before, a guardian" (Herophilus, c. 300 BC — the gland standing before the bladder). Do not misspell as *prostrate* ("lying flat").
[^ety-verumontanum]: **verumontanum** — Latin *veru*, "a spit, a dart" + *montanus*, "of a mountain" → a little mountain ridge (the colliculus seminalis) on the back wall of the prostatic urethra where the ejaculatory ducts and the prostatic utricle open.
[^ety-laplace]: **Laplace's law** — Pierre-Simon, Marquis de Laplace (1749–1827), French mathematician and astronomer, who described the pressure–tension–radius relation of curved surfaces (1806). P = 2T/r for a sphere: the larger the radius, the less pressure a given wall tension produces.
[^ety-cystometrogram]: **cystometrogram** — Greek *kystis*, "bladder, bag" + *metron*, "measure" + *gramma*, "something written, a record" → the written record of bladder (pressure–volume) measurement. **Differentiate:** *cystometrogram* (the graph) vs *cystometry* (the procedure) vs *cystoscopy* (looking inside the bladder).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: antidiuretic, antagonism, antiporter. Don't confuse with **ante-** before · **ana-** up, again.
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, across: diuretic, diuresis, diabetes, dialysis. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis- / dif-** apart (diffusion) · **dys-** bad, difficult.
[^pre-ad]: **ad- (af-, ac- …)** — to, towards, near: adrenal, afferent, accommodation. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from · **a- / an-** without.
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, excessive: hyperkalaemia, hypertension, hyperaldosteronism. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra- / super-** above (Latin).
[^suf-aemia]: **-aemia / -emia** — a condition of the blood: hyperkalaemia, hypovolaemia, acidaemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** a condition of the urine · **-osis** a process (acidosis).
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: receptor, reabsorption, release. Don't confuse with **ren-** kidney · **retro-** backward (retrograde).
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, over: epithelial, epinephrine, epididymis. Don't confuse with **endo-** within (endothelium) · **ecto-** outside.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: ATPase, kinase, adenylyl cyclase, carbonic anhydrase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar · **-in** a non-enzyme protein (aquaporin).
[^pre-inter]: **inter-** — between: intercalated, interstitial. Don't confuse with **intra-** within (intranasal) · **infra-** below.
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a process or condition: acidosis, alkalosis, osmosis. Don't confuse with **-aemia** of the blood (acidaemia — the resulting low pH) · **-itis** inflammation.
[^pre-ex]: **ex- (ef-, e-)** — out of: exchanger, excretion, ejaculation. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek) · **extra-** beyond.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together: syndrome, sympathetic. Don't confuse with **semi-** half.
[^pre-aqua]: **aqua-** — water (Latin): aquaporin, aquaresis. Don't confuse with **hydro-** water (Greek).
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, away, reversal: downregulation (decrease in receptor/channel number), dephosphorylation, desmopressin (*des-*, removal of the amino group), detrusor (thrusting down). Don't confuse with **dis-** apart · **dys-** bad.
[^pre-vaso]: **vaso-** — vessel (Latin *vas*): vasoconstriction, vasopressin, vasa recta. Don't confuse with **angio-** vessel (Greek equivalent) · **vagus** (Latin *vagus*, wandering — the nerve).
[^pre-tri]: **tri- / ter-** — three, three times (Greek *tri-*, Latin *ter-*): terlipressin (triglycyl), trigone (three corners), triamterene. Don't confuse with **tetra-** four · **di- / bi-** two · **trans-** across.
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endothelium, endocrine. Don't confuse with **epi-** upon (epithelium) · **exo-** outside.
[^pre-intra]: **intra-** — within, into: intranasal (route of administration of desmopressin), intravesical (pressure inside the bladder). Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **extra-** outside.
[^suf-uresis]: **-uresis / -uretic** — the passing of urine: natriuresis, diuresis, aquaresis; adjectives natriuretic, diuretic. Don't confuse with **-uria** a condition of the urine (glycosuria) · **-aemia** of the blood.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypovolaemia, hypothalamus, hypogastric. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep.
[^pre-peri]: **peri-** — around: peristalsis, pericardium. Don't confuse with **para-** beside · **per-** through.
[^pre-retro]: **retro-** — backwards, behind: retrograde, retroperitoneal. Don't confuse with **re-** back, again · **ante- / antero-** before, forward.
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside: parasympathetic, paracentral. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before.
[^suf-ule]: **-ule / -ulus / -ulum** — small: lobule, tubule, ventriculus (*-culus*). Don't confuse with **-ole** an alcohol (in chemistry) · **-oma** a tumour.
[^pre-in]: **in- (im-, il-, ir-)** — not (Latin negative): incontinence, inappropriate, insipidus. Don't confuse with **in- / im-** into (infusion) · **intra-** within.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, in front of, forward: prostate (standing before the bladder), protrusion. Don't confuse with **post-** after · **pre-** before (in time) · **para-** beside.
[^pre-cysto]: **cysto- / cyst-** — bladder, sac (Greek *kystis*): cystometrogram, cystitis, cystoscopy. Don't confuse with **-cyte / cyto-** cell · **cystine / cysteine** (amino acids — first found in bladder stones, hence the name).
[^suf-gram]: **-gram / -graph / -graphy** — a record / the instrument / the procedure: cystometrogram (the record), electrocardiograph (the machine), cystography (the procedure). Don't confuse with **-gram** as a unit of mass (kilogram) · **-scopy** looking (cystoscopy).
