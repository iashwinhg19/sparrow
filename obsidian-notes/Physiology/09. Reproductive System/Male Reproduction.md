# ♂️ Male Reproduction

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> Male **reproduction**[^ety-reproduction][^pre-re][^suf-ation] is the chain that starts with one gene[^ety-gene] on the Y **chromosome**[^ety-chromosome][^suf-some] (**SRY**), wakes up at **puberty**[^ety-puberty] when GnRH becomes **pulsatile**[^ety-pulsatile], and runs in the adult as **two cells answering two gonadotrophins**[^ety-gonadotrophin][^suf-trophin] — **Leydig**[^ety-leydig] **cells (LH → testosterone**[^ety-testosterone][^suf-one]**)** and **Sertoli**[^ety-sertoli] **cells (FSH → spermatogenesis**[^ety-spermatogenesis][^suf-genesis] **support, inhibin B**[^ety-inhibin][^suf-in]**)**. Every line in the chapter answers one of three questions:
> - ✅ **What makes the body male** — SRY → testis → testosterone (Wolffian, internal) + AMH (Müllerian regression) + DHT (external, puberty)?
> - ✅ **How are sperm and semen made and delivered** — spermatogenesis (74 days), maturation, semen composition, erection → emission → ejaculation?
> - ✅ **How is it regulated** — hypothalamus → GnRH → LH/FSH → testosterone / inhibin B, with negative feedback?

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Two-Worker Factory Switched On at Puberty

Picture the testis as a factory with **two workers**: the **Leydig cell** (the engine room, making testosterone on LH's order) and the **Sertoli cell** (the nursery, raising sperm on FSH's order and sending back inhibin B as the "enough" signal). The factory is built by **SRY** before birth, sits idle in childhood because the GnRH switch is held down, and starts work at puberty once fat stores (leptin) and kisspeptin make GnRH **pulse**.

```
BUILD (fetus)   SRY → testis → Leydig: testosterone → Wolffian (internal)   · Sertoli: AMH → Müllerian regress
                                     └→ 5α-reductase → DHT → external genitalia
SWITCH (puberty) fat → leptin → kisspeptin → GPR54 → PULSATILE GnRH → LH + FSH
RUN (adult)     LH → Leydig → testosterone ──(–)→ GnRH, LH
                FSH → Sertoli → spermatogenesis (74 d) + inhibin B ──(–)→ FSH
DELIVER         erection = Parasympathetic/NO/cGMP ("Point") · emission → ejaculation = Sympathetic ("Shoot")
```

The **SRY gene** sits on the **short arm** of the Y chromosome, just below the top **pseudoautosomal**[^ety-pseudoautosomal][^pre-pseudo][^pre-auto] **region**; the long arm, the **centromere**[^ety-centromere][^pre-centro] and a second pseudoautosomal region at the bottom make up the rest of the drawing.

```
SRY gene → Testis-determining gene produces (transcription factors)
                              │ acts on
              ┌───────────────┴────────────────┐
         Leydig cells                     Sertoli cells
              ↓                                ↓
        Testosterone                 Anti-mullerian hormone
              ↓                                ↓
   Wolffian system stimulation       Inhibits female organs development
              ↓
```

- **SRY → testis-determining gene** produces **transcription factors**[^ety-transcription][^pre-trans] that act on two cells:
  - **Leydig cells → testosterone** → **Wolffian**[^ety-wolffian] **system stimulation** → **male organs development**.
  - **Sertoli cells → anti-mullerian**[^ety-mullerian][^pre-anti] **hormone** → **inhibits female organs development**.[^1]
- **Absence of SRY → Female.**[^2]

## 🌱 Puberty

### Pre-pubertal

```
Continuous release of GnRH ──(–)──→ LH
  (maintained by GABA, melatonin)  └→ FSH
```

Before **puberty** the hypothalamus releases **GnRH**[^ety-gnrh] **continuously** (not in pulses), and a continuous signal **switches the pituitary off** (–) for LH and FSH. This brake is **maintained by GABA**[^ety-gaba] **and melatonin**[^ety-melatonin].[^3]

### At puberty

```
Adequate fat stores → ↑ leptin ──→ ↑↑ Kisspeptin ──(GPR54 in GnRH neuron)──→ Pulsatile release of GnRH
                   (permissive role                                                │ when GnRH ↓
                    in puberty)                                                    ↓ (+)
                                                                              LH       FSH
```

- **Adequate fat stores → ↑ leptin**[^ety-leptin] (**permissive**[^ety-permissive][^pre-per] **role in puberty**).[^4]
- **↑↑ Kisspeptin**[^ety-kisspeptin] acts on **GPR54 in the GnRH neuron**[^ety-neuron] → **pulsatile release of GnRH**.
- Pulsatile GnRH — with GnRH falling (**"when GnRH ↓"**) between pulses — **stimulates (+) LH**[^ety-lh] **and FSH**[^ety-fsh].[^5]

### Spermatogenesis

- **Location:** **seminiferous tubules**[^ety-seminiferous][^suf-ferous].
- **Duration:** **74 days.**[^7]
- **Process:**

```
Type B  Spermatogonium (stem cell)  [2n]  ◄─────┐
              │ mitosis                         │ (one daughter 2n returns to the stem pool)
              ↓                                 │
        Primary spermatocyte  [2n] ─────────────┘
              │ meiosis I (reductional division)
              ↓
        2 × Secondary spermatocyte  [1n]
              │ meiosis II
              ↓
        4 × Spermatid  [1n]
              │ Differentiation = Spermiogenesis
              ↓
        4 × Sperm  ──→ Release: Spermiation
```

**Spermatogenesis** runs in three steps: **mitosis**[^ety-mitosis][^suf-osis] keeps the **spermatogonium**[^ety-spermatogonium] pool going and makes the **primary spermatocyte**[^ety-spermatocyte][^suf-cyte]; **meiosis**[^ety-meiosis] **I** halves the chromosome number (2n → 1n, the **reductional division**[^ety-reduction]) and **meiosis II** splits the chromatids to give four **spermatids**[^ety-spermatid]; finally **spermiogenesis**[^ety-spermiogenesis] (**differentiation**[^ety-differentiation][^pre-dis]) remodels each spermatid into a sperm, which is set free into the lumen — **spermiation**[^ety-spermiation].[^8]

**Functions of Sertoli cells:**

## ❤️ Male Sexual Act

### 1. Erection[^ety-erection] of penis

**Facilitated by:**
1. **Parasympathetic**[^ety-parasympathetic][^pre-para][^pre-syn] **(S2, S3, S4 roots):** mediated by **acetylcholine**[^ety-acetylcholine].
2. **Non-adrenergic**[^ety-adrenergic][^suf-ergic] **non-cholinergic (NANC) system:**

```
Nitric oxide → Dilatation of arterioles → Compression of outflow veins → Engorgement & erection
                                                                        (acts via cGMP)
```

**Nitric oxide**[^ety-nitric-oxide] relaxes the smooth muscle of the penile **arterioles**[^ety-arteriole][^suf-ole] (**dilatation**[^ety-dilatation]); the swelling erectile tissue presses the **outflow veins** shut against the tough outer coat, so blood comes in but cannot get out — **engorgement**[^ety-engorgement][^pre-en] & erection. NO works through **cGMP**[^ety-cgmp].[^18]

**Applied aspect:**
- **cGMP degraded by phosphodiesterase**[^ety-phosphodiesterase][^pre-di].
- **Phosphodiesterase inhibitor (Sildenafil**[^ety-sildenafil]**) → ↑ cGMP → ↑ NO (nitric oxide) → erection.**[^19]

### 2. Ejaculation

- **Sympathetic**[^ety-sympathetic] **(T12–L2 nerve roots).**
- **Two steps:**

```
Emission ─────────────────────────────→ Ejaculation
(movement of semen into urethra)        (release of semen from urethra)
```

**Emission**[^ety-emission] moves semen from the vas deferens, seminal vesicle and prostate **into the urethra**[^ety-urethra]; **ejaculation** then expels it **from the urethra**.[^20]

### 3. Resolution (Detumescence)

```
Endothelin & Noradrenaline → Constriction of arterioles → Resolution
```

**Endothelin**[^ety-endothelin][^pre-endo] and **noradrenaline**[^ety-noradrenaline][^pre-nor] **constrict**[^ety-constriction] the arterioles, inflow falls, the veins reopen and the penis becomes flaccid — **resolution**[^ety-resolution] or **detumescence**[^ety-detumescence].[^21]

> [!tip] Memory — "Point and Shoot"
> **P**arasympathetic = **P**oint (erection, S2–S4, ACh + NO) · **S**ympathetic = **S**hoot (emission and ejaculation, T12–L2) · endothelin + noradrenaline = resolution.

## 🎛️ Regulation of Male Reproduction

### Two cell two gonadotrophin model — Hypothalamo pituitary testicular axis

```
                         Hypothalamus
                              ↓
    ┌ – ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ →  Gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH)
    ¦                         ↓ (+)
    ¦                  Anterior pituitary
    ¦               ┌─────────┴──────────┐
    ├ – ─ →  Luteinizing hormone (LH)   Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)  ← ─ – ┐
    ¦               ↓ (+)                         ↓ (+)                            ¦
    ¦          Leydig cells                  Sertoli cells                         ¦
    ¦               ↓                             ↓                                ¦
    └ ─ ─ ─  Testosterone                     Inhibin B  ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ┘
```

- **Hypothalamus**[^ety-hypothalamus][^pre-hypo] → **GnRH** → (+) **anterior pituitary**[^ety-pituitary] → **LH** and **FSH** (the two **gonadotrophins**).
- **LH → (+) Leydig cells → testosterone** —(–)→ GnRH and LH.
- **FSH → (+) Sertoli cells → Inhibin B** —(–)→ FSH.[^22]

> **Prolonged administration of testosterone causes azoospermia**[^ety-azoospermia][^suf-ia] **(d/t negative feedback on LH).**[^23]

## 🧔 Male Sex Hormones

![[Male Reproduction - testosterone structure.png]]
*Testosterone — the four-ring steroid with its 17-OH group; the two circled groups on the page are the 3-keto group of ring A and the C-19 methyl group at the A/B ring junction — ring A is where 5α-reductase (→ DHT) and aromatase (→ estradiol, removing C-19) act (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
Testosterone ───────── 5α reductase ─────────→ Di-hydro testosterone (DHT)
     │                                                (more potent)
     ↓                                                      ↓
 Testosterone actions                               DHT actions
```

**Testosterone**, converted by **5α reductase**[^ety-reductase] → **di-hydro testosterone (DHT)**[^ety-dht] — **more potent**.[^25]

| Testosterone | Di-hydro testosterone (DHT) |
|---|---|
| Stimulates **Wolffian duct** → formation of male **internal** genital organs | Responsible for **puberty-associated changes** |
| Stimulates **erythropoietin**[^ety-erythropoietin][^pre-erythro][^suf-poietin] production → ↑ RBC[^26] | **Masculinization**[^ety-masculinization] of **external genitalia**[^ety-genitalia] |
| **Protein anabolism**[^ety-anabolism][^pre-ana][^suf-ism] → ↑ muscle mass in males | **Development of prostate gland** (**5α reductase inhibitors — finasteride**[^ety-finasteride] — **used in Rx of benign prostatic hyperplasia**[^ety-benign][^ety-hyperplasia][^pre-hyper][^suf-plasia]) |
| Stimulates **bone growth**[^27] | Growth of **facial hair** |
| **Initiation & maintenance of spermatogenesis** | Responsible for **acne**[^ety-acne] |
| Stimulate synthesis of **ABP** from Sertoli cells | **Temporal recession of hairline** in males (**male pattern baldness**)[^ety-alopecia] |
| **Behavioral effects: ↑ libido**[^ety-libido] **in men** | — |

A simple split: **testosterone builds the inside** (Wolffian duct → epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle), **muscle, blood, bone, sperm and libido**; **DHT builds the outside** (penis, scrotum, prostate) and gives the **puberty look** — beard, acne and a receding hairline.[^28]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Maleness is a **cascade of local signals**, each acting only where its receptor and enzyme are: SRY makes a testis; the testis's two cells then make two signals — Leydig testosterone (Wolffian duct, internal organs) and Sertoli AMH (Müllerian regression); peripheral **5α-reductase** turns testosterone into DHT for the external genitalia and the prostate. Remove one link and you get a predictable phenotype (no SRY → female; no 5α-reductase → under-virilised external genitalia with normal internal ducts).
>
> In the adult the same two cells are run by **two gonadotrophins with two separate feedbacks** — LH ↔ testosterone and FSH ↔ inhibin B — and the hypothalamic GnRH must be **pulsatile** to work. Continuous GnRH (childhood, or a GnRH-agonist drug) and exogenous testosterone (feedback) both switch the system off, which is why testosterone injections cause azoospermia.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Pulsatile GnRH drives LH and FSH; Leydig cells make testosterone (kept high inside the tubules by ABP), Sertoli cells nurse spermatogenesis over 74 days behind the blood–testis barrier and return inhibin B. Semen (60 % seminal vesicle, 30 % prostate, 10 % vas) carries 90–120 million sperm/mL at pH 7.2–7.4; erection is parasympathetic/NO/cGMP, emission and ejaculation sympathetic.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Mild testicular damage (heat, varicocele, toxins) first lowers sperm production and inhibin B; FSH rises selectively to push the Sertoli cells harder while testosterone and LH stay normal — fertility falls before anything else. Exogenous testosterone suppresses LH and FSH quietly; serum testosterone looks fine while intratesticular testosterone and sperm count fall.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Primary testicular failure (e.g. Klinefelter) gives low testosterone with high LH and FSH (hypergonadotropic hypogonadism); hypothalamic failure (kisspeptin/GPR54 or leptin defects) gives absent puberty with low LH/FSH (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism). Azoospermia, gynaecomastia, small soft testes, anaemia, low muscle and bone mass and low libido follow the loss of each testosterone row.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → insult (heat / drug / genetic defect / exogenous testosterone) → ↓ sperm & inhibin B → ↑ FSH (compensation) → ↓ testosterone → ↑ LH → hypogonadism + infertility
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> The LH/FSH/testosterone pattern localises the lesion (testis vs pituitary/hypothalamus), and the treatment follows the physiology: pulsatile GnRH or hCG/FSH restore fertility in hypothalamic disease, whereas plain testosterone replacement treats symptoms but stops sperm production.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Absent SRY:** "46,XY with female phenotype", "Y chromosome without the SRY band" → no testis-determining factor → female development (absence of SRY → female).
>
> **Delayed / absent puberty with low LH/FSH:** "No puberty, anosmia, or very low body fat", "GPR54 mutation" → failure of leptin–kisspeptin–pulsatile GnRH.
>
> **Azoospermia after anabolic steroids:** "Bodybuilder with small testes and zero sperm count" → exogenous testosterone → negative feedback on LH (and FSH).
>
> **5α-reductase / DHT:** "Finasteride for BPH or male-pattern baldness", "external genitalia under-virilised but internal ducts normal" → DHT builds the prostate, external genitalia, facial hair, acne and the temporal hairline.
>
> **Forensic seminal stain:** "Florence test" (choline / phosphorylcholine, seminal vesicle), "Barberio's test" (spermine, prostate) → presumptive tests for semen in rape cases.
>
> **Erectile dysfunction drugs:** "PDE inhibitor, sildenafil, contraindicated with nitrates" → ↑ cGMP in the NANC/NO pathway.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Green SRY band on the short arm of Y; Sertoli cell spanning the tubule from basement membrane to lumen with germ cells in its folds; Leydig cells in the interstitium; sperm with acrosome cap, mitochondrial middle piece and 9 + 2 microtubule tail.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Spermatogenesis 74 days; sperm count 90–120 million/mL (page); semen pH 7.2–7.4; sperm life 1–2 days after ejaculation; velocity 1–4 mm/min; length 55–60 µ; liquefaction in 15–30 min; scrotum 1–2 °C below body temperature.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Swapping the jobs of testosterone and DHT**
>
> **What students think:** DHT forms the internal male organs; testosterone forms the external genitalia.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Testosterone itself stimulates the Wolffian duct (internal organs — epididymis, vas, seminal vesicle). DHT, made by 5α-reductase in the target tissue, masculinises the external genitalia and develops the prostate. 5α-reductase deficiency proves it: normal internal ducts, under-virilised external genitalia.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Testosterone — Inside; DHT — outside (and the prostate)."
>
> **Trap 2: Thinking more GnRH is always more stimulation**
>
> **What students think:** Continuous GnRH should switch on the pituitary more strongly than pulses.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Continuous GnRH desensitises (down-regulates) the gonadotroph receptors — that is why pre-pubertal continuous release keeps LH and FSH low and why long-acting GnRH agonists are used to suppress testosterone in prostate cancer. Only pulsatile GnRH stimulates.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Pulse = go; continuous = stop."
>
> **Trap 3: Matching the wrong feedback**
>
> **What students think:** Inhibin suppresses LH, or testosterone controls FSH alone.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In the two-cell two-gonadotrophin model each cell reports to its own hormone: Leydig testosterone feeds back on GnRH and LH, Sertoli inhibin B feeds back selectively on FSH. An isolated rise in FSH therefore marks damaged seminiferous tubules (Sertoli/germ cells) with intact Leydig cells.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "LH ↔ Leydig ↔ testosterone; FSH ↔ Sertoli ↔ inhibin B."
>
> **Trap 4: Giving testosterone to treat male infertility**
>
> **What students think:** Testosterone is needed for sperm, so testosterone injections will raise the count.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Spermatogenesis needs very high *intratesticular* testosterone made locally under LH. Exogenous testosterone suppresses LH by negative feedback, intratesticular testosterone collapses and the result is azoospermia — exactly the page's note.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Exogenous testosterone = contraceptive, not fertility drug."
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing up the sources in semen**
>
> **What students think:** The prostate makes most of the seminal fluid and the fructose.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The seminal vesicle gives 60 % — fructose (energy via GLUT-5), vitamin C, prostaglandins, phosphorylcholine (Florence test); the prostate gives 30 % — fibrinolysin (liquefaction), acid phosphatase, spermine (Barberio's test); the vas deferens 10 % — bicarbonate and phosphate buffers.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Vesicle = sugar & choline (Florence); prostate = enzymes & spermine (Barberio)."

[^1]: SRY (on Yp11.2) encodes an HMG-box transcription factor that switches on *SOX9* in the bipotential gonad, turning its supporting cells into Sertoli cells; the Sertoli cells then organise the testis and induce Leydig cells. Sertoli AMH (from ~week 7) makes the Müllerian ducts regress; Leydig testosterone (from ~week 8–9, driven first by placental hCG) stabilises the Wolffian ducts; the external genitalia need DHT.
[^2]: 46,XY individuals with an SRY deletion or mutation develop as females with streak gonads (Swyer syndrome); 46,XX individuals who carry SRY translocated onto an X develop as males (XX male syndrome). The pseudoautosomal regions are where X and Y pair and cross over in male meiosis — an SRY that crosses over with them causes these errors.
[^3]: Textbooks describe the pre-pubertal GnRH pulse generator as restrained — low-amplitude, infrequent GnRH under tonic GABA inhibition, with low kisspeptin; melatonin is thought to contribute. A *continuous* GnRH signal desensitises the gonadotrophs — the principle behind long-acting GnRH agonists for precocious puberty and prostate cancer. The page's wording ("continuous release of GnRH") is kept in the body.
[^4]: Leptin from adipose tissue signals that energy stores are adequate; it is permissive (necessary but not sufficient). Children with leptin or leptin-receptor deficiency do not enter puberty (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism), and very low body fat (athletes, anorexia) delays or stops it.
[^5]: Kisspeptin (from the *KISS1* gene) acts on its receptor GPR54 (KISS1R), a Gq-coupled receptor on GnRH neurons; loss-of-function mutations of GPR54 cause absent puberty. Pulsatile GnRH (about one pulse every 60–120 min) works because the GnRH receptors recover during the trough between pulses — the page's "when GnRH ↓"; the first sign of puberty is LH pulses during sleep.
[^6]: Textbooks put scrotal temperature about 2–3 °C below core (~34 °C), maintained by the pampiniform venous plexus (countercurrent heat exchange), scrotal sweat glands and the dartos and cremaster muscles. Undescended testes (cryptorchidism), fever, tight clothing and varicocele impair spermatogenesis.
[^7]: Guyton gives ~74 days (some texts 64–74) from spermatogonium to sperm, plus ~12 days of transit and maturation in the epididymis. Textbooks name the dark type A (Ad) spermatogonia as the true stem cells; type B spermatogonia are those committed to becoming primary spermatocytes — the page labels type B "(stem cell)".
[^8]: Each primary spermatocyte gives 4 sperm (unlike oogenesis, which gives one ovum and polar bodies); the germ cells stay joined by cytoplasmic bridges throughout. In spermiogenesis the Golgi forms the acrosome, the centriole forms the flagellum, mitochondria gather in the middle piece and excess cytoplasm is shed as a residual body that the Sertoli cell phagocytoses.
[^9]: The tight junctions divide the tubule into a basal compartment (spermatogonia, open to blood) and an adluminal compartment (spermatocytes and spermatids, which carry new haploid antigens the immune system has never seen). The barrier protects them from autoimmune attack and keeps a special fluid rich in ABP-bound testosterone; its breach (vasectomy, trauma) gives anti-sperm antibodies.
[^10]: Leydig cells (interstitial cells) carry LH receptors and make ~95 % of a man's testosterone (≈ 4–9 mg/day); the rest is adrenal. The myoid cells around the tubule contract to push sperm towards the rete testis.
[^11]: Normal ejaculate volume is about 2–5 mL. The WHO 2021 lower reference limits are 16 million sperm/mL and 39 million per ejaculate (WHO 2010: 15 million/mL); the page's 90–120 million/mL is the classical textbook average (Guyton ~120 million/mL). Semen pH is usually quoted 7.2–8.0.
[^12]: Ejaculated sperm survive about 1–2 days in the female tract (up to 3–5 days in the cervical crypts by some accounts), whereas stored in the epididymis and vas they stay fertile for weeks. They do not survive long in the acidic vagina (pH ≈ 4) — semen's buffers and rapid entry into cervical mucus protect them.
[^13]: Sperm leaving the testis are immotile; during the ~12-day passage through the epididymis they gain the capacity for progressive (forward) motility. CatSper ("cation channel of sperm") is a Ca²⁺ channel in the principal piece of the tail that drives the whip-like *hyperactivated* motility during capacitation in the female tract; CatSper mutations cause male infertility.
[^14]: The page's note means that the fluids of the female genital tract enhance sperm motility. Strictly, vaginal fluid is acidic (pH ≈ 4) and hostile; it is the neutral-to-alkaline uterine and tubal fluids that activate motility and capacitation (Guyton: sperm motility is greatest in neutral or slightly alkaline media).
[^15]: The acrosome is a cap-shaped lysosome (made by the Golgi) holding hyaluronidase (to get through the cumulus/corona radiata) and acrosin (a protease that digests the zona pellucida). The tail's axoneme is the 9 + 2 microtubule arrangement with dynein arms (defective in Kartagener / primary ciliary dyskinesia → immotile sperm). Guyton gives the velocity as 1–4 mm/min.
[^16]: Capacitation takes ~1–10 hours in the female tract: inhibitory (decapacitation) factors from seminal fluid are washed off, cholesterol leaves the head membrane, Ca²⁺ enters (CatSper) and motility becomes hyperactivated. Binding of the capacitated sperm to ZP3 of the zona pellucida triggers the acrosome reaction; fusion of sperm and oocyte membranes is fertilization (usually in the ampulla of the uterine tube).
[^17]: Fructose is the sperm's main energy substrate (GLUT-5 is the fructose transporter); absent seminal fructose with azoospermia suggests congenital absence of the vas deferens and seminal vesicles (as in cystic fibrosis) or ejaculatory duct obstruction. Seminal vesicle proteins (semenogelin) make semen clot; prostatic fibrinolysin/PSA liquefies it in 15–30 min. Prostaglandins (named for the prostate, where they were first found, though made mainly in the seminal vesicle) also make the uterus and tubes contract.
[^18]: Parasympathetic pelvic splanchnic nerves (nervi erigentes, S2–S4) release acetylcholine, which acts on the endothelium to release NO, and NANC nerve fibres release NO directly (neuronal NOS). NO activates soluble guanylyl cyclase → cGMP → ↓ intracellular Ca²⁺ → smooth-muscle relaxation in the helicine arteries and cavernous trabeculae; the filling sinusoids compress the subtunical venules against the tunica albuginea (veno-occlusion).
[^19]: Sildenafil inhibits **PDE-5**, so cGMP stays high longer — it potentiates NO's effect rather than raising NO itself (NO is upstream of cGMP; the page writes "↑ cGMP → ↑ NO"). It needs sexual stimulation to work, and with nitrates (which also raise cGMP) it can cause severe hypotension — an absolute contraindication.
[^20]: Emission is sympathetic (T11/T12–L2 via the hypogastric nerves): the vas, seminal vesicles and prostate contract and the internal urethral sphincter closes (preventing retrograde ejaculation into the bladder). Ejaculation proper is a spinal reflex expelling semen by rhythmic contraction of the bulbospongiosus and ischiocavernosus muscles through the somatic pudendal nerve (S2–S4).
[^21]: Detumescence follows sympathetic noradrenaline acting on α₁ receptors and endothelin contracting the arterioles and trabecular smooth muscle; PDE-5 clears the remaining cGMP. Failure of detumescence for > 4 hours is priapism.
[^22]: In the two-cell model LH stimulates Leydig testosterone, and FSH acts on Sertoli cells to make ABP, inhibin B and aromatase (which turns some Leydig testosterone into estradiol). Testosterone (partly after aromatisation to estradiol) inhibits GnRH and LH; inhibin B inhibits FSH selectively, so a rise in FSH alone signals tubular (Sertoli/germ-cell) damage.
[^23]: Spermatogenesis needs intratesticular testosterone about 50–100 × the serum level, made locally under LH. Exogenous testosterone or anabolic steroids suppress LH (and FSH), so intratesticular testosterone collapses → azoospermia and testicular atrophy, even while serum testosterone is high — the basis of male hormonal contraception trials.
[^24]: Growth hormone (via IGF-1) promotes the early mitotic divisions of the spermatogonia; estrogen made by Sertoli aromatase and thyroid hormone also contribute. FSH and testosterone are the two essential hormones; LH acts through testosterone.
[^25]: 5α-reductase type 2 (prostate, genital skin, hair follicles) reduces the C4–C5 double bond of ring A to make DHT, which binds the same androgen receptor about 2–10 × more tightly and dissociates more slowly. 46,XY 5α-reductase deficiency gives ambiguous external genitalia at birth with normal Wolffian structures, then virilisation at puberty. Finasteride blocks type 2 (dutasteride types 1 and 2) — used in BPH and male-pattern baldness.
[^26]: Testosterone increases erythropoietin production by the kidney and acts on the marrow directly — men's haemoglobin is about 1.5–2 g/dL higher than women's, and testosterone therapy can cause polycythaemia.
[^27]: Testosterone increases bone matrix and calcium retention and drives the pubertal growth spurt; closure of the epiphyses, however, is caused by estrogen made from testosterone by aromatase — men with aromatase or estrogen-receptor deficiency keep growing into adulthood.
[^28]: The testosterone/DHT split comes from where 5α-reductase is expressed: the Wolffian duct responds to testosterone itself, while the urogenital sinus and genital tubercle (prostate, penis, scrotum) and the sebaceous and hair follicles need DHT. Male-pattern baldness needs both androgens (DHT) and a genetic predisposition.

[^ety-puberty]: **puberty** — Latin *pubertas*, "adulthood, the age of manhood", from *pubes*, "grown-up; the hair that appears at that age" → the period of becoming sexually mature. **Differentiate:** *puberty* (gonadal maturation, GnRH-driven) vs *adrenarche* (adrenal androgens rise) vs *menarche* (first menses).
[^ety-gnrh]: **GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)** — *gonad* (Greek *gonē*, "seed, generation" + *-ad*) + *-tropin* (Greek *tropos*, "turning towards") + *releasing* + *hormone* (Greek *hormōn*, "setting in motion") → the hypothalamic hormone that releases the gonad-stimulating hormones LH and FSH; a decapeptide.
[^ety-gaba]: **GABA** — **g**amma-**a**mino**b**utyric **a**cid: *butyric* from Latin *butyrum*, "butter" (butyric acid smells of rancid butter) + *gamma*, the amino group on the third (γ) carbon → the brain's main inhibitory transmitter, which holds the GnRH pulse generator back before puberty.
[^ety-melatonin]: **melatonin** — Greek *melas*, "black" + *tonos*, "tension, tone" + *-in* → named (Lerner, 1958) because it lightens (blanches) the skin of frogs by acting on melanophores — the "melanin-tone" hormone of the pineal gland; it rises at night and is thought to restrain puberty.
[^ety-leptin]: **leptin** — Greek *leptos*, "thin" + *-in*, protein → the "thinness" hormone from fat cells (discovered by Friedman, 1994), signalling satiety and adequate energy stores; its permissive signal is needed for puberty.
[^ety-permissive]: **permissive** — Latin *per-*, "through" + *mittere*, "to send, let go" + *-ive* → letting through, allowing: a factor that does not trigger a process itself but must be present for it to happen (leptin for puberty).
[^ety-kisspeptin]: **kisspeptin** — from the *KISS1* gene, named (1996) after Hershey's Kisses chocolates, made in Hershey, Pennsylvania, where the gene was discovered (originally as a metastasis suppressor) + *peptide* + *-in* → the peptide that switches on GnRH neurons via GPR54.
[^ety-neuron]: **neuron** — Greek *neuron*, "sinew, nerve, cord" → the nerve cell; the GnRH neurons migrate from the olfactory placode into the hypothalamus (failure → Kallmann syndrome, with anosmia).
[^ety-pulsatile]: **pulsatile** — Latin *pulsare*, "to beat, push repeatedly" (*pellere*, to drive) + *-atile*, "able to" → coming in beats: GnRH released in bursts with gaps between. **Differentiate:** *pulsatile* (bursts — stimulates the pituitary) vs *continuous / tonic* (steady — desensitises it).
[^ety-lh]: **LH (luteinizing hormone)** — Latin *luteus*, "yellow" + *-ize*, "to make" → the hormone that makes the corpus luteum ("yellow body") in women; in men the same molecule drives the Leydig cells (older name ICSH, interstitial-cell-stimulating hormone).
[^ety-fsh]: **FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)** — Latin *folliculus*, "little bag" (*follis*, bag + *-culus*, small) → named for growing the ovarian follicles; in men it acts on the Sertoli cells to support spermatogenesis.
[^ety-vas-deferens]: **vas deferens** — Latin *vas*, "vessel, duct" + *deferens*, "carrying away" (*de-* away + *ferre* to carry) → the duct carrying sperm away from the epididymis to the ejaculatory duct; also called *ductus deferens*. *Vasectomy* = cutting it.
[^ety-glans]: **glans** — Latin, "acorn" → the acorn-shaped expanded tip of the penis (glans penis) or clitoris (glans clitoridis).
[^ety-penis]: **penis** — Latin, "tail" → the male copulatory organ, made of two corpora cavernosa and one corpus spongiosum (carrying the urethra).
[^ety-epididymis]: **epididymis** — Greek *epi-*, "upon" + *didymos*, "twin" (the testes, a pair) → the structure upon the twin (testis): a single coiled duct ~6 m long where sperm mature and are stored.
[^ety-prostate]: **prostate** — Greek *prostatēs*, "one who stands before, a guardian" (*pro-* before + *histanai* to stand) → the gland that stands before (below) the bladder; named by Herophilus (c. 300 BC). Don't confuse with *prostrate* (lying flat).
[^ety-seminal]: **seminal** — Latin *semen, seminis*, "seed" + *-al* → of the seed: semen, seminal vesicle, seminiferous. Same root as *disseminate* (to scatter seeds).
[^ety-vesicle]: **vesicle** — Latin *vesicula*, "little bladder" (*vesica*, bladder + *-ula*, small) → a small sac; the seminal vesicle is a coiled sac-like gland. *Vesical* (of the urinary bladder) shares the root.
[^ety-scrotum]: **scrotum** — Latin, "skin sac, pouch", perhaps related to *scortum*, "hide, leather" (origin uncertain) → the skin pouch holding the testes outside the body, where it is cooler.
[^ety-seminiferous]: **seminiferous** — Latin *semen*, "seed" + *ferre*, "to carry, bear" + *-ous*, "full of" → seed-bearing: the tubules that produce sperm (≈ 80 % of the testis volume).
[^ety-spermatogenesis]: **spermatogenesis** — Greek *sperma, spermatos*, "seed" + *genesis*, "origin, creation" → creation of sperm (the whole process from spermatogonium to sperm). **Differentiate:** *spermatogenesis* (the whole) · *spermatocytogenesis* (mitotic stage) · *spermiogenesis* (spermatid → sperm, no division) · *spermiation* (release).
[^ety-mitosis]: **mitosis** — Greek *mitos*, "thread" + *-ōsis*, a process → the thread process: chromosomes appear as threads; coined by Walther Flemming (1882). **Differentiate:** *mitosis* (2n → two identical 2n cells) vs *meiosis* (2n → four 1n cells, with crossing-over).
[^ety-spermatogonium]: **spermatogonium** — Greek *sperma*, "seed" + *gonē*, "generation, offspring" + *-ium* → the cell that generates seed: the diploid germ stem/precursor cell on the basement membrane. **Differentiate:** *-gonium* (germ stem cell: spermatogonium, oogonium) · *-cyte* (meiotic cell: spermatocyte, oocyte) · *-tid* (post-meiotic: spermatid).
[^ety-spermatocyte]: **spermatocyte** — Greek *sperma*, "seed" + *kytos*, "cell" → the seed cell that undergoes meiosis; *primary* (2n, before meiosis I) and *secondary* (1n, between meiosis I and II).
[^ety-meiosis]: **meiosis** — Greek *meiōsis*, "a lessening" (*meion*, less) → the lessening division that halves the chromosome number. Meiosis I is the *reductional* division (homologues separate, 2n → 1n); meiosis II is *equational* (sister chromatids separate).
[^ety-reduction]: **reductional division** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *ducere*, "to lead" + *-tion* → leading back, reducing: the division that reduces the chromosome number from diploid to haploid (meiosis I). **Differentiate:** *reductional* (meiosis I) vs *equational* (meiosis II, like mitosis).
[^ety-spermatid]: **spermatid** — Greek *sperma*, "seed" + *-id*, "offspring, descendant" → the haploid offspring of meiosis that will be remodelled (without dividing) into a sperm.
[^ety-spermiogenesis]: **spermiogenesis** — Latin/Greek *spermium*, "a sperm" + *genesis*, "creation" → the creation of the sperm form from the spermatid: acrosome, condensed nucleus, flagellum, mitochondrial sheath, shedding of cytoplasm.
[^ety-differentiation]: **differentiation** — Latin *dis-*, "apart" + *ferre*, "to carry" → *differre*, "to differ" + *-ation* → becoming different, specialised. **Differentiate:** *differentiation* (specialising) vs *proliferation* (dividing) vs *maturation* (finishing).
[^ety-spermiation]: **spermiation** — *spermium*, "sperm" + *-ation*, the process → the process of releasing mature sperm from the Sertoli cell into the tubular lumen. **Differentiate:** *spermiation* (release into the tubule) vs *emission* (into the urethra) vs *ejaculation* (out of the urethra).
[^ety-regression]: **regression** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *gradi*, "to step" + *-ion* → stepping back: shrinking and disappearance of a structure (the Müllerian duct under AMH). **Differentiate:** *regression* (going back) vs *involution* (rolling inward, e.g. thymus) vs *atrophy* (wasting).
[^ety-androgen]: **androgen** — Greek *anēr, andros*, "man, male" + *-gen*, "producing" → producer of maleness (testosterone, DHT). **Androgen-binding protein (ABP)** holds testosterone inside the tubule.
[^ety-aromatase]: **aromatase** — *aromatic* (a benzene-type ring; Greek *arōma*, spice) + *-ase* → the enzyme that makes ring A of an androgen aromatic: testosterone → estradiol.
[^ety-estrogen]: **estrogen** — Greek *oistros*, "gadfly, frenzy, heat" + *-gen*, "producing" → producer of oestrus (heat); in men it closes the epiphyses and helps feedback.
[^ety-inhibin]: **inhibin** — Latin *inhibere*, "to hold back, restrain" (*in-* + *habere*, to hold) + *-in*, protein → the protein that holds back FSH; inhibin B is the male form. **Differentiate:** *inhibin* (⊣ FSH) vs *activin* (stimulates FSH) vs *follistatin* (binds activin).
[^ety-phagocytic]: **phagocytic** — Greek *phagein*, "to eat" + *kytos*, "cell" + *-ic* → cell-eating: engulfing particles (dead sperm, residual bodies). **Differentiate:** *phagocytosis* (cell eating, solids) vs *pinocytosis* (cell drinking, fluid) vs *exocytosis* (releasing).
[^ety-interstitial]: **interstitial** — Latin *inter-*, "between" + *sistere*, "to stand, place" + *-al* → standing between: the Leydig cells lie in the spaces between the seminiferous tubules.
[^ety-ejaculation]: **ejaculation** — Latin *e-* (*ex-*), "out" + *jaculari*, "to throw, hurl" (*jaculum*, a javelin) + *-ation* → throwing out: expulsion of semen from the urethra. **Differentiate:** *emission* (sending semen into the urethra) vs *ejaculation* (throwing it out of the urethra).
[^ety-motility]: **motility** — Latin *motus*, "moved" (*movere*, to move) + *-ility*, "capacity" → the capacity to move by itself. **Differentiate:** *motility* (self-propelled movement — sperm, gut) vs *mobility* (being movable) vs *motion* (any movement).
[^ety-catsper]: **CatSper** — **Cat**ion channel of **Sper**m: a sperm-specific Ca²⁺-permeable channel in the tail (named on its discovery in 2001); it drives hyperactivated motility.
[^ety-acrosome]: **acrosome** — Greek *akron*, "tip, summit" + *sōma*, "body" → the tip body: the enzyme-filled cap over the front of the sperm head. **Acrosome reaction** = its exocytosis on contact with the zona pellucida.
[^ety-vagina]: **vagina** — Latin, "sheath, scabbard" → the sheath that receives the penis. Same root as *invaginate* (to fold in as into a sheath) and *vanilla* ("little sheath", its pod).
[^ety-hyaluronidase]: **hyaluronidase** — Greek *hyalos*, "glass" (clear, glassy) + *uronic acid* + *-idase* (an enzyme) → the enzyme that breaks down hyaluronic acid — the glue between the cumulus cells around the ovum.
[^ety-proteolytic]: **proteolytic** — *protein* (Greek *prōtos*, first) + Greek *lytikos*, "able to loosen" (*lyein*, to loosen) → protein-splitting; acrosin is the acrosome's proteolytic enzyme.
[^ety-zona-pellucida]: **zona pellucida** — Latin *zona*, "belt" + *pellucidus*, "transparent" (*per-* through + *lucere* to shine) → the transparent belt: the glycoprotein coat (ZP1–ZP4) around the oocyte that the sperm must cross.
[^ety-vacuole]: **vacuole** — Latin *vacuus*, "empty" + *-ole*, small → a small empty-looking space in the cell. The sperm-head vacuoles are small clear areas in the nucleus.
[^ety-mitochondria]: **mitochondria** — Greek *mitos*, "thread" + *chondrion*, "little grain" → thread-grains (Benda, 1898), for their shape under the light microscope; the cell's ATP factories, coiled round the middle piece of the sperm.
[^ety-flagellum]: **flagellum** — Latin, "little whip" (diminutive of *flagrum*, whip) → the whip-like tail. **Differentiate:** *flagellum* (one long tail, wave-like beat — sperm) vs *cilia* (many short hairs, oar-like beat — airway).
[^ety-microtubule]: **microtubule** — Greek *mikros*, "small" + Latin *tubulus*, "little tube" → small hollow protein tubes (tubulin); in the sperm tail arranged 9 + 2 with dynein motors.
[^ety-capacitation]: **capacitation** — Latin *capax, capacis*, "able to hold, capable" (*capere*, to take) + *-ation* → the process of making capable: the final maturation in the female tract that lets the sperm fertilise. **Differentiate:** *maturation* (in the epididymis) vs *capacitation* (in the female tract) vs *acrosome reaction* (at the zona).
[^ety-fertilization]: **fertilization** — Latin *fertilis*, "fruitful, bearing" (*ferre*, to bear) + *-ize* + *-ation* → making fruitful: union of sperm and ovum to form a zygote, usually in the ampulla of the uterine tube.
[^ety-fructose]: **fructose** — Latin *fructus*, "fruit" + *-ose*, a sugar → fruit sugar; the sperm's fuel in semen, taken in through GLUT-5.
[^ety-glut]: **GLUT-5** — **glu**cose **t**ransporter 5, a facilitated-diffusion carrier that in fact prefers fructose (gut, sperm). **Differentiate:** GLUT-4 (insulin-dependent, muscle and fat) vs GLUT-2 (liver, β-cell, basolateral gut) vs GLUT-5 (fructose).
[^ety-fibrinolysin]: **fibrinolysin** — Latin *fibra*, "fibre" → *fibrin* + Greek *lysis*, "loosening" + *-in* → the protein that dissolves fibrin-like clot; in semen it liquefies the coagulum (PSA, a kallikrein protease, does the same job).
[^ety-liquefaction]: **liquefaction** — Latin *liquere*, "to be fluid" + *facere*, "to make" + *-tion* → making fluid: semen coagulates on ejaculation and liquefies in 15–30 min. **Differentiate:** *liquefaction* (gel → liquid) vs *coagulation* (liquid → gel).
[^ety-bicarbonate]: **bicarbonate** — Latin *bi-*, "two" + *carbo*, "coal" + *-ate* → a salt with twice the carbonic acid relative to the base (old naming: "two carbonates"): HCO₃⁻, the main buffer against vaginal acid.
[^ety-ascorbic]: **ascorbic acid** — Greek *a-*, "without" + Latin *scorbutus*, "scurvy" + *-ic* → the anti-scurvy acid (vitamin C), named by Szent-Györgyi and Haworth (1933).
[^ety-antioxidant]: **antioxidant** — Greek *anti-*, "against" + *oxys*, "sharp, acid" (→ oxygen) + *-ide* + *-ant* → a substance that works against oxidation, protecting sperm membranes from reactive oxygen species.
[^ety-phosphatase]: **acid phosphatase** — Latin *acidus*, "sour" + *phosphate* + *-ase* → an enzyme that removes phosphate groups at acid pH; prostatic acid phosphatase is a classic marker of seminal fluid (and of prostate cancer). **Differentiate:** *acid* (prostate, osteoclast TRAP) vs *alkaline* phosphatase (bone, liver).
[^ety-prostaglandin]: **prostaglandin** — *prostate* + Latin *glans, glandis*, "acorn" → *gland* + *-in* → named by Ulf von Euler (1935) because it was first found in seminal fluid and thought to come from the prostate (it is mostly from the seminal vesicle).
[^ety-cervix]: **cervical** — Latin *cervix, cervicis*, "neck" + *-al* → of the neck (of the uterus here). Cervical mucus becomes thin and penetrable at ovulation; prostaglandins in semen help sperm pass it.
[^ety-mucus]: **mucus** — Latin, "slime, snot" → the slimy glycoprotein (mucin) secretion. **Differentiate:** *mucus* (noun) vs *mucous* (adjective — mucous membrane).
[^ety-spermine]: **spermine** — Greek *sperma*, "seed" + *-ine*, an amine → the polyamine first crystallised from semen (Leeuwenhoek saw its crystals in 1678); it gives semen part of its odour. Detected by Barberio's test.
[^ety-barberio]: **Barberio's test** — Michele Barberio, Italian physician, who described (1905) yellow needle-shaped **spermine picrate** crystals forming when seminal fluid is mixed with picric acid — a presumptive forensic test for semen.
[^ety-phosphorylcholine]: **phosphorylcholine** — *phosphoryl* (a phosphate group) + *choline* (Greek *cholē*, "bile", where it was first found) → choline carrying a phosphate; seminal choline is detected by the Florence test.
[^ety-florence]: **Florence test** — Albert Florence (1851–1927), French forensic physician in Lyon, who described (1895–96) dark-brown rhombic **choline periodide** crystals forming when semen is treated with iodine in potassium iodide — a presumptive test for a seminal stain.
[^ety-buffer]: **buffer** — Middle English *buff*, "a blow, a soft thud" → something that softens a blow: a solution that resists changes in pH (bicarbonate, phosphate) — here protecting sperm from the acidic vagina.
[^ety-erection]: **erection** — Latin *e-* (*ex-*), "out, up" + *regere*, "to direct, make straight" → *erigere*, to raise up + *-ion* → raising up. **Differentiate:** *erection* (parasympathetic) vs *emission / ejaculation* (sympathetic / somatic) vs *detumescence* (subsiding).
[^ety-parasympathetic]: **parasympathetic** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + *syn-*, "together" + *pathos*, "feeling" → the system beside the sympathetic (craniosacral outflow). Here its sacral (S2–S4) pelvic splanchnic nerves — the *nervi erigentes*, "erecting nerves".
[^ety-acetylcholine]: **acetylcholine** — *acetyl* (Latin *acetum*, vinegar → acetic acid) + *choline* (Greek *cholē*, bile) → the acetic ester of choline; Otto Loewi's *Vagusstoff* (1921). It causes erection mainly by releasing NO from the endothelium.
[^ety-adrenergic]: **adrenergic / cholinergic** — *adrenaline* or *acetylcholine* + Greek *ergon*, "work" + *-ic* → working by adrenaline (noradrenaline) or by acetylcholine. **NANC** = non-adrenergic non-cholinergic: nerves using other transmitters — here nitric oxide.
[^ety-nitric-oxide]: **nitric oxide (NO)** — Greek *nitron*, "soda" → *nitrogen* + *oxide* (*oxys*, sharp → oxygen + *-ide*) → nitrogen monoxide, a gas acting as a messenger; once called "endothelium-derived relaxing factor" (Furchgott, Ignarro, Murad — Nobel 1998).
[^ety-arteriole]: **arteriole** — Greek *artēria*, "windpipe, artery" (thought to carry air) + *-ole*, small → a small artery; the resistance vessels whose dilatation lets blood into the corpora.
[^ety-dilatation]: **dilatation** — Latin *dilatare*, "to spread out" (*dis-* apart + *latus* wide) + *-ation* → widening. **Differentiate:** *dilatation / dilation* (widening — NO) vs *constriction* (narrowing — endothelin, noradrenaline).
[^ety-engorgement]: **engorgement** — French *en-*, "in" + *gorge*, "throat" + *-ment* → filling up as if to the throat: distension of the erectile tissue with blood.
[^ety-cgmp]: **cGMP** — **c**yclic **g**uanosine **m**ono**p**hosphate: *guanine* from *guano* (Spanish from Quechua *wanu*, sea-bird dung, where guanine was first isolated) → the second messenger made by guanylyl cyclase when NO arrives; it relaxes smooth muscle.
[^ety-phosphodiesterase]: **phosphodiesterase** — *phospho-* (phosphate) + Greek *di-*, "two" + *ester* (German *Essig-Äther*, "vinegar ether") + *-ase* → the enzyme that splits a phosphodiester bond — opening cyclic GMP/AMP to inactive GMP/AMP. PDE-5 is the penile isoform.
[^ety-sildenafil]: **sildenafil** — a coined drug name: the stem *-afil* marks PDE-5 inhibitors (tadalafil, vardenafil); *silden-* is a distinctive syllable — origin uncertain. Discovered by Pfizer while testing it for angina.
[^ety-sympathetic]: **sympathetic** — Greek *syn-* (*sym-* before *p*), "together" + *pathos*, "feeling" → feeling together (Galen thought organs "sympathised" through these nerves); thoracolumbar outflow — here T12–L2 for emission.
[^ety-emission]: **emission** — Latin *e-* (*ex-*), "out" + *mittere*, "to send" + *-ion* → sending out: movement of semen from the vas, seminal vesicle and prostate into the posterior urethra. **Differentiate:** *emission* (sympathetic, into the urethra) vs *nocturnal emission* (involuntary ejaculation in sleep).
[^ety-urethra]: **urethra** — Greek *ourēthra*, from *ourein*, "to urinate" + *-thra*, "instrument, passage" → the urinary passage; in the male it also carries semen. **Differentiate:** *urethra* (bladder → outside) vs *ureter* (kidney → bladder).
[^ety-endothelin]: **endothelin** — Greek *endon*, "within" + *thēlē*, "nipple" → *endothelium* (the lining within vessels) + *-in* → the peptide made by endothelial cells; the most potent vasoconstrictor known (Yanagisawa, 1988).
[^ety-noradrenaline]: **noradrenaline** — *nor-* (the molecule without an N-methyl group) + Latin *ad-*, "near" + *ren*, "kidney" + *-ine* → adrenaline minus a methyl: the sympathetic transmitter (= norepinephrine) that constricts penile arterioles via α₁ receptors.
[^ety-constriction]: **constriction** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *stringere*, "to draw tight, bind" + *-ion* → drawing tight: narrowing of a vessel. **Differentiate:** *constriction* (active narrowing) vs *stenosis* (fixed narrowing) vs *occlusion* (complete block).
[^ety-resolution]: **resolution** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *solvere*, "to loosen" + *-tion* → loosening back: the return of the penis to its resting state (the last phase of the sexual response cycle).
[^ety-detumescence]: **detumescence** — Latin *de-*, "down, reverse" + *tumescere*, "to begin to swell" (*tumere*, to swell) + *-ence* → the reverse of swelling: subsidence of erection. **Differentiate:** *tumescence* (swelling — erection) vs *detumescence* (subsiding) vs *priapism* (failure to subside > 4 h).
[^ety-hypothalamus]: **hypothalamus** — Greek *hypo-*, "below" + *thalamos*, "inner chamber" → the region below the thalamus, home of the GnRH neurons.
[^ety-pituitary]: **pituitary** — Latin *pituita*, "phlegm" → Galen thought it drained mucus from the brain; its other name *hypophysis* (*hypo-* below + *physis* growth) is more accurate. Its gonadotrophs make LH and FSH.
[^ety-gonadotrophin]: **gonadotrophin / gonadotropin** — Greek *gonē*, "seed" → *gonad* + *trophē*, "nourishment" (-trophin) or *tropos*, "turning towards" (-tropin) → the hormones that nourish / act on the gonads: LH and FSH (and placental hCG). Both spellings are used.
[^ety-azoospermia]: **azoospermia** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *zōon*, "living being, animal" + *sperma*, "seed" + *-ia* → no living sperm (animalcules) in the semen. **Differentiate:** *azoospermia* (no sperm) · *oligospermia* (too few) · *asthenospermia* (poor motility) · *teratospermia* (abnormal forms) · *aspermia* (no ejaculate).
[^ety-reductase]: **5α-reductase** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *ducere*, "to lead" → *reduce* (chemically, to add hydrogen / electrons) + *-ase*, enzyme; *5α* = it adds hydrogen at carbon 5 on the α (lower) face → turns testosterone into dihydrotestosterone.
[^ety-dht]: **dihydrotestosterone (DHT)** — Greek *di-*, "two" + *hydro-*, hydrogen + *testosterone* → testosterone with two extra hydrogen atoms (the C4–C5 double bond reduced) — the more potent androgen.
[^ety-erythropoietin]: **erythropoietin** — Greek *erythros*, "red" + *poiēsis*, "making" + *-in*, protein → the red-(cell)-making hormone from the peritubular cells of the kidney; testosterone increases it.
[^ety-masculinization]: **masculinization** — Latin *masculus*, "male" (diminutive of *mas*, male) + *-ize* + *-ation* → the process of making male. **Differentiate:** *masculinization* (male development, normal in boys) vs *virilization* (Latin *vir*, man — androgen effects, used for abnormal change in females) vs *feminization*.
[^ety-genitalia]: **genitalia** — Latin *genitalis*, "of generation, of begetting" (*gignere*, to beget) → the organs of generation. **External** (penis, scrotum — DHT) vs **internal** (epididymis, vas, seminal vesicle — testosterone).
[^ety-anabolism]: **anabolism** — Greek *ana-*, "up" + *ballein*, "to throw" + *-ism* → throwing up, building up: synthesis of complex molecules (protein → muscle). **Differentiate:** *anabolism* (building) vs *catabolism* (breaking down) — together *metabolism*.
[^ety-finasteride]: **finasteride** — a coined drug name: the stem *-steride* marks 5α-reductase inhibitors (dutasteride, epristeride) and comes from *steroid*; *fina-* is a distinctive syllable — origin uncertain.
[^ety-benign]: **benign** — Latin *benignus*, "kind, good-natured" (*bene*, well + *gignere*, to beget) → good-natured: not invading or spreading. **Differentiate:** *benign* vs *malignant* (*malignus*, evil-natured).
[^ety-hyperplasia]: **hyperplasia** — Greek *hyper-*, "over" + *plasis*, "formation" + *-ia* → excessive formation: more cells. BPH = benign prostatic hyperplasia of the periurethral (transition-zone) prostate, DHT-dependent. **Differentiate:** *hyperplasia* (more cells) vs *hypertrophy* (bigger cells) vs *neoplasia* (autonomous new growth).
[^ety-acne]: **acne** — probably a copyist's error for Greek *akmē*, "point, peak" (the pimple's point, or the peak of youth) — origin disputed. DHT enlarges sebaceous glands and increases sebum.
[^ety-alopecia]: **male pattern baldness (androgenetic alopecia)** — Greek *alōpēx*, "fox" + *-ia* → fox-mange hair loss; *androgenetic* = due to androgens (DHT) acting on genetically susceptible follicles at the temples and crown — the "temporal recession of hairline".
[^ety-libido]: **libido** — Latin, "desire, longing" (*libere*, to please) → sexual desire; testosterone keeps it up in men.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: reproduction, regression, resolution, reductional, reductase. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards · **pre-** before.
[^suf-some]: **-some / soma** — body: chromosome, autosome, acrosome. Don't confuse with **soma-** the body as a whole (somatic) · **-stomy** an opening · **-some** in English adjectives (handsome).
[^pre-pseudo]: **pseudo-** — false: pseudoautosomal. Don't confuse with **proto-** first · **para-** beside.
[^pre-auto]: **auto-** — self: autosome, autosomal, autocrine, autoimmune. Don't confuse with **allo-** other · **hetero-** different.
[^pre-centro]: **centro- / centri-** — centre: centromere, centriole, centrosome. Don't confuse with **centi-** hundredth (centimetre) · **-centesis** puncture (amniocentesis).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: transcription, transporter. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **intra-** within.
[^suf-one]: **-one** — a ketone (C=O) in a chemical name: testosterone, dihydrotestosterone, progesterone. Don't confuse with **-ol** an alcohol (estradiol) · **-ine** an amine (spermine).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: anti-mullerian, antioxidant. Don't confuse with **ante-** before · **ana-** up.
[^suf-in]: **-in** — a protein or peptide: melatonin, leptin, inhibin, kisspeptin, endothelin, erythropoietin, fibrinolysin. Don't confuse with **-ine** an amine or alkaloid (spermine, noradrenaline) · **-ase** an enzyme.
[^pre-per]: **per-** — through, thoroughly: permissive (letting through), pellucida (*per-* + *lucere*). Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before.
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, over: epididymis (upon the twin). Don't confuse with **endo-** within · **ecto-** outside · **hypo-** under.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, in front, forward: prostate (standing before), reproduction (bringing forth), prostaglandin. Don't confuse with **pre-** before (time) · **post-** after · **proto-** first.
[^suf-icle]: **-icle / -icule / -cule** — small: vesicle (little bladder), follicle, particle. Don't confuse with **-cele** a hernia or swelling (varicocele, hydrocele) · **-coele** a cavity (blastocoele).
[^suf-ferous]: **-ferous** — bearing, carrying: seminiferous (seed-bearing), deferens (carrying away), lactiferous. Don't confuse with **ferro- / ferri-** iron · **-phore** carrier (Greek: chromophore).
[^suf-genesis]: **-genesis** — creation, origin: spermatogenesis, spermiogenesis, oogenesis. Don't confuse with **-gen** the producer (androgen) · **-genic** producing (pathogenic).
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a process or condition: mitosis, meiosis. Don't confuse with **-iasis** a disease state · **-esis** a process (diuresis) · **-itis** inflammation.
[^suf-cyte]: **-cyte** — a cell: spermatocyte, oocyte, lymphocyte. Don't confuse with **-blast** an immature cell · **-gonium** a germ stem cell (spermatogonium).
[^pre-dis]: **dis- (di-, dif-)** — apart, different: differentiation, dilatation (*dis-* + *latus*), disseminate. Don't confuse with **di-** two (dihydrotestosterone) · **dys-** bad.
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -tion / -ion** — the process or result of an action: spermiation, capacitation, fertilization, ejaculation, liquefaction, regression. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition · **-ism** a state.
[^suf-gen]: **-gen** — producer: androgen, estrogen, collagen. Don't confuse with **-genesis** the process of making · **gene** a unit of heredity.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: aromatase, reductase, phosphodiesterase, hyaluronidase, phosphatase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (fructose) · **-ate** a salt (bicarbonate).
[^pre-phago]: **phago- / -phage** — eating: phagocytic, macrophage. Don't confuse with **phaco-** lens (phacoemulsification) · **pharyng-** throat.
[^pre-inter]: **inter-** — between: interstitial. Don't confuse with **intra-** within · **infra-** below.
[^pre-e]: **e- / ex-** — out, up, from: ejaculation (throwing out), erection (raising up), emission. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek) · **en- / in-** in.
[^pre-acro]: **acro-** — tip, summit, extremity: acrosome, acromegaly, acrocyanosis. Don't confuse with **aero-** air · **agro-** field.
[^suf-lytic]: **-lytic / -lysis** — loosening, breaking down: proteolytic (adjective), fibrinolysin, lysis. Don't confuse with **-lyte** a substance that dissolves (electrolyte) · **-lyse** in enzyme names (lyase).
[^pre-mito]: **mito-** — thread: mitochondria, mitosis. Don't confuse with **meio-** less (meiosis) · **myo-** muscle.
[^suf-ellum]: **-ellum / -ella / -ule / -ole** — small: flagellum (little whip), arteriole, vacuole. Don't confuse with **-ium** a structure · **-oma** a tumour.
[^pre-micro]: **micro-** — small: microtubule. Don't confuse with **macro-** large · **mega-** very large.
[^suf-ose]: **-ose** — a sugar: fructose, glucose. Don't confuse with **-ase** an enzyme · **-ous** an adjective (seminiferous).
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis** — dissolution, breaking down: fibrinolysis (→ fibrinolysin). Don't confuse with **-lytic** (its adjective) · **dialysis** (separation through a membrane).
[^pre-bi]: **bi-** — two, twice (Latin): bicarbonate, bilateral. Don't confuse with **di-** two (Greek: dihydro-) · **bio-** life.
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — without: ascorbic (without scurvy), azoospermia (without living sperm). Don't confuse with **ad-** towards · **ana-** up · **ab-** away.
[^suf-ine]: **-ine** — an amine, alkaloid or basic chemical: spermine, choline, noradrenaline, acetylcholine. Don't confuse with **-in** a protein · **-ine** in adjectives (uterine, bovine).
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside: parasympathetic. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **para** (number of deliveries, obstetrics).
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together: sympathetic, parasympathetic, synthesis. Don't confuse with **sin-** without · **semi-** half.
[^suf-ergic]: **-ergic** — working by, acting through: adrenergic, cholinergic, dopaminergic. Don't confuse with **-ergy** energy / work as a noun (synergy) · **-urgy** (surgery — hand-work).
[^suf-ole]: **-ole** — small: arteriole, vacuole, centriole. Don't confuse with **-ol** an alcohol (estradiol) · **-ule** (also small: tubule).
[^pre-en]: **en- / em-** — in, into, to cause: engorgement (filled in). Don't confuse with **endo-** within (Greek) · **ex- / e-** out.
[^pre-di]: **di-** — two, twice (Greek): phosphodiesterase, dihydrotestosterone. Don't confuse with **dia-** through · **dis-** apart · **bi-** two (Latin).
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endothelin (from the endothelium, the lining within vessels), endocrine. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside · **ecto-** outer · **epi-** upon.
[^pre-nor]: **nor-** — chemical prefix for "parent compound minus a methyl group" (German *N ohne Radikal*): noradrenaline, norepinephrine. Don't confuse with **normo-** normal · **neuro-** nerve.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, away, reversing: deferens (carrying away), detumescence (reverse of swelling). Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis-** apart · **dys-** bad.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below: hypothalamus, hypophysis, hypogonadism. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep.
[^suf-trophin]: **-trophin / -tropin** — nourishing / turning towards (stimulating) a target: gonadotrophin, gonadotropin, corticotropin. Don't confuse with **-trophy** growth in size (hypertrophy) · **-tropy / -tropic** in physics (isotropic).
[^suf-ia]: **-ia** — a condition or state: azoospermia, genitalia (plural "-ia" here), dysplasia. Don't confuse with **-iasis** a disease process · **-ium** a structure.
[^pre-erythro]: **erythro-** — red: erythropoietin, erythrocyte. Don't confuse with **erythema** (skin redness — same root used as a noun) · **leuko-** white · **xantho-** yellow.
[^suf-poietin]: **-poietin / -poiesis** — making, producing: erythropoietin, thrombopoietin, haematopoiesis. Don't confuse with **-plasia** formation of tissue · **-genesis** creation.
[^pre-ana]: **ana-** — up, again: anabolism (building up). Don't confuse with **an- / a-** without · **ante-** before · **anti-** against.
[^suf-ism]: **-ism** — a state or process: anabolism, metabolism, hypogonadism. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation · **-osis** a condition.
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, excessive: hyperplasia, hypergonadotropic. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra-** above (Latin).
[^suf-plasia]: **-plasia** — formation, growth: hyperplasia, hypoplasia, dysplasia. Don't confuse with **-plasty** surgical repair · **-trophy** size (hypertrophy).
