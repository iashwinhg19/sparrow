# Physiology of GIT Absorption and Selective Malabsorption

## Basics of Absorption
### Stages
- Luminal → enzymes mediated
- Small intestinal mucosal → enterocyte; villi → brush border enzymes
- Post mucosal → mucosal epithelial cell → blood
### Anatomy of small intestine
- Proximal 2/5 → maximum absorption (jejunum > duodenum)
- Distal 3/5 (ileum) → bile acid, vitamin B12, Mg2+
### Histology
- Mucosa
  - Enterocyte → villi surrounded by crypts of Lieberkühn; brush border
  - Crypts → Paneth cells at base (alpha defensins, lysozyme, phospholipase A2)
  - Crypts → enteroendocrine cells, goblet cells
  - Continuous renewal every 48–72 h
  - Lamina propria → glands, blood vessels
  - Muscularis mucosa → tight attachment
- Submucosa
  - Brunner's glands (duodenum) → bicarbonate, alkaline
  - Peyer's patches (ileum) → macroscopic lymphoid aggregates; hypertrophy in typhoid
- Muscularis propria
- Serosa
### Notes
- Plica circularis → visible mucosal folds on luminal surface
- Interstitial cells of Cajal → pacemaker → basal electrical rhythm

## Fat Absorption
### Luminal phase
- TGA → pancreatic lipase (lipolysis) → fatty acids
- Fatty acids + bile acids + glycerol + phospholipid → micelle
- Micelle → passive diffusion into enterocytes
- Defects → chronic pancreatitis (lipase); bile acid defect
### Bile acid physiology
- Formation & excretion 500 mg/day
- Body pool 4 g → maintained by enterohepatic circulation
- Liver 7α-hydroxylase → rate limiting
- 1° bile acids → cholic acid, chenodeoxycholic acid
- Conjugation with glycine/taurine → bile salts
- D2 duodenum → micelle formation
- Ileum → reabsorbed → portal circulation → liver
- Colon → bacterial deconjugation → 2° bile acids (deoxycholic, lithocholic)
### Bile acid defect by step
- Synthesis → cirrhosis
- Early deconjugation in small intestine → SIBO
- Reabsorption → TB / Crohn's (ileal dysfunction)
- Transportation → primary biliary cholangitis, primary sclerosing cholangitis
### Mucosal phase
- Defect in small intestinal mucosal integrity
- Celiac disease, Whipple disease, tropical sprue
### Post-mucosal phase
- LCFA + glycerol → esterification → chylomicrons → intestinal lymphatics
- Abetalipoproteinemia ⊣ esterification
- Intestinal lymphangiectasia ⊣ lymphatic transport
### Types of fatty acids
- Long chain > 12 C → diet triglycerides; small intestine; needs lipolysis + micelle; stool minimal
- Medium chain 8–12 C → coconut oil; small intestine; no lipolysis, no micelle; stool none
- Short chain < 8 C → bacterial degradation of carbohydrate in colon; colon; stool substantial

## Carbohydrate Absorption
### Mechanism
- Disaccharides → disaccharidases (brush border) → monosaccharides
- Lactase → lactose → glucose + galactose
- Sucrase → sucrose → glucose + fructose
- Maltase, trehalase
### Receptors in the gut
- SGLT1 (AKA SLC5A) → glucose + galactose → 2° active transport
- GLUT5 → fructose → facilitated diffusion
- GLUT2 → basolateral → capillary
- SGLT1 defect → familial glucose-galactose malabsorption
### Receptors in the liver
- GLUT5 → galactose (galactokinase → galactose-1-P); fructose (fructokinase → fructose-1-P)
- GLUT2 → glucose (glucokinase → glucose-6-P)
### Defects
- Lactase deficiency → congenital AR; acquired primary/secondary → selective
- Small intestinal mucosal defect → celiac, Whipple's, tropical sprue → global

## Proteins
### Mechanism
- Enterokinase (brush border) → trypsinogen → trypsin
- Trypsin → activates chymotrypsin, elastase
- Exopeptidases → carboxypeptidase, aminopeptidase (activated by trypsin)
- Transport → sodium–amino acid co-transporters
### Defects
- Global → celiac, Whipple, tropical sprue
- Selective
  - Enterokinase deficiency
  - Neutral amino acid transport → Hartnup's disease → pellagra (diarrhoea, dermatitis, dementia)
  - Dibasic amino acid transport → cystinuria → COLA (cystine, ornithine, lysine, arginine)

## Carbohydrate Malabsorption
### Lactose intolerance
- Lactose (milk, cheese) → lactase (brush border) → glucose + galactose
- Congenital → AR, absent enzyme from birth
- Acquired → ↓ enzyme activity
  - Primary (m/c) → genetic, allele persistence
  - Secondary → infections, IBS
### Pathogenesis
- Undigested carbohydrate → ↑ unabsorbed carbohydrate in colon
- Fermented by Bacteroides fragilis, Bifidobacteria, Lactobacillus
- ↑ acid and gases → H2, methane, CO2
### Clinical
- 90 min post prandial → distension, bloating, cramps
- Diaper dermatitis (children, acidic pH)
- Severe watery diarrhea, failure to thrive, irritability
### Investigations
- Stool pH → acidic
- Lactose hydrogen breath test → positive H2 > 20 ppm
- Lactose–glucose blood test → obsolete
### Management
- Dietary management
- Ca2+ supplementation

## Fat Malabsorption
### Abetalipoproteinemia
- AKA neuroacanthocytosis; autosomal recessive
- MTTP defect → apoB48 defect → defective esterification → no chylomicrons
- Hypolipidemia
- Triad → ataxia (vit E); steatorrhea; retinitis pigmentosa (night blindness)
- Peripheral smear → acanthocytes
- Post-prandial endoscopic biopsy → 100 % diagnostic → vacuolation in enterocytes
- 100 % diagnostic on biopsy → Whipple's, abetalipoproteinemia, agammaglobulinemia
- Vit E deficiency → ataxia (pronounced)
  - Fat malabsorption → ↓ vit E absorption
  - ↓ VLDL → ↓ hepatic secretion of vit E
  - ↓ chylomicron → ↓ intestinal secretion of vit E into blood
### Intestinal lymphangiectasia
- Chylomicrons → intestinal lymphatics → blood
- Secondary → lymph node obstruction → ↑ pressure → exudation (lymphoma, Crohn's)
- Primary → dilated lymphatic channel → ↑ pressure → exudation into lumen
- Protein losing enteropathy → hypoalbuminemia + hypoglobulinemia; edema; ascites
  - D/d nephrotic syndrome (albumin in urine); liver disease (↓ production)
- Diarrhea
- Fecal α-1 antitrypsin → elevated, specific
- CT → small bowel wall thickening; video capsule endoscopy
- Management → medium chain triglyceride diet
