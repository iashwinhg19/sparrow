# Gastrointestinal Secretions

## Introduction
- Digestion & absorption need
  - GI secretions (with enzymes)
  - GI motility
- Both regulated by
  - Enteric nervous system
  - GI hormones

### Layers of GI tract
- Serosa (1) → outermost
- Muscularis propria (2) → GI motility
  - Longitudinal muscle
  - Circular muscle
  - Myenteric plexus = Auerbach's plexus
- Submucosa (3)
  - Submucosal plexus = Meissner's → regulates GI secretions
- Mucosa (4) → innermost
  - Muscularis mucosa → alters surface area, no role in motility

### Daily water turnover in GIT
- Total water 9000 mL/day → reabsorbed 8800 → 200 mL in stool
- Ingested fluids 2000 mL
- GI secretions
  - Saliva 1500 · stomach 2500
  - Exocrine pancreas 1500 · bile 500 · intestine 1000
- Reabsorbed
  - Jejunum 5500 mL (maximum)
  - Ileum 2000 mL
  - Colon 1300 mL

## Saliva
- Digestive & protective functions
### Salivary glands
- 3 major glands
  - Parotid → largest; parotid duct; accessory parotid gland
  - Submandibular → Wharton's duct
  - Sublingual
- Properties
  - pH 7
  - Hypotonic to plasma
- 2 minor glands
  - Weber's gland → tonsils
  - von Ebner's gland → base of tongue

### Functions of saliva
- Carbohydrate → salivary amylase / ptyalin
- Fats → lingual lipase (von Ebner's glands)
- Protein → no enzymes
- Lubrication → prevents injury to buccal mucosa by dry food
- Protection → immunity
  - Secretory IgA
  - Lactoferrin, lysozymes → anti-bacterial

### Salivon
- Functional unit of salivary gland
- Acinus (acinar cells) → 1° secretion, similar to plasma
- Duct (ductal cells) → 2° secretion
  - Reabsorbs Na⁺ & Cl⁻
  - Secretes K⁺ & HCO₃⁻
- High flow → ↓ time for reabsorption → ↑ Na⁺ & Cl⁻

### Regulation of secretion
- Salivation = unconditioned reflex
- ANS
  - Sympathetic → ↑ thick saliva
  - Parasympathetic → profound ↑ thin watery saliva
- Bradykinin → vasodilation → ↑ blood flow → ↑ secretion
- Increase → sight, smell, thought of food
- Decrease → sleep, fear, fatigue

### Innervation of salivary glands
- CN IX → otic ganglion → parotid gland
- CN VII → submandibular ganglion → submandibular & sublingual glands
- Parotid surgery → ↑ risk of CN VII injury

### Abnormalities of salivary secretion
- Absent secretion → aptyalism
- ↓ secretion → xerostomia (dry mouth)
  - Mumps
  - Sjögren syndrome
- Facial nerve injury in parotid surgery → misdirected regeneration
  - Into sweat glands → ↑ salivation → ↑ sweating
  - Into lacrimal gland → crocodile tears / gustatory lacrimation

## Gastric secretions
- Volume 2.5 L/day
- pH 1.5–3 → most acidic secretion
### Gastric glands (flask shaped)
- Surface cells
  - HCO₃⁻-rich mucus → acid neutralisation
  - Trefoil peptides → immunity
- Neck cells → stem cells
- Parietal cells
  - HCl
  - Intrinsic factor of Castle → vit B12 absorption
- ECL cells → histamine → ↑ acid
- Chief cells → pepsinogen
- Antral glands → G cells → gastrin → ↑ acid
- Pernicious anaemia
  - Autoantibodies to parietal cells → destruction
  - → Achlorhydria (↓ acid)
  - → Anaemia (↓ B12 absorption)
  - → Atrophic gastric mucosa

### Acid production in parietal cell
- CO₂ + H₂O → (carbonic anhydrase) → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
- a → H⁺/K⁺ ATPase proton pump → H⁺ to lumen
- c → Cl⁻ channel → Cl⁻ to lumen → HCl
- b → Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger → HCO₃⁻ to blood
- Post-prandial alkaline tide → ↑ HCl after meal → ↑ HCO₃⁻ in blood

### Regulation of gastric acid
- Increase
  - Histamine → ECL cells → H₂
  - Gastrin → antral G cells → CCK-B
  - ACh → vagal synapses → M₃
  - Coffee, alcohol, Ca²⁺ → ingestion
- Decrease
  - Excess acid → negative feedback
  - Somatostatin → universal inhibitor
  - Prostaglandin, CCK, secretin, calcitonin

### Functions of gastric secretion
- Carbohydrate → no enzymes
- Fats → gastric lipase
- Proteins → pepsinogen → (HCl) → pepsin
  - Rennin → in cows
- Protection → ↑ acidity → microbes killed

### Phases of gastric acid secretion
- Cephalic 20–30 %
  - In absence of food
  - Sight, smell, thought of food
- Gastric 60–70 %
  - Food enters stomach
  - Antral G cells → gastrin → ↑ acid
- Intestinal 10 %
  - Products of protein digestion
  - Intestinal G cells → gastrin → ↑ acid

## Exocrine pancreatic secretion
- Pancreatic juice
  - Digests all nutrients
  - 1500 mL
  - pH 8.3 (↑ HCO₃⁻)
- Acinar cells → digestive enzymes
### Function
- Carbohydrates → pancreatic amylase
- Fats → pancreatic lipase + colipase
- Proteins → zymogens (inactive)
  - Trypsinogen
  - Chymotrypsinogen
  - Procarboxypeptidase

### Activation of zymogens
- Trypsinogen → (enterokinase) → trypsin
- Trypsin activates
  - Chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin
  - Procarboxypeptidase → carboxypeptidase
- All → protein digestion
- Premature activation → acute pancreatitis (auto-digestion)

### Regulation (hormonal)
- CCK-PZ
  - Constriction of gall bladder
  - ↑ pancreatic enzymes
- Secretin = nature's antacid
  - S cells, stimulated by acid chyme
  - → ↑ HCO₃⁻ in pancreatic juice → acid neutralisation
  - First hormone discovered
  - ↓↓ enzyme concentration

### Mechanism of HCO₃⁻ secretion
- CO₂ + H₂O → (CA) → H₂CO₃ → HCO₃⁻ + H⁺
- a → Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger → HCO₃⁻ to lumen
- CFTR → Cl⁻ channel recycles Cl⁻ to lumen
  - Mutation → cystic fibrosis

## Biliary secretion
- Liver → synthesis of bile (97 % water)
- Gall bladder → storage of concentrated bile (87 % water)
### Functions of bile
- Digestion & absorption of lipids
- Excretion of bile pigments
- Excretion of cholesterol

### Constituents of bile
- Primary bile acids (liver)
  - Cholic acid
  - Chenodeoxycholic acid
- → intestinal flora → secondary bile acids
  - Deoxycholic acid
  - Lithocholic acid
- Conjugation in liver with glycine / taurine
  - + Na⁺ → bile salts (sodium salts)
  - Eg sodium glycocholate

### Enterohepatic circulation
- Purpose → minimise energy for bile synthesis
- Liver & gall bladder → terminal ileum → portal vein → liver
- 6–8 times/day

### Factors affecting bile synthesis
- Choleretics → ↑ bile synthesis
  - Bile salt
  - Secretin
  - Vagal stimulation
- Cholagogues → ↑ gall-bladder contraction
  - CCK

## Intestinal secretions
- AKA succus entericus
- Site → crypts of Lieberkühn
### Cell types
- Goblet cell → mucus (acid neutralisation)
- Epithelial cell → source of succus entericus
- Paneth cell (base)
  - ↑ zinc
  - Defensins, lysozymes → anti-bacterial

### Potassium note (mEq/L)
- Maximum K⁺ secretion → saliva
- Maximum K⁺ concentration → colonic secretion
