# 🍽️ Digestion and Absorption

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> **Digestion**[^ety-digestion][^pre-di] is the breakdown of food — polysaccharides, triglycerides and polypeptides — into units small enough to cross the gut wall; **absorption**[^ety-absorption][^pre-ab] is their transfer from the lumen, through the **enterocyte**[^ety-enterocyte][^suf-cyte], into blood or lymph. For every nutrient the chapter answers:
> - ✅ **Which enzyme cuts it, where** (mouth, stomach, pancreas, brush border)?
> - ✅ **Which transporter carries it in and out** of the enterocyte (SGLT-1, GLUT-5, GLUT-2, PepT, DMT-1, ferroportin)?
> - ✅ **What happens when that step fails** (CGGM, steatorrhoea, haemochromatosis)?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — Cut to Size, Then Use the Right Door

Every nutrient goes through the same two gates: **scissors** (enzymes that cut it to absorbable size) and a **door** (a membrane transporter, or for fat a "ferry" — the micelle). Carbohydrates and proteins enter the blood; fats are rebuilt inside the cell and leave by the lymph.

```
CARBOHYDRATE  polysaccharide ─amylase (α1:4)→ disaccharides ─brush border (α1:6, lactase, sucrase)→ glucose / fructose
              glucose + Na+ ─SGLT-1 (secondary active)→ cell ─GLUT-2 (facilitated)→ blood   ·   fructose ─GLUT-5→ cell → blood
LIPID         TG ─lingual / gastric / pancreatic lipase + colipase→ FFA ─bile-salt micelle→ passive diffusion
              → re-esterified to TG → chylomicrons → lymph   ·   bile salts recycled (enterohepatic, 6–8×/day)
PROTEIN       polypeptide ─pepsin, trypsin, chymotrypsin (endo) + carboxypeptidase (exo)→ di/tripeptides
              ─PepT (H+)→ cell ─cytoplasmic protease→ amino acids → basolateral transporter → blood
IRON          Fe3+ ─Dcytb→ Fe2+ ─DMT-1→ cell ─ferroportin→ Fe2+ ─hephaestin→ Fe3+ → transferrin   (hepcidin ⊖ ferroportin)
WATER         9 L in → 8.8 L back (max jejunum) → 200 mL stool
```

### ABSORPTION

![[Digestion and Absorption - carbohydrate absorption SGLT1 GLUT5 GLUT2.png]]
*Enterocyte between lumen and blood: fructose enters by GLUT 5 and leaves by GLUT-5 (page); glucose enters with Na⁺ by the sodium–glucose cotransporter (SGLT-1 — found in GIT, secondary active transport) and leaves by GLUT-2 (facilitated diffusion) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Sugar | Apical (lumen → cell) | Basolateral (cell → blood) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fructose (F)** | **GLUT 5**[^ety-glut] | **GLUT-5**[^2] |
| **Glucose** (with **Na⁺**) | **Sodium glucose cotransporter**[^ety-cotransport][^pre-co][^pre-trans] **(SGLT-1)** • **Found in GIT.** • **Secondary active transport.** | **GLUT-2 (facilitated diffusion**[^ety-diffusion]**)** |

🧠 *In plain words:* the Na⁺/K⁺ pump on the blood side keeps cell Na⁺ low; Na⁺ rushing into the cell down that gradient drags glucose with it through SGLT-1 (that is why it is *secondary* active — the energy is spent by the pump, not by SGLT-1). Glucose, now concentrated in the cell, flows out down its own gradient through GLUT-2.[^3]

> [!info] Clinical aspect
> - **ORS**[^ety-ors]**: Na⁺ absorption is facilitated by glucose absorption (via SGLT-1).**
> 1. **Congenital**[^ety-congenital] **glucose galactose**[^ety-galactose] **malabsorption**[^pre-mal] **(CGGM).**
>    - **Congenital absence of SGLT-1.**
>    - **Genetics: autosomal**[^ety-autosomal] **recessive**[^ety-recessive].
>    - **Presentation: chronic refractory**[^ety-refractory] **diarrhoea**[^ety-diarrhoea][^suf-rrhoea].
>
> **Note: SGLT-2 is found in kidney.**[^4]

*Lipid digestion along the gut — mouth (lingual lipase), stomach (gastric lipase) and pancreas (pancreatic lipase, colipase, cholesterol esterase), which act on TG and form free fatty acids (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Site | Enzyme |
|---|---|
| **Mouth** | **Lingual lipase**[^ety-lipase] |
| **Stomach** | **Gastric lipase** |
| **Pancreas** | **Pancreatic lipase** · **Colipase**[^ety-colipase] · **Cholesterol esterase**[^ety-cholesterol][^ety-ester] — **act on TG and form free fatty acids** |

### ABSORPTION

![[Digestion and Absorption - micelle and mucosal cell.png]]
*Micelle formation: bile-salt micelles carry free fatty acids, cholesterol and fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K at high concentration → passive diffusion into the mucosal cell (low concentration) → free fatty acids are re-esterified to triglycerides → transported by chylomicrons (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
Bile-salt micelles (HIGH concentration)               Mucosal cell (LOW concentration)
 • Free fatty acids, cholesterol        ──Passive──►    Free fatty acids
 • Fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K       Diffusion          ↓ Reesterification
                                                         Triglycerides
                                                            ↓ Transported by
                                                         Chylomicrons
```

- **Micelle**[^ety-micelle] **formation:** **bile-salt micelles** hold **free fatty acids, cholesterol, fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, K** at **high concentration**.
- → **Passive diffusion** into the **mucosal cell (low concentration)**.
- **Free fatty acids → re-esterification**[^pre-re] **→ triglycerides → transported by chylomicrons**[^ety-chylomicron][^pre-chylo].[^5]

![[Digestion and Absorption - emulsification of fat globules.png]]
*Emulsification — a large fat globule coated with bile salts is broken into smaller droplets (cropped from the handwritten page).*

**Emulsification**[^ety-emulsification]
- **Mechanism: bile salt + fat.**
- **Globule**[^ety-globule][^suf-ule] → **prevent formation of large fat globule.**
- **Use: ↑ action of lipase (facilitates digestion & absorption of lipids).**

🧠 *In plain words:* fat floats as big drops that lipase (a water-soluble enzyme) can only nibble at the surface. Bile salts, acting like detergent, break the drops into tiny droplets — far more surface — and then gather the products into micelles that ferry them through the watery layer to the cell surface.

### Enterohepatic[^ety-enterohepatic][^pre-entero][^pre-hepato] circulation

![[Digestion and Absorption - enterohepatic circulation.png]]
*Enterohepatic circulation — bile salts go from the liver to the gut and are taken back at the terminal ileum to the liver; this occurs 6–8 times/day (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
        Liver
       ▲     │
       │     │  occurs 6–8 times / day
       │     ▼
   Terminal ileum
```

- **Liver ⇄ terminal ileum — occurs 6–8 times/day.**
- **Reduce forming new bile salt: conserve energy.**

> [!note] Note — **Short chain fatty acids (SCFAs)**
> - **Eg: propionate**[^ety-propionate]**, butyrate**[^ety-butyrate]**, acetate**[^ety-acetate]**.**
> - **Formed by colonic flora**[^ety-flora]**.**
> - **Functions:**
>   - **Trophic**[^ety-trophic] **action: ↑ mucosal growth.**
>   - **Acid base balance: absorbed in exchange for H⁺.**
>   - **Prevents inflammation**[^ety-inflammation].[^6]

## 🥩 Protein[^ety-protein]

### DIGESTION — Diet: **Polypeptides**[^ety-peptide]

![[Digestion and Absorption - protein digestion along the gut.png]]
*Protein digestion along the gut — mouth (no enzymes for protein digestion), stomach (pepsin), pancreas/small intestine (trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Site | Enzyme |
|---|---|
| **Mouth** | **No enzymes for protein digestion** |
| **Stomach** | **Pepsin**[^ety-pepsin] |
| **Pancreas** (acting in the small intestine) | **Trypsin**[^ety-trypsin] · **Chymotrypsin**[^ety-chymotrypsin] · **Carboxypeptidase**[^ety-carboxypeptidase] |

The pancreatic enzymes:
- **Present in form of zymogen**[^ety-zymogen][^suf-ogen] **(inactive).**
  - **Prevent autodigestion**[^pre-auto] **of pancreas.**
- **Activated in small intestine.**
- **Form dipeptides & tripeptides.**

**Types of pancreatic enzymes:**

![[Digestion and Absorption - endopeptidases and exopeptidases.png]]
*Endopeptidases (trypsin, chymotrypsin) add H₂O across inner peptide bonds and split a chain into shorter chains; exopeptidases — aminopeptidase at the H₂N end and carboxypeptidase at the COOH end — clip off terminal amino acids (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Type | Example | Acts on |
|---|---|---|
| **Endopeptidase**[^ety-endopeptidase][^pre-endo] | **Eg: trypsin, chymotrypsin** | **The inner region peptide bonds** (+ H₂O — hydrolysis[^ety-hydrolysis][^pre-hydro][^suf-lysis]) |
| **Exopeptidase**[^pre-exo] | **Aminopeptidase**[^ety-amino] (H₂N end) · **Carboxypeptidase** (COOH end) | **The end region peptide bonds** |

### ABSORPTION

![[Digestion and Absorption - protein absorption PepT1.png]]
*Dipeptides and tripeptides enter the cell with H⁺ through the peptide transporter (PepT); the Na⁺–H⁺ exchanger recycles the H⁺; a cytoplasmic protease splits them into amino acids, which leave through the basolateral amino-acid transporter into the blood (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
Lumen: Dipeptides/Tripeptides + H+ ──Peptide Transporter (PepT)──► Cell: Dipeptides/Tripeptides
                                                                        ↓ Cytoplasmic protease
                                                                    Amino acids
                                                                        ↓ Basolateral amino acid transporter
                                                                    Blood: Amino acids
Lumen: H+ ◄──Na+–H+ exchange──► Cell: Na+
```

- **Dipeptides, tripeptides** enter with **H⁺** through the **peptide transporter (PepT)**.
- **Na⁺–H⁺ exchange** pumps H⁺ back to the lumen (keeps the H⁺ gradient).
- **Cytoplasmic**[^ety-cytoplasm][^pre-cyto] **protease**[^ety-protease] → **amino acids**.
- **Basolateral**[^ety-basolateral][^pre-baso] **amino acid transporter** → **blood**.[^7]

## 🧲 Iron Absorption

### MECHANISM

- **Site: duodenum**[^ety-duodenum].

![[Digestion and Absorption - iron absorption in duodenal enterocyte.png]]
*Iron absorption in the duodenal enterocyte: heme iron enters by the heme transporter; non-heme Fe³⁺ is reduced to Fe²⁺ by duodenal cytochrome B and enters by DMT-1; Fe²⁺ is stored as mucosal ferritin (lost by shedding of epithelial cells) or exported by ferroportin 1, oxidised to Fe³⁺ by hephaestin and carried by plasma transferrin in blood to the erythroid marrow; the liver's hepcidin inhibits ferroportin (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
FOOD ─┬─ Heme iron ──Heme transporter──► heme → Fe2+ ─┐
      │                                              ├─► Fe2+ ─┬─► Mucosal ferritin ──► lost by shedding of epithelial cells
      └─ Non-heme Fe3+ ──Duodenal cytochrome B──► Fe2+ ──DMT-1──┘    │
                                                                  └─► Ferroportin 1 ──► Fe2+ ──Hephaestin──► Fe3+
                                                                         ▲ (⊖)                                 ↓
                                                                      Hepcidin ◄── Liver          Plasma transferrin (blood)
                                                                                                         ↓
                                                                                                  Erythroid marrow
```

| Step | Label on the page |
|---|---|
| Heme iron in | **Heme**[^ety-heme] **transporter** |
| Non-heme Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺ | **Duodenal cytochrome B**[^ety-cytochrome] |
| Fe²⁺ in | **DMT-1**[^ety-dmt1] |
| Storage in cell | **Mucosal ferritin**[^ety-ferritin] → lost by shedding of epithelial cells |
| Fe²⁺ out to blood | **Ferroportin 1**[^ety-ferroportin] |
| Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ | **Hephaestin**[^ety-hephaestin] |
| Carrier in blood | **Plasma transferrin**[^ety-transferrin] → **erythroid**[^ety-erythroid][^pre-erythro] **marrow** |
| Brake | **Hepcidin**[^ety-hepcidin] (from **liver**) **⊖ ferroportin** |

**Iron absorption depends on:**
1. **Dietary iron intake.**
2. **Body iron stores.**
3. **State of erythropoietic**[^suf-poiesis] **activity (in bone marrow).**

| **Factors ↑** | **Factors ↓** |
|---|---|
| **Vitamin C**[^ety-ascorbic] | **Tannins**[^ety-tannin] |
| | **Oxalates**[^ety-oxalate] |
| | **Phytates**[^ety-phytate] |

### REGULATION

**Hepcidin (negative regulator):**
- **Hepcidin → released from liver.**
- **Genetics: HFE gene —(mutation)→ hemochromatosis**[^ety-haemochromatosis][^pre-haemo][^suf-osis].
- **Function: represses basolateral transport of iron into blood.**
  - **Binds to ferroportin → iron not released from macrophages**[^ety-macrophage][^pre-macro][^suf-phage] **& other cells.**
  - **Links body iron stores with iron absorption.**[^9]

🧠 *In plain words:* hepcidin is the liver's "full" signal. When iron stores are high, hepcidin rises, locks ferroportin, and iron stays trapped in enterocytes (lost when they shed) and in macrophages. In HFE haemochromatosis hepcidin is inappropriately low, ferroportin stays open, and iron floods in year after year.

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Absorption is **two-step transport across a polarised cell**: a specific **apical door** brings the nutrient in (SGLT-1, GLUT-5, PepT, DMT-1, passive diffusion from micelles) and a different **basolateral door** lets it out (GLUT-2, amino-acid transporter, ferroportin, chylomicron exocytosis to lymph). Digestion exists only to cut food down to the size those doors accept — monosaccharides, di/tripeptides and amino acids, free fatty acids and monoglycerides.
>
> The energy for "uphill" entry is borrowed from gradients set up elsewhere — the Na⁺ gradient (SGLT-1, Na⁺-dependent vitamin carriers) or the H⁺ gradient (PepT, DMT-1) — which is why glucose rescues Na⁺ absorption in ORS. Iron is the exception that is **regulated at the exit door** (hepcidin closing ferroportin), because the body has no way to excrete excess iron.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Amylase, lipases and proteases hand monosaccharides, fatty acids and small peptides to the brush border; SGLT-1/GLUT-2, micelles/chylomicrons and PepT/amino-acid transporters absorb them, mostly in the duodenum and jejunum; bile salts and B₁₂ are taken up in the terminal ileum; iron in the duodenum according to hepcidin.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A partial loss is absorbed by the gut's large reserve: lactase falls after weaning but small loads of lactose are tolerated; a short ileal resection is compensated by increased hepatic bile-salt synthesis; early iron deficiency lowers hepcidin and upregulates DMT-1 and ferroportin to absorb more.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> When the defect is complete the unabsorbed nutrient produces disease: absent SGLT-1 (CGGM) gives chronic refractory osmotic diarrhoea from birth that stops when glucose and galactose are removed; loss of bile salts or lipase gives steatorrhoea and deficiency of vitamins A, D, E, K; HFE mutation gives haemochromatosis (liver cirrhosis, diabetes, bronze skin, cardiomyopathy).
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → loss of an enzyme / transporter / regulator → reserve compensates → reserve exceeded → malabsorption (diarrhoea, steatorrhoea, deficiency) or overload (haemochromatosis)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Treatment follows the missing step: fructose-based feeds for CGGM (GLUT-5 still works), pancreatic enzymes for exocrine insufficiency, medium-chain triglycerides (absorbed without micelles) for fat malabsorption, vitamin C with iron tablets and avoidance of tea (tannins) at meals, phlebotomy for haemochromatosis, and glucose-containing ORS for secretory diarrhoea.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Congenital glucose–galactose malabsorption:** "Neonate with watery diarrhoea from the first feeds, stops on fasting, tolerates fructose", "autosomal recessive", "absent SGLT-1" → glucose and galactose share SGLT-1; fructose uses GLUT-5.
>
> **ORS:** "Glucose added to salt solution", "cholera" → SGLT-1 co-transports Na⁺ with glucose even when cAMP-driven secretion is maximal.
>
> **Fat malabsorption:** "Pale, bulky, foul-smelling floating stools", "deficiency of A, D, E, K" → no lipase (pancreatic insufficiency) or no bile salts (obstruction, ileal resection).
>
> **Hereditary haemochromatosis:** "HFE gene (C282Y)", "bronze diabetes", "high ferritin and transferrin saturation" → low hepcidin keeps ferroportin open.
>
> **Iron-deficiency diet clues:** "Tea with meals", "high-phytate cereal diet" → tannins and phytates bind non-heme iron; vitamin C reduces Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ and helps.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Micelle = a sphere of bile salts with hydrophilic heads out and fat inside; enterocyte diagrams: Na⁺-glucose in at the top, GLUT-2 out at the bottom.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Water: 9 L enters, 8.8 L is absorbed, so only 200 mL reaches the stool — a small fall in absorption (e.g. 8.8 → 7.8 L) multiplies stool water fivefold and causes diarrhoea.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "SGLT-1 is primary active transport"**
>
> **What students think:** SGLT-1 pumps glucose uphill, so it uses ATP.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** SGLT-1 uses no ATP; it couples glucose to Na⁺ moving down the gradient that the basolateral Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase creates. That is **secondary active transport**. Exit by GLUT-2 is **facilitated diffusion**. The ATP is spent by the pump, one step removed.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Who burns the ATP?" — if it is a different protein, the transport is secondary.
>
> **Trap 2: SGLT-1 vs SGLT-2**
>
> **What students think:** The gliflozin drugs block intestinal glucose absorption.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **SGLT-1 is the gut transporter** (glucose and galactose); **SGLT-2 is in the kidney** (proximal tubule, ~90 % of filtered glucose). Gliflozins (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin) block SGLT-2 and cause glycosuria, not malabsorption.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "1 = intestine first; 2 = kidney second."
>
> **Trap 3: All fat goes into the portal blood**
>
> **What students think:** Absorbed fat travels to the liver like glucose.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Long-chain fatty acids are re-esterified into triglycerides and packaged into **chylomicrons**, which are too large for capillaries and enter the **lacteals (lymph)**, reaching the blood through the thoracic duct. Short- and medium-chain fatty acids are the ones that go directly into portal blood.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Long chain → lymph; short chain → portal."
>
> **Trap 4: Stomach digests carbohydrate**
>
> **What students think:** Salivary amylase keeps working in the stomach, so the stomach digests starch.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The page is explicit: **no enzymes play a role in carbohydrate digestion in the stomach** — salivary amylase is **inhibited by gastric acid**. The **major** enzyme is **pancreatic amylase**.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Remember the two amylase facts on the page: activated by Cl⁻, inhibited by acid.
>
> **Trap 5: Hepcidin increases iron absorption**
>
> **What students think:** Hepcidin is made in the liver, which stores iron, so it must help absorption.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Hepcidin is the **negative regulator** — it binds **ferroportin** and causes its degradation, trapping iron in enterocytes and macrophages. It rises with high stores and inflammation (anaemia of chronic disease) and is low in HFE haemochromatosis and iron deficiency.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Hepcidin = iron's handcuffs."

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Gastrointestinal Secretions]]**
> All the enzymes here (salivary and pancreatic amylase, lipases, colipase, trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase) and bile salts are produced there; enterokinase activation and the enterohepatic circulation are shared.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Membrane Transport]]**
> SGLT-1 (secondary active symport), GLUT-2/GLUT-5 (facilitated diffusion), Na⁺–H⁺ exchange (antiport) and passive diffusion of fatty acids are the general transport mechanisms applied to the gut.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Proximal Convoluted Tubule]]**
> The renal tubule reabsorbs glucose (SGLT-2 and SGLT-1), amino acids and peptides with the same apical-in/basolateral-out logic — the note "SGLT-2 is found in kidney" links here.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Hematopoiesis and Blood Groups]]**
> Iron, B₁₂ and folate are absorbed for erythropoiesis; failures give iron-deficiency (microcytic) and megaloblastic anaemias.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Gastrointestinal Hormones]]**
> CCK (fat and protein) and secretin (acid) release the enzymes and bicarbonate that digestion needs; GIP is triggered by absorbed glucose.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Acid Base Balance]]**
> Short-chain fatty acids are absorbed in exchange for H⁺; loss of HCO₃⁻-rich intestinal fluid in diarrhoea causes normal-anion-gap metabolic acidosis.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Oral rehydration therapy, lactose intolerance, CGGM, pancreatic insufficiency, ileal resection, coeliac disease, pernicious anaemia and haemochromatosis are all failures or exploitations of one step in this chapter.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Typical questions: the transporter for fructose (GLUT-5), glucose exit (GLUT-2), the basis of ORS (SGLT-1), the site of iron (duodenum), B₁₂ (terminal ileum) and maximum water absorption (jejunum), the negative regulator of iron absorption (hepcidin), and dietary factors that increase (vitamin C) or decrease (tannins, oxalates, phytates) iron absorption.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Carbohydrates: **salivary amylase — activated by Cl⁻, inhibited by gastric acid**; **stomach — no enzymes**; **pancreatic amylase = major enzyme, α1:4 → disaccharides**; **brush-border disaccharidases (lactase, isomaltase, sucrase), α1:6 → monosaccharides**.
> 2. **Glucose (+ Na⁺) in by SGLT-1 (secondary active), out by GLUT-2 (facilitated diffusion)**; **fructose by GLUT-5**.
> 3. **ORS works because glucose facilitates Na⁺ absorption via SGLT-1**; **CGGM = congenital absence of SGLT-1, autosomal recessive, chronic refractory diarrhoea**; **SGLT-2 is in the kidney**.
> 4. Lipids: **lingual lipase (mouth), gastric lipase, pancreatic lipase + colipase + cholesterol esterase → free fatty acids**.
> 5. **Emulsification (bile salt + fat) prevents large fat globules → ↑ lipase action**; **micelles (FFA, cholesterol, vitamins A, D, E, K) → passive diffusion → re-esterified TG → chylomicrons**.
> 6. **Enterohepatic circulation: liver ⇄ terminal ileum, 6–8 times/day — conserves energy (fewer new bile salts)**.
> 7. **SCFAs (propionate, butyrate, acetate) from colonic flora: trophic, absorbed in exchange for H⁺, anti-inflammatory**.
> 8. Protein: **no enzymes in mouth; pepsin (stomach); trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase as zymogens (prevent autodigestion), activated in small intestine → di/tripeptides**.
> 9. **Endopeptidases (trypsin, chymotrypsin) — inner bonds; exopeptidases (aminopeptidase, carboxypeptidase) — end bonds**.
> 10. **Di/tripeptides in with H⁺ by PepT**, split by cytoplasmic protease, **amino acids out by basolateral transporter**.
> 11. **Fat-soluble vitamins with micelles; water-soluble (B complex) by Na⁺-dependent cotransporters — except B₁₂ and folate (Na⁺-independent)**.
> 12. Water: **9 L in, 8.8 L reabsorbed, 200 mL in stool; maximum in jejunum**.
> 13. Iron: **site duodenum**; Fe³⁺ → (Dcytb) → Fe²⁺ → **DMT-1** → **ferroportin** → **hephaestin** → transferrin; depends on **intake, stores, erythropoietic activity**; **↑ vitamin C; ↓ tannins, oxalates, phytates**.
> 14. **Hepcidin (liver) = negative regulator: binds ferroportin, links stores with absorption; HFE mutation → haemochromatosis**.

[^1]: Standard texts: α-amylase (salivary and pancreatic) splits internal **α-1,4** glycosidic bonds of starch into maltose, maltotriose and α-limit dextrins; it cannot split **α-1,6** branch points, which are cut by brush-border **isomaltase (α-dextrinase)**. Brush-border maltase, sucrase and lactase then split the disaccharides into glucose, fructose and galactose. The page's simplified scheme is kept in the body.
[^2]: The page draws GLUT-5 on both membranes. Most textbooks give **GLUT-5 for apical fructose entry and GLUT-2 for basolateral exit of glucose, galactose and fructose** (some GLUT-5 is present basolaterally too).
[^3]: SGLT-1 carries 2 Na⁺ with each glucose (or galactose) molecule; its basolateral partner, the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase, spends the ATP. Galactose shares SGLT-1 and GLUT-2 with glucose — so both are malabsorbed in CGGM, while fructose (GLUT-5) is not.
[^4]: SGLT-2 (low-affinity, high-capacity) reabsorbs ~90 % of filtered glucose in the early proximal tubule; SGLT-1 (high-affinity) handles the rest in the late proximal tubule. SGLT-2 inhibitors (gliflozins) are antidiabetic drugs.
[^5]: Inside the cell, monoglycerides and fatty acids are re-esterified in the smooth ER, combined with cholesterol esters, phospholipids and apolipoprotein B-48 into chylomicrons, which leave by exocytosis into the lacteals (lymph). Short- and medium-chain fatty acids (< 12 carbons) go directly into portal blood — the basis of MCT oil in fat malabsorption.
[^6]: Butyrate is the main energy source of colonocytes; SCFAs are made by bacterial fermentation of dietary fibre and supply ~5–10 % of daily energy. Their absorption stimulates Na⁺ and water absorption — one reason starch-based ORS works well.
[^7]: The apical peptide transporter is **PepT1** (H⁺-coupled, tertiary active — the H⁺ gradient is kept by the Na⁺–H⁺ exchanger NHE3, which depends in turn on the Na⁺/K⁺ pump). Free amino acids also enter by several Na⁺-dependent carriers. Peptide absorption is faster than free amino-acid absorption, which is why Hartnup disease (neutral amino-acid transporter defect) causes little protein malnutrition.
[^8]: B₁₂ binds intrinsic factor and is absorbed by receptor-mediated endocytosis (cubilin receptor) in the **terminal ileum**; folate uses the proton-coupled folate transporter (PCFT) in the **duodenum and upper jejunum**. Both are therefore "Na⁺-independent".
[^9]: Hepcidin, a 25-amino-acid peptide from hepatocytes, binds ferroportin and causes its internalisation and degradation. It rises with iron overload and inflammation (IL-6 — anaemia of chronic disease) and falls with iron deficiency, hypoxia and increased erythropoiesis. HFE (C282Y) mutation impairs hepcidin production → hereditary haemochromatosis.

[^ety-trypsin]: **trypsin** — Greek *tripsis*, "rubbing" + *-in* → Kühne (1876) obtained it by rubbing the pancreas with glycerine; an endopeptidase cutting after lysine and arginine.
[^ety-chymotrypsin]: **chymotrypsin** — Greek *chymos*, "juice" + *trypsin* → the trypsin-like enzyme that clots milk; an endopeptidase cutting after aromatic amino acids (Phe, Tyr, Trp).
[^ety-carboxypeptidase]: **carboxypeptidase** — *carboxyl* (–COOH: *carbon* + *oxygen* + *-yl*) + *peptide* + *-ase* → removes one amino acid at a time from the carboxyl end — an exopeptidase.
[^ety-zymogen]: **zymogen** — Greek *zymē*, "leaven" + *-gen*, "producer" → an inactive enzyme precursor that "produces" the enzyme when cleaved (trypsinogen, pepsinogen).
[^ety-endopeptidase]: **endopeptidase / exopeptidase** — Greek *endon*, "within" / *exō*, "outside" + *peptide* + *-ase* → enzymes that cut bonds inside a chain (trypsin, chymotrypsin, pepsin, elastase) or remove terminal amino acids (amino- and carboxypeptidases).
[^ety-hydrolysis]: **hydrolysis** — Greek *hydōr*, "water" + *lysis*, "loosening" → splitting a bond by adding water across it — every digestive enzyme is a hydrolase. **Differentiate:** *hydrolysis* (water added) · *dehydration synthesis / condensation* (water removed, bond made).
[^ety-amino]: **amino / aminopeptidase** — from *ammonia* (Latin *sal ammoniacus*, salt of Ammon, near whose Egyptian temple it was found) → the –NH₂ group; *aminopeptidase* removes amino acids from the NH₂ (N-) end; a brush-border exopeptidase.
[^ety-cytoplasm]: **cytoplasm / cytoplasmic** — Greek *kytos*, "cell" + *plasma*, "something formed, moulded" + *-ic* → the cell contents outside the nucleus; *cytoplasmic proteases* (peptidases) finish di/tripeptides to amino acids.
[^ety-protease]: **protease** — *prote(in)* + *-ase* → an enzyme that splits proteins/peptides (synonym: peptidase, proteinase).
[^ety-basolateral]: **basolateral** — Latin *basis*, "base" + *latus, lateris*, "side" + *-al* → the base and sides of an epithelial cell, facing the blood. **Differentiate:** *apical / luminal / brush-border* (facing the lumen) · *basolateral* (facing the interstitium and blood).
[^ety-vitamin]: **vitamin** — Latin *vita*, "life" + *amine* → "vital amine", coined by Casimir Funk (1912) because he thought all were amines (the final *e* was dropped when most proved not to be).
[^ety-cobalamin]: **vitamin B₁₂ / cobalamin** — *cobalt* (German *Kobold*, a goblin of the mines) + *vitamin* → the cobalt-containing vitamin; absorbed with intrinsic factor in the terminal ileum.
[^ety-folate]: **folate / folic acid** — Latin *folium*, "leaf" → first extracted from spinach leaves (1941); needed for DNA synthesis.
[^ety-jejunum]: **jejunum** — Latin *jejunus*, "fasting, empty" → usually found empty at autopsy because contents pass fast; maximum water (and most nutrient) absorption.
[^ety-duodenum]: **duodenum** — Latin *duodeni*, "twelve each" → twelve finger-breadths long; the site of iron (and calcium, folate) absorption.
[^ety-heme]: **heme / haem** — Greek *haima*, "blood" → the iron-porphyrin ring of haemoglobin and myoglobin; heme iron (meat) is absorbed better than non-heme iron and is not affected by tannins or phytates.
[^ety-cytochrome]: **cytochrome (duodenal cytochrome B, Dcytb)** — Greek *kytos*, "cell" + *chrōma*, "colour" → a coloured (iron-containing) cell protein; Dcytb is a ferric reductase that converts dietary Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺ for DMT-1.
[^ety-dmt1]: **DMT-1 (divalent metal transporter 1)** — Latin *di-*, "two" + *valens*, "strong" → a transporter for metal ions with two charges (Fe²⁺, also Mn²⁺, Cd²⁺, Pb²⁺); H⁺-coupled, on the apical membrane.
[^ety-ferritin]: **ferritin** — Latin *ferrum*, "iron" + *-itin*, a protein → the iron-storage protein (a shell holding up to ~4500 Fe atoms); serum ferritin reflects body iron stores.
[^ety-ferroportin]: **ferroportin** — Latin *ferrum*, "iron" + *porta*, "gate, door" + *-in* → the iron exit door on the basolateral enterocyte, macrophages and hepatocytes — the only known cellular iron exporter.
[^ety-hephaestin]: **hephaestin** — named after **Hephaestus**, the Greek god of fire and metalworking (the blacksmith of the gods) → the copper-containing ferroxidase that oxidises Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺ as iron leaves the enterocyte (ceruloplasmin does this elsewhere).
[^ety-transferrin]: **transferrin** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *ferrum*, "iron" + *-in* → the plasma protein that carries iron across the body (2 Fe³⁺ per molecule). **Differentiate:** *transferrin* (transport) · *ferritin* (storage) · *haemosiderin* (insoluble storage aggregate).
[^ety-erythroid]: **erythroid / erythropoietic** — Greek *erythros*, "red" + *-oid*, "like"; *poiēsis*, "making" → the red-cell line; erythropoietic activity of the marrow is the main consumer of iron (≈ 20–25 mg/day recycled).
[^ety-hepcidin]: **hepcidin** — *hep*(atic) + *-cidin*, "killer" (Latin *caedere*, to kill) → a liver peptide first found (2000) as an antimicrobial peptide in urine; later shown to be the master iron regulator.
[^ety-ascorbic]: **vitamin C / ascorbic acid** — Greek *a-*, "without" + Latin *scorbutus*, "scurvy" → the anti-scurvy acid (Szent-Györgyi, 1928–32); it reduces Fe³⁺ to the absorbable Fe²⁺ and chelates it in a soluble form.
[^ety-tannin]: **tannins** — Medieval Latin *tannum*, "oak bark" (used to *tan* leather) → plant polyphenols of tea and coffee that bind non-heme iron.
[^ety-oxalate]: **oxalates** — Greek *oxalis*, "sorrel" (*oxys*, sour) + *-ate* → salts of oxalic acid, first found in sorrel; abundant in spinach — bind iron and calcium.
[^ety-phytate]: **phytates** — Greek *phyton*, "plant" + *-ate* → phytic acid (inositol hexaphosphate), the phosphorus store of cereal grains and legumes; chelates iron, zinc and calcium.
[^ety-haemochromatosis]: **haemochromatosis / hemochromatosis** — Greek *haima*, "blood" + *chrōma*, "colour" + *-osis*, condition → a disease with blood-derived pigment (iron) deposited in tissues — "bronze diabetes" (von Recklinghausen named it in 1889).
[^ety-macrophage]: **macrophage** — Greek *makros*, "large" + *phagein*, "to eat" → the big eater: tissue cells that engulf old RBCs and recycle their iron through ferroportin. **Differentiate:** *macrophage* (large, tissue) · *microphage* (old name for the neutrophil) · *phagocyte* (any eating cell).
[^pre-di]: **di- / dis- / dia-** — Latin *dis-* apart (digestion, diffusion) · Greek *di-* two (disaccharide, dipeptide) · Greek *dia-* through (diarrhoea). Decide by the root's meaning.
[^pre-ab]: **ab-** — away from: absorption, abnormal, abduction. Don't confuse with **ad-** towards (adsorption) · **a- / an-** without.
[^suf-cyte]: **-cyte / cyto-** — cell: enterocyte, hepatocyte, erythrocyte, cytoplasm. Don't confuse with **-cele** swelling, hernia · **-cide** killer · **cysto-** bladder, cyst.
[^pre-poly]: **poly-** — many: polysaccharide, polypeptide, polyunsaturated. Don't confuse with **oligo-** few (oligosaccharide) · **multi-** many (Latin twin) · **pan-** all.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: amylase, lipase, lactase, sucrase, protease, esterase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (lactose, sucrose) · **-ate** a salt or ion (acetate, butyrate).
[^pre-iso]: **iso-** — equal, same: isomaltase, isomer, isotonic, isoform. Don't confuse with **homo-** same, identical (homozygous) · **hetero-** different · **iso-** vs **an-iso-** (anisocytosis — unequal).
[^pre-mono]: **mono-** — one, single: monosaccharide, monoglyceride, monocyte. Don't confuse with **uni-** one (Latin twin) · **mono-** in *mononucleosis* (one-nucleus cells) · **poly-** many.
[^suf-ose]: **-ose** — a sugar (carbohydrate): glucose, fructose, galactose, lactose, sucrose, maltose. Don't confuse with **-ase** an enzyme · **-ose / -osa** full of (adipose, mucosa).
[^pre-co]: **co- / con- / com-** — together, with: cotransporter, colipase, congenital, conjugation. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** opposite (countertransport = antiport).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: transporter, transferrin, transmembrane. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **trans** in chemistry (opposite sides — trans fats) · **per-** through.
[^pre-mal]: **mal-** — bad, faulty, inadequate: malabsorption, malnutrition, malformation. Don't confuse with **male** (sex) · **mal-** in *malleolus* (little hammer, Latin *malleus*) · **dys-** bad (Greek twin).
[^suf-rrhoea]: **-rrhoea / -rrhea** — flow, discharge: diarrhoea, steatorrhoea, rhinorrhoea. Don't confuse with **-rrhage** bursting forth of blood · **-rrhaphy** suturing · **-rrhexis** rupture.
[^pre-tri]: **tri-** — three: triglyceride, tripeptide, tricarboxylic. Don't confuse with **tri-** in *tribein* (rub — trypsin) · **di-** two · **tetra-** four.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — again, back: re-esterification, reabsorption, recessive, refractory, rehydration. Don't confuse with **retro-** behind · **pre-** before.
[^pre-chylo]: **chylo-** — chyle (milky lymph): chylomicron, chylothorax, chyluria. Don't confuse with **chymo-** chyme, juice (chymotrypsin) · **cholo- / chole-** bile.
[^suf-ule]: **-ule / -ulus / -ula** — small (diminutive): globule, tubule, venule, micelle (*-elle*). Don't confuse with **-ole** also small (arteriole) · **-ule** in *capsule* (little box — still diminutive).
[^pre-entero]: **entero-** — intestine: enterohepatic, enterocyte, enterokinase, enteritis. Don't confuse with **gastro-** stomach · **colo-** colon.
[^pre-hepato]: **hepat- / hepato-** — liver: enterohepatic, hepatocyte, hepcidin, hepatitis. Don't confuse with **hepta-** seven · **hemi-** half · **haem-** blood.
[^suf-ogen]: **-ogen / -gen** — producer, precursor: zymogen, trypsinogen, pepsinogen, glycogen. Don't confuse with **-genesis** formation (erythropoiesis is *-poiesis*) · **-genic** producing.
[^pre-auto]: **auto-** — self: autodigestion, autosomal, autoimmune. Don't confuse with **allo-** other · **iso-** equal.
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endopeptidase, endocytosis, endocrine. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside · **ecto-** outer · **entero-** intestine.
[^pre-hydro]: **hydro- / hydr-** — water: hydrolysis, carbohydrate, rehydration, achlorhydria. Don't confuse with **hydroxy-** an –OH group (dehydroxylation) · **hygro-** moisture.
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis** — loosening, breaking down, dissolving: hydrolysis, lipolysis, haemolysis, glycolysis. Don't confuse with **-lytic** (adjective: haemolytic) · **-lyte** a dissolved substance (electrolyte) · **-stasis** standing still.
[^pre-exo]: **exo-** — outside: exopeptidase, exocytosis, exocrine. Don't confuse with **ex-** out of (excretion) · **ecto-** outer layer · **extra-** beyond.
[^pre-cyto]: **cyto-** — cell: cytoplasmic, cytochrome, cytokine. Don't confuse with **cysto-** bladder · **-cyte** (the suffix form: enterocyte).
[^pre-baso]: **baso-** — base: basolateral, basement membrane; also "basic dye" in *basophil* (base-loving). Don't confuse with **baro-** pressure · **bas-** in *basilic* (royal — basilic vein).
[^pre-erythro]: **erythro-** — red: erythroid, erythrocyte, erythropoiesis, erythromycin. Don't confuse with **erythema** (redness of skin — same root) · **eryth-** vs **leuko-** white · **cyano-** blue.
[^suf-poiesis]: **-poiesis / -poietic** — making, production: erythropoietic, haematopoiesis, erythropoietin. Don't confuse with **-plasia** formation of cells/tissue (hyperplasia) · **-genesis** origin · **-penia** deficiency (leukopenia).
[^pre-haemo]: **haem- / haemo- / hem-** — blood: haemochromatosis, heme, haemoglobin, haemorrhage. Don't confuse with **hemi-** half · **hepat-** liver · **-aemia** (blood condition as a suffix).
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a condition or disease state, often non-inflammatory: haemochromatosis, fibrosis, cirrhosis. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation · **-oma** tumour · **-ose** sugar.
[^pre-macro]: **macro-** — large: macrophage, macrocytic, macronutrient. Don't confuse with **micro-** small (microvilli, chylomicron) · **mega-** very large (megaloblastic).
[^suf-phage]: **-phage / -phagia / phago-** — eating: macrophage, phagocyte, dysphagia, oesophagus (food-carrier). Don't confuse with **-phasia** speech (aphasia) · **-phagia** is the same eating root used for swallowing (dysphagia) · **phago-** vs **phaco-** lens.
