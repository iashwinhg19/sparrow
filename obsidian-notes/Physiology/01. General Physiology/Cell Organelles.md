# 🏭 Cell Organelles

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> **Organelles**[^ety-organelle][^suf-elle] are the membrane-bound "little organs" of the cell, each with one job: the **ER** makes and checks proteins and lipids, the **Golgi** labels and dispatches them, **lysosomes** destroy, **peroxisomes** burn very-long-chain fats and neutralise H₂O₂, **mitochondria** make ATP, and the **nucleus** holds the instructions. For every organelle the exam asks three things:
> - ✅ **What does it do?** (function)
> - ✅ **What disease follows when it fails?** (I-cell, Chédiak–Higashi, Zellweger, Refsum, ALD, MELAS, prion and Alzheimer's disease)
> - ✅ **Which enzyme identifies it?** (marker enzymes)

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — The Cell as a Factory

Run the cell like a factory: head office issues blueprints, an assembly line builds and inspects the product, a dispatch unit packs and addresses it, and there is a recycling bin, a hazardous-waste unit and a power plant.

```
NUCLEUS        = HEAD OFFICE      → DNA + histones = chromatin · nuclear pores (importins in, exportins out)
ROUGH ER       = ASSEMBLY LINE    → ribosomes translate · chaperones fold · quality control → ERAD → ubiquitin → proteasome
SMOOTH ER      = CHEMICAL PLANT   → steroids (adrenal cortex, testis, ovary) · Ca2+ store (SR) · detox (liver CYP450)
GOLGI          = PACKING & POST   → cis (receiving) → glycosylation → trans (releasing) · M6P "address label" → lysosome
LYSOSOME       = RECYCLE BIN      → acid destruction · autophagy in starvation · LYST defect = Chediak-Higashi
PEROXISOME     = HAZARD UNIT      → VLCFA oxidation → H2O2 → catalase · plasmalogens · Zellweger / Refsum / ALD
MITOCHONDRION  = POWER PLANT      → cristae → oxidative phosphorylation → ATP · own maternal DNA · MELAS
```

### ROUGH ENDOPLASMIC RETICULUM

**Functions:**
1. **Protein synthesis / translation**[^ety-translation][^pre-trans][^suf-ation].[^1]
2. **Folding of proteins** — using **chaperones**[^ety-chaperone] / **heat shock proteins (HSP)**.[^2]
3. **Degradation**[^ety-degradation][^pre-de] **of misfolded proteins & quality control of proteins** by the **unfolded protein response** or **ER stress response**.[^3]

**When misfolded proteins pile up** — the ER defends itself:

```
↑↑ misfolded proteins → ER → 1. Halts protein synthesis
                             2. Endoplasmic reticulum associated degradation (ERAD)
```

#### ERAD

🧠 A misfolded protein is first branded with **ubiquitin**[^ety-ubiquitin] — the "kiss of death" — and then fed into the **proteasome**[^ety-proteasome], a barrel-shaped protein shredder, the "graveyard of the cell".[^4]

```
Misfolded proteins are tagged to UBIQUITIN (kiss of death)
                 ↓
      Protein–ubiquitin complex
                 ↓
Moves to PROTEASOME (graveyard of the cell)
                 ↓
            Degradation
```

**Applied aspect: misfolded protein diseases**
1. **Alzheimer's disease**[^ety-alzheimer] — **more common**.
   - Due to **accumulation of misfolded Aβ amyloid**[^ety-amyloid][^suf-oid] **protein**.[^5]
2. **Prion**[^ety-prion] **disease:**
   - **PrPc** (normal protein in CNS) → **PrPsc** (**prion protein: misfolded**).
   - **Eg: Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease**[^ety-cjd] (**very fatal**).[^6]

## 📦 Golgi Apparatus

- **Location:** close to the RER.

**Functions:**

**1. Post-translational**[^pre-post] **modifications:**

```
Proteins synthesised by RER
          ↓
Cis end of Golgi apparatus (GA)
          ↓
GLYCOSYLATION (carbohydrate side chain is added to the protein)
          ↓
Glycoprotein (↑ stability of protein)
          ↓
Stored in vesicles
          ↓  stimulus
Released from trans end
```

🧠 The **Golgi**[^ety-golgi] works like a post office: proteins arrive at the **cis**[^ety-cis-trans] face (receiving end), get a sugar "stamp" by **glycosylation**[^ety-glycosylation][^pre-glyco] on the way through, and leave from the **trans** face (releasing end) in **vesicles**[^ety-vesicle] that wait for a stimulus to be **secreted**[^ety-secretory].[^10]

![[Cell Organelles - Golgi apparatus cis and trans ends.png]]
*Golgi apparatus — vesicles from the rough ER reach the cis end (receiving end), pass through the stacked cisternae and bud from the trans end (releasing end) to the cell membrane for exocytosis (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- **GA are abundant in secretory cells.**
- **Eg:** **antibody-secreting plasma cells**; **goblet**[^ety-goblet] **cells of the GI tract**.

**2. Sorting of proteins:**
- **GA determines where a protein is delivered.**
- **Eg:**

```
Protein ──(phosphotransferase)──► Protein + mannose-6-phosphate
                                          ↓
                                  Moves to lysosome
                                          ↓
                                      Degraded
```
The enzyme is **phospho**transferase[^ety-phosphotransferase] and the address label is **mannose**[^ety-mannose]-6-phosphate (M6P).[^11]

**Applied aspect — I cell disease**[^ety-i-cell]**:**

```
Absence of phosphotransferase
          ↓
Mannose-6-phosphate not added
          ↓
Lysosomal function affected
          ↓
Proteins not degraded
          ↓
INCLUSION BODIES accumulate in cell
```
🧠 Without the M6P "address", the lysosome's own enzymes are never delivered to it — they leak out of the cell — so the lysosome is empty-handed and undigested material piles up as **inclusion**[^ety-inclusion] **bodies**.

## ♻️ Lysosomes

- **Recycle bin of the cell.** **Lysosomes**[^ety-lysosome] break down worn-out organelles, engulfed bacteria and debris.

**Functions:**
- **Acid-mediated destruction:**[^12]
  - **H⁺ATPase**[^suf-ase] **acid hydrolase**[^ety-hydrolase][^pre-hydro].
  - **Acid hydroxylase enzyme.**
- **Note:** acid in the lysosome **can destroy itself** → **suicidal bags / residual bodies**.[^13]

![[Cell Organelles - lysosome phagocytosis.png]]
*Phagocytosis[^ety-phagocytosis][^suf-cytosis] — engulfed particles (dark bodies) are carried inward and fused with a lysosome, whose acid enzymes digest them (cropped from the handwritten page).*

### Autophagy[^ety-autophagy][^pre-auto][^suf-phagy]

- **Destruction of the cell's own part.**

```
Late stages of starvation
          ↓
Lysosomes engulf mitochondria
          ↓
Proteins released from mitochondria
          ↓
Undergo metabolism
          ↓
Energy for survival
```
🧠 In **starvation**[^ety-starvation] the cell eats part of itself — the proteins of its own mitochondria are broken down and their amino acids **metabolised**[^ety-metabolism][^pre-meta] for energy.[^14]

### Clinical application

- **Neutrophil**[^ety-neutrophil][^suf-phil] — **high content of lysosomes**.

```
                Normal
CHS1 gene ─────────────────► LYST (lysosomal transport protein)
    │
    ↓ mutation
CHEDIAK HIGASHI SYNDROME
    ↓
Patient is more prone to bacterial infections
```
**Chediak–Higashi syndrome**[^ety-chediak-higashi] — the neutrophil's lysosomes cannot be moved to and fused with the phagosome, so bacteria are swallowed but not killed.[^15]

## 🫧 Peroxisomes

![[Cell Organelles - peroxisome crystalline core.png]]
*Peroxisomes / microbodies — a single lipid bilayer (plasma membrane) around a granular matrix with a dense crystalline core that is rich in proteins (enzymes) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

🧠 **Peroxisomes**[^ety-peroxisome][^pre-per] are named for the hydrogen **per**oxide (H₂O₂) they both make and destroy; the **crystalline**[^ety-crystalline] core is packed enzyme.[^16]

**Function:**
1. **Oxidation**[^ety-oxidation] **of very long chain fatty acids (VLCFAs).**
   - **Generates H₂O₂** (free radical).[^17]
2. **Degradation of H₂O₂ — by catalase**[^ety-catalase].
   - **Pseudocatalase**[^pre-pseudo] — commercial / drug form of catalase.
   - **Used to treat free-radical-mediated disorders. Eg: vitiligo**[^ety-vitiligo].[^18]

**Enzymes produced by peroxisomes:**[^19]
1. **Plasmalogens**[^ety-plasmalogen] — **abundant in the myelin sheath of nerve fibres**.
2. **Luciferase**[^ety-luciferase] — **responsible for the glow in fireflies**.

### Peroxisomal storage disorders

**Zellweger syndrome**[^ety-zellweger]**:**
- **PEX gene mutation.**
- **PEX codes for peroxins** (proteins for peroxisome synthesis).
- **Features:**
  - Accumulation of **VLCFAs** } → **neurological impairment**
  - ↓ **plasmalogens** }[^20]

**Refsum's disease**[^ety-refsum]**:**
- **PAHX gene mutation.**
- **Defect in phytanyl CoA α hydroxylase.**
- **Defective oxidation of phytanic**[^ety-phytanic] **acid.**[^21]

**Adrenoleukodystrophy**[^ety-adrenoleukodystrophy][^pre-leuko][^pre-dys][^suf-trophy]**:**
- **Defect in transport proteins.**
- **↑↑ VLCFAs → neurological impairment.**[^22]

> [!tip] One-glance comparison (built from the three lists above)
> | Disorder | Gene / defect | What accumulates | Result |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Zellweger | **PEX** → no peroxins → no peroxisomes | VLCFA ↑, plasmalogens ↓ | Neurological impairment |
> | Refsum | **PAHX** → phytanyl-CoA α-hydroxylase | Phytanic acid | (neurological — see footnote) |
> | Adrenoleukodystrophy | **Transport protein** | VLCFA ↑↑ | Neurological impairment |

## ⚡ Mitochondria

![[Cell Organelles - mitochondrion structure.png]]
*Mitochondria / powerhouse of the cell — intermembranous space, inner membrane, outer membrane and cristae; the cristae contain the enzymes for oxidative phosphorylation → ATP synthesis (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- **Mitochondria**[^ety-mitochondria] = **powerhouse of the cell**.
- **Cristae**[^ety-cristae] — contain enzymes for **oxidative phosphorylation**[^ety-phosphorylation] → **ATP synthesis**.

### Mitochondrial DNA

- Mitochondria has its **own DNA** (**endosymbiotic**[^ety-endosymbiotic][^pre-syn] **theory**).
- **Source: ovum → inheritance is always maternal**[^ety-maternal].
- **ds DNA with 16 500 base pairs.**[^23]
- **10 times** more prone to **mutation**[^ety-mutation] than nuclear DNA.
  - Repair of mutations in mitochondrial DNA is **ineffective**.[^24]
- **Cannot function without nuclear DNA** (to produce enzymes for ATP synthesis).

### Mitochondrial disorders

- **Affect organs with high metabolic requirements.**
- **Features:**
  - **Skeletal muscles** — contraction & relaxation affected.
  - **CNS** — stroke-like symptoms.
  - **GI tract** is affected.
  - ↑ **susceptibility to infection**.
  - **Lactic acidosis**[^ety-lactic-acidosis][^suf-osis] is common.

> [!tip] Mnemonic: **MELAS**[^25]
> - **M**itochondrial **E**ncephalopathy[^ety-encephalopathy][^pre-en][^suf-pathy]
> - **L**actic **A**cidosis
> - **S**troke (-like episodes)

![[Cell Organelles - nucleus structure.png]]
*Nucleus — nuclear envelope with nuclear pores (continuous with the endoplasmic reticulum), nucleoplasm, chromatin and the nucleolus, which contains RNA & ribosomes (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- **Nucleolus** — contains **RNA & ribosomes**.[^26]

**Features:**
- **Controls all cellular functions.**
- **Contains DNA (chromosomes**[^ety-chromosome]**).**
  - **DNA + histones**[^ety-histone] **→ chromatin**[^ety-chromatin].
  - **Structural repeating unit of chromatin: nucleosome**[^ety-nucleosome].[^27]
- **Entry / exit of substances into the nucleus** is regulated by the **nuclear pore complex (NPC)**.

```
              ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
          Exportins                       Importins
              ↓                               ↓
  move substances OUT of nucleus    move substances INTO nucleus
```
**Exportins** and **importins**[^ety-importin] are the two families of transport receptors of the **nucleus**[^ety-nucleus].

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Each organelle is a compartment with **one job and one failure mode**, and the diseases follow the pathway of a protein through the cell. A protein is **translated** on the RER, **folded** by chaperones, **checked** (misfolded → ubiquitin → proteasome; failure → Alzheimer's and prion disease), **labelled** in the Golgi (glycosylation; M6P address → failure → I-cell disease), **delivered** to the lysosome (failure of transport → Chédiak–Higashi). Lipids run a parallel line: SER makes steroids and detoxifies drugs, peroxisomes shorten VLCFAs and make plasmalogens (failure → Zellweger, Refsum, ALD), mitochondria burn fuel to ATP (failure → MELAS with lactic acidosis).
>
> So when a question names a disease, ask: *which compartment and which step of the protein/lipid pathway broke?* — the features (inclusion bodies, infections, neurological impairment, lactic acidosis) follow from what piles up or what runs short.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Proteins are made and folded in the RER, quality-checked by the unfolded protein response, glycosylated and sorted in the Golgi; lysosomes recycle; peroxisomes oxidise VLCFAs and remove H₂O₂ with catalase; mitochondria (own maternal DNA plus nuclear-coded enzymes) supply ATP through oxidative phosphorylation.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Stress is absorbed by the organelle's own defences: misfolded proteins trigger the ER stress response (halt synthesis, ERAD); in starvation, autophagy feeds the cell from its own mitochondria; in a mitochondrial mutation the normal copies of mtDNA (heteroplasmy) still carry the load, so symptoms appear only in high-demand tissues during stress.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> The defence is overwhelmed or absent: misfolded Aβ amyloid or PrPsc accumulates (dementia, rapidly fatal CJD); without phosphotransferase, inclusion bodies fill the cells (I-cell disease); LYST loss leaves neutrophils unable to kill (recurrent bacterial infections); VLCFAs build up in the brain (Zellweger, ALD); mitochondrial failure produces encephalopathy, stroke-like episodes, myopathy and lactic acidosis (MELAS).
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal organelle → genetic defect or overload → organelle defences (UPR, ERAD, autophagy, heteroplasmy) → defences fail → accumulation (inclusions, VLCFA, amyloid, lactate) → organ disease (brain, muscle, immune)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> The organelle tells you where to look and what to measure: plasma VLCFA for peroxisomal disease, serum lysosomal enzymes for I-cell disease, giant granules on the blood film for Chédiak–Higashi, lactate and muscle biopsy (ragged red fibres) for mitochondrial disease, and CYP450 interactions whenever two drugs share the liver's SER.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **ERAD / proteasome:** "kiss of death", "graveyard of the cell", "ubiquitin tag" → misfolded proteins degraded after leaving the ER.
>
> **Misfolded protein diseases:** "Aβ amyloid" → Alzheimer's disease (more common); "PrPc → PrPsc", "very fatal", "rapid dementia" → prion disease / Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease.
>
> **I-cell disease:** "absent phosphotransferase", "mannose-6-phosphate not added", "inclusion bodies" → Golgi sorting failure.
>
> **Chediak–Higashi syndrome:** "CHS1 / LYST", "neutrophils", "prone to bacterial infections" → lysosomal transport defect.
>
> **Zellweger:** "PEX", "peroxins", "VLCFA ↑ plasmalogens ↓" → absent peroxisomes. **Refsum:** "phytanic acid", "PAHX". **Adrenoleukodystrophy:** "transport protein defect", "↑↑ VLCFA".
>
> **Mitochondrial disease:** "maternal inheritance", "high metabolic organs", "stroke-like episodes", "lactic acidosis", "MELAS".
>
> **SER:** "cytochrome P450", "xenobiosis", "sarcoplasmic reticulum stores Ca²⁺", "steroid hormones".
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Ribosome-studded flattened sacs = RER; tubular network = SER; stacked curved cisternae with a cis and a trans face = Golgi; a single membrane around a crystalline core = peroxisome; double membrane with folded cristae = mitochondrion; double envelope with pores and a dark nucleolus = nucleus.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> mtDNA ≈ 16 500 bp and 10× the nuclear mutation rate; CJD is "very fatal"; the lower the fraction of normal mtDNA (heteroplasmy), the earlier and more severe the disease.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Mixing up the cis and trans faces of the Golgi**
>
> **What students think:** Proteins leave from the cis face.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The **cis** face (Latin *cis*, "this side") faces the RER and **receives**; the **trans** face ("far side") faces the cell membrane and **releases** vesicles. Glycosylation happens on the way through.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Cis = Coming in; Trans = Transport out."
>
> **Trap 2: Calling I-cell disease a problem of the lysosome's enzymes**
>
> **What students think:** In I-cell disease the lysosomal enzymes are absent.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The enzymes are made normally — the **Golgi** fails to add the mannose-6-phosphate address (absent phosphotransferase), so they are secreted out of the cell instead of reaching the lysosome. The lysosome is starved of enzymes and inclusion bodies build up; the plasma has *high* lysosomal enzyme levels.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "I-cell = a Golgi (post-office) error, not a factory error."
>
> **Trap 3: Thinking mitochondria are self-sufficient**
>
> **What students think:** Because mitochondria have their own DNA, they make all their own proteins.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** mtDNA codes for only a handful of respiratory-chain proteins plus its own tRNAs and rRNAs; most mitochondrial proteins (including many enzymes for ATP synthesis) are coded by **nuclear DNA**, made in the cytosol and imported. That is why mitochondria "cannot function without nuclear DNA" — and why some mitochondrial diseases are inherited in a Mendelian, not maternal, pattern.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Maternal inheritance only for mtDNA mutations; nuclear genes follow Mendel."
>
> **Trap 4: Confusing lysosomes and peroxisomes**
>
> **What students think:** Both are "digestive bags", so their enzymes and diseases overlap.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Lysosomes digest with **acid hydrolases** (marker: **acid phosphatase**) and fail in storage diseases such as I-cell and Chédiak–Higashi. Peroxisomes **oxidise** VLCFAs, make and destroy **H₂O₂** (marker: **catalase**, urate oxidase) and make plasmalogens; they fail in Zellweger, Refsum and ALD — with **neurological** impairment because myelin needs plasmalogens and is poisoned by VLCFAs.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Lyso = acid + hydrolysis; Peroxi = oxygen + H₂O₂ + VLCFA."
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing the marker enzymes of the ER, Golgi and lysosome**
>
> **What students think:** Glucose-6-phosphatase marks the Golgi or the lysosome.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Each fraction has its own: **ER — glucose-6-phosphatase**, **Golgi — galactosyl transferase**, **lysosome — acid phosphatase**, **peroxisome — catalase**, **mitochondria — ATP synthase**, **membrane — Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase / adenylyl cyclase**, **nucleus — RNA polymerase / histone deacetylase**.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Follow the protein's path — ER (glucose) → Golgi (galactose sugar added) → lysosome (acid) — and the sugar enzymes line up in order.

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Cellular Physiology]]**
> The membrane that wraps every organelle — and the lipids the ER makes (lecithin, cardiolipin of the inner mitochondrial membrane) — are introduced there.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Adrenal Gland]]**
> The adrenal cortex is the showcase of SER steroid synthesis (aldosterone, cortisol); adrenoleukodystrophy also destroys the adrenal cortex, giving adrenal insufficiency.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Skeletal Muscles]]**
> The sarcoplasmic reticulum — specialised SER — stores and releases the Ca²⁺ that triggers contraction; mitochondrial disease weakens the same muscles.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Characteristics of Nerve Fibres]]**
> Myelin is rich in peroxisome-made plasmalogens, which is why Zellweger syndrome and adrenoleukodystrophy present with neurological impairment.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Cytoskeletal Filaments and Cellular Junctions]]**
> Microtubules and their motors (kinesin, dynein) carry vesicles from the Golgi and move lysosomes; the LYST defect of Chédiak–Higashi is a failure of this lysosomal traffic.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Exercise Physiology]]**
> Mitochondria and creatine kinase (their marker enzyme) supply ATP to working muscle; lactic acidosis appears when oxidative phosphorylation cannot keep up.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Drug interactions (CYP450 inducers and inhibitors), neonatal neurological syndromes (Zellweger), recurrent pyogenic infections in a child with partial albinism (Chédiak–Higashi), stroke-like episodes in a young patient with lactic acidosis (MELAS) and rapidly progressive dementia (CJD) are all organelle diseases met at the bedside.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Expect matches — organelle ↔ function, organelle ↔ marker enzyme, disease ↔ gene (PEX, PAHX, CHS1/LYST, PIGA in the previous chapter) — plus the numbers of mtDNA (16 500 bp, 10× mutation rate) and the ubiquitin/proteasome nicknames.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **RER** = granular (ribosomes); **SER** = agranular (no ribosomes).
> 2. RER functions: **translation**, **folding by chaperones/HSP**, **quality control** (unfolded protein response / ER stress response) → **ERAD**.
> 3. ERAD: misfolded protein + **ubiquitin (kiss of death)** → **proteasome (graveyard of the cell)** → degradation.
> 4. Misfolded protein diseases: **Alzheimer's (Aβ amyloid, more common)**; **prion disease** (PrPc → PrPsc) — **Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease, very fatal**.
> 5. SER: **steroid hormones** (adrenal cortex — aldosterone, cortisol; testis — testosterone; ovary — estrogen), **Ca²⁺ storage** (sarcoplasmic reticulum), **drug detoxification** (liver SER, **cytochrome P450**).
> 6. Golgi: close to RER; **cis = receiving end**, **trans = releasing end**; **glycosylation** → glycoprotein (↑ stability); abundant in **plasma cells** and **goblet cells**.
> 7. Sorting: **phosphotransferase** adds **mannose-6-phosphate** → protein to lysosome; absent → **I-cell disease** (inclusion bodies).
> 8. Lysosome = **recycle bin**; acid-mediated destruction; **suicidal bags**; **autophagy** in late starvation (eats mitochondria for energy).
> 9. **Neutrophils** have the most lysosomes; **CHS1 → LYST** mutation → **Chédiak–Higashi** → bacterial infections.
> 10. Peroxisome: **VLCFA oxidation → H₂O₂**, broken down by **catalase** (pseudocatalase for **vitiligo**); makes **plasmalogens** (myelin) and **luciferase** (fireflies).
> 11. **Zellweger** (PEX/peroxins; VLCFA ↑, plasmalogen ↓), **Refsum** (PAHX; phytanic acid), **ALD** (transport protein; ↑↑ VLCFA) → **neurological impairment**.
> 12. Mitochondria: **own ds DNA (16 500 bp)**, **endosymbiotic**, **maternal inheritance**, **10× mutation rate**, need nuclear DNA; disorders hit high-metabolic organs — **MELAS**.
> 13. Nucleus: DNA + histones = **chromatin**; repeating unit = **nucleosome**; **NPC** with NLS; **importins in, exportins out**. Markers: membrane **Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase/adenylyl cyclase**, ER **G-6-Pase**, Golgi **galactosyl transferase**, lysosome **acid phosphatase**, peroxisome **catalase/urate oxidase**, mitochondria **ATP synthase/CK**, nucleus **RNA polymerase/HDAC**.

[^1]: Ribosomes bound to the RER make proteins that are secreted, built into membranes or sent to lysosomes (a signal sequence steers them into the ER lumen); free ribosomes in the cytosol make cytosolic, nuclear, mitochondrial and peroxisomal proteins. *Translation* (mRNA → protein, at the ribosome) follows *transcription* (DNA → mRNA, in the nucleus).
[^2]: Chaperones such as HSP70 and HSP90 (and calnexin/calreticulin in the ER) hold a new chain so it folds correctly instead of clumping. They were first noticed as proteins switched on by heat stress, hence "heat shock proteins".
[^3]: The unfolded protein response is run by three ER sensors (IRE1, PERK, ATF6): PERK phosphorylates eIF2α to **halt translation**, and IRE1/ATF6 switch on more chaperones and ERAD machinery. If the stress does not settle, the same pathway triggers apoptosis.
[^4]: Ubiquitin is a 76-amino-acid protein; a chain of four or more ubiquitins is the signal read by the 26S proteasome. The ubiquitin–proteasome system won the 2004 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (Ciechanover, Hershko, Rose).
[^5]: Alzheimer's disease has two lesions: extracellular **amyloid (Aβ) plaques**, from cleavage of amyloid precursor protein, and intracellular **neurofibrillary tangles** of hyperphosphorylated tau. It is the commonest cause of dementia.
[^6]: The misfolded prion protein (PrP^Sc, "scrapie" form, rich in β-sheet) converts normal PrP^C into more of itself and resists proteases. CJD presents as rapidly progressive dementia with myoclonus and periodic sharp-wave complexes on EEG; most patients die within a year. Stanley Prusiner received the 1997 Nobel Prize for prions.
[^7]: Steroidogenesis shuttles between two organelles: the first step (cholesterol side-chain cleavage to pregnenolone) and some later hydroxylations happen in **mitochondria**, the others in the **SER**. Steroid-secreting cells therefore show abundant SER, mitochondria with tubular cristae and lipid droplets.
[^8]: The sarcoplasmic reticulum releases Ca²⁺ through ryanodine receptors (RyR1) when the muscle is excited and pumps it back with SERCA (a Ca²⁺-ATPase) for relaxation — see [[Skeletal Muscles]].
[^9]: CYP450 enzymes carry out phase I (oxidation) reactions of drug metabolism. Inducers (rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine) speed up and inhibitors (ketoconazole, erythromycin, cimetidine) slow down the clearance of other drugs — the basis of many drug interactions.
[^10]: N-linked glycosylation (sugar on asparagine) **begins in the ER** and is trimmed and completed in the Golgi; O-linked glycosylation (on serine/threonine) occurs in the Golgi. The page's flow shows the Golgi part.
[^11]: The enzyme is **N-acetylglucosamine-1-phosphotransferase**; mannose-6-phosphate receptors in the trans-Golgi then bind the tagged enzymes and send them to the lysosome.
[^12]: A V-type H⁺-ATPase in the lysosomal membrane pumps protons in and keeps the lumen at pH about 4.5–5; the 50-plus digestive enzymes are **acid hydrolases** (proteases, lipases, nucleases, glycosidases, phosphatases) that work only at that pH. The page's "acid hydroxylase" is read here as acid **hydrolase**; hydroxylases are a different (oxidising) enzyme family.
[^13]: Christian de Duve called lysosomes "suicide bags" because their enzymes would digest the cell if the membrane burst. **Residual bodies** are lysosomes left full of material they cannot digest — such as the brown "wear-and-tear" pigment lipofuscin in ageing cells.
[^14]: The autophagosome (a double-membrane sac around the organelle) fuses with a lysosome to digest it; selective autophagy of mitochondria is called **mitophagy**. Yoshinori Ohsumi received the 2016 Nobel Prize for autophagy.
[^15]: Chédiak–Higashi syndrome is autosomal recessive (LYST = lysosomal trafficking regulator, also called CHS1). Neutrophils show giant fused granules and cannot fuse lysosomes with phagosomes; patients also have partial oculocutaneous albinism (melanosomes are lysosome-like), peripheral neuropathy and a bleeding tendency.
[^16]: In many animals the crystalline core is the enzyme urate oxidase; human peroxisomes lack a typical core because humans lost functional urate oxidase. Peroxisomes have a **single** membrane, unlike the double membrane of mitochondria.
[^17]: Peroxisomes shorten VLCFAs (≥ 22 carbons) by β-oxidation and hand the shorter chains to mitochondria. Their first oxidase passes electrons straight to O₂, which makes H₂O₂. Strictly, H₂O₂ is a reactive oxygen species rather than a free radical (it has no unpaired electron), but it readily forms the hydroxyl radical.
[^18]: H₂O₂ accumulates in the epidermis of vitiligo patches; topical pseudocatalase with narrow-band UVB has been tried to remove it. The evidence for benefit is limited.
[^19]: Strictly, plasmalogens are **ether phospholipids** whose synthesis begins in peroxisomes — products, not enzymes; luciferase is an enzyme imported into the peroxisomes of firefly light organs. The page lists both under "enzymes produced by peroxisomes".
[^20]: Zellweger syndrome (cerebro-hepato-renal syndrome) is the most severe peroxisome biogenesis disorder: profound hypotonia, seizures, characteristic facies, hepatomegaly and renal cysts, with death usually in infancy.
[^21]: The gene is **PHYH** (formerly PAHX), coding phytanoyl-CoA hydroxylase, the first enzyme of α-oxidation of phytanic acid (from dairy products and ruminant fat). Classic features: retinitis pigmentosa, peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar ataxia and ichthyosis; treatment is dietary restriction of phytanic acid. The page reads "phytanyl CoA α hydroxylase".
[^22]: X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy is caused by mutations of **ABCD1**, which codes ALDP, the peroxisomal membrane transporter that carries VLCFA-CoA into the peroxisome. VLCFAs build up in the white matter (demyelination) and the adrenal cortex (adrenal insufficiency) — hence *adreno-leuko-dystrophy*.
[^23]: Human mtDNA is a closed circle of **16 569 bp** coding 37 genes: 13 proteins of the respiratory chain/ATP synthase, 22 tRNAs and 2 rRNAs. Sperm mitochondria are destroyed after fertilisation, so mtDNA comes from the ovum. A cell carries hundreds of copies, often a mixture of normal and mutant (heteroplasmy).
[^24]: mtDNA sits next to the electron transport chain, which leaks reactive oxygen species; it has no protective histones and fewer repair pathways — the reasons for its higher mutation rate.
[^25]: MELAS is most often caused by the m.3243A>G mutation in the mitochondrial tRNA-Leu gene. Other classic mitochondrial syndromes: MERRF (myoclonic epilepsy with ragged red fibres), LHON (Leber hereditary optic neuropathy) and Kearns–Sayre syndrome.
[^26]: The nucleolus has no membrane; it is where ribosomal RNA is transcribed and the two ribosomal subunits are assembled before export through the nuclear pores.
[^27]: A nucleosome is about 147 bp of DNA wrapped 1.65 turns around an octamer of histones (two each of H2A, H2B, H3, H4), joined by linker DNA with histone H1 — the "beads on a string" of electron micrographs.
[^28]: An NLS is a short run of basic amino acids (lysine, arginine). Small molecules (below ~40 kDa) diffuse through the pore freely; larger cargo needs importins/exportins, whose direction is set by the Ran-GTP gradient across the envelope.
[^29]: Other markers quoted in textbooks: plasma membrane — 5′-nucleotidase; mitochondria — succinate dehydrogenase or cytochrome oxidase (inner membrane), glutamate dehydrogenase (matrix), monoamine oxidase (outer membrane); nucleus — DNA polymerase; cytosol — lactate dehydrogenase. Mitochondrial creatine kinase sits in the intermembranous space.
[^30]: Urate oxidase (uricase) is a marker of peroxisomes in animal tissues such as rat liver; humans and apes lost the functional gene, which is why our uric acid levels are high enough to cause gout.

[^ety-endoplasmic-reticulum]: **endoplasmic reticulum** — Greek *endon*, "within" + *plasma*, "formed substance" → "within the cytoplasm"; Latin *reticulum*, "little net" (diminutive of *rete*, net) → a little net within the cytoplasm, named by Keith Porter in the early 1950s from electron micrographs. **Differentiate:** *endoplasmic reticulum* (any cell) · *sarcoplasmic reticulum* (muscle SER) · *reticular* fibres (connective tissue) · *reticulocyte* (young RBC with a net of RNA).
[^ety-granular]: **granular / agranular** — Latin *granulum*, "small grain" (*granum*, grain) → grainy; RER looks grainy because of its ribosomes, SER is *a-granular*, "without grains".
[^ety-ribosome]: **ribosome** — *ribo-* from ribonucleic acid (RNA; *ribose* is the sugar, a rearrangement of *arabinose*) + Greek *sōma*, "body" → the RNA-body; name proposed by Richard Roberts in 1958 for the "Palade granules" described by George Palade.
[^ety-translation]: **translation** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *latus*, "carried" + *-ion*, the process → carrying across from one language (nucleotide code) into another (amino-acid sequence). **Differentiate:** *replication* (DNA → DNA) · *transcription* (DNA → mRNA, "writing across" in the same language) · *translation* (mRNA → protein) · *post-translational modification* (changes after the chain is made — glycosylation).
[^ety-chaperone]: **chaperone** — French *chaperon*, "hood", then the older lady who escorted a young woman in society → a protein that escorts a new chain and prevents improper "liaisons" (misfolding); the molecular use dates from Ron Laskey (1978).
[^ety-degradation]: **degradation** — Latin *de-*, "down" + *gradus*, "step" + *-ation* → stepping down; breaking a molecule into smaller pieces. **Differentiate:** *degradation* (breakdown of molecules) · *degeneration* (*genus*, kind — a tissue falling from its kind) · *digestion* (*di-* + *gerere*, to carry apart — breakdown of food).
[^ety-ubiquitin]: **ubiquitin** — Latin *ubique*, "everywhere" + *-in* → the small protein found in every eukaryotic cell (Gideon Goldstein, 1975); attached as a chain, it marks proteins for destruction — the "kiss of death".
[^ety-proteasome]: **proteasome** — *prote(ase)* + Greek *sōma*, "body" → a protease-body, the barrel-shaped complex (named in the late 1980s) that unfolds and chops ubiquitin-tagged proteins — the "graveyard of the cell".
[^ety-alzheimer]: **Alzheimer's disease** — Alois Alzheimer (1864–1915), German psychiatrist and neuropathologist, who in 1906 described plaques and tangles in the brain of his patient Auguste Deter.
[^ety-amyloid]: **amyloid** — Greek *amylon*, "starch" + *-oid*, "like" → starch-like; Rudolf Virchow (1854) thought the deposits were starch because they stained with iodine — in fact they are misfolded proteins in β-sheets. **Differentiate:** *amyloid* (protein) · *amylase* (enzyme that digests starch) · *amylopectin* (a starch).
[^ety-prion]: **prion** — coined by Stanley Prusiner (1982) from **pr**oteinaceous **in**fectious particle (rearranged to *pr-i-on*) → an infectious agent made only of misfolded protein, with no nucleic acid. **Differentiate:** *prion* (protein only) · *virus* (nucleic acid + protein) · *viroid* (naked RNA).
[^ety-cjd]: **Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease** — Hans Gerhard Creutzfeldt (1885–1964, 1920) and Alfons Maria Jakob (1884–1931, 1921), German neurologists who described the rapidly fatal spongiform dementia now known to be a prion disease.
[^ety-steroid]: **steroid** — *ster(ol)* (Greek *stereos*, "solid" — cholesterol was a solid alcohol) + *-oid*, "like" → sterol-like; molecules built on the four-ring cholesterol skeleton (cortisol, aldosterone, sex hormones, vitamin D).
[^ety-hormone]: **hormone** — Greek *hormaein*, "to set in motion, urge on" → a chemical messenger carried by the blood that sets a distant organ in motion; term introduced by Ernest Starling in 1905.
[^ety-adrenal]: **adrenal** — Latin *ad-*, "near, towards" + *renes*, "kidneys" + *-al* → the gland near the kidney. Greek equivalent *epi-nephros* (upon the kidney) → *epinephrine*.
[^ety-cortex]: **cortex** — Latin, "bark, rind, outer shell" → the outer layer of an organ (adrenal cortex, cerebral cortex, renal cortex); the inside is the *medulla* (marrow).
[^ety-aldosterone]: **aldosterone** — *ald(ehyde)* + *-sterone* (a steroid ketone) → the steroid with an aldehyde group at carbon 18; the mineralocorticoid of the adrenal zona glomerulosa.
[^ety-cortisol]: **cortisol** — *cort(ex)* + *-ol*, an alcohol → the adrenal-cortex steroid alcohol (the reduced form of cortisone); the main human glucocorticoid.
[^ety-testis]: **testis** — Latin *testis*, "witness" — probably the "witness" of virility; origin debated. Plural *testes*; the adjective is *testicular*.
[^ety-testosterone]: **testosterone** — *testo-* (testis) + *-ster-* (sterol) + *-one* (ketone) → the ketone steroid of the testis, isolated and named in 1935.
[^ety-ovary]: **ovary** — Latin *ovum*, "egg" + *-arium*, "a place for" → the egg-place; same root as *ovulation* and *oocyte*.
[^ety-estrogen]: **estrogen / oestrogen** — Greek *oistros*, "gadfly, frenzy" (the mad passion of the breeding season, *oestrus*) + *-gen*, "producing" → the hormone that produces oestrus.
[^ety-sarcoplasmic]: **sarcoplasmic reticulum** — Greek *sarx, sarkos*, "flesh, muscle" + *plasma*, "formed substance" → the cytoplasm of muscle; its smooth ER is the *sarcoplasmic reticulum*, the Ca²⁺ store. Same root as *sarcomere*, *sarcolemma* and *sarcoma* (a "fleshy" tumour of mesenchyme).
[^ety-detoxification]: **detoxification** — Latin *de-*, "removing" + Greek *toxikon*, "poison" + Latin *-ficatio*, "making" → making a poison harmless. **Differentiate:** *detoxification* (making less toxic) vs *bioactivation* (some drugs become *more* toxic after CYP450 — paracetamol → NAPQI).
[^ety-xenobiosis]: **xenobiosis / xenobiotic** — Greek *xenos*, "stranger, foreign" + *bios*, "life" → substances foreign to the body's own chemistry (drugs, pollutants); their handling in the liver SER is xenobiotic metabolism.
[^ety-cytochrome]: **cytochrome P450** — Greek *kytos*, "cell" + *chrōma*, "colour" → a "cell pigment" (term of David Keilin, 1925; these haem proteins are coloured); **P450** = a **P**igment whose absorption peaks at **450** nm when bound to carbon monoxide (Omura and Sato, 1964).
[^ety-golgi]: **Golgi apparatus** — Camillo Golgi (1843–1926), Italian physician and histologist, who described the "internal reticular apparatus" in 1898 with his silver ("black reaction") stain; he shared the 1906 Nobel Prize with Santiago Ramón y Cajal.
[^ety-cis-trans]: **cis / trans** — Latin *cis*, "on this side" and *trans*, "on the far side" → the Golgi face nearer the ER (cis — receiving) and the face nearer the cell membrane (trans — releasing); the same words describe isomers in chemistry (*cis*-, *trans*-fatty acids).
[^ety-glycosylation]: **glycosylation** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" + *-osyl* (a sugar group attached) + *-ation*, process → the process of adding sugar chains to a protein or lipid. **Differentiate:** *glycosylation* (enzymatic, controlled — Golgi) vs *glycation* (non-enzymatic sugar sticking — HbA1c, diabetic damage).
[^ety-vesicle]: **vesicle** — Latin *vesicula*, "little bladder", diminutive of *vesica*, "bladder" → a small membrane sac that carries cargo; *vesicular* transport. Same root as *vesical* (of the urinary bladder).
[^ety-secretory]: **secretory / secretion** — Latin *secernere*, "to separate, set apart" (*se-* "apart" + *cernere* "to sift") + *-ion* → setting apart a useful product and releasing it. **Differentiate:** *secretion* (useful product released — hormones, mucus) vs *excretion* (*ex-* + *cernere* — waste removed) · *exocytosis* (the membrane mechanism of release).
[^ety-goblet]: **goblet cell** — Old French *gobelet*, "small cup" → a mucus-secreting cell shaped like a wine glass, swollen with mucin granules (made in its large Golgi).
[^ety-phosphotransferase]: **phosphotransferase** — *phospho-* (phosphate) + Latin *transferre*, "to carry across" + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that carries a phosphate group across from a donor onto a substrate (here onto mannose).
[^ety-mannose]: **mannose** — from *manna*, the sweet exudate of the manna ash from which the sugar alcohol mannitol was obtained + *-ose*, a sugar → the sugar whose 6-phosphate is the lysosomal "address label".
[^ety-i-cell]: **I-cell disease** — "**I**nclusion-cell" disease (mucolipidosis type II), named for the dense inclusions seen in cultured fibroblasts of patients.
[^ety-inclusion]: **inclusion** — Latin *in-*, "in" + *claudere*, "to shut" + *-ion* → something shut inside; an inclusion body is a mass of material shut up in the cell. **Differentiate:** *inclusion* (stored or abnormal material — glycogen, pigment, viral bodies) vs *organelle* (a working structure).
[^ety-lysosome]: **lysosome** — Greek *lysis*, "loosening, dissolving" + *sōma*, "body" → the dissolving body; discovered and named by Christian de Duve in 1955 (Nobel Prize 1974).
[^ety-hydrolase]: **hydrolase** — Greek *hydōr*, "water" + *lysis*, "loosening" + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that breaks bonds by adding water (hydrolysis). **Differentiate:** *hydrolase* (splits with water) · *hydroxylase* (adds an –OH group using O₂, e.g. phytanoyl-CoA hydroxylase) · *hydratase* (adds water across a double bond).
[^ety-phagocytosis]: **phagocytosis** — Greek *phagein*, "to eat" + *kytos*, "cell" + *-osis*, process → cell-eating; described by Élie Metchnikoff in the 1880s. **Differentiate:** *phagocytosis* (eating particles) · *pinocytosis* (*pinein*, to drink — fluid) · *exocytosis* (releasing) · *autophagy* (eating one's own parts).
[^ety-autophagy]: **autophagy** — Greek *autos*, "self" + *phagein*, "to eat" + *-y* → self-eating; term coined by Christian de Duve (1963). **Differentiate:** *autophagy* (survival — recycles own parts) · *apoptosis* (programmed death) · *autolysis* (self-digestion after death).
[^ety-starvation]: **starvation** — Old English *steorfan*, "to die" (compare German *sterben*) + Latin *-ation* → originally dying of any cause, then dying (or suffering) from lack of food.
[^ety-metabolism]: **metabolism** — Greek *metabolē*, "change" (*meta-*, "change, beyond" + *ballein*, "to throw") + *-ism* → the sum of chemical changes in the body. **Differentiate:** *anabolism* (*ana-*, up — building) · *catabolism* (*kata-*, down — breaking).
[^ety-neutrophil]: **neutrophil** — Latin *neuter*, "neither" + Greek *philos*, "loving" → loving neither acid nor basic dyes — its granules take up neutral stain (Paul Ehrlich). **Differentiate:** *eosinophil* (loves the acid dye eosin — red) · *basophil* (loves basic dyes — blue).
[^ety-chediak-higashi]: **Chédiak–Higashi syndrome** — Moisés Chédiak, Cuban physician (report 1952), and Ototaka Higashi, Japanese paediatrician (report 1954), who described the giant granules in leucocytes; an earlier case was reported by Béguez César (1943).
[^ety-peroxisome]: **peroxisome** — *peroxide* (Latin *per-*, "thoroughly" — in chemistry the oxide with the most oxygen — + *oxide*) + Greek *sōma*, "body" → the hydrogen-peroxide body; named by Christian de Duve (1965) for the microbodies first seen by Johannes Rhodin (1954).
[^ety-crystalline]: **crystalline** — Greek *krystallos*, "ice, rock crystal" (*kryos*, frost) → crystal-like; the peroxisome's core is an enzyme packed so densely it forms a crystal lattice.
[^ety-oxidation]: **oxidation** — Greek *oxys*, "sharp, acid" (Lavoisier's *oxygène*, "acid-maker") + *-ation* → originally combining with oxygen; now losing electrons. **Differentiate:** *oxidation* (lose electrons) vs *reduction* (gain electrons) — "OIL RIG"; *β-oxidation* (fatty acids cut two carbons at a time) vs *α-oxidation* (one carbon — phytanic acid, Refsum).
[^ety-catalase]: **catalase** — Greek *katalysis*, "dissolution" (*kata-*, "down" + *lyein*, "to loosen") + *-ase* → the enzyme that breaks down hydrogen peroxide (2 H₂O₂ → 2 H₂O + O₂); named by Oscar Loew (1900).
[^ety-vitiligo]: **vitiligo** — Latin *vitiligo*, a skin eruption; perhaps from *vitium*, "blemish", or *vitulus*, "calf" (white calf patches) — origin uncertain. A disorder of white patches from loss of melanocytes.
[^ety-plasmalogen]: **plasmalogen** — *plasmal* + *-ogen*, "producer" → Feulgen and Voit (1924) found that these lipids released an aldehyde ("plasmal") from tissue on acid treatment; they are ether phospholipids rich in myelin and heart.
[^ety-luciferase]: **luciferase** — Latin *lucifer*, "light-bearing" (*lux*, light + *ferre*, to carry) + *-ase* → the light-making enzyme that oxidises luciferin in fireflies; names coined by Raphaël Dubois (1885).
[^ety-zellweger]: **Zellweger syndrome** — Hans Zellweger (1909–1990), Swiss-born American paediatrician, who with Bowen and colleagues described the cerebro-hepato-renal syndrome in 1964.
[^ety-refsum]: **Refsum's disease** — Sigvald Refsum (1907–1991), Norwegian neurologist, who described *heredopathia atactica polyneuritiformis* in 1946.
[^ety-phytanic]: **phytanic acid** — Greek *phyton*, "plant" → derived from phytol, the side chain of plant chlorophyll, which reaches us through dairy products and ruminant fat.
[^ety-adrenoleukodystrophy]: **adrenoleukodystrophy** — Latin *ad-* + *renes* (adrenal) + Greek *leukos*, "white" + *dys-*, "faulty" + *trophē*, "nourishment" → faulty nourishment of the adrenal and the brain's white matter (demyelination + adrenal failure). **Differentiate:** *leukodystrophy* (white-matter disease of myelin formation) · *leukaemia* (white blood) · *leukoplakia* (white plaque).
[^ety-mitochondria]: **mitochondria** — Greek *mitos*, "thread" + *chondrion*, "little granule" → "thread-granules", named by Carl Benda (1898) from their light-microscope appearance; singular *mitochondrion*.
[^ety-cristae]: **cristae** — Latin *crista*, "crest, comb (of a cock)" → the folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane, which multiply the surface area for the respiratory chain. Singular *crista*; same word in *crista galli* of the ethmoid.
[^ety-phosphorylation]: **oxidative phosphorylation** — *oxidative* (removing electrons from fuel) + *phosphorylation* (*phosphoryl* group + *-ation*, process → adding a phosphate) → making ATP (adding phosphate to ADP) with energy released by oxidation along the electron transport chain. **Differentiate:** *substrate-level phosphorylation* (direct, in glycolysis and the TCA cycle) · *photophosphorylation* (in plants).
[^ety-endosymbiotic]: **endosymbiotic theory** — Greek *endon*, "within" + *syn-*, "together" + *bios*, "life" → living together inside; mitochondria descend from bacteria engulfed by an ancestral cell (championed by Lynn Margulis, 1967), which is why they have their own circular DNA and ribosomes.
[^ety-maternal]: **maternal** — Latin *mater*, "mother" + *-nal* → from the mother; mitochondrial DNA comes only from the ovum. **Differentiate:** *maternal* inheritance (all children of an affected mother; none from an affected father) vs *X-linked* (sons of carrier mothers).
[^ety-mutation]: **mutation** — Latin *mutare*, "to change" + *-ation* → a change in the DNA sequence. **Differentiate:** *mutation* (change in sequence) · *polymorphism* (a common harmless variant, > 1 % of people) · *heteroplasmy* (a mixture of normal and mutant mtDNA in one cell).
[^ety-lactic-acidosis]: **lactic acidosis** — Latin *lac, lactis*, "milk" (lactic acid was first isolated from sour milk by Scheele, 1780) + *acidus*, "sour" + *-osis*, condition → acid build-up from lactate when oxidative phosphorylation fails and cells fall back on anaerobic glycolysis.
[^ety-encephalopathy]: **encephalopathy** — Greek *en-*, "in" + *kephalē*, "head" (→ *enkephalos*, the brain, "what is in the head") + *pathos*, "suffering" → disease of the brain. **Differentiate:** *encephalopathy* (diffuse brain dysfunction) · *encephalitis* (inflammation) · *encephalomalacia* (softening).
[^ety-chromosome]: **chromosome** — Greek *chrōma*, "colour" + *sōma*, "body" → coloured body, because it stains deeply; named by Wilhelm Waldeyer (1888).
[^ety-histone]: **histone** — Greek *histos*, "web, tissue, loom" + *-one* → named by Albrecht Kossel (1884); the basic proteins around which DNA is wound.
[^ety-chromatin]: **chromatin** — Greek *chrōma*, "colour" + *-in* → the material of the nucleus that takes up stain (Walther Flemming, 1880s). **Differentiate:** *euchromatin* (loose, active — pale) vs *heterochromatin* (condensed, silent — dark).
[^ety-nucleosome]: **nucleosome** — *nucleo-* (nucleus) + Greek *sōma*, "body" → the nuclear body, the bead of DNA wrapped on eight histones that repeats along chromatin (named in the 1970s).
[^ety-importin]: **importin / exportin** — Latin *importare*, "to carry in" and *exportare*, "to carry out" + *-in*, a protein → the transport receptors that carry cargo into and out of the nucleus through the nuclear pore.
[^ety-nucleus]: **nucleus** — Latin *nucleus*, "kernel, inner part of a nut" (diminutive of *nux*, nut) → the kernel of the cell; Robert Brown named it in 1831. Same root as *nucleolus* ("little nucleus") and *nucleic acid*.
[^ety-adenylyl-cyclase]: **adenylyl cyclase** — *adenylyl* (from adenine — Greek *adēn*, "gland", as Kossel first isolated it from pancreas) + *cyclase* (Greek *kyklos*, "circle" + *-ase*) → the enzyme that closes AMP into a ring, making cyclic AMP from ATP.
[^ety-phosphatase]: **phosphatase** — *phosphate* + *-ase* → an enzyme that removes a phosphate group (glucose-6-phosphatase frees glucose; acid phosphatase works at acid pH in the lysosome). **Differentiate:** *phosphatase* (removes phosphate) · *kinase* (adds phosphate from ATP) · *phosphorylase* (splits a bond using inorganic phosphate).
[^ety-galactosyl]: **galactosyl transferase** — Greek *gala, galaktos*, "milk" (galactose is half of milk sugar, lactose) + *-osyl* + *transferase* → the enzyme that transfers galactose onto glycoproteins in the trans-Golgi.
[^ety-creatine]: **creatine kinase** — Greek *kreas*, "flesh, meat" (creatine was first isolated from meat by Chevreul, 1832) + *kinase* (Greek *kinein*, "to move" — it moves a phosphate) → the enzyme that moves phosphate between creatine and ADP to buffer ATP.
[^ety-polymerase]: **polymerase** — Greek *poly-*, "many" + *meros*, "part" + *-ase* → the enzyme that joins many parts (nucleotides) into a chain; RNA polymerase makes RNA in the nucleus.
[^ety-deacetylase]: **histone deacetylase** — Latin *de-*, "removing" + *acetyl* (Latin *acetum*, "vinegar" — the acetic group) + *-ase* → the enzyme that removes acetyl groups from histones, tightening chromatin and silencing genes.
[^suf-elle]: **-elle / -ella / -ulum / -ule / -ole** — small, diminutive: organelle, reticulum (little net), vesicle (little bladder), nucleolus, arteriole. Don't confuse with **-oma** a tumour · **-oid** resembling.
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endoplasmic, endosymbiotic, endocrine, endothelium. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (exocytosis) · **ecto-** outer · **en-** in (encephalon).
[^suf-ulum]: **-ulum / -ulus / -ula** — Latin diminutive: reticulum (little net), alveolus, fibula, tubule. Don't confuse with **-ium** a structure or region (cranium) · **-um** a plain noun ending (septum).
[^suf-some]: **-some / -soma** — body (Greek *sōma*): ribosome, lysosome, peroxisome, proteasome, nucleosome, chromosome. Don't confuse with **-stoma** mouth, opening (colostomy) · **soma** the cell body of a neuron.
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — without, not: agranular, atrophy, anaemia, aplasia. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from (abduction) · **ad-** towards · **ana-** up, again (anabolism).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, beyond, through: translation, transferase, trans face of the Golgi, transport. Don't confuse with **cis-** this side (cis face) · **inter-** between · **intra-** within.
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -ition / -ion** — the process or result of an action: translation, glycosylation, oxidation, degradation, mutation. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition (acidosis) · **-ism** a state or doctrine (metabolism).
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, away, removing: degradation, detoxification, deacetylase, dehydrogenase. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis-** apart (dissociate) · **dia-** through.
[^suf-oid]: **-oid** — like, resembling (Greek *eidos*, form): amyloid (starch-like), steroid (sterol-like), crystalloid, sphenoid. Don't confuse with **-ode** a way, path (electrode, cathode) · **-ol** an alcohol · **-one** a ketone.
[^pre-ad]: **ad- (af-, ac-, ag- …)** — to, towards, near: adrenal (near the kidney), afferent, accumulation. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from · **adeno-** gland (adenylyl, adenoma).
[^suf-one]: **-one** — a ketone (C=O inside a chain): aldosterone, testosterone, cortisone, progesterone. Don't confuse with **-ol** an alcohol (cortisol, estradiol) · **-ose** a sugar · **-ine** an amine.
[^suf-gen]: **-gen / -genic / -genesis** — producing, origin: estrogen, plasmalogen, antigen, pathogen, gluconeogenesis. Don't confuse with **-gene** a unit of heredity · **-ogen** an inactive precursor (pepsinogen — same root, "producer of pepsin").
[^pre-sarco]: **sarco-** — flesh, muscle: sarcoplasmic reticulum, sarcomere, sarcolemma, sarcoma. Don't confuse with **sacro-** the sacrum · **sarcoidosis** (flesh-like granulomas, not muscle).
[^pre-xeno]: **xeno-** — foreign, stranger: xenobiotic, xenobiosis, xenograft. Don't confuse with **xero-** dry (xerostomia) · **allo-** other (of the same species: allograft) · **auto-** self.
[^pre-cyto]: **cyto- / -cyte** — cell: cytochrome, cytoplasm, cytosol, phagocyte. Don't confuse with **cysto-** bladder, sac (cystitis) · **-cytosis** a cell process (exocytosis) or an excess of cells (leucocytosis).
[^pre-post]: **post-** — after, behind: post-translational, postprandial, posterior. Don't confuse with **pre-** before · **para-** beside · **peri-** around.
[^pre-glyco]: **glyco- / gluco-** — sweet, sugar: glycosylation, glycoprotein, glucose-6-phosphatase. Don't confuse with **glycero-** glycerol · **glia** glue (glial cells).
[^pre-hydro]: **hydro- / hydr-** — water: hydrolase, hydrolysis, dehydration. Don't confuse with **hydroxy- / hydroxyl** an –OH group (hydroxylase) · **hydrogen** (the element: *hydro-* + *-gen*, "water-maker").
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: ATPase, hydrolase, catalase, luciferase, polymerase, phosphatase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (mannose — the *substrate*) · **-in** a non-enzyme protein (ubiquitin).
[^suf-cytosis]: **-cytosis** — a cell process, or an excess of cells: phagocytosis, pinocytosis, exocytosis; leucocytosis, spherocytosis. Don't confuse with **-cyte** a single cell · **-penia** a deficiency of cells (neutropenia).
[^pre-auto]: **auto-** — self: autophagy, autoimmune, autolysis, autosomal. Don't confuse with **allo-** other (allograft) · **xeno-** foreign · **iso-** equal.
[^suf-phagy]: **-phagy / -phage / -phagia** — eating: autophagy, mitophagy, macrophage, dysphagia. Don't confuse with **-phasia** speech (aphasia) · **-phylaxis** protection (anaphylaxis).
[^pre-meta]: **meta-** — change, after, beyond: metabolism, metastasis, metaplasia, metacarpal. Don't confuse with **meso-** middle · **para-** beside · **epi-** upon.
[^suf-phil]: **-phil / -philia** — loving, affinity for: neutrophil, eosinophil, basophil, haemophilia. Don't confuse with **-phobia** fear (hydrophobic — water-fearing) · **-phyll** leaf (chlorophyll).
[^pre-per]: **per-** — through, thoroughly; in chemistry, the most of an element: peroxide, peroxisome, permeability, perchlorate. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before · **pro-** forward.
[^pre-pseudo]: **pseudo-** — false, imitation: pseudocatalase, pseudohypertrophy, pseudocyst, pseudopodia. Don't confuse with **proto-** first · **para-** beside.
[^pre-leuko]: **leuko- / leuco-** — white: leukodystrophy (white matter), leucocyte (white cell), leukaemia. Don't confuse with **lyso-** dissolving · **luteo-** yellow · **melano-** black.
[^pre-dys]: **dys-** — bad, faulty, difficult: dystrophy, dysplasia, dyspnoea. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart (Latin: dissect) · **a-** without (atrophy) · **eu-** good.
[^suf-trophy]: **-trophy** — nourishment, growth: dystrophy, atrophy, hypertrophy. Don't confuse with **-tropic** turning towards (gonadotropic) · **-plasia** formation of cells.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together, with: endosymbiotic, synthesis, synapse, syndrome. Don't confuse with **semi-** half · **sin-** left (Latin *sinister*).
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a condition or state (often abnormal): acidosis, xenobiosis, fibrosis, phagocytosis. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation · **-iasis** an infestation · **-ation** an active process.
[^pre-en]: **en- / em-** — in, within (Greek): encephalopathy (*en* + *kephalē*, what is in the head), enzyme (in yeast), empyema. Don't confuse with **endo-** within (a longer form) · **an-** without · **ana-** up.
[^suf-pathy]: **-pathy** — disease, suffering, feeling: encephalopathy, neuropathy, myopathy, sympathy. Don't confuse with **-pathic** in *idiopathic* (disease of unknown cause) · **-path** a practitioner (osteopath).
[^pre-poly]: **poly-** — many: polymerase, polypeptide, polymorphism, polyunsaturated. Don't confuse with **multi-** many (Latin equivalent) · **pan-** all · **oligo-** few.
