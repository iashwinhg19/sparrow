# 🧪 Physiology of GIT Absorption and Selective Malabsorption

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> Absorption[^ety-absorption][^pre-ab][^suf-tion] happens in three stages — luminal (enzymes), mucosal (the enterocyte[^ety-enterocyte][^pre-entero][^suf-cyte]) and post-mucosal (into blood or lymph). Every nutrient can fail at one of those three steps, and every malabsorption[^ety-malabsorption][^pre-mal] syndrome on this page is just a named failure of one step for one nutrient.
> The questions this note answers: *where* is each nutrient absorbed, *which* enzyme or transporter does it, and *which disease* breaks that step (selective) versus the whole mucosa (global).

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — Three stages, three ways to fail
Think of the gut as a three-station assembly line. Station 1 (lumen) chops: pancreatic lipase, bile acids, disaccharidases, enterokinase. Station 2 (mucosa) takes up: SGLT1, GLUT5, sodium–amino-acid co-transporters, lactase on the brush border. Station 3 (post-mucosal) ships: chylomicrons into lymph, monosaccharides and amino acids into portal blood. A defect at one station for one nutrient is *selective* malabsorption; destruction of station 2 itself (celiac, Whipple, tropical sprue) is *global*.

```
            LUMINAL              MUCOSAL                 POST-MUCOSAL
FAT    TGA -lipase-> FA -bile-> micelle -> enterocyte -> chylomicron -> lymphatics
       ✗ chronic pancreatitis    ✗ celiac / Whipple /   ✗ abetalipoproteinemia
       ✗ bile acid defect          tropical sprue        ✗ intestinal lymphangiectasia
CHO    disaccharidases ---------> SGLT1 / GLUT5 -------> GLUT2 -> portal blood
       ✗ lactase deficiency       ✗ SGLT1 defect
PROT   enterokinase -> trypsin -> Na+-amino acid cotransporter -> portal blood
       ✗ enterokinase def.        ✗ Hartnup / cystinuria
```

## 🧱 Basics of Absorption

**STAGES OF ABSORPTION**
1. Luminal: Enzymes mediated.
2. Small intestinal mucosal: Enterocyte. Villi[^ety-villus] → Brush borders contains enzymes.
3. Post mucosal: Mucosal epithelial cell → Into blood.

**ANATOMY — Small intestine**

| Segment | Share | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Proximal | 2/5 | Maximum absorption (Jejunum[^ety-jejunum] > Duodenum[^ety-duodenum]) |
| Distal | 3/5 | Absorption of **Bile acid, Vit B12, Mg2+** (Ileum[^ety-ileum]) |

**Histology**
1. Mucosa:[^ety-mucosa]
   - a. Enterocyte:
     - Villi (specific feature): surrounded by **crypts of Lieberkühn**[^ety-lieberkuhn]; contains brush-border.
     - Crypts: **Paneth cells (at base)**[^ety-paneth] — produce immunological substances (alpha defensins, lysozymes, phospholipase A2). Enteroendocrine[^pre-endo] cells. Goblet cells.
     - Continuous renewal: **every 48–72 hrs.**
   - b. Lamina propria: Glands, blood vessels.
   - c. Muscularis mucosa: Tight attachment.

## 🧈 Fat Absorption

**LUMINAL PHASE OF ABSORPTION — Mechanism**

```
                 ① Pancreatic lipase            ② Bile acids
                        │                             │
   TGA ─────────────────▼──────────► Fatty acids ─────▼──────────► Micelle
             Lipolysis                        + Glycerol                │
                                              + Phospholipid            ▼
                                                           Passive diffusion into enterocytes
   ① fails in CHRONIC PANCREATITIS        ② fails in BILE ACID DEFECT
```

> [!danger] The two luminal defects (boxed in red on the page)
> 1. Chronic pancreatitis[^ety-pancreatitis][^suf-itis]
> 2. Bile acid defect

**Bile Acid Physiology**
- Formation & excretion: 500 mg.
- Body pool: 4 g (maintained by enterohepatic[^ety-enterohepatic][^pre-hepat] circulation).

**Enterohepatic circulation**

![[GIT Absorption - enterohepatic circulation.png]]
*Enterohepatic circulation as drawn on the page: liver 7α-hydroxylase (rate limiting) → 1° bile acids (cholic, chenodeoxycholic acid) → conjugation[^ety-conjugation][^pre-con] with glycine/taurine → bile salts → D2 duodenum (micelle formation), ileum (reabsorbed → portal circulation → liver), colon (bacterial deconjugation[^pre-de] → 2° bile acids: deoxycholic, lithocholic[^ety-lithocholic] acid) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
Liver: 7α-hydroxylase (RATE LIMITING)
   └─► 1° bile acids: cholic acid, chenodeoxycholic acid
          └─ conjugation (glycine / taurine) ─► BILE SALTS
                 ├─► D2 duodenum ──► micelle formation
                 ├─► ileum ──► reabsorbed ──► portal circulation ──► liver
                 └─► colon ──► bacterial deconjugation ──► 2° bile acids: deoxycholic, lithocholic
```

**Defects in luminal absorption**
1. Chronic pancreatitis → Affects pancreatic lipase → Lipolysis[^ety-lipolysis][^suf-lysis] affected.
2. Bile acid defect — defect in:

| Defect in | Disease |
|---|---|
| Synthesis | Cirrhosis[^ety-cirrhosis][^suf-osis] |
| Early deconjugation in small intestine | Small intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) |
| Reabsorption[^pre-re] | TB/Crohn's[^ety-crohn] (Ileal dysfunction[^pre-dys]) |
| Transportation | Primary biliary cholangitis[^ety-cholangitis][^pre-chol] & primary sclerosing cholangitis (Destruction of bile ducts) |

**MUCOSAL PHASE OF ABSORPTION**
- Pathology: Defect in small intestinal mucosal integrity.
- Etiology: Celiac disease[^ety-celiac] · Whipple disease[^ety-whipple] · Tropical sprue[^ety-sprue].

**POST-MUCOSAL PHASE OF ABSORPTION**

```
                       Glycerol
                          │
 Long chain fatty acids ──▼── Esterification ──► Chylomicrons ──► Transport via intestinal lymphatics
         (LCFA)               ▲                                        ▲
                              ⊣ 1. Abetalipoproteinemia               ⊣ 2. Intestinal lymphangiectasia
```

**TYPES OF FATTY ACIDS**

| | Long chain | Medium chain | Short chain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon chain length | >12 | 8–12 | <8 |
| Origin | Large quantity in diet as triglycerides | Small quantity in diet (Coconut oil) | **Bacterial degradation** of unabsorbed carbohydrates in colon |
| Primary absorption site | Small intestine | Small intestine | Colon |
| Require pancreatic lipolysis | Yes | No | No |
| Require micelle formation | Yes | No | No |
| Present in stool | Minimal | No | Substantial |

## 🍚 Carbohydrate Absorption

**MECHANISM OF CARBOHYDRATE ABSORPTION**

```
                    Disaccharidases  ◄── small intestinal brush border epithelium
                          │
 Disaccharides ───────────▼─────────────► Monosaccharides
        │
   ┌────┴────────┬─────────────┬────────────┐
 Lactase       Maltase       Sucrase      Trehalase
   │                            │
 Lactose → Glucose + Galactose  Sucrose → Glucose + Fructose
```

**Receptors for absorption**

In the gut:

![[GIT Absorption - gut receptors SGLT1 GLUT2 GLUT5.png]]
*Receptors in the gut: sodium-glucose cotransporter 1 (SGLT1, AKA SLC5A) takes glucose and galactose by 2° active transport; GLUT5 takes fructose by facilitated diffusion; GLUT2 on the basolateral side passes all three into the capillary (cropped from the handwritten page).*

In the liver:

![[GIT Absorption - liver receptors GLUT5 GLUT2.png]]
*Receptors in the liver: GLUT5 → galactose (galactokinase + ATP → galactose-1-phosphate) and fructose (fructokinase + ATP → fructose-1-phosphate); GLUT2 → glucose (glucokinase + ATP → glucose-6-phosphate) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Transporter | Substrate | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| SGLT1 (AKA SLC5A)[^1] | Glucose, Galactose | 2° active transport |
| GLUT5 | Fructose | Facilitated diffusion |
| GLUT2 (basolateral) | Glucose, galactose, fructose → capillary | Facilitated diffusion |

> [!warning] Note
> SGLT-1 defect → **Familial glucose-galactose malabsorption syndrome.**

**DEFECTS IN CARBOHYDRATE ABSORPTION**

| Pathology | Etiology | Malabsorption |
|---|---|---|
| Lactase deficiency | Congenital:[^ety-congenital] Autosomal recessive. Acquired: Primary / Secondary. | Selective |
| Small intestinal mucosal defect | Celiac disease · Whipple's disease · Tropical sprue | Global |

## 🥩 Proteins

**Mechanism of absorption**
- Endopeptidase:[^ety-peptidase][^pre-endo-exo][^suf-ase] **Enterokinase**[^ety-enterokinase] (Produced by enterocyte brush border).

```
 Enterokinase
      │
 Trypsinogen ──► Trypsin ──► Activate chymotrypsin, elastase
```

- Exopeptidase: Carboxypeptidase and aminopeptidase (Activated by trypsin[^ety-trypsin]).
- Transport in small intestinal mucosa: Sodium–amino acid co-transporters.

**Defects in protein absorption**
- Global malabsorption: Celiac disease, Whipple disease, Tropical sprue.
- Selective malabsorption:
  - Enterokinase deficiency.
  - Neutral amino acid transport defect: Hartnup's disease[^ety-hartnup] — presents as pellagra:[^ety-pellagra] Diarrhoea, dermatitis, dementia.[^2]
  - Dibasic amino acid transport defect → Cystinuria[^ety-cystinuria][^suf-uria] → Excretion of **COLA** (Cystine, ornithine, lysine, arginine).

## 🥛 Carbohydrate Malabsorption

**LACTOSE INTOLERANCE (LACTASE DEFICIENCY)**

Physiology:
```
 Lactose (Dietary: milk, cheese)
        │ Lactase (brush border)
        ▼
 Glucose + Galactose
```

Etiology:
- Congenital: Autosomal recessive (Absent enzyme from birth).
- Acquired: ↓ enzyme activity.
  - **Primary** (m/c): Genetic defect (allele persistence).[^3]
  - Secondary: Infections · Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS).

Pathogenesis:[^ety-pathogenesis][^pre-patho][^suf-genesis]
```
 Undigested carbohydrate
        │
        ▼
 ↑ Unabsorbed carbohydrates in colon
        │  Fermented by colonic bacteria: Bacteroides fragilis · Bifido bacteria · Lactobacillus
        ▼
 ↑ Acid and gases (H2, methane, CO2)
```

Clinical presentation:
- 90 min post prandial:[^pre-post] **Abdominal distension, bloating, cramps.**
- **Diaper dermatitis**:[^ety-dermatitis][^pre-derm] in children d/t acidic pH · Severe watery diarrhea · Failure to thrive · Irritability.

![[GIT Absorption - diaper dermatitis.png]]
*Diaper dermatitis — the perianal erythema produced by acidic stool in a lactose-intolerant child (cropped from the handwritten page).*

Investigations:
1. Stool pH: Acidic.
2. Lactose hydrogen breath test:
   - Principle: Excess lactose in colon —(fermented by bacteria)→ Methane + H2 → Absorbed → Tested on breath.
   - Positive test: **H2 > 20 ppm.**
3. Lactose–Glucose blood test: Obsolete.

Management: Dietary management · Ca2+ supplementation.

> [!note] Note — Protein malabsorption (boxed on the page)
> - Hartnup's disease: Similar to pellagra.
> - Cystinuria: Excretion of COLA (Cystine, ornithine, lysine, arginine).

## 🫀 Fat Malabsorption

Post mucosal absorption disorders: 1. Abetalipoproteinemia. 2. Intestinal lymphangiectasia.

### ABETALIPOPROTEINEMIA[^4]
AKA Neuroacanthocytosis[^ety-acanthocyte][^pre-neuro].

Etiopathogenesis:
- Autosomal recessive.
- Defect in **MTTP** (Microsomal Triglyceride Transport Protein) → Defect in apoB48 → Defective esterification → No chylomicron[^ety-chylomicron][^pre-chylo] formation.

Clinical presentation:
- Hypolipidemia[^pre-hypo][^suf-emia].
- Triad of:

```
                 Neurological: ATAXIA (Vit E deficiency)
                          ▲
                         / \
                        /   \
      GI: Steatorrhea  ◄─────► Retinopathy: Retinitis pigmentosa (Night blindness)
```

Investigations:
1. Peripheral smear: **Acanthocytes.**
2. Post prandial endoscopic biopsy:[^ety-biopsy] **100 % diagnostic**; **Vacuolation**[^ety-vacuolation] in enterocytes.

![[GIT Absorption - duodenal biopsy abetalipoproteinemia.png]]
*Duodenal biopsy of abetalipoproteinemia — the arrow on the page points to vacuolation of the enterocytes, lipid that cannot be packaged into chylomicrons (cropped from the handwritten page).*

> [!success] GI disorders which are 100 % diagnostic on biopsy
> - Whipple's disease.
> - Abetalipoproteinemia.
> - Agammaglobulinemia[^pre-a].

**Vit E deficiency in Abetalipoproteinemia**
- Clinical features: Ataxia[^ety-ataxia] (Pronounced).
- Pathogenesis:

```
                    ┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────────┐
         Fat malabsorption        ↓ VLDL formation          ↓ Chylomicron formation
                    │                      │                          │
                    ▼                      ▼                          ▼
   ↓ Vit E absorption (fat soluble)   ↓ Secretion of vit E by liver   ↓ Secretion of Vit E by intestine into blood
```

![[GIT Absorption - vit E pathogenesis gut and liver.png]]
*The page's drawings under the pathogenesis flow: the intestine with absorption of vitamin E blocked and the liver with its secretion of vitamin E blocked (cropped from the handwritten page).*

### INTESTINAL LYMPHANGIECTASIA[^ety-lymphangiectasia][^suf-ectasia]

Physiology: Chylomicrons —(across intestinal lymphatics)→ Blood.

Etiopathogenesis:
- Secondary: Lymph node obstruction → ↑ Lymphatic pressure → Exudation[^ety-exudation][^pre-ex]. Lymphoma[^suf-oma] · Crohn's disease.
- Primary: Abnormally dilated lymphatic channel → ↑ pressure → Exudation into intestinal lumen.

Clinical presentation:
1. Protein losing enteropathy:[^suf-pathy]
   - Most important: Primary intestinal lymphangiectasis.
   - **Hypoalbuminemia** with hypoglobulinemia.
   - Edema · Ascites[^ety-ascites].
   - D/d: Nephrotic syndrome (Loss of albumin in urine) · Liver disease (↓ albumin production).
2. Diarrhea.

Investigation:
- **Fecal α-1 antitrypsin**:[^pre-anti] Elevated (specific test).[^5]
- CT scan: Wall thickening of small intestine.
- Video capsule endoscopy.

Management: Medium chain triglyceride diet.[^6]

![[GIT Absorption - lymphangiectasia on endoscopy.png]]
*Lymphangiectasia on endoscopy — dilated lymphatics in panels A and B, diffuse white lesions in the third panel (cropped from the handwritten page).*

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Every nutrient must pass three gates — luminal digestion, mucosal uptake, post-mucosal export — and a disease is defined by *which gate* it closes and *for which nutrient*. Fat needs all three gates and the most machinery (lipase, bile, micelle, chylomicron, lymphatic), so fat is the first and most specific nutrient to fail; that is why steatorrhea is the hallmark and why 72-hour fecal fat is the gold standard.
>
> A defect in one enzyme or transporter (lactase, SGLT1, enterokinase, MTTP, a lymphatic channel) is *selective*; destruction of the mucosa itself (celiac, Whipple, tropical sprue) is *global*. If you can name the gate and the nutrient, you can name the disease and its test.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Pancreatic lipase splits triglycerides, bile salts (4 g pool, 500 mg made daily, 7α-hydroxylase rate limiting) build micelles, long-chain fatty acids are re-esterified into chylomicrons and leave through the lacteals. Brush-border disaccharidases and enterokinase finish carbohydrate and protein digestion; SGLT1, GLUT5 and sodium–amino acid co-transporters take the products in.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> One gate narrows. In chronic pancreatitis lipase falls but steatorrhea appears only when output drops below roughly 10 % of normal; in early ileal Crohn's the liver up-regulates synthesis and keeps the bile pool going; in primary lactase decline the colonic flora ferment the spilled lactose and the patient has only bloating 90 minutes after milk. Stool fat is minimal, weight is kept, and the only clue is a symptom tied to a specific food.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> The gate closes. Pale, bulky, greasy stools with > 7 g fat/day; fat-soluble vitamin loss (vitamin E ataxia and retinitis pigmentosa in abetalipoproteinemia); protein loss with hypoalbuminemia, edema and ascites in lymphangiectasia; in infants with congenital lactase deficiency, acidic watery stools, diaper dermatitis and failure to thrive.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → [enzyme / transporter / lymphatic defect] → food-specific bloating or loose stool → compensation by liver synthesis or colonic salvage (short-chain fatty acids) → steatorrhea or protein loss → vitamin deficiency syndrome (ataxia, night blindness, tetany, edema)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> The stage decides the test and the treatment. A compensated lactase deficiency needs a hydrogen breath test (> 20 ppm) and a diet, not a biopsy; a decompensated post-mucosal block needs a biopsy (100 % diagnostic vacuolation in abetalipoproteinemia) and a medium-chain triglyceride diet that bypasses lipase, micelles and chylomicrons altogether.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Abetalipoproteinemia:** "acanthocytes + ataxia + retinitis pigmentosa + very low lipids in a child with steatorrhea" → MTTP defect, no apoB48, no chylomicrons; vitamin E cannot be absorbed or exported, so the neurological and retinal signs follow.
>
> **Intestinal lymphangiectasia:** "edema, ascites, hypoalbuminemia *with* hypoglobulinemia, normal urine protein, normal liver" → lymph leaking into the lumen; fecal α-1 antitrypsin elevated; white villi / dilated lymphatics on endoscopy.
>
> **Lactose intolerance:** "bloating and cramps 90 minutes after milk, acidic stool, breath H2 > 20 ppm" → brush-border lactase deficiency; in an infant add diaper dermatitis and failure to thrive.
>
> **Familial glucose-galactose malabsorption:** "neonatal watery diarrhoea with every feed containing glucose or galactose but not fructose" → SGLT1 (SLC5A) defect.
>
> **Hartnup's disease:** "pellagra-like dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia with neutral aminoaciduria" → neutral amino acid (tryptophan) transport defect.
>
> **Cystinuria:** "recurrent cystine stones; COLA in urine" → dibasic amino acid transporter defect.
>
> **SIBO:** "steatorrhea with early deconjugation of bile salts" → bile acids deconjugated before the ileum cannot form micelles.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Vacuolated enterocytes on post-prandial biopsy = abetalipoproteinemia. Spiculated red cells on smear = acanthocytes. Diffuse white lesions / dilated lymphatics on endoscopy and small-bowel wall thickening on CT = lymphangiectasia. Pale raised nodules in the distal ileum on ileocolonoscopy = Peyer's patches (hypertrophied in typhoid).
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Breath H2 > 20 ppm (positive lactose breath test). Fatty-acid chain length > 12 carbons = needs lipase + micelle; 8–12 = neither (why MCT diet works); < 8 = colonic bacterial product. Enterocyte renewal every 48–72 h (why mucosal injury recovers fast once the insult stops).

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Calling every low albumin a liver or kidney problem**
>
> **What students think:** Hypoalbuminemia with edema means nephrotic syndrome or cirrhosis.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Protein-losing enteropathy from lymphangiectasia drops albumin *and* globulins together, because whole lymph is lost, whereas the kidney loses mainly albumin and the liver fails to make it. Urine protein is normal and liver synthetic function is intact.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Low albumin + low globulin + normal urine → think gut; ask for fecal α-1 antitrypsin.
>
> **Trap 2: Treating abetalipoproteinemia as a lipid-lowering success story**
>
> **What students think:** Very low cholesterol and triglycerides must be benign or protective.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The lipids are low because apoB48 chylomicrons (and apoB100 VLDL) cannot be made at all; fat and fat-soluble vitamins are trapped in the enterocyte (the vacuolation on biopsy). Vitamin E deficiency then causes a spinocerebellar ataxia and retinitis pigmentosa that are irreversible if missed.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Hypolipidemia + steatorrhea + acanthocytes = post-mucosal block; give high-dose vitamin E and A and a medium-chain triglyceride diet.
>
> **Trap 3: Expecting a medium-chain triglyceride diet to fail in luminal disease**
>
> **What students think:** If lipase or bile salts are missing, no fat can be absorbed.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Medium-chain fatty acids (8–12 carbons) need neither pancreatic lipolysis nor micelle formation, and they enter portal blood rather than lymph. They bypass all three fat gates, which is exactly why they are the dietary treatment in lymphangiectasia and abetalipoproteinemia.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Memorise the fatty-acid table — long chain needs everything, medium chain needs nothing, short chain is made by colonic bacteria.
>
> **Trap 4: Confusing primary and congenital lactase deficiency**
>
> **What students think:** Primary lactase deficiency means the baby is born without lactase.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Congenital deficiency is the rare autosomal recessive absence from birth. Primary (acquired) deficiency is the most common type and is the normal genetically programmed decline after weaning (the "allele persistence" line on the page refers to the lactase-persistence allele that some populations carry); secondary deficiency follows mucosal injury such as infection.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Congenital = from birth, rare; primary = adult decline, m/c; secondary = after mucosal damage.
>
> **Trap 5: Reading the lactose breath test backwards**
>
> **What students think:** Hydrogen on the breath comes from the small intestine absorbing lactose.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Hydrogen is only made when *unabsorbed* lactose reaches colonic bacteria; absorbed lactose produces no gas. A rise > 20 ppm is therefore evidence of malabsorption, and an early peak (within 90 minutes, with lactulose) points to bacteria living too high up — SIBO.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "No bacteria, no hydrogen": the test measures what the gut failed to absorb.

> The tests that confirm each gate: 72-hour fecal fat (luminal/global), D-xylose (mucosa), lactose and lactulose breath tests (lactase, SIBO), fecal α-1 antitrypsin (lymphangiectasia), Schilling test (ileum, intrinsic factor, pancreas).
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Celiac Disease]]**
> The prototype global mucosal defect: villous atrophy wipes out brush-border lactase and all transporters at once, giving secondary lactase deficiency plus fat, protein and carbohydrate malabsorption.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Chronic Pancreatitis]]**
> The prototype luminal defect: loss of lipase gives steatorrhea without any mucosal disease, with a normal D-xylose test and a positive Schilling phase II with pancreatic enzymes.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Vitamin E Deficiency]] and [[Pellagra]]**
> Fat-soluble vitamin loss (ataxia, retinitis pigmentosa) and tryptophan loss (Hartnup) show how a transport defect becomes a neurological syndrome.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> A patient with chronic diarrhoea is worked up by gate: stool fat (is fat failing?), D-xylose (is the mucosa intact?), breath tests (is a disaccharidase or bacteria the problem?), biopsy (is it one of the three 100 % diagnostic diseases?). Treatment follows the gate — enzyme replacement for the lumen, gluten withdrawal for the mucosa, medium-chain triglycerides for the post-mucosal block.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Favourite one-liners: rate-limiting enzyme of bile acid synthesis (7α-hydroxylase), site of bile acid / B12 / Mg2+ absorption (ileum), SGLT1 defect (glucose-galactose malabsorption), 100 % diagnostic biopsies (Whipple, abetalipoproteinemia, agammaglobulinemia), specific test for lymphangiectasia (fecal α-1 antitrypsin), positive breath test (H2 > 20 ppm).

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)
> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Three stages of absorption: luminal (enzymes) → mucosal (enterocyte, brush border) → post-mucosal (into blood/lymph).
> 2. Proximal 2/5 of small intestine (jejunum > duodenum) does maximum absorption; the **ileum** absorbs **bile acids, vitamin B12 and Mg2+**.
> 3. Villi are surrounded by **crypts of Lieberkühn**; **Paneth cells** at the crypt base make alpha defensins, lysozyme, phospholipase A2; the epithelium renews **every 48–72 h**.
> 4. **Plica circularis** = visible mucosal folds; **interstitial cells of Cajal** = pacemaker (basal electrical rhythm); **Brunner's glands** (duodenal submucosa) secrete bicarbonate; Peyer's patches hypertrophy in typhoid.
> 5. Fat: TGA → (pancreatic lipase) fatty acids → (bile acids) micelle → passive diffusion; fails in **chronic pancreatitis** and **bile acid defect**.
> 6. Bile acids: 500 mg made/excreted daily, 4 g pool; **7α-hydroxylase is rate limiting**; 1° = cholic, chenodeoxycholic; 2° = deoxycholic, lithocholic after colonic bacterial deconjugation.
> 7. Bile acid defect by step: synthesis (cirrhosis), early deconjugation (SIBO), reabsorption (TB/Crohn's ileum), transport (PBC/PSC).
> 8. Post-mucosal blocks: **abetalipoproteinemia** (⊣ esterification/chylomicron) and **intestinal lymphangiectasia** (⊣ lymphatic transport).
> 9. Fatty acids: long chain > 12 C needs lipase + micelle; medium chain 8–12 C (coconut oil) needs neither; short chain < 8 C comes from **bacterial degradation** in the colon.
> 10. SGLT1 (SLC5A) = glucose + galactose, 2° active; GLUT5 = fructose, facilitated; GLUT2 = basolateral exit; **SGLT1 defect = familial glucose-galactose malabsorption**.
> 11. **Enterokinase** (brush border) → trypsinogen → trypsin → chymotrypsin, elastase; Hartnup = neutral amino acids (pellagra); cystinuria = dibasic (COLA).
> 12. Lactose intolerance: **primary (m/c)**; symptoms 90 min post-prandial; **diaper dermatitis** in children; **breath H2 > 20 ppm**; Rx diet + Ca2+.
> 13. Abetalipoproteinemia: AR, **MTTP** defect → no apoB48/chylomicrons; triad **ataxia (vit E)**, steatorrhea, retinitis pigmentosa; **acanthocytes**; biopsy **100 % diagnostic (vacuolation)**.
> 14. Lymphangiectasia: protein-losing enteropathy with **hypoalbuminemia + hypoglobulinemia**; **fecal α-1 antitrypsin** specific; Rx medium-chain triglyceride diet.

## 🔁 One-Line Recall
```
lumen (lipase, bile, disaccharidase, enterokinase)
  → mucosa (SGLT1 / GLUT5 / lactase / Na-aa cotransporter)
    → export (chylomicron → lymph | sugars + amino acids → portal)
      → selective block = one enzyme/transporter (lactase, SGLT1, enterokinase, MTTP, lymphatic)
      → global block = mucosa gone (celiac, Whipple, tropical sprue)
        → fat fails first → steatorrhea → fat-soluble vitamin loss (E: ataxia, RP)
          → test the gate: fecal fat / D-xylose / breath H2 > 20 ppm / α-1 antitrypsin / biopsy
```

[^pre-hepat]: **hepat- / hepato-** — Greek *hēpar*, liver: enterohepatic, hepatocyte. Don't confuse with **hepatic** (adjective) · **hepar-in** (first isolated from liver) · **chol-** bile, which comes from the liver but names the fluid, not the organ.
[^pre-con]: **con- / com-** — with, together: conjugation (yoking together), congenital (born with), cotransporter. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **de-** reversal (deconjugation).
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, away, reversal: deconjugation (undoing conjugation), deoxycholic (oxygen removed), degradation. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart (disaccharide = two sugars; dissection) · **dys-** disordered.
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis** — loosening, splitting, dissolution: lipolysis, hydrolysis; the enzyme is a *-lysin* or *lipase*. Don't confuse with **-lytic** (adjective: proteolytic) · **-clasis** breaking (osteoclast).
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — condition, abnormal state, often degenerative or excessive: cirrhosis, acanthocytosis, neuroacanthocytosis. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation · **-iasis** infestation or formation · **-esis** a process (pathogenesis, genesis).
[^pre-re]: **re-** — again, back: reabsorption (taking bile acids back in the ileum), renewal. Don't confuse with **retro-** backward in position (retroperitoneal) · **de-** reversal.
[^pre-dys]: **dys-** — bad, difficult, disordered: dysfunction (ileal dysfunction), dyspeptic, dysplasia. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart or separation (disaccharide) · **mal-** badly (malabsorption) · **a-** absent.
[^pre-chol]: **chol- / chole-** — Greek *cholē*, bile: cholangitis, cholic acid, lithocholic, deoxycholic, cholesterol. Don't confuse with **col- / colo-** colon (ileocolonoscopy) · **chyl-** chyle, milky lymph (chylomicron).
[^pre-endo-exo]: **endo- vs exo- in peptidases** — endopeptidases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, elastase) cut peptide bonds *inside* the chain; exopeptidases (carboxypeptidase, aminopeptidase) remove the *end* residue. Same pair elsewhere: endocrine (secretes inward, into blood) vs exocrine (secretes outward, into a duct: pancreatic lipase).
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: lipase, lactase, maltase, sucrase, trehalase, enterokinase, galactokinase, fructokinase, glucokinase, peptidase, 7α-hydroxylase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (lactose, sucrose, fructose, galactose, D-xylose) · **-ogen** an inactive precursor (trypsinogen).
[^suf-uria]: **-uria** — in the urine: cystinuria, aminoaciduria, oxaluria. Don't confuse with **-emia** in the blood (hypolipidemia, hypoalbuminemia) · **-rrhoea** flow or discharge (diarrhoea, steatorrhoea).
[^pre-patho]: **patho-** — Greek *pathos*, disease, suffering: pathogenesis, pathology; as a suffix **-pathy** = disease of (enteropathy, retinopathy). Don't confuse with **-pathic** (adjective: idiopathic = of unknown cause).
[^suf-genesis]: **-genesis** — origin, production: pathogenesis, etiopathogenesis. Don't confuse with **-genic** producing (pathogenic) · **-gen** a substance that produces (trypsinogen) · **-genous** originating from (endogenous).
[^pre-post]: **post-** — after: post-prandial (after a meal), post-mucosal (beyond the mucosa). Don't confuse with **pre-** before · **para-** beside · **peri-** around.
[^pre-derm]: **derm- / dermat-** — skin: dermatitis; **-dermatitis** in diaper dermatitis, acrodermatitis. Don't confuse with **-derm** a germ layer (endoderm) · **dermat-oma** a skin tumour.
[^pre-neuro]: **neuro-** — nerve, nervous system: neuroacanthocytosis, neurological, neuropathy. Don't confuse with **neur-algia** nerve pain · **neur-itis** nerve inflammation.
[^pre-chylo]: **chylo- / chyle** — Greek *chylos*, juice: chylomicron, chyle (the milky lymph of the lacteals after a fatty meal). Don't confuse with **chol-** bile · **chym-** chyme, the semifluid stomach contents (chymotrypsin was named for digesting it).
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, deficient: hypolipidemia, hypoalbuminemia, hypoglobulinemia. Don't confuse with **hyper-** excessive (hypertrophy) · **sub-** beneath in position (submucosa).
[^suf-emia]: **-emia / -aemia** — in the blood: hypolipidemia, hypoalbuminemia, hypoglobulinemia, agammaglobulinemia, hyperamylasemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** in the urine (cystinuria) · **-rrhoea** flow (steatorrhoea).
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — without, absence of: abetalipoproteinemia (no β-lipoprotein in blood), agammaglobulinemia (no γ-globulin), ataxia (no order), atresia. Don't confuse with **ab-** away (absorption) · **ad-** toward · **hypo-** too little (hypolipidemia is *low*, abetalipoproteinemia is *absent*).
[^suf-ectasia]: **-ectasia / -ectasis** — Greek *ektasis*, stretching, dilatation: lymphangiectasia, bronchiectasis, telangiectasia. Don't confuse with **-ectomy** surgical removal · **-ectopia** displacement.
[^suf-oma]: **-oma** — tumour, swelling: lymphoma (here a cause of secondary lymphangiectasia), VIPoma. Don't confuse with **-osis** condition · **-itis** inflammation; note benign vs malignant is not encoded (lipoma vs lymphoma).
[^suf-pathy]: **-pathy** — disease of: enteropathy (protein-losing enteropathy), retinopathy, neuropathy. Don't confuse with **-pathic** (adjective) · **-plegia** paralysis · **-algia** pain.
[^pre-ex]: **ex- / e-** — out of: exudation (sweating out), excretion, exopeptidase. Don't confuse with **extra-** outside of (extrahepatic) · **ecto-** outer.
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: α-1 antitrypsin (the protein that inhibits trypsin), antibiotics. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (antenatal) · **anto-** facing.
