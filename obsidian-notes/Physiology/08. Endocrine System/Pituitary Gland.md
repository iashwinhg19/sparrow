# 🧠 Pituitary Gland

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The pituitary[^ety-pituitary] (hypophysis[^ety-hypophysis][^pre-hypo]) is the pea-sized gland hanging from the hypothalamus in the sella turcica. Its **anterior lobe (adenohypophysis[^ety-adenohypophysis][^pre-adeno]) — the "master gland"** — **synthesises** six trophic hormones under hypothalamic control; its **posterior lobe (neurohypophysis[^ety-neurohypophysis][^pre-neuro]) synthesises nothing** and only **stores and releases ADH and oxytocin**. The chapter answers:
> - ✅ **Which cell makes which hormone, and which hypothalamic hormone drives or brakes it?**
> - ✅ **How are GH and prolactin regulated, how do they act (JAK–STAT), and what happens with too much or too little?**

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Company with a Board, a Manager and Branch Offices

The **hypothalamus** is the board: it sends memos (releasing / inhibiting hormones) down a private mail chute (the portal vessels). The **anterior pituitary** is the general manager who turns each memo into an order (trophic hormone) for a branch office (**target gland**). The branch's product (**peripheral hormone**) is reported back to the board and the manager, who cut their memos when output is high — **negative feedback**. The **posterior pituitary** is only a warehouse: the hypothalamus makes ADH and oxytocin and ships them down its own axons for storage.

```
HYPOTHALAMUS ──(releasing hormone, e.g. TRH)──► ANTERIOR PITUITARY ──(trophic hormone, e.g. TSH)──► TARGET GLAND (thyroid)
      ▲                                                ▲                                               │
      └────────────────(−)─────────────────────────────┴───────────(−)──── PERIPHERAL HORMONE (T3, T4) ◄┘

ANTERIOR (adenohypophysis)  = makes:  GH · PRL (acidophils) · TSH · LH · FSH · ACTH (basophils)
POSTERIOR (neurohypophysis) = stores: ADH · oxytocin (made in hypothalamus, kept in Herring bodies)
EXCEPTION: prolactin is held DOWN by the hypothalamus (dopamine) → cut the stalk and PRL rises, all others fall
```

#### Posterior pituitary

- **No synthesis.**
- **Posterior pituitary also known as neurohypophysis.**
- **Only storage (Herring bodies[^ety-herring]) & release of two hormones:**
  - **ADH / vasopressin**[^ety-adh][^pre-anti][^ety-vasopressin].
  - **Oxytocin**[^ety-oxytocin].

🧠 ADH and oxytocin are made in the hypothalamus (supraoptic and paraventricular nuclei) and carried down the hypothalamohypophyseal tract inside axons; the posterior lobe is only their storage and release site.[^1]

## 🏛 Anterior Pituitary & Hypothalamus

### Anterior pituitary — cell types

**1. Acidophilic**[^ety-acidophilic][^suf-philic] **cells:**
- **Somatotrophs**[^ety-somatotroph][^pre-somato][^suf-troph] **(50 % of cells): Growth Hormone**
- **Lactotrophs**[^ety-lactotroph]**: Prolactin**[^ety-prolactin][^pre-pro]

> [!tip] GH and prolactin = **"Twin hormones"** (similar receptors & actions)
> Both are single-chain proteins from the same gene family, and both act on **cytokine receptors → JAK–STAT**.[^2]

**2. Basophilic**[^ety-basophilic] **cells:**
- **Thyrotrophs**[^ety-thyrotroph]**: Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH).**
- **Gonadotrophs**[^ety-gonadotroph][^pre-gonado]**:**
  - **Luteinizing hormone (LH)**[^ety-lh].
  - **Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)**[^ety-follicle].
- **Corticotrophs**[^ety-corticotroph][^pre-cortico]**:** contain precursor molecule **pro opio melanocortin (POMC)**[^ety-pomc].

| Cell type | Staining | Hormone |
|---|---|---|
| **Somatotrophs (50 %)** | Acidophil | **GH** |
| **Lactotrophs** | Acidophil | **Prolactin** |
| **Thyrotrophs** | Basophil | **TSH** |
| **Gonadotrophs** | Basophil | **LH, FSH** |
| **Corticotrophs** | Basophil | **POMC → ACTH** (+ MSH, endorphin) |

### Negative feedback system

```
Hypothalamus  ◄──────────────(−)──────────────┐
     │                                          │
Releasing hormones (TRH)                        │
     │ (+)                                      │
Anterior pituitary  ◄──────────(−)──────────────┤
     │                                          │
Trophic hormones (TSH)                          │
     │ (+)                                      │
Target glands (Thyroid)                         │
     │                                          │
Peripheral hormones (T3, T4) ───────────────────┘
```

- **Hypothalamus → releasing hormones (TRH) → (+) anterior pituitary → trophic**[^ety-trophic] **hormones (TSH) → (+) target glands (thyroid) → peripheral**[^pre-peri] **hormones (T3, T4)** → (−) back on the anterior pituitary and the hypothalamus.
- **Negative feedback**[^ety-feedback] **system** — the end hormone switches off its own drive at both higher levels.

### POMC

```
                         POMC
                           │ produces
        ┌──────────────────┼──────────────────┐
     (Opio)             (Melano)            (Cortin)
   Endorphin    Melanocyte stimulating        ACTH
                    hormone (MSH)
```

- **POMC produces:** **(Opio) → Endorphin**[^ety-endorphin] · **(Melano) → Melanocyte**[^ety-melanocyte][^pre-melano][^suf-cyte] **stimulating hormone (MSH)** · **(Cortin) → ACTH**[^ety-acth].
- 🧠 The name is the recipe: *pro-* (precursor) + *opio-* (endorphin, the body's opioid) + *melano-* (MSH) + *cortin* (ACTH) — one molecule cut into three families.

> [!danger] Applied Aspect — **Addison's disease**[^ety-addison]
> **Pathologically ↑↑↑ ACTH** → **action of MSH** → **skin hyperpigmentation**[^ety-hyperpigmentation][^pre-hyper].
>
> 🧠 In primary adrenal failure, cortisol is low, so feedback is lost and the pituitary pours out POMC; ACTH (and the MSH made alongside it — ACTH itself carries the MSH sequence) stimulates melanocytes.[^3]

```
Addison's disease → ↑↑↑ ACTH (pathologically) → action of MSH → SKIN HYPERPIGMENTATION
```

## 💪 Anterior Pituitary Hormones

### 1. Growth Hormone

- **Maximum levels at adolescence**[^ety-adolescence].
- **Minimum levels at senescence**[^ety-senescence] **(old age).**

#### Regulation

```
                        Hypothalamus  ◄──────────────────────────────┐
                      ┌──────┴──────┐                                 │
      GH-releasing hormone (GHRH)   Somatostatin                      │
                 │ (+)                   │ (−)                        │
Ghrelin ──(+)──► SOMATOTROPHS IN ANTERIOR PITUITARY ◄──────(−)────────┤
                           │                                          │
                  Growth Hormone (GH) ──► Direct actions              │
                           │                                          │
                         Liver                                        │
                           │                                          │
        Insulin Like Growth Factor-1 (IGF-1) or Somatomedin-C ────────┘
                           │
                    Indirect actions
```

- Hypothalamus → **GHRH**[^ety-ghrh] **(+)** and **somatostatin (−)** → **somatotrophs in anterior pituitary**.
- **Ghrelin**[^ety-ghrelin] **(+)** → somatotrophs.
- **GH → direct actions**; GH → **liver** → **Insulin Like Growth Factor-1 (IGF-1) or Somatomedin-C**[^ety-somatomedin] → **indirect actions**.
- **IGF-1** feeds back to the **hypothalamus** and **(−)** on the **somatotrophs**.[^4]

| Drug form of | Drug | Action | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GHRH** | **Sermorelin**[^ety-sermorelin] | ↑ GH release | **Rx of pituitary dwarfism**[^ety-dwarfism] |
| **Somatostatin** | **Octreotide**[^ety-octreotide][^pre-octa] | (−) GH release | **Rx of acromegaly** |

#### Factors affecting secretion

| ↑ Growth hormone secretion | ↓ Growth hormone secretion |
|---|---|
| **1. Hypoglycemia**[^ety-hypoglycaemia][^suf-aemia] → GH release → ↑ blood glucose (**diabetogenic hormone**[^ety-diabetogenic][^suf-genic]) • **Fasting** • **Starvation** • **Exercise** • **Stress** | **1. Hyperglycemia** |
| **2. Deep sleep (NREM stage III & IV)** | **2. Somatostatin** · **3. IGF-1** · **4. REM sleep** |

🧠 GH is a "fasting and sleeping" hormone: it rises when fuel is short (hypoglycaemia, fasting, exercise, stress) to spare glucose and burn fat, and pulses in slow-wave sleep.[^5]

#### Actions

```
GH ──acts on──► Cytokine receptor ──activates──► Janus kinase
└──────────────────── JAK – STAT pathway ─────────────────────┘
```

- **GH acts on cytokine receptor**[^ety-cytokine] → **activates Janus kinase**[^ety-janus][^suf-ase] → **JAK – STAT pathway**[^ety-stat].
- **Direct actions: mediated by GH (anti-insulin like actions):**
  - **Lipolysis**[^ety-lipolysis][^pre-lipo][^suf-lysis].
  - **Bone growth: prechondrocytes → chondrocytes**[^ety-chondrocyte][^pre-chondro][^pre-pre].
- **Indirect actions: mediated by IGF-1 (insulin like actions):**
  - **Bone growth: all remaining actions at level of bone.**

#### Disorders

```
                           Disorders
              ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
           Children                           Adults
        ┌──────┴──────┐                          │
  ↑ GH: Gigantism   ↓ GH: Dwarfism          Acromegaly
                                (d/t GH-releasing pituitary micro-adenoma)
```

- **Children: ↑ GH → gigantism**[^ety-gigantism]**; ↓ GH → dwarfism.**
- **Adults → acromegaly**[^ety-acromegaly][^pre-acro][^suf-megaly] **(d/t GH-releasing pituitary micro-adenoma**[^ety-adenoma][^suf-oma][^pre-micro]**)**.[^6]

#### Acromegaly — features

| Bones & soft tissue | Other |
|---|---|
| **Bony overgrowth: frontal bossing**[^ety-bossing] | **Carpal tunnel syndrome**[^ety-carpal] |
| **↑ Hand + foot size** | **Sleep apnea**[^ety-apnoea][^pre-a] **(d/t upper airway obstruction)** |
| **Prognathism**[^ety-prognathism]**: mandibular enlargement** | **Diabetes mellitus** |
| **↑ Heel pad thickness (d/t soft tissue overgrowth)** | — |

*Acromegalic facies: frontal bossing, a widened nose, prominent cheekbones and mandibular enlargement (prognathism) — features from the page's list, not a figure on the page (Wikimedia Commons).*

#### Treatment

```
                          Treatment
              ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
          Surgical                    Pharmacological
 Trans-sphenoidal resection     ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
     of microadenoma     Somatostatin analogues     GH receptor antagonist
                              Octreotide                Pegvisomant
```

- **Surgical: Trans-sphenoidal**[^ety-sphenoid][^pre-trans] **resection**[^ety-resection] **of microadenoma.**
- **Pharmacological:**
  - **Somatostatin analogues: Octreotide.**
  - **GH receptor antagonist**[^ety-antagonist]**: Pegvisomant**[^ety-pegvisomant].

> [!warning] **Laron dwarfism**[^ety-laron] **/ GH insensitivity syndrome**
> - **Etiology: defective receptors** (GH receptor).
> - **Hormone levels: (N) / ↑↑ GH, ↓ plasma levels of IGF-1.**
> - **Rx: Mecasermin**[^ety-mecasermin] **(recombinant form of IGF-1).**
>
> 🧠 GH is present (even high, because low IGF-1 removes the feedback) but the liver cannot respond — so giving GH is useless; you must give IGF-1 itself.

#### Recombinant[^ety-recombinant] Human Growth Hormone — used in

- **Rx of dwarfism.**
- **Rx of muscle wasting in AIDS**[^ety-aids] **patients (↑ lean body mass & ↑ muscle mass).**
- **Anti-aging Rx.**[^7]

### 2. Prolactin

- **Stimulates milk production.**

#### Regulation

```
                          Hypothalamus
               ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
   Thyrotrophin releasing hormone      Prolactin inhibiting factor
             (TRH)                            Dopamine
               │ (+)                             │ (−)
               └──► LACTOTROPHS IN ANTERIOR PITUITARY ◄──┘
               ┌ (+)                             (−) ┐
   Vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP)     Glucocorticoids, Thyroid hormones
                               │
                           Prolactin
```

- **Stimulate (+):** **TRH**, **vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP)**[^ety-vip].
- **Inhibit (−):** **prolactin inhibiting factor — dopamine**, **glucocorticoids**[^ety-glucocorticoid][^pre-gluco], **thyroid hormones**.

> [!danger] Applied Aspect — **Stalk effect**
> **Lesion at pituitary stalk → loss of releasing factors & inhibitory factors → ↑ Prolactin (↓ dopamine) (all other hormones ↓).**
>
> 🧠 Prolactin is the only anterior pituitary hormone under dominant *inhibitory* control. Cut the stalk and the brake (dopamine) is gone, so prolactin rises while every hormone that needed a releasing hormone falls.

```
Lesion at pituitary stalk
        ↓
Loss of releasing factors & inhibitory factors
        ↓
↑ Prolactin (↓ dopamine)   ·   all other anterior pituitary hormones ↓
```

#### Factors affecting secretion

| ↑ Secretion | ↓ Secretion |
|---|---|
| • **Pregnancy & lactation**[^ety-lactation] | • **Dopamine** |
| • **Sexual intercourse** | • **L-dopa**[^ety-ldopa] |
| • **Suckling of nipples** | • **Bromocriptine**[^ety-bromocriptine] |
| • **REM sleep** | — |

#### Actions

```
Prolactin ──acts on──► Cytokine receptor ──activates──► Janus kinase
└─────────────────────── JAK–STAT pathway ────────────────────────┘
```

- **Proliferation**[^ety-proliferation] **of lobulo-alveolar**[^ety-lobule] **epithelial**[^ety-epithelium][^pre-epi] **cells in breast → milk production.**
- **Negative regulator of reproductive functions: inhibit GnRH → inhibit FSH & LH** → **lactational amenorrhea**[^ety-amenorrhoea][^suf-rrhoea] **in ♀ (form of contraception**[^ety-contraception][^pre-contra]**).**
- **Loss of libido**[^ety-libido] **& ↓ spermatogenesis**[^ety-spermatogenesis][^suf-genesis] **in ♂.**

```
Prolactin → inhibit GnRH → inhibit FSH & LH → lactational amenorrhoea (♀, contraception)
                                           → loss of libido & ↓ spermatogenesis (♂)
```

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> The anterior pituitary is a **relay under two bosses**: above it, the hypothalamus decides through releasing and inhibiting hormones; below it, the peripheral hormone it ultimately controls shuts it down by **negative feedback**. Every disorder is a broken link in that loop — a tumour that ignores the brake (micro-adenoma → acromegaly), a gland that cannot answer (Laron: GH receptor defect), a target that fails so the feedback disappears (Addison → ↑↑ ACTH → pigmentation), or a cut wire (stalk lesion).
>
> Prolactin is the exception that proves the rule: it alone is held **down** (by dopamine), so losing hypothalamic input raises it while every other hormone falls. GH and prolactin — the acidophil "twins" — share the **cytokine receptor → JAK–STAT** mechanism; GH works directly (anti-insulin: lipolysis, ↑ glucose) and indirectly through liver **IGF-1** (insulin-like: growth).

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> GHRH and ghrelin stimulate, somatostatin inhibits; GH pulses in deep (NREM III–IV) sleep and with hypoglycaemia, exercise and stress; liver IGF-1 drives growth and feeds back. Dopamine keeps prolactin low except in pregnancy, lactation and suckling. Peak GH in adolescence, lowest in old age.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A GH-secreting micro-adenoma escapes feedback. In adults the epiphyses are closed, so change is slow and insidious — rings and shoes stop fitting, the heel pad thickens, the jaw protrudes; glucose intolerance appears because GH is anti-insulin. A prolactin rise from a stalk lesion or a dopamine-blocking drug first shows as subtle menstrual irregularity or low libido.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Full acromegaly: frontal bossing, prognathism, enlarged hands and feet, carpal tunnel syndrome, sleep apnoea from upper-airway soft-tissue overgrowth, diabetes mellitus. The same excess before epiphyseal fusion produces gigantism; deficiency in childhood produces dwarfism. In Addison's disease the absent cortisol feedback drives ACTH (and MSH) up until the skin darkens.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal axis → micro-adenoma / receptor defect / stalk lesion / target-gland failure → loss of feedback → hormone excess or deficiency → soft-tissue and metabolic change → overt syndrome (acromegaly, gigantism, dwarfism, galactorrhoea–amenorrhoea, pigmentation)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> The defect's level decides the treatment: remove the tumour (trans-sphenoidal resection), brake the gland (octreotide), block the target (pegvisomant), bypass the missing receptor (mecasermin in Laron) or replace the hormone (recombinant GH in dwarfism).

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Acromegaly:** "Rings and shoes no longer fit", "frontal bossing", "prognathism", "increased heel pad thickness", "carpal tunnel syndrome", "sleep apnoea", "new diabetes" → GH-secreting pituitary micro-adenoma in an adult; treat by trans-sphenoidal resection, octreotide or pegvisomant.
>
> **Gigantism vs dwarfism:** "Tall child with open epiphyses" → GH excess before fusion; "short child, low GH" → pituitary dwarfism (recombinant GH; sermorelin).
>
> **Laron dwarfism:** "Short stature with normal or high GH but low IGF-1" → GH receptor defect; treat with mecasermin (recombinant IGF-1).
>
> **Addison's disease:** "Hyperpigmentation of skin, buccal mucosa and palmar creases with hypotension" → primary adrenal failure → ↑↑ ACTH/MSH from POMC.
>
> **Stalk effect:** "Mild hyperprolactinaemia with low other pituitary hormones after a stalk lesion" → loss of dopamine inhibition.
>
> **Lactational amenorrhoea:** "Breastfeeding woman without periods" → prolactin inhibits GnRH → ↓ FSH/LH.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> A small sellar mass on MRI = micro-adenoma (< 1 cm); a widened sella and thick heel pad on X-ray = acromegaly; Herring bodies = axonal dilatations storing ADH and oxytocin in the posterior lobe.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Somatotrophs = 50 % of anterior pituitary cells; GH highest in adolescence, lowest in senescence; GH pulses in NREM III–IV and falls in REM; IGF-1 (not a random GH) reflects the 24-hour GH output.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "The posterior pituitary makes ADH and oxytocin"**
>
> **What students think:** Both lobes of the pituitary synthesise hormones.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis) is neural tissue with **no synthesis** — ADH and oxytocin are made in hypothalamic neurones, travel down the hypothalamohypophyseal tract and are only **stored (Herring bodies) and released** here. The anterior lobe (adenohypophysis) is the true gland.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Adeno = gland = makes; neuro = nerve endings = stores."
>
> **Trap 2: Expecting every pituitary hormone to fall after a stalk lesion**
>
> **What students think:** Cutting the stalk lowers all anterior pituitary hormones.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Prolactin is under dominant inhibition by dopamine. Cutting the stalk removes both the releasing hormones (so TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH, GH fall) and the dopamine brake (so prolactin rises) — the **stalk effect**.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Prolactin is the hormone the hypothalamus holds down."
>
> **Trap 3: Mixing up the direct and indirect actions of GH**
>
> **What students think:** GH is insulin-like because it promotes growth.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** GH's **direct** actions are **anti-insulin** — lipolysis and a rise in blood glucose (diabetogenic; acromegaly causes diabetes). The **insulin-like** growth actions are **indirect**, through liver IGF-1 (somatomedin C). Bone growth is split: prechondrocyte → chondrocyte differentiation is direct; all remaining bone actions are via IGF-1.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "GH itself fights insulin; its messenger IGF-1 imitates insulin."
>
> **Trap 4: Treating Laron dwarfism with growth hormone**
>
> **What students think:** Short stature with a GH problem → give GH.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In Laron syndrome the **receptor** is defective; GH is normal or ↑↑ (feedback from IGF-1 is lost), and IGF-1 is low. Extra GH has nothing to bind. Mecasermin (recombinant IGF-1) bypasses the receptor.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** High GH + low IGF-1 = receptor problem → give IGF-1.
>
> **Trap 5: Swapping the hyperglycaemia and hypoglycaemia effects on GH**
>
> **What students think:** GH rises after a meal like insulin.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** GH is a counter-regulatory (diabetogenic) hormone: **hypoglycaemia stimulates** it and **hyperglycaemia suppresses** it — the basis of the glucose-suppression test for acromegaly (GH fails to fall) and the insulin-tolerance test for GH deficiency.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** GH behaves like glucagon and cortisol, not like insulin.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **Posterior pituitary = neurohypophysis: no synthesis**, only **storage (Herring bodies) and release of ADH/vasopressin and oxytocin**; anterior = **adenohypophysis = master gland**.
> 2. **Acidophils:** **somatotrophs (50 %) → GH**, **lactotrophs → prolactin** — **"twin hormones"** (similar receptors & actions).
> 3. **Basophils:** thyrotrophs (TSH), gonadotrophs (LH, FSH), **corticotrophs (POMC)**.
> 4. **POMC → endorphin (opio) · MSH (melano) · ACTH (cortin)**; **Addison's → ↑↑↑ ACTH → MSH action → skin hyperpigmentation**.
> 5. **TRH → TSH and prolactin**; CRH → ACTH; GnRH → LH, FSH; GHRH → GH; **PIH = dopamine** (−PRL); **GHIH = somatostatin** (−GH).
> 6. GH: **max in adolescence, min in senescence**; stimulated by **GHRH, ghrelin**; inhibited by **somatostatin, IGF-1**; liver makes **IGF-1 = somatomedin C**.
> 7. **↑ GH:** hypoglycaemia (**diabetogenic**), fasting, starvation, exercise, stress, **deep sleep (NREM III & IV)**; **↓ GH:** hyperglycaemia, somatostatin, IGF-1, **REM sleep**.
> 8. GH and prolactin → **cytokine receptor → Janus kinase → JAK–STAT**.
> 9. **Direct GH actions = anti-insulin** (lipolysis, prechondrocyte → chondrocyte); **indirect via IGF-1 = insulin-like** (rest of bone growth).
> 10. **Children: ↑ GH gigantism, ↓ GH dwarfism; adults: acromegaly (GH-secreting micro-adenoma)** — frontal bossing, ↑ hand/foot, prognathism, ↑ heel pad, carpal tunnel, sleep apnoea, DM.
> 11. Drugs: **sermorelin** (GHRH analogue, dwarfism); **octreotide** (somatostatin analogue, acromegaly); **pegvisomant** (GH receptor antagonist); **trans-sphenoidal resection**.
> 12. **Laron dwarfism:** defective GH receptor, **N/↑↑ GH, ↓ IGF-1 → mecasermin**; recombinant GH: dwarfism, **AIDS wasting**, anti-ageing.
> 13. Prolactin: **TRH, VIP (+)**; **dopamine, glucocorticoids, thyroid hormones (−)**; **↑** pregnancy/lactation, intercourse, suckling, REM sleep; **↓** dopamine, L-dopa, **bromocriptine**; **stalk effect → ↑ PRL, all others ↓**; inhibits GnRH → **lactational amenorrhoea**, ↓ libido and spermatogenesis.

[^1]: ADH comes mainly from the supraoptic nucleus and oxytocin mainly from the paraventricular nucleus; each nucleus makes some of both. Herring bodies are the swellings of these axon terminals packed with neurosecretory granules.
[^2]: GH (191 amino acids) and prolactin (199) belong to the same somatotropin/prolactin family with human placental lactogen; GH can bind the prolactin receptor, which is why GH excess can cause galactorrhoea.
[^3]: ACTH(1–13) is identical to α-MSH, so high ACTH itself stimulates melanocortin-1 receptors on melanocytes. Secondary (pituitary) adrenal insufficiency has **low** ACTH and therefore **no** pigmentation — a classic differentiating point.
[^4]: Guyton: IGF-1 inhibits GH release directly at the somatotroph and indirectly by stimulating hypothalamic somatostatin; GH also feeds back on its own secretion (short-loop feedback).
[^5]: About 70 % of daily GH secretion occurs in the first bouts of slow-wave sleep. Other textbook stimulators: amino acids (arginine), glucagon, oestrogens, α-adrenergic agonists; inhibitors: free fatty acids, obesity, cortisol excess.

[^ety-basophilic]: **basophilic** — Greek *basis*, "base" + *philos*, "loving" → takes up basic (blue) dyes; thyrotrophs, gonadotrophs and corticotrophs make glycoproteins and stain blue/PAS-positive.
[^ety-thyrotroph]: **thyrotroph** — Greek *thyreos*, "oblong door-shaped shield" (the thyroid cartilage looked like one) + *trophē* → the cell that drives the thyroid (makes TSH).
[^ety-gonadotroph]: **gonadotroph** — Greek *gonē*, "seed, generation" + *-ad* + *trophē* → the cell that drives the gonads (makes LH and FSH).
[^ety-lh]: **LH (luteinizing hormone)** — Latin *luteus*, "yellow" + *-izing*, "making into" → it turns the ruptured follicle into the corpus luteum ("yellow body"); in males it drives Leydig cells to make testosterone.

[^ety-endorphin]: **endorphin** — *endo*genous + m*orphine* (Greek *Morpheus*, god of dreams) → the body's own morphine; β-endorphin is cut from the lipotropin end of POMC.
[^ety-melanocyte]: **melanocyte** — Greek *melas, melanos*, "black" + *kytos*, "hollow vessel, cell" → the pigment cell of the skin; MSH (*melanocyte-stimulating hormone*) makes it produce melanin.
[^ety-acth]: **ACTH (adrenocorticotropic hormone)** — Latin *ad-* "near" + *ren* "kidney" (adrenal) + *cortex* "bark" + Greek *tropos* "turning" → the hormone that turns on the adrenal cortex. Also called corticotropin.
[^ety-addison]: **Addison's disease** — Thomas Addison (1793–1860), English physician at Guy's Hospital, who described primary adrenal insufficiency with bronzing of the skin in 1855.
[^ety-hyperpigmentation]: **hyperpigmentation** — Greek *hyper-*, "over" + Latin *pigmentum*, "colouring matter, paint" (*pingere*, to paint) + *-ation* → excessive colouring. **Differentiate:** *hyper*pigmentation (Addison, ↑ melanin) · *hypo*pigmentation (less) · *de*pigmentation (complete loss — vitiligo).

[^ety-somatostatin]: **somatostatin** — Greek *sōma*, "body" + *statos*, "standing, stopped" + *-in* → the hormone that stops body (growth hormone) release; discovered by Brazeau and Guillemin (1973). It is also the δ-cell "universal inhibitor" of the pancreas and gut.
[^ety-adolescence]: **adolescence** — Latin *ad-*, "towards" + *olescere*, "to grow" (*alere*, to nourish) + *-ence* → the state of growing up. **Differentiate:** *adolescence* (growing, max GH) · *adult* (the past participle *adultus*, "grown up") · *senescence* (growing old).
[^ety-senescence]: **senescence** — Latin *senex*, "old man" + *-escere*, "to begin to become" → the process of growing old; GH output falls to its minimum (somatopause). Same root as *senile*, *senate*.
[^ety-ghrh]: **GHRH (growth hormone–releasing hormone, somatocrinin)** — English *release* from Latin *relaxare*, "to loosen" → the hypothalamic peptide that loosens (releases) GH from somatotrophs.

[^ety-lipolysis]: **lipolysis** — Greek *lipos*, "fat" + *lysis*, "loosening, breaking down" → breakdown of stored triglyceride into free fatty acids and glycerol. **Differentiate:** *lipolysis* (breaking fat) · *lipogenesis* (making fat) · *lipoprotein* (a fat–protein carrier).
[^ety-chondrocyte]: **chondrocyte** — Greek *chondros*, "cartilage, grain" + *kytos*, "cell" → a cartilage cell; *prechondrocyte* (Latin *prae-*, "before") is its precursor. **Differentiate:** *chondro-* (cartilage) · *osteo-* (bone) · *chondroblast* (immature, building) vs *chondrocyte* (mature, embedded).
[^ety-gigantism]: **gigantism** — Greek *gigas, gigantos*, "giant" + *-ism*, "condition" → excessive linear growth from GH excess **before** epiphyseal fusion. **Differentiate** from *acromegaly* (after fusion — width, not height).
[^ety-acromegaly]: **acromegaly** — Greek *akron*, "extremity, tip" + *megas, megalou*, "great" + *-y* → enlargement of the extremities (hands, feet, jaw, nose); named by Pierre Marie (1886).
[^ety-adenoma]: **adenoma** — Greek *adēn*, "gland" + *-ōma*, "tumour, swelling" → a benign tumour of glandular epithelium; *micro-adenoma* < 1 cm.
[^ety-bossing]: **frontal bossing** — Latin *frons, frontis*, "forehead" + Old French *boce*, "a swelling, a hump" → a prominent, bulging forehead from overgrowth of the frontal bone and sinuses.
[^ety-carpal]: **carpal tunnel syndrome** — Greek *karpos*, "wrist" + English *tunnel* (Old French *tonel*, a cask) + Greek *syn-* "together" + *dromos* "running" → a set of symptoms that run together from compression of the median nerve in the wrist's tunnel (soft-tissue overgrowth in acromegaly).
[^ety-apnoea]: **apnoea** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *pnoia*, "breathing" → a pause in breathing (≥ 10 s). **Differentiate:** *obstructive* sleep apnoea (airway collapses — acromegaly's thick soft tissue and large tongue) · *central* sleep apnoea (no drive from the brainstem) · *dyspnoea* (difficult breathing).
[^ety-prognathism]: **prognathism** — Greek *pro-*, "forward" + *gnathos*, "jaw" + *-ism* → a jaw that juts forward; in acromegaly the mandible keeps growing.
[^ety-sphenoid]: **trans-sphenoidal** — Latin *trans-*, "across, through" + Greek *sphēn*, "wedge" + *-oid*, "like" → through the wedge-shaped sphenoid bone (and its sinus), the route to the sella turcica.
[^ety-resection]: **resection** — Latin *re-*, "back, again" + *secare*, "to cut" + *-ion* → cutting out part of an organ or a tumour. **Differentiate:** *resection* (part removed) · *excision* (cut out completely) · *incision* (cut into) · *dissection* (cut apart to expose).
[^ety-antagonist]: **antagonist** — Greek *anti-*, "against" + *agōnistēs*, "contender" → a drug that binds a receptor without activating it, blocking the agonist. **Differentiate:** *agonist* (activates) · *antagonist* (blocks) · *inverse agonist* (does the opposite).
[^ety-pegvisomant]: **pegvisomant** — **PEG** (polyethylene glycol, to prolong its half-life) + *-vis-* + *-som-* (from somatotropin) + *-ant* (antagonist stem) → a pegylated GH analogue that blocks GH-receptor dimerisation, so IGF-1 falls.
[^ety-laron]: **Laron syndrome** — Zvi Laron (1927–), Israeli paediatric endocrinologist, who described it in 1966 in children with high GH but low IGF-1 (GH insensitivity).
[^ety-mecasermin]: **mecasermin** — a coined name for recombinant human IGF-1 (the stem *-sermin* marks IGF analogues); used for primary IGF-1 deficiency (Laron syndrome).
[^ety-recombinant]: **recombinant** — Latin *re-*, "again" + *com-*, "together" + *binare*, "to join in pairs" + *-ant* → recombined; made by joining the human gene into bacterial or yeast DNA (recombinant DNA technology).
[^ety-aids]: **AIDS** — **A**cquired **I**mmuno**d**eficiency **S**yndrome: Latin *immunis*, "exempt" + *deficere*, "to fail"; GH's anabolic action counters the wasting (cachexia) of advanced HIV.
[^ety-vip]: **VIP (vasoactive intestinal peptide)** — Latin *vas*, "vessel" + *activus*, "active" + *intestinum*, "gut" → a gut and brain peptide that dilates vessels; it also releases prolactin.
[^ety-glucocorticoid]: **glucocorticoid** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" + Latin *cortex*, "bark" + Greek *-oeidēs*, "like" → an adrenal-cortex steroid that raises glucose (cortisol). **Differentiate:** *glucocorticoid* (zona fasciculata, glucose) · *mineralocorticoid* (zona glomerulosa, salt — aldosterone).
[^ety-lactation]: **lactation** — Latin *lactare*, "to suckle, give milk" (*lac*, milk) + *-ation* → the process of milk secretion. **Differentiate:** *lactation* / *galactopoiesis* (making milk — prolactin) vs *milk ejection / let-down* (expelling it — oxytocin).
[^ety-ldopa]: **L-dopa (levodopa)** — **L**- (*laevus*, left-handed isomer) + **d**ihydr**o**xy**p**heny**la**lanine → the precursor of dopamine; it raises dopamine and so lowers prolactin.
[^ety-bromocriptine]: **bromocriptine** — *bromo-* (Greek *brōmos*, "stench" — bromine) + ergo*cryptine* (Greek *kryptos*, "hidden" — an ergot alkaloid) → a brominated ergot dopamine agonist used for prolactinoma and to stop lactation.
[^ety-proliferation]: **proliferation** — Latin *proles*, "offspring" + *ferre*, "to bear" + *-ation* → bearing offspring; rapid multiplication of cells. **Differentiate:** *proliferation* (more cells) · *hypertrophy* (bigger cells) · *hyperplasia* (more cells in an organ, the result) · *differentiation* (cells specialising).
[^ety-lobule]: **lobulo-alveolar** — Latin *lobulus*, "small lobe" + *alveolus*, "small hollow" → the milk-secreting units of the breast: alveoli grouped into lobules.
[^ety-epithelium]: **epithelium** — Greek *epi-*, "upon" + *thēlē*, "nipple, teat" → originally the thin skin over the lip's papillae (Ruysch); now any sheet of cells covering a surface or lining a gland.
[^ety-amenorrhoea]: **amenorrhoea** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *mēn*, "month" + *rhoia*, "flow" → absence of menstrual flow. **Differentiate:** *amenorrhoea* (none) · *oligomenorrhoea* (infrequent) · *menorrhagia* (heavy) · *dysmenorrhoea* (painful).
[^ety-contraception]: **contraception** — Latin *contra-*, "against" + (con)*ception* (*concipere*, "to take in, conceive") → prevention of conception; lactational amenorrhoea is a natural method in exclusive breastfeeding.
[^ety-libido]: **libido** — Latin, "desire, longing" (*libet*, it pleases) → sexual drive; popularised by Freud. High prolactin suppresses GnRH and testosterone/oestrogen → ↓ libido.
[^ety-spermatogenesis]: **spermatogenesis** — Greek *sperma*, "seed" + *genesis*, "origin, production" → production of sperm. **Differentiate:** *spermatogenesis* (the whole process) · *spermiogenesis* (spermatid → spermatozoon, the last step) · *spermiation* (release into the lumen).
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypophysis, hypothalamus, hypoglycaemia. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep · **sub- / infra-** (Latin equivalents).
[^pre-adeno]: **adeno-** — gland (Greek *adēn*): adenohypophysis, adenoma, adenoids. Don't confuse with **adreno-** adrenal gland (adrenocorticotropic) · **andro-** male.
[^pre-neuro]: **neuro-** — nerve (Greek *neuron*): neurohypophysis, neurosecretion, neuroendocrine. Don't confuse with **nephro-** kidney · **neo-** new.
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endocrine, endorphin (*endo*genous), endothelium. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (exocrine) · **ecto-** outer · **epi-** upon.
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: antidiuretic, antagonist, anti-insulin, anti-aging. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (antenatal) · **ana-** up, again.
[^suf-philic]: **-philic / -phil** — loving, having affinity for: acidophilic, basophilic, eosinophil, hydrophilic. Don't confuse with **-phobic / -phobe** fearing (chromophobe, hydrophobic) · **-phyll** leaf (chlorophyll).
[^pre-somato]: **somato- / soma-** — body (Greek *sōma*): somatotroph, somatostatin, somatomedin, somatic. Don't confuse with **soma-** in *chromosome* (*-some*, body as a suffix) · **somno-** sleep (Latin *somnus*).
[^suf-troph]: **-troph / -trophic / -trophy** — nourishment, growth: somatotroph, trophic hormone, hypertrophy, atrophy. Don't confuse with **-tropic / -tropin** turning towards, stimulating (thyrotropin, gonadotropin) · **-trope** a turning.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, forward, for, on behalf of: prolactin (for milk), POMC (precursor), prognathism (forward jaw), proliferation (offspring). Don't confuse with **pre-** before in time or place (prechondrocyte) · **post-** after.
[^pre-gonado]: **gonado-** — gonad, organ of generation (Greek *gonē*, seed): gonadotroph, gonadotropin. Don't confuse with **gono-** in *gonorrhoea* (*gonos*, seed) · **gonio-** angle (gonioscopy).
[^pre-cortico]: **cortico-** — cortex, outer layer (Latin *cortex*, bark): corticotroph, corticotropin, glucocorticoid. Don't confuse with **corpo-** body (*corpus*) · **cardio-** heart.
[^pre-peri]: **peri-** — around; *peripheral* = around the edge (Greek *periphereia*, "the line around"): peripheral hormones, peripheral conversion, pericardium. Don't confuse with **para-** beside · **pre-** before.
[^pre-melano]: **melano-** — black, dark (Greek *melas*): melanocyte, melanin, melanocortin, melanoma. Don't confuse with **mela-** in *melaena* (black stool — same root) · **meli-** honey (diabetes mellitus).
[^suf-cyte]: **-cyte** — cell (Greek *kytos*, hollow vessel): melanocyte, chondrocyte, lymphocyte. Don't confuse with **-blast** immature cell (chondroblast) · **-clast** breaker (osteoclast).
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, above, excessive: hyperpigmentation, hyperglycaemia, hyperprolactinaemia. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra- / super-** above (Latin equivalents).
[^suf-tropin]: **-tropin / -tropic** — "turning towards", a stimulating hormone: thyrotropin, corticotropin, gonadotropin, somatotropin. Don't confuse with **-trophin / -trophic** nourishing (used interchangeably for pituitary hormones) · **-tropism** a growth response (phototropism).
[^suf-statin]: **-statin / -stat** — stopping, holding still (Greek *statos*): somatostatin, thermostat. Don't confuse with the drug class **statins** (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors, from *-statin* "stopping" cholesterol synthesis — same idea, different target) · **-stasis** standing still.
[^pre-octa]: **oct- / octa- / octo-** — eight: octreotide (8 amino acids), octet. Don't confuse with **oxy-** sharp, swift, oxygen · **ocul- / oculo-** eye.
[^suf-aemia]: **-aemia / -emia** — a state of the blood: hypoglycaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperprolactinaemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** a state of the urine (glycosuria).
[^suf-genic]: **-genic** — producing, caused by: diabetogenic, goitrogenic, iatrogenic. Don't confuse with **-genesis** the process of making (spermatogenesis) · **-genous** born of (endogenous).
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: kinase, phosphatase, lipase, ATPase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar · **-ate** a salt.
[^pre-lipo]: **lipo-** — fat (Greek *lipos*): lipolysis, lipogenesis, lipoprotein. Don't confuse with **lipo-** in *lipothymia* (*leipein*, to fail — fainting) · **litho-** stone.
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis / -lytic** — loosening, breaking down: lipolysis, glycolysis, proteolysis. Don't confuse with **-lithiasis** stones · **-ptosis** drooping.
[^pre-chondro]: **chondro-** — cartilage (Greek *chondros*, grain, cartilage): chondrocyte, chondroitin, achondroplasia. Don't confuse with **chondrio-** grain in *mitochondria* (same root, different sense) · **chorio-** membrane.
[^pre-pre]: **pre- (prae-)** — before (in time or place): prechondrocyte, precursor, preganglionic. Don't confuse with **pro-** forward, for · **post-** after · **peri-** around.
[^pre-acro]: **acro-** — tip, extremity, height (Greek *akron*): acromegaly, acromion, acrocyanosis, acrophobia. Don't confuse with **acu-** needle, sharp (acupuncture) · **agro-** field.
[^suf-megaly]: **-megaly** — enlargement (Greek *megas*, great): acromegaly, hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, cardiomegaly. Don't confuse with **mega- / megalo-** as prefix (megaloblast) · **-malacia** softening.
[^suf-oma]: **-oma** — tumour, swelling: adenoma, prolactinoma, insulinoma, lipoma. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition · **-ema** a state (oedema, emphysema — not a tumour).
[^pre-micro]: **micro-** — small (Greek *mikros*): micro-adenoma (< 1 cm), microscope. Don't confuse with **macro-** large (macro-adenoma ≥ 1 cm) · **mega-** great.
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — without, not: apnoea, amenorrhoea, anaemia. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from · **ad-** towards · **ana-** up.
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: trans-sphenoidal, transducer, transport. Don't confuse with **trachy-** rough · **tri-** three · **dia-** through (Greek equivalent).
[^pre-gluco]: **gluco- / glyco-** — sweet, sugar (Greek *glykys*): glucocorticoid, glucagon, glycogen. Don't confuse with **glyc-** in *glycine* (sweet amino acid — same root) · **gluteo-** buttock.
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, over: epithelium, epiphysis, epinephrine. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **para-** beside · **peri-** around.
[^suf-rrhoea]: **-rrhoea / -rrhea** — flow, discharge: amenorrhoea, galactorrhoea, diarrhoea. Don't confuse with **-rrhage** bursting forth (haemorrhage) · **-rrhaphy** suturing.
[^pre-contra]: **contra-** — against, opposite: contraception, contralateral, contraindication. Don't confuse with **con- / com-** together · **counter-** against (countercurrent — the French-derived form of the same word).
[^suf-genesis]: **-genesis** — origin, production: spermatogenesis, lipogenesis, gluconeogenesis. Don't confuse with **-genic** producing (adjective: diabetogenic) · **-gnosis** knowledge (diagnosis).
