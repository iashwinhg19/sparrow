# 🦋 Thyroid Gland

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The thyroid[^ety-thyroid][^suf-oid] is a butterfly-shaped gland in front of the trachea whose **follicular cells** make **T3 and T4** from **tyrosine + iodine**, and whose **parafollicular C cells** make **calcitonin**. Its hormone is the body's **metabolic accelerator** — it raises BMR and heat, speeds catabolism, builds the developing brain and bones, and sensitises the heart to catecholamines. The chapter answers:
> - ✅ **How is T3/T4 made?** — NIS traps iodide → pendrin moves it to the lumen → TPO oxidises and organifies it → coupling on thyroglobulin → stored in colloid
> - ✅ **How is it carried and how does it act?** — 99 % protein-bound (TBG highest affinity), 1 % free; T4 → T3 by deiodinase; T3 binds a **nuclear receptor**
> - ✅ **What does it do?** — nine actions, each with a hyper- and hypothyroid picture

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Factory, a Warehouse and a Thermostat Set by the Boss

The follicle is a **factory with a warehouse in the middle**. Iodide is pumped in at the back door (NIS), pushed through the front door into the warehouse (pendrin), "activated" by the foreman (TPO) and bolted onto a conveyor protein (thyroglobulin) — then finished T4/T3 is **stored for 2–3 months** in the colloid warehouse. The boss (pituitary TSH) decides how fast the factory runs; finished product in the blood tells the boss to slow down. Out in the body, **T4 is the shipping form and T3 the working form** — deiodinase unpacks it at the destination.

```
BLOOD ─NIS (2° active, Na+–I−)─► FOLLICULAR CELL ─pendrin (I−/Cl−)─► LUMEN (colloid)
                                                        I− ─TPO─► I2 ─organification─► MIT, DIT on Tg
                                                        MIT+DIT = T3 · DIT+DIT = T4 · DIT+MIT = rT3 (inactive)
                                                        STORAGE: colloid = reserve for 2–3 months

TRH → TSH → ↑ vascularity + ↑ iodide trapping → ↑ T3, T4 ──(−)──► pituitary & hypothalamus
BLOOD: 99 % bound (TBG highest affinity · albumin · transthyretin) · 1 % FREE = active
TARGET: T4 ─deiodinase (selenocysteine)─► T3 → NUCLEAR receptor → ↑ BMR, heat, catabolism, growth, heart, lungs, gut
```

## 🧪 Thyroid Hormone

### Synthesis[^ety-synthesis][^pre-syn]

#### Raw materials

- **Tyrosine**[^ety-tyrosine][^suf-ine]**: from thyroglobulin (Tg)**[^ety-thyroglobulin][^suf-in].
- **Iodine**[^ety-iodine]**: present as iodide in diet.**[^1]

![[Thyroid Gland - hormone synthesis in the follicular cell.png]]
*The follicular cell between blood (left) and lumen (right). Basolateral end: Na⁺–I⁻ symporter brings in I⁻ with Na⁺; Na⁺-K⁺ ATPase (3Na⁺ out, 2K⁺ in) maintains low Na⁺ inside the cell. Apical end: pendrin (I⁻ out, Cl⁻ in), TPO: I⁻ (reduced) → I₂ (oxidized) → organification onto thyroglobulin → MIT, DIT → coupling reactions (MIT + DIT = T3, DIT + DIT = T4, DIT + MIT = rT3, inactive) → storage in colloid, reserve 2–3 months (cropped from the handwritten page).*

![Thyroid hormone synthesis — iodide uptake, oxidation, iodination of thyroglobulin, coupling, endocytosis and release of T3 and T4 (Wikimedia Commons)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Thyroid_hormone_synthesis.svg/800px-Thyroid_hormone_synthesis.svg.png)
*The same pathway drawn step by step, including the later steps the page leaves out — endocytosis of colloid, proteolysis of thyroglobulin and release of T4 and T3 into the blood (Mikael Häggström, Wikimedia Commons).*

| Step | Where | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Iodide trapping** | Basolateral end | **Na⁺–I⁻ symporter (NIS)**; Na⁺-K⁺ **ATPase** (3Na⁺ out, 2K⁺ in) **maintains low Na⁺ inside the cell** |
| **Efflux into lumen** | Apical end | **Pendrin**: I⁻ out, Cl⁻ in |
| **Oxidation** | Apical membrane | **I⁻ (reduced) —TPO→ I₂ (oxidized)**[^ety-oxidation] |
| **Organification**[^ety-organification] | Lumen, on Tg | I₂ attaches to tyrosines of **thyroglobulin** → MIT, DIT |
| **Coupling reactions** | On Tg | **MIT + DIT = T3** · **DIT + DIT = T4** · **DIT + MIT = rT3 (inactive)**[^2] |
| **Storage** | **Colloid** | **Reserve 2–3 months** |

### Mechanism

#### 1. Sodium Iodide Symporter (NIS)[^ety-symporter]

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | **Basolateral**[^ety-basolateral] **end of follicular cells** |
| **Function** | **Iodide trapping** |
| **Type** | **2° active transport**[^ety-secondary-active] |
| **Other locations** | **Salivary gland, mammary gland**[^ety-mammary]**, placenta**[^ety-placenta] |

> [!warning] **Wolff–Chaikoff effect**[^ety-wolff-chaikoff]
> - **Concept: excess iodide through NIS → inhibits organification → ↓ T3, T4.**
> - **Use: Rx with Lugol's iodine**[^ety-lugol] **prior to thyroid Sx.**
>
> 🧠 A flood of iodide paradoxically switches the factory off for a few days — useful to shrink and quieten a toxic gland before surgery.[^3]

```
Excess iodide through NIS → inhibits organification → ↓ T3, T4
       └── Use: Lugol's iodine before thyroid surgery
```

#### 2. Iodide–chloride antiporter[^pre-anti] / pendrin[^ety-pendrin]

- **Locations: thyroid gland, inner ear.**
- **Mutation:**
  - **Goiter**[^ety-goitre].
  - **Sensorineural**[^ety-sensorineural] **hearing loss.**
  - → together = **Pendred syndrome**[^ety-pendred].

#### 3. Thyroid Peroxidase (TPO)[^ety-peroxidase][^pre-per][^suf-ase]

- **Function: reduced iodide → oxidised I₂ → T3, T4.**
- **Anti-thyroid drugs** block this step (✗ on the arrow): **Propylthiouracil**[^ety-ptu]**, carbimazole**[^ety-carbimazole]**, methimazole.**

```
Reduced iodide ──TPO──✗──► oxidised I2 ──► T3, T4
                   ▲
     Anti-thyroid drugs: propylthiouracil, carbimazole, methimazole
```

### Regulation

**Negative feedback control system: actions of thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH):**

```
↑ vascularity of gland      ↑ iodide trapping
            └───────────┬───────────┘
                ↑ T3, T4 synthesis
```

- TSH → **↑ vascularity**[^ety-vascularity][^pre-vaso] **of gland** + **↑ iodide trapping** → **↑ T3, T4 synthesis**.

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Thyroid hormone is the body's **gas pedal**: by binding a **nuclear receptor**, T3 switches on genes that raise oxygen consumption, heat production (UCP1), catabolism, cardiac β-sensitivity, gut motility and — in the fetus and infant — brain myelination and bone growth. Every clinical sign is that pedal pressed too hard (heat intolerance, tachycardia, weight loss, thyroid storm) or released (cold intolerance, high cholesterol, myxoedema, constipation, cretinism).
>
> Making it is an **iodine-trapping assembly line** with named, testable steps — NIS (2° active, blocked by excess iodide = Wolff–Chaikoff), pendrin (Pendred syndrome), TPO (blocked by thionamides), coupling on thyroglobulin and a 2–3-month colloid store. In the blood **T4 is the long-lived, tightly bound reservoir** (TBG, t½ 7 days) and **T3 the potent, short-lived active form** made by selenium-containing deiodinase in the tissues; only the **1 % free** hormone acts.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> TRH → TSH keeps the gland vascular and trapping iodide; T3/T4 feed back on the pituitary and hypothalamus. 99 % of hormone is protein-bound, 1 % free; T4 is converted to T3 in the tissues. BMR, heat production, cholesterol, heart rate and bowel habit sit in the normal range.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> When the gland begins to fail (iodine deficiency, autoimmune damage, a pendrin defect), falling T4 releases the pituitary from feedback; TSH rises, the gland becomes more vascular and enlarges (goitre) and keeps T3/T4 near normal — subclinical hypothyroidism with only mild fatigue or a rising cholesterol. In early overactivity a patient notices heat intolerance and palpitations while values are only slightly high.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Hypothyroidism: cold intolerance, weight gain, constipation, hypercholesterolaemia and atherosclerosis, puffy face and non-pitting myxoedema from GAG accumulation; in the newborn, cretinism with low IQ and stunted growth. Hyperthyroidism: heat intolerance, tachycardia, ↑ stroke volume and contractility, ↓ peripheral resistance, tachypnoea, diarrhoea — and in thyroid storm a life-threatening adrenergic crisis treated with propranolol.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal axis → gland injury or overstimulation → feedback change in TSH → goitre / compensated (subclinical) state → overt hypo- or hyperthyroidism → myxoedema coma or thyroid storm
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Each synthesis step is a drug target or a disease: thionamides block TPO, Lugol's iodine exploits Wolff–Chaikoff before surgery, β-blockers blunt the catecholamine sensitisation, and early neonatal screening and replacement prevent the most common reversible cause of mental retardation.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Pendred syndrome:** "Goitre + sensorineural hearing loss" → pendrin (iodide–chloride antiporter) mutation in thyroid and inner ear.
>
> **Wolff–Chaikoff effect:** "Excess iodide transiently stops hormone synthesis", "Lugol's iodine before thyroidectomy" → iodide excess inhibits organification.
>
> **Hypothyroidism:** "Cold intolerance, constipation, weight gain, high cholesterol, puffy face, non-pitting oedema" → loss of thermogenic, catabolic and GAG-clearing actions of T3.
>
> **Congenital hypothyroidism (cretinism):** "Neonate with low IQ, stunted growth, large tongue" → T3 needed for CNS myelination and bone growth — the commonest reversible cause of mental retardation.
>
> **Hyperthyroidism / thyroid storm:** "Heat intolerance, tachycardia, palpitations, fever, agitation after surgery or infection" → ↑ cardiac responsiveness to norepinephrine (β-receptor mediated) → treat with propranolol.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Histology: follicles full of colloid lined by follicular cells (T3, T4), with pale parafollicular C cells (calcitonin). A goitre = enlarged gland from TSH drive or defective synthesis.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> T3 half-life 1 day vs T4 7 days; colloid reserve 2–3 months; 99 % bound / 1 % free; organs that do not raise O₂ consumption — adult brain, testes, uterus, spleen.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "T4 is the active hormone because the gland makes more of it"**
>
> **What students think:** T4, being the main secretory product, is the hormone that acts.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** T4 is more plentiful in colloid and longer-lived (tight binding, t½ 7 days) but **least potent** — it is a prohormone/reservoir. Tissue deiodinase (with selenocysteine) converts it to **T3, the most potent, rapid-acting** form that binds the nuclear receptor. Actions of thyroid hormone are mediated by T3.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "T4 = travel form (and drug form); T3 = work form."
>
> **Trap 2: Thinking excess iodine always causes more hormone**
>
> **What students think:** More iodide → more T3/T4.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Above a threshold, excess iodide entering through NIS **inhibits organification** (Wolff–Chaikoff effect) and T3/T4 fall for a few days — the basis for Lugol's iodine before thyroid surgery. Normal glands then "escape" from the block.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Remember the exam pair: Wolff–Chaikoff = iodide inhibits; Jod–Basedow = iodide load causes hyperthyroidism in an iodine-deficient nodular gland.
>
> **Trap 3: Saying T3 raises O₂ consumption in every organ**
>
> **What students think:** BMR rises because every tissue burns more oxygen.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The calorigenic effect spares the **adult brain, testes, uterus and spleen** (plus, in many texts, lymph nodes and anterior pituitary). The fetal/infant brain, in contrast, depends on T3 for myelination.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "B-T-U-S" — **B**rain (adult), **T**estes, **U**terus, **S**pleen are spared.
>
> **Trap 4: Calling the heart effect a direct inotropic hormone action only**
>
> **What students think:** T3 itself is what speeds the heart in thyrotoxicosis.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The page stresses **↑ responsiveness to circulating catecholamines** — T3 up-regulates β-receptors, so normal levels of norepinephrine produce tachycardia, ↑ stroke volume and contractility. That is why a **β-blocker (propranolol)** is the immediate treatment of thyroid storm, before antithyroid drugs act.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Thyroid storm = "adrenergic storm" → block β first.
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing up which binding protein matters most**
>
> **What students think:** Albumin binds most T4 because it is most abundant, so it is the key carrier.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Albumin has the greatest capacity but the lowest affinity; **TBG has the highest affinity** and carries most T4; transthyretin (pre-albumin) is intermediate. Changes in TBG (pregnancy, oestrogen) change total T4 but not free T4.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "TBG — Tightest Binding Globulin."

> 10. **T3:** most potent, t½ **1 day**, less bound, rapid, physiological. **T4:** least potent, t½ **7 days**, tight binding, long acting, more in colloid, **drug form**.
> 11. **T4 → T3 by deiodinase (21st amino acid: selenocysteine)**; actions **mediated by T3**.
> 12. **↑ BMR** except **adult brain, testes, uterus, spleen**; **UCP1** thermogenesis → heat/cold intolerance; catabolism (glycogenolysis, lipolysis, proteolysis, **↓ cholesterol**).
> 13. Hypothyroidism → **myxoedema** (GAGs), **↑ cholesterol → atherosclerosis**, constipation; **congenital hypothyroidism = m/c reversible cause of mental retardation**; heart: ↑ catecholamine responsiveness (β) → **thyroid storm → propranolol**; lungs: hypoxia → ODC right shift, ↑ RR; liver: ↑ SHBG.

[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a process or abnormal state: atherosclerosis, thyrotoxicosis, fibrosis. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation (thyroiditis) · **-oma** a tumour (adenoma).
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with: congenital, constipation, coupling (*copulare*), contractility. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** against.
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, over: epinephrine (upon the kidney), epithelium, epiphysis. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **para-** beside · **peri-** around.
[^pre-dis]: **dis- / di-** — apart, away, reversal: dissociation, dissolve, diffusion. Don't confuse with **dys-** bad, difficult (dyspnoea) · **di-** two (diiodotyrosine) · **de-** removal.
