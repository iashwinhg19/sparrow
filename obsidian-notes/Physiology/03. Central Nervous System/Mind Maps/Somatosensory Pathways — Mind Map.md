# Somatosensory Pathways

## Levels of control for general sensations
- Skin → spinal cord → thalamus → sensory cortex
- Four-neuron chain
  - Receptor → 1st order neuron (dorsal root ganglion)
  - 2nd order → spinal cord / medulla, ascends through brainstem
  - 3rd order → thalamus
  - 4th order → cerebral cortex

## Spinothalamic pathways
- From spinal cord to thalamus
### Pathways
- Posterior column pathway (dorsal column) — 1st
- Anterolateral spinothalamic pathway — 2nd
- Spinocerebellar tract — 3rd → unconscious proprioception to cerebellum

### DCML vs anterolateral
- Myelination
  - DCML → myelinated (majority)
  - Anterolateral → unmyelinated
- Evolution
  - DCML → new
  - Anterolateral → old
- Conduction speed
  - DCML → fast
  - Anterolateral → slow
- DCML sensations
  - Conscious proprioception (joint position sense)
  - Fine touch
  - Vibration
  - Stereognosis → identify objects by touch, eyes closed
- Anterolateral sensations
  - Lateral pathway → pain
  - Anterior pathway → crude touch, pressure
  - Temperature
  - Itch
- Level of decussation
  - DCML → medulla
  - Anterolateral → spinal cord

### Course of the pathways
- DCML
  - Right side of body → dorsal root ganglion
  - → fasciculus gracilis / cuneatus (same side)
  - → nucleus gracilis / cuneatus (medulla) → crosses
  - → medial lemniscus → thalamus → sensory cortex
- Anterolateral
  - Right side of body → dorsal root ganglion
  - → crosses over in spinal cord
  - → anterolateral ST tract → medulla → thalamus → sensory cortex
- Lower limb → gracile fasciculus → nucleus gracilis
- Upper limb → cuneate fasciculus → nucleus cuneatus

### Somatotopic arrangement
- C cervical · T thoracic · L lumbar · S sacral
- DCML → (medial) S L T C (lateral)
- Anterolateral → (medial) C T L S (lateral)

### Brown-Séquard syndrome
- Hemisection of spinal cord → injury to one half
- Ipsilateral
  - Loss of dorsal column sensations → proprioception, vibration, fine touch
  - Motor paralysis
- Contralateral
  - Loss of anterolateral sensations → pain, temperature
- Same side = uncrossed tracts · opposite side = already-crossed tract

## Thalamus
- Hallmark → sensory relay station
- Order of neurons → 3rd order
- Nuclei → ventropostero-lateral (VPL)
  - General senses → touch, pain, temperature
- Dejerine-Roussy syndrome (thalamic pain syndrome)
  - Stroke (infarction) → irritable VPL nerve fibres
  - → ↑ neuronal signalling
  - Symptom → excruciating pain

## Sensory cortex
- Location → postcentral gyrus (parietal cortex)
  - Brodmann areas 3, 1, 2
  - Motor cortex = precentral gyrus
- Function → localization of sensation
### Sensory homunculus
- Penfield homunculus
- Medial → leg
- Lateral → face
- Maximum → thumb, lip (based on use)
- Minimum → hip, back, trunk
- No representation of visceral organs
- Referred pain
  - Visceral pain → somatic structure of same embryonic origin

## Laws in sensory physiology
### Bell–Magendie law
- Dorsal root → sensory
- Ventral root → motor
### Law of specific nerve energies
- AKA Müller's doctrine
- Receptors detect specific sensations
  - Pain receptors → pain
  - Touch receptors → touch
  - Cold receptors → cold
### Law of projection
- Receptor stimulated → cortex projects response to lowest receptor level
### Cortical plasticity
- Reorganization of cortical representation by adjacent areas after injury
- Clinical significance → phantom limb pain
  - Hand amputation → cortical takeover by arm
  - → arm pain → perceived as hand pain
### Weber–Fechner law
- Magnitude of sensation ∝ log intensity of initial stimulus
- 1 kg + 10 g → increase not perceived
- 1 kg + 100 g → increase perceived
