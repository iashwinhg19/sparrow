# Characteristics of Nerve Fibres

## Introduction to nerve cells
- Neuron
  - Functional unit of CNS
- Glial cells
  - Supporting cells of nervous system
  - Numerous
- Neurons : glial cells = 1 : 10

## Glial cells
### Classification (based on size)
- Macroglial cells
  - Astrocytes
  - Oligodendrocytes
  - Schwann cells
  - Ependymal cells
  - Satellite cells
- Microglial cells
  - Microglia

### Microglia
- Function → modified immune cells
- Precursor → monocyte macrophage system

### Astrocytes
- Star-shaped cells
- Types
  - Protoplasmic → grey matter
  - Fibrous → white matter
- Significance → GFAP
  - Glial fibrillary acidic protein
  - Intermediate filament
  - Anti-GFAP antibodies → visualise astrocytes
  - Tumour marker

### Functions of astrocytes
- 1 Blood brain barrier → most critical
  - Endothelial cells → tight junctions
  - Astrocytes → foot process
- 2 Migration of neurons
  - Radial glial cells (astrocytes) → pathway during development
- 3 Synapse formation
  - Growth factor → thrombospondin (from astrocytes)
- 4 Environmental modulation (synaptic cleaning)
  - NT + ions from microenvironment → astrocytes → perivascular space
- 5 Detoxification of ammonia
  - Glutamate–glutamine cycle
  - Neuronal signalling → glutamate
  - Glutamate → glutamine (glutamine synthase)
  - ↑ NH3 → toxic to brain
- 6 Neurovascular coupling
  - Principle of functional MRI
  - Functional hyperaemia → ↑ blood flow to active areas
  - Glutamate → neuroligin receptors of astrocytes
  - → ↑ vasodilators (arachidonic acid derivatives, prostaglandins)
  - → ↑ blood flow

### Oligodendrocytes vs Schwann cells
- Oligodendrocytes
  - Myelinate multiple axons
  - CNS
- Schwann cells
  - Myelinate single axon
  - PNS

## Neurons
### Classification
- Unipolar
  - Single process (dendrite/axon)
  - Invertebrates
- Bipolar
  - 2 processes
  - Retina
- Pseudounipolar
  - Single process split into 2
  - Dorsal root ganglia → branches to skin and spinal cord
- Multipolar
  - Multiple processes
  - Spinal α motor neuron
  - Most numerous

### Multipolar neuron structure
- Cell body (soma)
  - Nucleus → controls functions
  - Nissl bodies (RER) → synthesise proteins
    - Affected during nerve cell injury
- Dendrites
  - Finger-like projections
  - Receiving end of stimulus
- Axon
  - Axon hillock → part of soma giving rise to axon
  - Initial segment → first proper beginning of axon
  - Conduction based on myelin
    - Fast → myelinated
    - Slow → unmyelinated
  - Node of Ranvier
    - Devoid of myelin
    - Max Na+ channels (2000–12000/µm²) → action potential
- Synaptic terminal (axonal terminal)
  - Ending of a neuron

### Significance
- Most important electrical activity → action potential
- Site of initiation
  - Motor neuron → axon hillock + initial segment
  - Sensory neuron → 1st node of Ranvier

### Factors affecting NCV
- Myelin
  - Insulation → thickness ∝ NCV
  - Lipids 80 % → sphingomyelin
  - Proteins 20 % → MBP, zero protein
  - Multiple sclerosis → autoantibodies vs MBP → ↓ NCV
  - Myelinated axon → ↑ NCV
  - ↑ axonal resistance → ions move only at nodes (↓ loss)
  - ↓ membrane capacitance → thickness ∝ 1/capacitance
- Size of neuron
  - Diameter ∝ NCV
  - Big neuron ↑ NCV, small neuron ↓ NCV
- Temperature
  - Temperature ∝ NCV

## Action potential of neuron
### Phases
- Latent period → gap between stimulus and response
- I Local potential
  - −70 (RMP) → −55 mV (firing level)
  - Slow Na+ influx (less)
- II Depolarisation
  - −55 (threshold) → +35 mV
  - Fast Na+ influx via voltage-gated Na+ channels
  - Overshoot → 0 → +35 mV
- III Repolarisation
  - +35 → −70 mV
  - K+ efflux
- IV Hyperpolarisation
  - −70 → −90 mV
  - Slow closure of K+ channels
  - Influx of Cl− ions
- E_Na = +60 mV but peak ≈ +35 mV

### Clinical significance of hyperpolarisation
- Principle → ↑ intracellular −ve charge → inhibits neurons
- Physiology → GABA → inhibitory neurotransmitter
- Applied → GABA agonists for sedation
  - Barbiturates
  - Benzodiazepines

### Ion permeability
- Na+ permeability → fast, tall → depolarisation
- K+ permeability → slow, prolonged → repolarisation

### Refractory period
- Absolute (ARP)
  - Threshold → 1/3rd repolarisation
  - Second stimulus → no response
  - Inactivated voltage-gated Na+ channels
- Relative (RRP)
  - 1/3rd repolarisation → end of AP
  - Maximum-strength stimulus → response

### Voltage-gated Na+ channel
- m gate → activation gate
- h gate → inactivation gate
- Open state → m open, h open
- Closed state → m closed, h open
- Inactivation state → m open, h closed

### Types of action potential
- Biphasic
  - Electrodes on surface of neuron
  - +ve phase → isoelectric → −ve phase
- Compound
  - Multiple peaks
  - Mixed nerve → many fibres, varying velocity

### Graded potential vs action potential
- Cause
  - GP → local potential
  - AP → summation of local potentials → threshold
- Summation → GP possible, AP not possible
- Threshold → GP not needed, AP needed
- Amplitude → GP changes, AP constant
- All-or-none law → GP does not obey, AP obeys
