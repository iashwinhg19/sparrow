# Spinal Reflexes

## Reflex arc
- ① Stimulus
- ② Receptor (sensory)
- ③ Afferent (input)
- ④ Spinal cord (centre)
- ⑤ Efferent (output)
- ⑥ Effector (skeletal muscle)
- ⑦ Response

## Stretch reflex
### Stimulus
- Passive stretch → induces muscle contraction
  - Eg: knee jerk
### Receptor — muscle spindle
- Length sensor, in parallel with muscle fibres
- Extrafusal fibres (EFF)
  - Actin & myosin → supplied by α motor neuron
- Intrafusal fibres (IFF) = muscle spindle
  - Supplied by γ motor neuron
- Sensory innervation → group Ia, group II
- Types of intrafusal fibre
  - Nuclear bag fibre (NBF) → static (SBF), dynamic (DBF)
  - Nuclear chain fibre (NCF)
### Innervation
- Group Ia
  - AKA annulospiral endings = primary
  - Innervates SBF, DBF, NCF
- Group II
  - AKA flower-spray endings = secondary
  - Innervates SBF, NCF
- γ motor neuron → intrafusal fibres
  - Dynamic (end plates) → DBF
  - Static (trail plates) → SBF, NCF
- α motor neuron → extrafusal fibres
### Centre — spinal cord
- Afferent (Ia, II) → α motor neuron directly
- Effector → skeletal muscle (extrafusal fibre)
- Response → muscle contraction
- Monosynaptic reflex (simplest)
### Response — α–γ coactivation
- Direct → α motor neuron → actin–myosin contract
- Indirect → γ motor neuron (innervates periphery)
  - → central portion stretch
  - → stretch reflex ⊕
  - → muscle contracts
### Applied aspect
- At rest → spindle taut
- On α contraction alone → spindle flabby
- On γ action → spindle ends shorten
  - Spindle shape maintained
  - Spindle functions properly
- Jendrassik maneuver
  - Clenching teeth or interlocking fingers
  - Followed by knee jerk → brisk knee jerk
  - D/t α & γ motor neuron coactivation

## Inverse stretch / relaxation reflex
### Nature
- Protective reflex
### Stimulus
- Overstretching of muscle
  - Eg: weight lifting
### Receptor
- Golgi tendon organ → tension sensor, in series
- Afferent → group Ib (sensory)
### Centre — spinal cord
- Ib afferent → 1st synapse → inhibitory neuron
- Inhibitory neuron → 2nd synapse → α motor neuron
- α motor neuron (efferent) inhibited
- Disynaptic reflex
### Response
- Relaxation of muscle
### Summary
- Stimulus → stretch vs overstretch
- Receptor → spindle (length) vs GTO (tension)
- Sensory input → Ia, II vs Ib
- Centre → spinal cord (both)
- Motor output → α stimulated vs α inhibited
- Response → contraction vs relaxation
- Other names → myotatic reflex vs lengthening reaction
- Synapses → monosynaptic vs disynaptic
### Clinical aspects
- Clasp-knife spasticity
  - Initial ↑ resistance → stretch reflex
  - Then ↓ resistance → inverse stretch reflex
- Ankle clonus
  - Sudden & sustained stretch → ankle clonus
  - Cycle of stretch & inverse stretch reflex

## Other reflexes
### Withdrawal reflex
- Polysynaptic reflex
- Affected leg → flexion (flexor ⊕, extensor ⊖)
- Balancing leg → extension (flexor ⊖, extensor ⊕)
  - = crossed extensor reflex
- Myogram flexor reflex
  - No latency → immediate, earliest response
  - Fatigue on continuous stimulus
  - After-discharge → move limb away from pain
- Myogram crossed extensor reflex
  - Latency (+)
  - After-discharge → maintain posture / balance
### Positive supporting / magnet reaction
- Foot pad pressure
- Integration → spinal cord
- Limb extension → support / balance
### Placing reaction
- Dorsum of foot on table
- Flexion of knee & hip
- Integration → cerebral cortex
- Firm placement of foot on table
### Righting reflex
- Laid on side → series of body movements
- Integration → midbrain
- → standing
### Hopping reflex
- Standing on two legs → moved horizontally
- Integration → cerebral cortex
- → quickly hop (postural reflex)
### Mass reflex
- Paraplegic patient → mild painful stimuli
- Profuse sweating, systolic BP > 200 mmHg
  - D/t activation of multiple spinal segments
- Bowel & bladder evacuation
