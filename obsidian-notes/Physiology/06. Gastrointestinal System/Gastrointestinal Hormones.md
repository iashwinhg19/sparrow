# 🧬 Gastrointestinal Hormones

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> GI **hormones**[^ety-hormone] are peptides released by scattered **enteroendocrine**[^ety-enteroendocrine][^pre-entero][^pre-endo][^suf-crine] cells of the gut wall when food arrives; they **act upon the GI tract and the brain**. Every hormone in this chapter answers four questions:
> - ✅ **Which cell, and where?** (G, I, S, K, Mo cells)
> - ✅ **What releases it?** (distension, protein, fat, acid, glucose, fasting)
> - ✅ **What does it do** to acid, enzymes, bicarbonate, motility, insulin and appetite?
> - ✅ **What disease or drug** is linked to it (VIPoma, gastroparesis → erythromycin, incretins)?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — Couriers Sent Out by Each Station

Think of the gut as a chain of stations that send couriers ahead and back. The stomach sends **gastrin** to say "make acid"; the duodenum, when acid and fat pour in, sends **secretin** ("send bicarbonate, stop the acid") and **CCK** ("send bile and enzymes, slow the stomach"); the glucose that arrives makes **GIP** warn the pancreas ("insulin needed"); the far ileum sends **peptide YY** as an "ileal brake"; and between meals the empty stomach sends **ghrelin** ("hungry!") and the small gut sends **motilin** ("sweep the tube clean").

```
CELL (site)          HORMONE     RELEASED BY                    KEY ACTIONS
G   (antrum)         Gastrin     distension, protein, GRP, Ca2+ ↑ acid (CCK-B) · LES contraction · trophic
I   (duodenum)       CCK-PZ      fat, protein                   GB contraction + Oddi relax · enzymes · ↓ gastric emptying · satiety
S   (duodenum)       Secretin    acid chyme                     ↑ HCO3- (natural antacid) · ↓ acid · pylorus contracts
K   (duodenum/jej.)  GIP         oral glucose                   ↑ insulin (incretin)
Mo  (duodenum/jej.)  Motilin     fasting                        MMC (stomach → colon)
L   (ileum/colon)    Peptide YY  fat-rich diet                  ↓ gastric motility & acid · ileal brake
oxyntic glands       Ghrelin     fasting                        hunger · ↑ acid & motility · ↑ GH
```

## 📖 Introduction — Classification and Enteroendocrine Cells

**GI hormones act upon the GI tract and the brain.**

**Classification of GI hormones: 3 main families.**

| **Gastrin family** | **Secretin family** | **Others** |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Gastrin**[^ety-gastrin] | 1. **Secretin**[^ety-secretin] | 1. **Motilin**[^ety-motilin] |
| 2. **Cholecystokinin (CCK)**[^ety-cholecystokinin][^pre-chole] | 2. **Glucagon**[^ety-glucagon] | 2. **Guanylin**[^ety-guanylin] |
| — | 3. **Vasoactive**[^pre-vaso] **Intestinal Peptide (VIP)** | 3. **Peptide YY**[^ety-pyy] |
| — | 4. **Gastric Inhibitory Peptide (GIP)** | 4. **Ghrelin**[^ety-ghrelin] |

🧠 *Why families?* Members of one family share part of their amino-acid sequence: gastrin and CCK end in the same five amino acids (so both can act on CCK receptors); secretin, glucagon, VIP and GIP are structurally homologous.[^1]

## 🧪 Gastrin Family

### GASTRIN

**Secretion:**

```
Antral distention (on eating food)
            ↓
Stimulation of G-cells & gastrin secretion  ◄──────┐
            ↓                                      │  (+)
          ↑ HCl                                    │
            ↓                                      │
Pepsinogen ───────────────► Pepsin ────────────────┘
                              ↓
                       Protein digestion
```

- **Antral**[^ety-antrum] **distention**[^ety-distension][^pre-dis] (on eating food) → stimulation of G-cells & gastrin secretion → **↑ HCl** → **pepsinogen**[^ety-pepsin][^suf-ogen] → **pepsin** → **protein digestion**.
- **Pepsin (+)** feeds back on the G cells — the protein fragments it releases stimulate still more gastrin (a positive loop that runs while food is in the stomach).[^3]

**Factors affecting release of gastrin:**

| **Increase** | **Decrease** |
|---|---|
| • **Antral distention.** | • **Excess acid.** |
| • **Protein-rich diet** (↑ pepsin). | • **Somatostatin**[^ety-somatostatin] **(universal inhibitor).** |
| • **Gastrin-releasing peptide (GRP)** by **vagal**[^ety-vagus] **nerve endings.** | • **GIP.** |
| • **Ca²⁺.** | • **Secretin.** |
| • **Epinephrine**[^ety-epinephrine][^pre-epi]. | — |

**Actions:**

```
                                         GASTRIN — Actions
     ┌───────────────────┬─────────────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────────────┐
Increases acid      Lowers esophageal     ↑ gastric       ↑ pepsin      Trophic action:
production via      sphincter (LES)       motility        levels        stimulates GI tract
CCK-B receptor      contraction                                         mucosal growth
                          ↓
                   Prevents reflux
                    (Protective)
```

- **Increases acid production via CCK-B receptor**[^ety-receptor].
- **Lowers esophageal**[^ety-esophagus] **sphincter**[^ety-sphincter] **(LES) contraction → prevents reflux**[^ety-reflux][^pre-re] **(protective).**[^4]
- **↑ gastric motility**[^ety-motility].
- **↑ pepsin levels.**
- **Trophic**[^ety-trophic][^suf-trophic] **action: stimulates GI tract mucosal growth.**

### CHOLECYSTOKININ – PANCREOZYMIN (CCK-PZ)

**Secretion and actions of CCK-PZ:**

```
Intake of food rich in fats, proteins ──► Stimulation of I-cells ──Release──► CCK-PZ
```

**Actions:**

| Actions | **CCK** | **PZ** |
|---|---|---|
| **Specific actions** | **Via CCK-A receptor:** • **Cholagogue**[^ety-cholagogue][^suf-gogue] **contraction of gall bladder.** • **Release of bile: relaxation of sphincter of Oddi**[^ety-oddi]**.** | **Stimulates secretion of enzyme-rich pancreatic juice** (pancreozymin[^ety-pancreozymin]) |

| Actions of CCK-PZ | Details |
|---|---|
| **GI actions** | • **Augments**[^ety-augment] **action of secretin.** • **Stimulates intestinal and colonic motility.** • **Inhibits gastric emptying.** |
| **CNS actions** | • **↓ food intake by ↑ satiety**[^ety-satiety]. • **Implicated in anxiety**[^ety-anxiety]. • **Aids in analgesia**[^ety-analgesia][^pre-an][^suf-algesia]. |

🧠 *In plain words:* CCK is the "fat has arrived" signal — it empties the gall bladder and opens the sphincter of Oddi so bile meets the fat, makes the pancreas pour enzymes, slows the stomach so the duodenum is not flooded, and tells the brain "enough".

## 🧫 Secretin Family

### SECRETIN

- **AKA natural antacid**[^ety-antacid][^pre-anti].
- **First hormone to be discovered (Bayliss & Starling**[^ety-bayliss-starling]**).**

**Secretion:**

```
Acid-rich chyme ──────► Duodenum ──Stimulates──► S-cells ──Secrete──► Secretin
```

- **Acid-rich chyme**[^ety-chyme] → **duodenum**[^ety-duodenum] → stimulates **S-cells** → secrete **secretin**.

**Actions:**
- **Release of HCO₃⁻-rich (alkaline) pancreatic juice: for acid neutralisation**[^ety-neutralisation].
- **Augments action of CCK.**
- **Decreases gastric acid secretion.**
- **Contraction of pyloric**[^ety-pylorus] **sphincter: prevents food from moving into intestine.**

🧠 *In plain words:* secretin is a pH-driven brake and buffer — acid in the duodenum calls up bicarbonate to neutralise it, shuts off more acid from the stomach, and closes the pylorus until the duodenum has caught up.

### GASTRIC INHIBITORY POLYPEPTIDE (GIP)

- **Now known as glucose-dependent insulinotropic**[^ety-insulinotropic][^suf-tropic] **polypeptide**[^ety-polypeptide][^pre-poly] **(GIP).**[^5]

**Secretion:**

```
Oral intake of glucose ──Stimulates──► K-cells ──release──► GIP ──Stimulates──► β cells in pancreatic islets ──► ↑ insulin
```

- **Oral intake of glucose** → stimulates **K-cells** → release **GIP** → stimulates **β cells in pancreatic islets**[^ety-islet] → **↑ insulin**[^ety-insulin].

> [!info] GI hormones that ↑ insulin secretion: **Incretins**[^ety-incretin]
> **Eg: GIP and glucagon-like peptide (GLP).**[^6]

### VASOACTIVE INTESTINAL POLYPEPTIDE (VIP)

**Actions:**

```
                                 Actions
          ┌─────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────┐
   Salivary glands              Stomach                          Intestine
   Potentiate action of     ↓ acid production       • ↑ fluid and electrolyte secretions
   acetylcholine                                    • Relaxation of smooth muscle
```

| Target | Action |
|---|---|
| **Salivary glands** | **Potentiate**[^ety-potentiate] **action of acetylcholine**[^ety-acetylcholine] |
| **Stomach** | **↓ acid production** |
| **Intestine** | • **↑ fluid and electrolyte**[^ety-electrolyte][^pre-electro] **secretions** • **Relaxation of smooth muscle** |

#### VIPoma[^ety-vipoma][^suf-oma]

- **Neuroendocrine**[^pre-neuro] **tumor (↑ secretion of VIP).**
- **Leads to: WDHA syndrome**[^ety-syndrome][^pre-syn] — **AKA pancreatic cholera**[^ety-cholera] / **Verner–Morrison syndrome**[^ety-verner-morrison].

| **W**atery **D**iarrhoea | **H**ypokalaemia | **A**chlorhydria |
|---|---|---|
| **Watery diarrhea**[^ety-diarrhoea][^pre-dia][^suf-rrhoea] (d/t ↑ fluid/electrolyte secretion) | **Hypokalemia**[^ety-hypokalaemia][^pre-hypo][^suf-aemia] (↑ K⁺ secretion) | **Achlorhydria**[^ety-achlorhydria][^pre-a] |

```
↑ VIP ──► ↑ fluid/electrolyte secretion ──► Watery diarrhoea ──► Hypokalaemia (↑ K+ secretion)
      └─► ↓ acid production ───────────────────────────────────► Achlorhydria
```

## 🌀 Other Hormones

### MOTILIN

- **Seen in all fasting individuals.**
- **Causes ↑ GI motility: regulates migratory motor complex (MMC)**[^ety-migratory] **from stomach to colon.**
- **Erythromycin**[^ety-erythromycin]**: agonist**[^ety-agonist] **to motilin receptor → used to treat gastroparesis**[^ety-gastroparesis][^pre-gastro][^suf-paresis] **in diabetic**[^ety-diabetes] **patients.**[^7]

### GUANYLIN

- **↑ Cl⁻ secretion in GI tract.**
- **Structurally similar to E-coli**[^ety-escherichia] **→ molecular mimicry**[^ety-mimicry]**: E. coli act through guanylin receptor.**[^8]

### PEPTIDE YY

- **Stimulus: fat-rich diet.**

![[Gastrointestinal Hormones - peptide YY actions.png]]
*Peptide YY from the ileum (red) and large intestine: arrows to the stomach (decreases gastric motility, decreases acid secretion) and to the ileum itself (inhibits ileal motility — "ileal brake"); rectum, anus and sigmoid colon labelled (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Target | Action of PYY |
|---|---|
| **Stomach** | • **Decreases gastric motility.** • **Decreases acid secretion.** |
| **Ileum** | • **Inhibits ileal**[^ety-ileum] **motility (ileal brake).** |

🧠 *In plain words:* when undigested fat reaches the far end of the small intestine, PYY puts on the brakes upstream (stomach, ileum) so the remaining fat stays longer where it can still be absorbed.[^9]

### GHRELIN

- **AKA Hunger hormone (causes hunger contractions).**
- **Secretion: from oxyntic**[^ety-oxyntic] **gland in stomach.**
  - **Levels peak during fasting.**
  - **Levels are:** **high in anorexia nervosa**[^ety-anorexia] **patients**; **low in obese**[^ety-obese] **patients.**

**Actions:**
1. **Orexinergic**[^ety-orexinergic][^suf-ergic]**: ↑ food intake.**
2. **Increases:** gastric acid · gastric motility.
3. **↑ adipogenesis**[^ety-adipogenesis][^suf-genesis].
4. **↑ release of Growth Hormone (GH): GH-REL-IN** — **REL** → "**(Releasing)**".[^10]

> [!tip] Mnemonic on the page
> **GH**-**REL**-**IN** → **G**rowth **H**ormone **REL**easing — ghrelin is the gut hormone that releases GH.

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Each GI hormone is released by **what has just arrived at its own station** and acts to **prepare the next station or protect the current one**. Protein and distension in the antrum → gastrin → acid and pepsin to digest that protein. Acid in the duodenum → secretin → bicarbonate to neutralise it and a closed pylorus. Fat and protein in the duodenum → CCK → bile, enzymes and a slower stomach. Glucose in the gut → GIP/GLP → insulin *before* the blood sugar rises (feedforward). Fat reaching the ileum → PYY → an ileal brake. An empty gut → ghrelin (hunger) and motilin (clean-up sweep).
>
> Structural families explain the overlaps: gastrin and CCK share a C-terminal pentapeptide, so both bind CCK receptors (gastrin → CCK-B for acid; CCK → CCK-A for the gall bladder), and secretin, glucagon, VIP and GIP share homologous sequences.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Hormones rise and fall with each meal: gastrin with the gastric phase, secretin and CCK as chyme enters the duodenum, GIP within minutes of oral glucose (the incretin effect — oral glucose releases more insulin than the same glucose IV), PYY late in the meal; ghrelin and motilin peak in fasting, with MMC sweeps every ~90 minutes.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A hormone's feedback loop compensates for a change: achlorhydria (atrophic gastritis, PPIs) removes the acid brake, so gastrin rises — hypergastrinaemia with trophic mucosal growth but no symptoms; slow gastric emptying in early diabetic autonomic neuropathy is partly overcome by motilin-driven MMCs.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Autonomous hormone excess breaks the loop: a VIPoma pours VIP regardless of food — profuse watery diarrhoea (up to litres a day), hypokalaemia and achlorhydria (WDHA); a gastrinoma drives relentless acid (Zollinger–Ellison: multiple ulcers); in established diabetic gastroparesis the stomach fails to empty and erythromycin (a motilin agonist) is used.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal meal-linked release → change in input (acid loss, nerve damage, tumour) → feedback compensation (↑ gastrin, ↑ motilin drive) → autonomous secretion or end-organ failure → clinical syndrome (WDHA, ulcers, gastroparesis)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Hormone actions are drug targets: erythromycin (motilin receptor) for gastroparesis, GLP-1 analogues and DPP-4 inhibitors (incretins) for type 2 diabetes and obesity, octreotide (a somatostatin analogue — the universal inhibitor) for VIPoma diarrhoea, and secretin stimulation for testing pancreatic function and gastrinoma.

>

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: M cells make motilin**
>
> **What students think:** M cells = motilin cells.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Mo cells** make motilin. **M cells** are **microfold cells** over Peyer's patches in the terminal ileum that sample antigens for the gut immune system — nothing to do with hormones. The page adds this note precisely because the letters look alike.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "M**o** = M**o**tilin; M = Microfold (immune)."
>
> **Trap 2: Putting CCK in the secretin family**
>
> **What students think:** CCK works with secretin on the pancreas, so they are one family.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Families are structural, not functional. CCK shares its C-terminal pentapeptide with **gastrin** (gastrin family), whereas secretin, glucagon, VIP and GIP form the **secretin family**. Motilin, guanylin, PYY and ghrelin are "others".
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Gastrin family = **G**astrin + **C**CK (2 members); Secretin family = **S**ecretin, **G**lucagon, **V**IP, **G**IP (4 members)."
>
> **Trap 3: Gastrin relaxes the LES**
>
> **What students think:** The page's "lowers oesophageal sphincter contraction" means gastrin weakens the sphincter.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Read it as "**Lower** oesophageal sphincter contraction": gastrin **contracts** the LES, which is why the page says it **prevents reflux (protective)** — the stomach is about to churn acid, and the gate above it closes.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Ask what the arrow leads to — "prevents reflux" can only follow contraction.
>
> **Trap 4: Secretin "speeds up" the stomach**
>
> **What students think:** Secretin pushes food along to the intestine.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Secretin **contracts the pyloric sphincter** and **decreases gastric acid**, preventing more acid from entering the duodenum until it is neutralised. CCK likewise **inhibits gastric emptying**. The duodenal hormones slow the stomach down.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Duodenal hormones = brakes on the stomach."
>
> **Trap 5: Ghrelin is low in anorexia nervosa**
>
> **What students think:** Patients who do not eat must have little hunger hormone.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Ghrelin rises with fasting and energy deficit, so it is **high in anorexia nervosa** (the drive to eat is overridden psychologically) and **low in obesity**. It peaks before meals.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Ghrelin tracks the empty stomach, not the appetite you see.

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Gastrointestinal Secretions]]**
> Gastrin, histamine and ACh drive acid; secretin and CCK-PZ drive the two parts of pancreatic juice; CCK is the cholagogue and bile salt the choleretic — the effector side of every hormone here.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Gastrointestinal Motility]]**
> Motilin runs the migrating motor complex of fasting; CCK and secretin slow gastric emptying; gastrin raises gastric motility and LES tone; PYY is the ileal brake.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Endocrine Pancreas]]**
> GIP and GLP-1 are incretins acting on β cells; glucagon belongs to the secretin family; the islet δ-cell somatostatin is the same universal inhibitor.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Limbic System and Hypothalamus]]**
> Ghrelin (orexinergic) and CCK/PYY (satiety) act on the hypothalamic feeding and satiety centres.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Pituitary Gland]]**
> Ghrelin is a growth-hormone secretagogue — GH-REL-IN — acting on the GHS receptor of somatotrophs.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Digestion and Absorption]]**
> The meal components that trigger each hormone (protein → gastrin, fat → CCK and PYY, glucose → GIP) are digested and absorbed there.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Neuroendocrine tumours (VIPoma, gastrinoma) present through hormone excess; prokinetics (erythromycin) and incretin-based drugs exploit normal hormone receptors; octreotide controls secretory diarrhoea; E. coli heat-stable toxin causes traveller's diarrhoea by mimicking guanylin.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Questions ask: which cell makes which hormone (G, I, S, K, Mo), which is the first hormone discovered (secretin), which is the natural antacid (secretin), which is an incretin (GIP, GLP), what WDHA stands for, which drug acts on motilin receptors (erythromycin) and which hormone is high in anorexia nervosa (ghrelin).

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. GI hormones act on the **GI tract and the brain**; **3 families** — **gastrin family** (gastrin, CCK), **secretin family** (secretin, glucagon, VIP, GIP), **others** (motilin, guanylin, PYY, **ghrelin**).
> 2. Cells: **G → gastrin, I → CCK, S → secretin, K → GIP, Mo → motilin**; **M cells = microfold cells of Peyer's patches** (terminal ileum).
> 3. **Gastrin**: ↑ by **antral distention**, protein (pepsin), **GRP from vagal endings**, Ca²⁺, epinephrine; ↓ by **excess acid, somatostatin (universal inhibitor)**, GIP, secretin.
> 4. Gastrin actions: **↑ acid via CCK-B**, **LES contraction → prevents reflux**, ↑ gastric motility, ↑ pepsin, **trophic to GI mucosa**.
> 5. **CCK-PZ** from **I cells** (fat, protein): via **CCK-A** → **gall-bladder contraction (cholagogue) + sphincter of Oddi relaxation**; PZ → **enzyme-rich pancreatic juice**; **augments secretin**, ↑ intestinal & colonic motility, **inhibits gastric emptying**; CNS → **satiety**, anxiety, analgesia.
> 6. **Secretin = natural antacid, first hormone discovered (Bayliss & Starling)**; acid chyme → S cells → **HCO₃⁻-rich pancreatic juice**, augments CCK, ↓ acid, **contracts pyloric sphincter**.
> 7. **GIP = glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide**: oral glucose → K cells → β cells → ↑ insulin. **Incretins = GIP and GLP**.
> 8. **VIP**: potentiates ACh on salivary glands, ↓ acid, **↑ intestinal fluid & electrolyte secretion**, relaxes smooth muscle.
> 9. **VIPoma → WDHA (pancreatic cholera / Verner–Morrison)**: **watery diarrhoea, hypokalaemia, achlorhydria**.
> 10. **Motilin**: fasting; **regulates MMC** stomach → colon; **erythromycin = motilin agonist → diabetic gastroparesis**.
> 11. **Guanylin**: ↑ Cl⁻ secretion; **molecular mimicry — E. coli acts through the guanylin receptor**.
> 12. **Peptide YY**: fat-rich diet → ↓ gastric motility & acid, **ileal brake**.
> 13. **Ghrelin = hunger hormone** from **oxyntic glands**; peaks in fasting; **high in anorexia nervosa, low in obesity**; **orexinergic**, ↑ acid & motility, ↑ adipogenesis, **↑ GH (GH-REL-IN)**.

[^1]: Gastrin and CCK share an identical C-terminal pentapeptide (Gly-Trp-Met-Asp-Phe-NH₂), which carries their activity — so gastrin binds CCK-B (= gastrin) receptors and CCK binds both CCK-A and CCK-B. Secretin, glucagon, VIP, GIP (and GLP-1) share sequence homology and act on class B G-protein-coupled receptors.
[^2]: Standard locations: G cells — gastric antrum (and some in the duodenum); I cells — duodenum and jejunum; S cells — duodenum; K cells — duodenum and jejunum; Mo cells — duodenum and jejunum; L cells (GLP-1, PYY) — ileum and colon; D cells (somatostatin) — throughout.
[^3]: Strictly, peptides and amino acids released by pepsin digestion (especially aromatic amino acids) stimulate G cells directly; the page's "+" arrow from pepsin back to the G cells summarises this.
[^4]: The page reads "Lowers esophageal sphincter (LES) contraction". Standard texts: gastrin **increases** lower oesophageal sphincter tone (contracts it), which fits the page's next line, "prevents reflux (protective)". The page's wording is kept in the body.
[^5]: GIP was named "gastric inhibitory peptide" (Brown, 1970) because large doses inhibit acid secretion; at physiological levels its main effect is to stimulate insulin release only when blood glucose is raised — hence "glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide".
[^6]: GLP-1 comes from L cells of the ileum and colon. The incretin effect — oral glucose stimulates 50–70 % more insulin than the same glucose given IV — is reduced in type 2 diabetes; GLP-1 agonists (liraglutide, semaglutide) and DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin, which stop incretin breakdown) exploit it.
[^7]: Erythromycin, a macrolide antibiotic, binds the motilin receptor in sub-antibiotic doses and triggers phase-III-like contractions of the stomach — a prokinetic for diabetic gastroparesis and before endoscopy for upper GI bleeding. Tachyphylaxis limits long-term use.
[^8]: More precisely, guanylin is structurally similar to the **heat-stable enterotoxin (STa) of enterotoxigenic E. coli**. Both bind guanylyl cyclase C on enterocytes → ↑ cGMP → CFTR opens → Cl⁻ and water secretion. The toxin over-activates the receptor and causes traveller's (secretory) diarrhoea.
[^9]: PYY (and GLP-1) are released from L cells of the ileum and colon in proportion to fat and calories reaching them. The "ileal brake" slows gastric emptying and small-bowel transit so that nutrients that escaped upstream absorption get more time. PYY₃₋₃₆ also reduces appetite via hypothalamic Y2 receptors.
[^10]: Ghrelin was discovered in 1999 (Kojima and Kangawa) as the natural ligand of the growth-hormone secretagogue receptor; the name was built from *ghre*, the Proto-Indo-European root of "grow", and *relin*, releasing — the page's GH-REL-IN mnemonic matches it. It is the only known circulating orexigenic (appetite-raising) hormone.

[^ety-enteroendocrine]: **enteroendocrine** — Greek *enteron*, "intestine" + *endon*, "within" + *krinein*, "to secrete" → gut cells that secrete into the blood (within the body) rather than into the lumen. They are scattered single cells, not glands.
[^ety-gastrin]: **gastrin** — Greek *gastēr*, "stomach, belly" + *-in*, a substance → the stomach hormone; John Edkins (1905) proposed it from antral extracts; Gregory and Tracy isolated it in 1964.
[^ety-secretin]: **secretin** — Latin *secernere*, "to separate, secrete" + *-in* → the substance that calls up secretion (of pancreatic juice).

[^ety-incretin]: **incretin** — from *in*testine *secret*ion *in*sulin (La Barre, 1932) → a gut hormone that increases insulin secretion after oral glucose (GIP, GLP-1). **Differentiate:** *incretin* (gut hormone → insulin) · *insulin secretagogue* (any stimulus, including sulphonylurea drugs).

[^ety-electrolyte]: **electrolyte** — Greek *ēlektron*, "amber" (electricity) + *lytos*, "loosened, soluble" → a substance that dissolves into charged ions (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻) and conducts electricity (Faraday, 1834).
[^ety-vipoma]: **VIPoma** — *VIP* + Greek *-ōma*, "a tumour, a mass" → a tumour secreting VIP, usually a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour of non-β islet cells.
[^ety-syndrome]: **syndrome** — Greek *syn-*, "together" + *dromos*, "running" → features that run together. **Differentiate:** *syndrome* (a recognised cluster, cause may vary) · *disease* (a defined cause/pathology) · *sign* (seen by the examiner) · *symptom* (felt by the patient).
[^ety-cholera]: **cholera / pancreatic cholera** — Greek *cholera*, from *cholē*, "bile" (the ancients blamed a flow of bile) → profuse watery diarrhoea; "pancreatic cholera" because a pancreatic tumour causes cholera-like stools.
[^ety-verner-morrison]: **Verner–Morrison syndrome** — John V. Verner and Ashton B. Morrison, American physicians, who described in 1958 two patients with islet-cell tumours, watery diarrhoea and hypokalaemia.
[^ety-diarrhoea]: **diarrhoea** — Greek *dia-*, "through" + *rhoia*, "flow" (*rhein*, to flow) → a flowing through: frequent loose stools. **Differentiate:** *secretory* (VIPoma, cholera, E. coli — persists on fasting) · *osmotic* (lactase deficiency — stops on fasting).

[^ety-migratory]: **migrating / migratory motor complex** — Latin *migrare*, "to move from place to place" + *-ory*; *motor*, "mover"; *complex*, "a woven-together whole" → a wave of contraction that travels (migrates) from the stomach to the ileum/colon in fasting.
[^ety-erythromycin]: **erythromycin** — Greek *erythros*, "red" + *mykēs*, "fungus" + *-in* → named after *Saccharopolyspora* (formerly *Streptomyces*) *erythraea*, the red-pigmented soil organism it came from (1952).
[^ety-agonist]: **agonist** — Greek *agōnistēs*, "contestant, actor" (*agōn*, contest) → a drug that binds a receptor and activates it. **Differentiate:** *agonist* (activates) · *antagonist* (blocks) · *partial agonist* · *inverse agonist*.
[^ety-gastroparesis]: **gastroparesis** — Greek *gastēr*, "stomach" + *paresis*, "letting go, slackening" → partial paralysis of the stomach — delayed emptying without obstruction; commonest cause diabetic autonomic (vagal) neuropathy.
[^ety-diabetes]: **diabetes / diabetic** — Greek *dia-*, "through" + *bainein*, "to go" → a siphon: water passing straight through (polyuria); *mellitus*, Latin "honeyed" (sweet urine). Aretaeus of Cappadocia used the name.
[^ety-escherichia]: **Escherichia coli** — Theodor Escherich (1857–1911), German-Austrian paediatrician, who isolated *Bacterium coli commune* ("common colon bacterium") from infant stools in 1885.
[^ety-mimicry]: **molecular mimicry** — Greek *mimos*, "imitator, actor" + *-ry* → a microbial molecule imitating a host molecule — here the E. coli toxin imitating guanylin to use its receptor. (In autoimmunity the same word means host antibodies cross-reacting with a microbe — rheumatic fever.)
[^ety-ileum]: **ileum / ileal** — from Greek *eilein*, "to roll, twist" → the coiled terminal small intestine. Don't confuse with **ilium** (hip bone).
[^ety-oxyntic]: **oxyntic** — Greek *oxynein*, "to sharpen, make acid" (*oxys*, sharp, sour) + *-ic* → acid-making; oxyntic glands = the body/fundus glands with parietal (oxyntic) cells, where ghrelin is also made (X/A-like cells).

[^pre-micro]: **micro-** — small: microfold, microvilli, microbiota. Don't confuse with **macro-** large · **mega- / megalo-** very large (megaloblast) · **mio-** less (miosis).
[^pre-dis]: **dis- / di-** — apart, in different directions: distension, digestion, dissection. Don't confuse with **dys-** bad, faulty (dyspepsia) · **di-** two (disaccharide) · **dia-** through (diarrhoea).
[^suf-ogen]: **-ogen / -gen** — producer, precursor: pepsinogen, trypsinogen, zymogen, glycogen (sugar-producer). Don't confuse with **-genesis** formation (adipogenesis) · **-genic** producing (orexigenic, pathogenic).
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, over: epinephrine (upon the kidney), epithelium, epigastrium. Don't confuse with **endo-** within · **meta-** after · **hypo-** under.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: reflux, receptor, releasing, reabsorption. Don't confuse with **retro-** backward, behind (retroperitoneal) · **pre-** before.
[^suf-trophic]: **-trophic / -trophy** — nourishment, growth: trophic action of gastrin, hypertrophy, atrophy. Don't confuse with **-tropic / -tropin** turning towards, acting on (insulinotropic, gonadotropin) · **-plasia** formation of cells (hyperplasia).
[^suf-gogue]: **-gogue / -agogue** — leader, that which draws out: cholagogue (bile), sialagogue (saliva), galactagogue (milk), secretagogue (secretion). Don't confuse with **-retic** flow (choleretic, diuretic).
[^pre-an]: **an- (before a vowel or h)** — without: analgesia, anorexia, anaemia, anhydrase. Don't confuse with **ana-** up, again (anabolism) · **ante-** before · **anti-** against.
[^suf-algesia]: **-algesia / -algia** — pain: analgesia, hyperalgesia, neuralgia, myalgia. Don't confuse with **-aesthesia** sensation (anaesthesia) · **-algos** in *nostalgia* (pain of returning home — same root).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: antacid, antibody, antidiuretic. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (antenatal) · **antero-** in front.
[^suf-tropic]: **-tropic / -tropin** — turning towards, acting on: insulinotropic, gonadotropin, thyrotropin (TSH). Don't confuse with **-trophic** nourishing (trophic action) · **-tropy** in *entropy* (transformation).
[^pre-poly]: **poly-** — many: polypeptide, polyuria, polysaccharide. Don't confuse with **oligo-** few (oligosaccharide) · **pleo-** more (pleomorphic) · **multi-** many (Latin twin).
[^pre-electro]: **electro-** — electricity, charge: electrolyte, electrocardiogram, electrochemical. Don't confuse with **electron** (the particle, same amber root) · **elastic** (unrelated: Greek *elastos*, ductile).
[^suf-oma]: **-oma** — tumour, mass: VIPoma, gastrinoma, insulinoma, adenoma. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition (fibrosis) · **-oma** in *glaucoma, trachoma* (old names that are not tumours).
[^pre-neuro]: **neuro-** — nerve: neuroendocrine, neurocrine, neuron. Don't confuse with **nephro-** kidney (epinephrine) · **neo-** new.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together: syndrome, synapse, sympathetic. Don't confuse with **semi-** half · **sin-** left (sinistral).
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, across: diarrhoea, diabetes, diaphragm, dialysis. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis-** apart · **dys-** bad.
[^suf-rrhoea]: **-rrhoea / -rrhea** — flow, discharge: diarrhoea, steatorrhoea, rhinorrhoea, amenorrhoea. Don't confuse with **-rrhage** bursting forth of blood (haemorrhage) · **-rrhaphy** suturing · **-rrhexis** rupture.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, deficient: hypokalaemia, hypoglycaemia, hypochlorhydria. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over, excessive · **hypno-** sleep · **sub-** (Latin twin).
[^suf-aemia]: **-aemia / -emia** — condition of the blood: hypokalaemia, anaemia, hypergastrinaemia, hyperglycaemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** condition of the urine (glycosuria) · **haem- / haemato-** blood (as a prefix).
[^pre-a]: **a- (before a consonant)** — not, without: achlorhydria, atrophy, aplasia, asymptomatic. Don't confuse with **ab-** away · **ad-** towards · **an-** (the same prefix before a vowel: anaemia, analgesia).
[^pre-gastro]: **gastro- / gastr-** — stomach: gastroparesis, gastrin, gastritis, gastroileal reflex. Don't confuse with **entero-** intestine · **gastrocnemius** (the calf muscle — "belly of the leg", same root *gastēr*, belly).
[^suf-paresis]: **-paresis** — partial paralysis, weakness: gastroparesis, hemiparesis. Don't confuse with **-plegia** complete paralysis (hemiplegia) · **-paresthesia** abnormal sensation (*para-* + *aesthesia*, a different word).
[^suf-ergic]: **-ergic** — Greek *ergon*, "work": acting through or releasing a transmitter — orexinergic, cholinergic, adrenergic. Don't confuse with **-genic** producing (orexigenic, pathogenic) · **-ergy** (allergy, synergy — same root, "work").
[^suf-genesis]: **-genesis** — origin, formation: adipogenesis, gluconeogenesis, spermatogenesis. Don't confuse with **-gen / -ogen** a producer (pepsinogen) · **-genic** producing (orexigenic) · **-lysis** breakdown.
