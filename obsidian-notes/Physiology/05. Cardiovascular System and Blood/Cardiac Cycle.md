# 🔄 Cardiac Cycle

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The **cardiac cycle**[^ety-cycle] is the sequence of mechanical events of one heartbeat — **systole**[^ety-systole][^pre-syn] (pumping / ejection) and **diastole**[^ety-diastole][^pre-dia] (filling) — lasting **0.8 s** at a normal heart rate. Valves open and close purely by **pressure differences**, so the cycle can be read as one loop of pressure against volume.
> - ✅ How long is each phase, and which phase is cut short when the heart rate rises?
> - ✅ Which valve closes or opens at each phase boundary, and which heart sound marks it?
> - ✅ How do SV, EF, EDV and ESV relate, and how do valve lesions distort the pressure–volume loop?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Syringe with Two One-Way Valves

The left ventricle is a **syringe** with an inlet valve (mitral) and an outlet valve (aortic). Both valves are shut while the plunger first squeezes (pressure ↑, volume same) and while it first lets go (pressure ↓, volume same) — the two **isovolumetric** phases. The outlet opens only when the syringe beats aortic pressure; the inlet opens only when the syringe falls below atrial pressure.

```
          S1 (mitral closes)                     S2 (aortic closes)
FILL ──► IVC ──► RAPID EJECTION ──► SLOW EJECTION ──► PROTODIASTOLE ──► IVR ──► RAPID FILL ──► SLOW FILL ──► ATRIAL KICK ──► (repeat)
EDV 120  vol same   2/3 out         1/3 out → ESV 50                    vol same   passive 80 % ─────────┘    active 20 % → EDV 120
         P ↑↑       (aortic opens)                                      P ↓        (mitral opens)
|←────────── VENTRICULAR SYSTOLE 0.3 s (3 phases) ──────────→|←───────────── VENTRICULAR DIASTOLE 0.5 s (5 phases) ─────────────→|
SV = 120 − 50 = 70 mL · EF = 70/120 ≈ 58 % (normal 55–60 %)
```

### 🏃 Clinical aspect

- **Exercise → ↑ HR → ↓ cardiac cycle (0.3 s).**
  - **Ventricular systole : 0.12 s**
  - **Ventricular diastole : 0.18 s** } → **ventricular filling compromised.**
- **Cardiac cycle = 60 / HR** (**CC ∝ 1 / HR**).[^2]

🧠 *As the rate rises, diastole is shortened far more than systole (0.5 → 0.18 s vs 0.3 → 0.12 s). Because the ventricles fill (and the coronaries perfuse) in diastole, very fast rates eventually cut filling and cardiac output.*

## 🫀 Phases of Cardiac Cycle

### 💪 Ventricular systole

**1. Isovolumetric**[^ety-isovolumetric][^pre-iso][^suf-metric] **ventricular contraction**[^ety-contraction][^pre-con] **:**
- **Mitral**[^ety-mitral] **valve**[^ety-valve] **closes (1st heart sound (S1) heard).**
- **Already closed aortic**[^ety-aorta] **valve.**
- **Volume : same; Pressure ↑↑.**
- **At end of phase → aortic valve opens.**

**2. Rapid ejection**[^ety-ejection][^pre-ex] **phase :**
- **2/3rd of ventricular volume** ejected.
- **Aortic valve opened.**
- ↓ **Followed by**

**3. Slow ejection phase :**
- **Around 1/3rd ejected.**
- → **End systolic volume = 50 ml.**[^3]

```
Isovolumetric contraction ──(end of phase: aortic valve opens)──► Rapid ejection (2/3) ──► Slow ejection (1/3) ──► ESV = 50 mL
mitral closes → S1 · aortic already closed · volume same · pressure ↑↑
```

![[Cardiac Cycle - phases of ventricular systole.png]]
*Ventricular systole on the page — isovolumetric contraction (mitral closes → S1; aortic already closed; volume same, pressure ↑↑), then rapid ejection (aortic valve opened, 2/3 of volume) and slow ejection (1/3) ending at an ESV of 50 mL (cropped from the handwritten page).*

### 🛌 Ventricular diastole

**1. Protodiastole**[^ety-protodiastole][^pre-proto] **:**
- **Closed mitral valve.**
- **Aortic valve closes (and heart sound (S2) heard).**

**2. Isovolumetric relaxation**[^ety-relaxation][^pre-re] **:**
- **Volume : same; Pressure ↓.**

**3. Rapid filling phase :** } **Passive (80 %)**
- **Mitral valve opens.**

**4. Slow filling phase :** } **Passive (80 %)**
- **(AKA diastasis**[^ety-diastasis][^suf-sis]**)**

**5. Filling d/t atrial**[^ety-atrium] **contraction :**
- **20 % active ventricular filling (atrial kick).**[^4]
- → **End diastolic volume = 120 ml.**

```
Protodiastole ──► Isovolumetric relaxation ──► Rapid filling ──► Slow filling (diastasis) ──► Atrial contraction ──► EDV = 120 mL
aortic closes → S2   volume same, pressure ↓     mitral opens      └──── passive 80 % ────┘       active 20 % (atrial kick)
```

![[Cardiac Cycle - phases of ventricular diastole.png]]
*Ventricular diastole on the page — protodiastole (aortic valve closes → S2), isovolumetric relaxation (volume same, pressure ↓), rapid filling (mitral valve opens) and slow filling / diastasis (together passive, 80 %), then atrial contraction (20 %, atrial kick) to an EDV of 120 mL (cropped from the handwritten page).*

## 🔊 Heart Sounds

| Heart sound | Etiology[^ety-etiology] | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| **S1** | **Closure of mitral & tricuspid**[^ety-tricuspid][^pre-tri] **valve** | **Physiological** |
| **S2** | **Closure of aortic & pulmonary**[^ety-pulmonary] **valve** | **Physiological** |
| **S3** | **Vibration**[^ety-vibration] **of ventricular wall during 3rd phase of diastole (rapid filling)** | **Physiological : children & following exercise**[^5] |
| **S4** | **Stiff ventricles → forceful atrial contraction (> 20 % filling)** | **Pathological**[^ety-pathological][^suf-logy] **: hypertension**[^ety-hypertension][^pre-hyper] **& restrictive**[^ety-restrictive] **cardiomyopathy**[^ety-cardiomyopathy][^pre-myo][^suf-pathy][^6] |

🧠 *S1 and S2 are valve **closures** (opening is silent). S3 and S4 are both filling sounds — S3 when blood rushes into a ventricle early (rapid filling), S4 when the atrium has to force blood into a stiff ventricle late (atrial kick).*

## 📊 Pressure Volume Changes (PVC)

### Normal physiology — the pressure–volume graph

| Point / segment | Event on the page |
|---|---|
| **A** | **Mitral valve opens** — ESV (**50 mL**) |
| **A → B** | Filling: **RF** (rapid filling) → **SF** (slow filling) → **AC** (atrial contraction) |
| **B** | **Mitral valve closes** — **S1** — EDV (**120 mL**) |
| **B → C** | **IVC** — isovolumetric contraction (volume same, pressure ↑) |
| **C** | **Aortic valve opens** |
| **C → D → E** | **RE** (rapid ejection) to the peak D, then **SE** (slow ejection) |
| **E** | **Aortic valve closes** — **S2** (≈ 100 mmHg); **PD** (protodiastole) |
| **E → A** | **IVR** — isovolumetric relaxation (volume same, pressure ↓) |
| **Width of the loop (B − A)** | **Stroke volume** |

**Legend on the page:**
- **Systolic phase** — IVC : isovolumetric contraction · RE : rapid ejection · SE : slow ejection.
- **Diastolic phase** — IVR : isovolumetric relaxation · RF : rapid filling · SF : slow filling · AC : atrial contraction · PD : protodiastole.

![[Cardiac Cycle - pressure volume loop.png]]
*The page's pressure–volume graph — x-axis volume (mL: ESV 50, EDV 120), y-axis pressure (mmHg); A mitral opens, B mitral closes (S1), C aortic opens, D peak, E aortic closes (S2); stroke volume = width of the loop (cropped from the handwritten page).[^7]*

### 🧮 Formulas

1. **Stroke volume (SV) : End diastolic volume (EDV) − End systolic volume (ESV)** = 120 − 50 = **70 mL**.
2. **Ejection fraction (EF)**[^ety-fraction] **: SV / EDV × 100** = 70 / 120 × 100 (≈ 58 %).
   - **Normal EF = 55 – 60 %.**[^8]
3. **End diastolic volume (EDV) : ESV / (1 − EF).**
4. **End systolic volume (ESV) : EDV (1 − EF).**

### 🩺 Cardiac pathologies

| Feature | **Mitral stenosis**[^ety-stenosis][^suf-osis] | **Aortic stenosis** | **Aortic regurgitation**[^ety-regurgitation] | **Mitral regurgitation** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pressure–volume alteration** | **Left shift** | *(loop drawn tall and narrow)* | *(loop drawn wide and tall)* | *(loop drawn wide and low)* |
| **EDV** | **↓** | Normal | **↑↑** | **↑↑** |
| **ESV** | Normal | **↑↑** | Normal | — |
| **Other features** | **↓ stroke volume** | **↑ left ventricular pressure** | • **Isovolumetric phase not straight line** · • **Height ↑, width ↑** | • **Isovolumetric phase not straight line** · • **Height ↓, width ↑** |

![[Cardiac Cycle - PV loops in valvular disease.png]]
*The page's four loops, each drawn over a normal "control loop" (LV pressure against LV volume): mitral stenosis (left shift, smaller), aortic stenosis (tall), aortic regurgitation (wide and tall), mitral regurgitation (wide, low) (cropped from the handwritten page).[^9]*

🧠 *Read each loop from the valve that is wrong: a **stenotic** valve blocks one direction (MS → can't fill → small EDV; AS → can't empty → high pressure, high ESV); a **regurgitant** valve leaks, so there is no true "both valves shut" phase — the isovolumetric sides of the loop are no longer straight vertical lines.*

### 📐 Terminologies

1. **Total electromechanical**[^ety-electromechanical][^pre-electro] **systole (Q–S2) :**
   - **Onset of the QRS complex to the closure of aortic valves (S2).**
2. **Left ventricular ejection time (LVET) :**
   - **Beginning of the carotid**[^ety-carotid] **pressure rise to dicrotic notch.**
   - **Correlates with ejection fraction.**
3. **Pre-ejection**[^pre-pre] **period (PEP) :**
   - **Difference b/w QS2 & LVET** (PEP = QS2 − LVET).
   - **PEP / LVET = 0.35 ; ↑ ratio ∝ 1 / ejection fraction.**[^10]

![[Cardiac Cycle - QS2 and LVET.png]]

```
Q (onset of QRS) ─────────────────────────────── S2 (aortic closure)
|←──── PEP ────→|←──────────── LVET ───────────→|
|←────────────── QS2 (total electromechanical systole) ─────────→|
PEP / LVET ≈ 0.35 ;  ↑ ratio  ⇒  ↓ ejection fraction
```

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> **Valves are passive: they open and close only because pressure on one side exceeds the other.** Every event in the cycle therefore lies where two pressure curves cross — ventricular pressure rising above atrial closes the mitral valve (S1), rising above aortic opens the aortic valve, falling below aortic closes it (S2, dicrotic notch), falling below atrial opens the mitral valve. Between a closure and the next opening both valves are shut, so the ventricle changes pressure without changing volume — the two isovolumetric phases that form the vertical sides of the pressure–volume loop.
>
> Volume is the other half: the ventricle fills to an EDV of 120 mL (80 % passively, 20 % by the atrial kick) and empties to an ESV of 50 mL, so SV = 70 mL and EF ≈ 58 %. Shortening the cycle (exercise) steals mostly from diastole, and every valve lesion distorts the loop in the direction of the valve's failure.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> At HR 75 the cycle lasts 0.8 s (ventricular systole 0.3 s, diastole 0.5 s). EDV 120 mL, ESV 50 mL, SV 70 mL, EF 55–60 %. S1 and S2 are the only audible sounds in an adult; S3 may be heard in children and after exercise.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A stiff, hypertrophied ventricle (hypertension, aortic stenosis) relies more on the atrial kick — an S4 appears and the atrium contributes > 20 % of filling. A leaking valve raises EDV (wider loop) so that forward stroke volume is maintained; EF may still look normal.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Tachycardia shortens diastole until filling fails (0.18 s at 0.3-s cycles); loss of the atrial kick (atrial fibrillation) drops output in stiff hearts; the dilated failing ventricle has a high ESV, low EF and an S3; the PEP/LVET ratio rises as EF falls.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → pressure or volume load → hypertrophy (S4) or dilatation (↑EDV, wide loop) → compensation → rising ESV, falling EF, S3 → heart failure
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Rate control (to lengthen diastole) helps stenotic and stiff hearts; restoring sinus rhythm returns the atrial kick; EF on echo (and the PV-loop pattern) guides when a valve must be replaced.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Isovolumetric contraction:** "Mitral closes, aortic already closed", "volume same, pressure ↑↑", "S1" → first phase of ventricular systole.
>
> **Protodiastole / isovolumetric relaxation:** "Aortic closes", "S2", "pressure ↓, volume same" → start of ventricular diastole.
>
> **Diastasis:** "Slow filling phase" → 4th phase of diastole; shortened first in tachycardia.
>
> **Atrial kick:** "20 % active filling", "lost in atrial fibrillation" → 5th phase of diastole; S4 when forceful.
>
> **S3:** "Rapid filling", "children, after exercise" → physiological in the young; ventricular gallop in failure.
>
> **S4:** "Stiff ventricle", "hypertension", "restrictive cardiomyopathy", "P wave" → always pathological.
>
> **PV loops:** "Left shift, ↓EDV" → mitral stenosis; "↑ESV, ↑LV pressure" → aortic stenosis; "↑↑EDV, height ↑, width ↑" → aortic regurgitation; "↑↑EDV, height ↓, width ↑" → mitral regurgitation.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Dicrotic notch = aortic valve closure = S2; a pressure–volume loop whose vertical sides are slanted = a regurgitant valve; S1 at the R wave, S2 at the end of the T wave.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> EF < 40 % = reduced (systolic failure); PEP/LVET > 0.35 (rising) = falling EF; cycle < 0.3 s (HR ~200) = diastole 0.18 s, filling compromised.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "Tachycardia shortens systole and diastole equally"**
>
> **What students think:** All phases shrink in proportion.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** At rest systole is 0.3 s and diastole 0.5 s; at a 0.3-s cycle systole is 0.12 s but diastole only 0.18 s — diastole loses 0.32 s, systole 0.18 s. Since filling and coronary flow happen in diastole, very high rates reduce EDV and myocardial perfusion.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Speed steals from diastole."
>
> **Trap 2: Which valve makes which sound**
>
> **What students think:** S1 is the aortic valve opening.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Valve opening is silent in a normal heart. S1 = closure of the AV valves (mitral + tricuspid) at the start of isovolumetric contraction; S2 = closure of the semilunar valves (aortic + pulmonary) at protodiastole.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Sounds = closures; S1 starts systole, S2 starts diastole.
>
> **Trap 3: Thinking atrial contraction does most of the filling**
>
> **What students think:** The atria pump blood into the ventricles.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** About 80 % of filling is passive (rapid filling + diastasis) as soon as the mitral valve opens; the atrial kick adds only ~20 %. It matters most when the ventricle is stiff (then it becomes > 20 % and produces S4) or the rate is high.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "80 passive, 20 kick."
>
> **Trap 4: S3 vs S4 timing and meaning**
>
> **What students think:** Both are always pathological, or S3 is the stiff-ventricle sound.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** S3 comes early in diastole (rapid filling) and is physiological in children and after exercise (pathological in older adults with a dilated ventricle); S4 comes late (atrial contraction, with the P wave) against a stiff ventricle and is pathological (hypertension, restrictive cardiomyopathy).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** S3 = early, "rushing into a big bucket"; S4 = late, "pushing into a stiff wall".
>
> **Trap 5: Misreading the EF formulas**
>
> **What students think:** EF = SV / ESV.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** EF = SV / **EDV** (the fraction of what was in the ventricle that is ejected). Rearranging gives ESV = EDV(1 − EF) and EDV = ESV / (1 − EF). With EDV 120 and ESV 50, EF = 70/120 ≈ 58 %.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Fraction of the full ventricle" — denominator is always EDV.

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[ECG and JVP]]**
> The ECG and JVP sit on the same time line as the Wiggers diagram: P → atrial systole (a wave), QRS → isovolumetric contraction (S1, c wave), T end → S2, v wave during late systole.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Cardiac Output]]**
> SV = EDV − ESV is one half of CO = HR × SV; preload (EDV), afterload and contractility (EF) are the determinants that shape the PV loop.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Cardiac Action Potentials]]**
> The AV nodal delay (0.1 s) lets the atrial kick finish before isovolumetric contraction; the long plateau keeps systole and refractoriness matched.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Hemodynamics]]**
> Pressure gradients across valves, turbulence (murmurs) and flow follow the same pressure-difference rules.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Regulation of Blood Pressure]]**
> The aortic pressure curve — systolic peak, dicrotic notch, diastolic run-off — is the arterial pulse that the baroreceptors sense.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Echocardiography measures EDV, ESV and EF; auscultation times murmurs between S1 and S2; S3/S4 point to dilated vs stiff ventricles; rate control and restoring sinus rhythm protect diastolic filling.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Expect the durations (0.8 / 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.1 / 0.7 s), the phase lists (3 systolic, 5 diastolic) with their valve events, the heart sound table, SV/EF calculations, PV-loop shapes for each valve lesion, S1–S4 on the Wiggers diagram and the systolic time intervals (QS2, LVET, PEP/LVET 0.35).

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **Cardiac cycle = 0.8 s** — atrial systole 0.1 s, atrial diastole 0.7 s, **ventricular systole 0.3 s (3 phases)**, **ventricular diastole 0.5 s (5 phases)**.
> 2. **Cardiac cycle = 60/HR (∝ 1/HR)**; exercise → cycle 0.3 s → **systole 0.12 s, diastole 0.18 s → ventricular filling compromised.**
> 3. Systole: **isovolumetric contraction** (mitral closes → **S1**, aortic already closed, volume same, pressure ↑↑) → **rapid ejection (2/3)** → **slow ejection (1/3)** → **ESV 50 mL**.
> 4. Diastole: **protodiastole** (aortic closes → **S2**) → **isovolumetric relaxation** (volume same, pressure ↓) → **rapid filling** (mitral opens) → **slow filling = diastasis** (passive 80 %) → **atrial contraction (20 %, atrial kick)** → **EDV 120 mL**.
> 5. **S1 = mitral + tricuspid closure; S2 = aortic + pulmonary closure** (physiological).
> 6. **S3 = vibration during rapid filling** — physiological in **children & after exercise**; **S4 = stiff ventricle, forceful atrial contraction (> 20 %)** — **pathological (hypertension, restrictive cardiomyopathy)**.
> 7. **SV = EDV − ESV = 70 mL; EF = SV/EDV × 100; normal EF 55–60 %; EDV = ESV/(1 − EF); ESV = EDV(1 − EF).**
> 8. PV loop: A mitral opens (ESV) · B mitral closes (S1, EDV) · C aortic opens · E aortic closes (S2); width = stroke volume.
> 9. **MS: left shift, ↓EDV, ↓SV · AS: ↑↑ESV, ↑LV pressure · AR: ↑↑EDV, height ↑ width ↑ · MR: ↑↑EDV, height ↓ width ↑**; regurgitation → isovolumetric phases not straight.
> 10. Wiggers: **S1 = R wave · S2 = end of T wave · S3 = rapid inflow · S4 = P wave**; phonocardiogram = recording heart sounds.
> 11. **Dicrotic notch = aortic valve closure (S2).**
> 12. **QS2** = onset of QRS → S2; **LVET** = carotid upstroke → dicrotic notch (correlates with EF); **PEP = QS2 − LVET; PEP/LVET = 0.35; ↑ ratio ∝ 1/EF.**

```
0.8 s = A-sys 0.1 + A-dia 0.7 · V-sys 0.3 (3 phases) + V-dia 0.5 (5 phases) · CC = 60/HR · exercise: 0.12 + 0.18 → filling ↓
SYSTOLE: IVC (mitral shuts = S1, P↑↑) → rapid ejection 2/3 → slow ejection 1/3 → ESV 50
DIASTOLE: protodiastole (aortic shuts = S2) → IVR (P↓) → rapid fill → diastasis (passive 80 %) → atrial kick 20 % → EDV 120
SV 70 · EF = SV/EDV (55–60 %) · ESV = EDV(1−EF) · EDV = ESV/(1−EF)
S3 rapid filling (kids, exercise) · S4 stiff ventricle (HTN, RCM) · S1 R wave · S2 end T · S4 P wave
PV: MS left shift · AS tall, ↑ESV · AR wide + tall · MR wide + low · dicrotic notch = S2 · PEP/LVET 0.35 ∝ 1/EF
```

[^1]: 0.8 s corresponds to a heart rate of 75/min (60 ÷ 75 = 0.8). At 72/min the cycle is ~0.83 s.
[^2]: Example: at HR 200/min the cycle is 60 ÷ 200 = 0.3 s — the page's exercise figure. Systole shortens relatively little because it is set mainly by the duration of the ventricular action potential; diastole absorbs most of the shortening.
[^3]: Guyton: about 70 % of emptying occurs during the first third of ejection (rapid ejection) and 30 % in the next two-thirds (slow ejection); the page rounds this to 2/3 and 1/3. Isovolumetric contraction lasts ~0.02–0.03 s (Guyton) to ~0.05 s (Ganong).
[^4]: Guyton: ~80 % of ventricular filling is passive and the atria add ~20 % ("primer pump"). The atrial kick is lost in atrial fibrillation; its loss matters most in stiff ventricles (hypertrophy, aortic stenosis) and at high heart rates.
[^5]: S3 is normal in children, young adults, athletes and pregnancy; after ~40 years it usually indicates a dilated, volume-loaded ventricle (heart failure, mitral regurgitation) — the "ventricular gallop" ("Kentucky").
[^6]: S4 ("Tennessee") is heard just before S1, coincides with the P wave and is absent in atrial fibrillation (no atrial contraction). Causes: LV hypertrophy (hypertension, aortic stenosis, HCM), restrictive cardiomyopathy, acute MI.
[^7]: The diagonal line on the page's graph (from E down to B on the volume axis) is not labelled; in textbooks a line from the end-systolic point to EDV on the volume axis is the effective arterial elastance (Ea = end-systolic pressure ÷ stroke volume), an index of afterload.
[^8]: Echocardiographic categories: EF ≥ 50 % preserved, 41–49 % mildly reduced, ≤ 40 % reduced (heart failure with reduced EF). With EDV 120 and SV 70, EF = 58 %.
[^9]: Standard teaching: in mitral regurgitation part of the stroke volume goes back into the left atrium, so there is no true isovolumetric contraction; ESV is often normal or ↓ early (the page leaves the ESV cell blank, "—"). In aortic regurgitation blood leaks back from the aorta during diastole, so there is no true isovolumetric relaxation. Aortic stenosis gives a tall, narrow loop with high systolic pressure.
[^10]: Normal values (Weissler): PEP ≈ 0.10 s, LVET ≈ 0.30 s, PEP/LVET ≈ 0.35. In left ventricular failure PEP lengthens and LVET shortens, so the ratio rises as ejection fraction falls (ratio > 0.44 suggests reduced EF).
[^ety-wiggers]: **Wiggers diagram** — **Carl John Wiggers** (1883–1963), American cardiovascular physiologist (Western Reserve University, Cleveland), who recorded and published these combined pressure, volume, ECG and heart-sound tracings in the 1910s–1920s; the composite plot of one cardiac cycle carries his name.
[^ety-cycle]: **cycle** — Greek *kyklos*, "circle, wheel" → a series of events that returns to its start; the cardiac cycle runs from one beat to the next. Same root as *bicycle* and *cyclone*.
[^ety-systole]: **systole** — Greek *systolē*, "a drawing together, contraction" (*syn-*, together + *stellein*, to place, draw in) → contraction of a heart chamber. **Differentiate:** *systole* (contraction, emptying) · *diastole* (relaxation, filling) · *diastasis* (the slow-filling phase of diastole).
[^ety-diastole]: **diastole** — Greek *diastolē*, "a drawing apart, dilatation" (*dia-*, apart + *stellein*) → relaxation and filling of a chamber. Not the same as *diastasis* (a pause / separation, the 4th phase of diastole).
[^ety-isovolumetric]: **isovolumetric** — Greek *isos*, "equal" + Latin *volumen*, "a roll, volume" + Greek *metron*, "measure" + *-ic* → with equal (unchanged) measured volume: both valves are closed, so pressure changes but volume cannot. Also *isovolumic*. **Differentiate:** *isovolumetric* (volume constant, pressure changes — ventricle) · *isometric* (length constant, tension changes — skeletal muscle) · *isotonic* (tension constant, length changes).
[^ety-contraction]: **contraction** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *trahere*, "to draw" + *-ion* → drawing together, shortening or tensing of muscle. **Differentiate:** *contraction* (the act) · *contractility* (the capacity, inotropy) · *contracture* (a fixed, permanent shortening).
[^ety-mitral]: **mitral** — Latin *mitra*, "a headband, a bishop's mitre" → the two-cusped left AV valve looks like a bishop's mitre (named by Vesalius). Synonym *bicuspid valve*.
[^ety-valve]: **valve** — Latin *valva*, "a leaf of a folding door" → a flap that lets flow pass one way. The heart's valves are *atrioventricular* (mitral, tricuspid) and *semilunar* (aortic, pulmonary — Latin *semi-*, half + *luna*, moon).
[^ety-aorta]: **aorta / aortic** — Greek *aortē*, "that which is hung up, lifted" (*aeirein*, to lift) — Aristotle's name for the great artery from which the heart seems to hang. *Aortic valve* = the semilunar valve at its root.
[^ety-ejection]: **ejection** — Latin *e-* (*ex-*), "out" + *iacere*, "to throw" + *-ion* → throwing out; the ventricles throw out the stroke volume. Same root as *project*, *inject*, *reject*. **Differentiate:** *ejection* (out of the ventricle) · *regurgitation* (backward leak) · *injection* (into).
[^ety-protodiastole]: **protodiastole** — Greek *prōtos*, "first" + *diastolē* → the first moment of diastole: ventricular pressure is falling and the aortic valve closes (S2). Same *proto-* as *protozoa* (first animals) and *prototype*.
[^ety-relaxation]: **relaxation** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *laxare*, "to loosen" + *-ation* → loosening back; cardiac relaxation (lusitropy) is an active, energy-using step (Ca²⁺ re-uptake into the sarcoplasmic reticulum).
[^ety-diastasis]: **diastasis** — Greek *diastasis*, "separation, a standing apart" (*dia-*, apart + *histanai*, to stand) → the quiet pause of slow filling in mid-diastole when atrial and ventricular pressures are almost equal. Also used in surgery (*diastasis recti*, separation of the recti). **Differentiate** from *diastole*.
[^ety-atrium]: **atrium / atrial** — Latin *atrium*, "entrance hall" of a Roman house → the receiving chamber; the atrial kick tops up ventricular filling. Do not confuse with *auricle* (the ear-shaped appendage).
[^ety-etiology]: **etiology / aetiology** — Greek *aitia*, "cause" + *-logia*, "study of" → the study (here: statement) of the cause. **Differentiate:** *aetiology* (cause) · *pathogenesis* (mechanism by which the cause produces disease) · *pathology* (the resulting changes).
[^ety-tricuspid]: **tricuspid** — Latin *tri-*, "three" + *cuspis*, "point" → the right AV valve with three cusps. Closes together with the mitral valve to make S1.
[^ety-pulmonary]: **pulmonary** — Latin *pulmo, pulmonis*, "lung" + *-ary* → of the lungs; the pulmonary valve guards the outflow of the right ventricle into the pulmonary trunk. Greek equivalent *pneumon* (pneumonia).
[^ety-vibration]: **vibration** — Latin *vibrare*, "to shake, set in tremulous motion" + *-ation* → shaking; heart sounds are vibrations of valves, walls and blood set off by sudden changes in flow.
[^ety-pathological]: **pathological** — Greek *pathos*, "suffering, disease" + *-logia*, "study" + *-ical* → belonging to disease. **Differentiate:** *physiological* (normal function) vs *pathological* (due to disease); *pathognomonic* (*gnōmōn*, judge — a sign that alone proves a disease).
[^ety-hypertension]: **hypertension** — Greek *hyper-*, "over" + Latin *tensio*, "stretching" → excessive arterial pressure; the pressure-loaded ventricle hypertrophies, becomes stiff and gives an S4.
[^ety-restrictive]: **restrictive** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *stringere*, "to bind tight" → binding back; a *restrictive cardiomyopathy* has a stiff, non-compliant ventricle that resists filling (amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, haemochromatosis). **Differentiate** from *constrictive* pericarditis (a stiff pericardium squeezing a normal heart).
[^ety-cardiomyopathy]: **cardiomyopathy** — Greek *kardia*, "heart" + *mys*, "muscle" + *pathos*, "disease" + *-y* → disease of the heart muscle itself. Types: dilated, hypertrophic, restrictive, arrhythmogenic.
[^ety-fraction]: **fraction (ejection fraction)** — Latin *fractio*, "a breaking" (*frangere*, to break) → a broken-off part; EF is the part of the EDV that is ejected. Same root as *fracture* and *fragment*.
[^ety-stenosis]: **stenosis** — Greek *stenos*, "narrow" + *-osis*, condition → narrowing; a stenotic valve fails to open fully. **Differentiate:** *stenosis* (narrowing — obstruction to forward flow) · *regurgitation / insufficiency / incompetence* (failure to close — backward leak) · *atresia* (no opening at all).
[^ety-regurgitation]: **regurgitation** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *gurgitare*, "to flood, engulf" (*gurges*, a whirlpool) + *-ation* → flooding back; blood leaks backwards through an incompetent valve. Same root as *gurgle*.
[^ety-phonocardiogram]: **phonocardiogram** — Greek *phōnē*, "sound, voice" + *kardia*, "heart" + *gramma*, "something written" → a written record of heart sounds. **Differentiate:** *phonocardiogram* (record of sounds) · *electrocardiogram* (record of electrical activity) · *echocardiogram* (ultrasound image).
[^ety-dicrotic]: **dicrotic** — Greek *di-*, "twice" + *krotos*, "a beat, a striking" + *-ic* → double-beating; the notch and the small second wave after it make the pulse feel "double". **Differentiate:** *dicrotic notch* (aortic valve closure) · *anacrotic* (*ana-*, up — a notch on the upstroke, aortic stenosis) · *bisferiens* (two systolic peaks, aortic regurgitation).
[^ety-electromechanical]: **electromechanical** — Greek *ēlektron*, "amber → electricity" + *mēchanikos*, "of machines" (*mēchanē*, contrivance) → covering both the electrical (QRS) and the mechanical (valve closure) events; *total electromechanical systole* = Q–S2.
[^ety-carotid]: **carotid** — Greek *karōtides*, from *karoun*, "to stupefy, plunge into deep sleep" → ancient physicians noted that pressing these arteries makes a person faint. The carotid pulse tracing is used to time LVET.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym- / sy- (before s)** — together, with: systole (*sy-* + *stellein*), synapse, syncytium, symptom. Don't confuse with **semi-** half · **sin-** (Latin *sinus*, a hollow).
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, apart, across (Greek): diastole, diastasis, dialysis, diaphragm. Don't confuse with **di-** two (dicrotic) · **dys-** bad · **trans-** across (Latin).
[^pre-iso]: **iso-** — equal (Greek *isos*): isovolumetric, isometric, isotonic, isoelectric. Don't confuse with **homo-** same · **equi-** equal (Latin) · **ischo-** hold back (ischaemia).
[^suf-metric]: **-metric / -metry** — measuring: isovolumetric, isometric, spirometry, audiometry. Don't confuse with **-meter** the instrument (manometer) · **-graphy** recording.
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with: contraction, compliance, conduction, congestive (but not *coronary*, from *corona*, crown). Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** against.
[^pre-ex]: **ex- / e- / ef-** — out of: ejection, efflux, expiration. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek) · **extra-** beyond · **ec- / ecto-** out, outer.
[^pre-proto]: **proto-** — first: protodiastole, prototype, protoplasm, protozoa. Don't confuse with **pro-** before, forward (prolapse) · **pre-** before · **post-** after.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: relaxation, regurgitation, restrictive. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards, behind · **pre-** before.
[^suf-sis]: **-sis / -asis / -esis** — a process, action or state (Greek): diastasis, haemostasis, diuresis. Don't confuse with **-osis** an abnormal condition (stenosis) · **-itis** inflammation · **-iasis** infestation.
[^pre-tri]: **tri-** — three: tricuspid, triangle. Don't confuse with **trans-** across · **bi- / di-** two · **tetra-** four.
[^suf-logy]: **-logy / -logical** — the study of, or belonging to: pathological, physiological, aetiology. Don't confuse with **-logue** a speech (dialogue) · **-lysis** breaking down.
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, excessive: hypertension, hypertrophy, hyperkalaemia. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra- / super-** above (Latin).
[^pre-myo]: **myo-** — muscle: cardiomyopathy, myocardium, myocyte. Don't confuse with **myelo-** marrow, spinal cord · **myco-** fungus · **myxo-** mucus.
[^suf-pathy]: **-pathy** — disease, suffering (Greek *pathos*): cardiomyopathy, neuropathy, nephropathy. Don't confuse with **-pathic** "of unknown cause" in *idiopathic* · **sympathy / empathy** (feeling) · **-plasty** surgical repair.
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — an abnormal condition or process: stenosis, fibrosis, thrombosis. Don't confuse with **-sis** a neutral process (diastasis) · **-itis** inflammation · **-oma** a tumour.
[^pre-phono]: **phono- / phon-** — sound, voice: phonocardiogram, phonation, dysphonia. Don't confuse with **photo-** light · **phobo-** fear · **phago-** eating.
[^suf-gram]: **-gram / -graph / -graphy** — *-gram* the record (phonocardiogram, electrocardiogram); *-graph* the instrument; *-graphy* the technique. Don't confuse with **gram** the unit of mass · **-scopy** viewing.
[^pre-di]: **di-** — two, twice (Greek *dis*): dicrotic, diploid, dioxide. Don't confuse with **dia-** through, apart (diastole) · **dis-** apart (Latin) · **bi-** two (Latin).
[^pre-electro]: **electro-** — electricity (Greek *ēlektron*, amber): electromechanical, electrocardiogram, electrolyte. Don't confuse with **electron** the particle · **elect-** to choose.
[^pre-pre]: **pre-** — before: pre-ejection period, preload, prepotential. Don't confuse with **pro-** forward · **post-** after · **peri-** around.
