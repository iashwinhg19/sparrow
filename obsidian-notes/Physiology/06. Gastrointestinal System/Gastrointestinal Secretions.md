# 💧 Gastrointestinal Secretions

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> Gastrointestinal **secretions**[^ety-secretion] are the juices that the glands of the gut pour into its lumen — **saliva, gastric juice, pancreatic juice, bile and intestinal juice (succus entericus)** — to make **digestion**[^ety-digestion][^pre-di] and **absorption**[^ety-absorption][^pre-ab] of food possible. Every juice answers the same three questions:
> - ✅ **What does it digest** (carbohydrate, fat, protein) and with which **enzyme**[^ety-enzyme]?
> - ✅ **What does it protect** against (dry food, acid, microbes)?
> - ✅ **Who switches it on** — the **enteric**[^ety-enteric][^pre-entero] **nervous system** or the **GI hormones**[^ety-hormone]?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Five-Station Food Factory

Picture the gut as a disassembly line. Each station adds one juice, and each juice has one job it does better than any other: the mouth wets and starts on starch, the stomach sterilises and starts on protein, the pancreas does the heavy cutting of everything and neutralises the acid, bile turns fat into droplets that enzymes can reach, and the intestinal crypts finish the job and guard the wall. Nine litres of water pour through the line every day — and all but 200 mL is pulled back.

```
STATION        JUICE (mL/day)        pH          STAR CELL / ENZYME                         SWITCHED ON BY
MOUTH          Saliva 1500           7           amylase (ptyalin), lingual lipase          PNS (thin, watery) > SNS (thick)
STOMACH        Gastric 2500          1.5–3       parietal → HCl + IF · chief → pepsinogen   histamine (H2) · gastrin (CCK-B) · ACh (M3)
PANCREAS       Pancreatic 1500       8.3         amylase · lipase + colipase · zymogens     CCK-PZ (enzymes) · secretin (HCO3-)
LIVER/GB       Bile 500              —           no enzyme — bile salts emulsify fat        choleretics (bile salt) · cholagogue (CCK)
INTESTINE      Intestinal 1000       —           goblet (mucus) · Paneth (defensins)        local
                ↓
  2000 (ingested) + 7000 (secretions) = 9000 mL → 8800 reabsorbed → 200 mL in stool
```

## 📖 Introduction

**Digestion & absorption of food requires:**

```
• GI secretions (with enzymes) ─┐                          ┌──► Enteric nervous system
                                ├──── regulated by ────────┤
• GI motility ──────────────────┘                          └──► GI hormones
```

- **GI secretions** — the juices and their enzymes break food down chemically.
- **GI motility**[^ety-motility] — movement that mixes the food with the juices and pushes it along.
- Both are **regulated by** the **enteric nervous system** (the gut's own nerve plexuses) and the **GI hormones** ([[Gastrointestinal Hormones]]).

**Digestive & protective functions.**

### Salivary glands

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - salivary glands.png]]

### FUNCTIONS OF SALIVA

| Function → **Digestion of** | Carbohydrate | Fats | Protein |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Enzyme involved in digestion** | **Salivary amylase**[^ety-amylase][^suf-ase] / **ptyalin**[^ety-ptyalin] | **Lingual lipase**[^ety-lipase] (by **von Ebner's glands**) | **No enzymes** |

| Function | Details |
|---|---|
| **Lubrication**[^ety-lubrication] | To prevent injury to **buccal**[^ety-buccal] **mucosa** by dry foods |
| **Protection** | **Immunity conferred through:** • **Secretory IgA**[^ety-immunoglobulin] • **Lactoferrin**[^ety-lactoferrin] • **Lysozymes**[^ety-lysozyme] — lactoferrin and lysozymes are **anti-bacterial**[^pre-anti] |

### Salivon[^ety-salivon]

**Functional unit of salivary gland.**

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - salivon.png]]
*The salivon — a blind-ended acinus (makes the primary, plasma-like secretion) drained by a duct (reabsorbs Na⁺ & Cl⁻, secretes K⁺ & HCO₃⁻ — the secondary secretion) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Part | Cells | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Acinus**[^ety-acinus] | Acinar cells | **1° secretion** (**similar to plasma**) |
| **Duct** | Ductal cells | **Reabsorption of Na⁺ & Cl⁻** · **Secretion of K⁺ & HCO₃⁻** → **2° secretion** |

- During **high flow rates, ↓ time for resorption → secretion has ↑ Na⁺ and Cl⁻.**[^3]

🧠 *In plain words:* the acinus makes a plasma-like fluid; the duct then pulls out salt (Na⁺, Cl⁻) but lets little water follow, so the saliva that reaches the mouth is **hypotonic**. When saliva gushes (high flow), the duct has no time to pull the salt back, so the saliva is saltier — closer to plasma.

### Regulation of secretion

**Salivation is an unconditioned reflex.**[^4]

**a. Regulation by autonomic**[^ety-autonomic][^pre-auto] **nervous system**

| **Sympathetic**[^ety-sympathetic][^pre-syn] **system** | **Parasympathetic system** |
|---|---|
| • **↑ in secretion of thick saliva** | • **Profound ↑ in secretion of thin watery saliva** |

**b.** **Bradykinin**[^ety-bradykinin][^pre-brady][^suf-kinin] → **vasodilation**[^ety-vasodilation][^pre-vaso] → ↑ blood flow to salivary gland → ↑ secretion.

**Physiological factors that affect saliva secretion:**
- **Increase:** sight, smell or thought of food.
- **Decrease:** sleep, fear & fatigue.

### Innervation of salivary glands

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - innervation of salivary glands.png]]
*Parotid gland ← otic ganglion ← CN IX; submandibular and sublingual glands ← submandibular ganglion ← CN VII. The facial nerve runs through the parotid — ↑ chances of injury to CN VII during parotid surgery (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
CN IX  ──►  Otic ganglion  ──────────►  Parotid gland
CN VII ──►  Submandibular ganglion ──►  Submandibular gland  ──►  Sublingual gland
```

- **CN IX**[^ety-glossopharyngeal] → **otic ganglion**[^ety-otic][^ety-ganglion] → **parotid gland**.[^5]
- **CN VII**[^ety-facial] → **submandibular ganglion** → **submandibular gland** & **sublingual gland**.
- **↑ chances of injury to CN VII during parotid surgery** — the facial nerve branches inside the parotid.

### Abnormalities of salivary secretions

```
                                      SALIVA
        ┌───────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┐
 Absent secretion                ↓ secretion                     Associated abnormalities
        ↓                              ↓                                    ↓
   Aptyalism              Xerostomia (dry mouth)            Injury to facial nerve during parotid surgery
                                       ↓                                    ↓
                          Commonly seen in:                       During regeneration
                           • Mumps                     ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
                           • Sjogren syndrome   Innervates sweat glands               Innervates lacrimal gland
                                                       ↓                                         ↓
                                          ↑ salivation → ↑ sweating          ↑ salivation → ↑ lacrimation
                                                                       (Crocodile tears / gustatory lacrimation)
```

- **Absent secretion → Aptyalism**[^ety-aptyalism][^pre-a][^suf-ism].
- **↓ secretion → Xerostomia**[^ety-xerostomia][^pre-xero] **(dry mouth)** — commonly seen in **mumps**[^ety-mumps] and **Sjögren syndrome**[^ety-sjogren].
- **Associated abnormalities:** **injury to facial nerve during parotid surgery** → during **regeneration**[^ety-regeneration] the cut parasympathetic (salivary) fibres grow into the wrong targets:
  - **Innervates sweat glands** → **↑ salivation → ↑ sweating** (gustatory sweating).[^6]
  - **Innervates lacrimal**[^ety-lacrimal] **gland** → **↑ salivation → ↑ lacrimation** — **Crocodile tears / gustatory lacrimation**.[^7]

## 🧪 Gastric Secretions

- **Volume: 2.5 L/day.**
- **pH: 1.5–3 (most acidic secretions).**

### Gastric glands

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - gastric gland cells.png]]
*Flask-shaped gastric gland — surface cells at the mucosal surface, neck (stem) cells, parietal cells, enterochromaffin-like cells and chief cells down the gland; G cells in the antral glands (cropped from the handwritten page).*

**Flask shaped gastric glands** — six cell types from the surface down:

| No. | Cell | Produces / role |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Surface cell** (at the mucosal surface) | • **HCO₃⁻-rich mucus** for **acid neutralisation**[^ety-neutralisation] • **Trefoil peptides**[^ety-trefoil]: **confers immunity** |
| **2** | **Neck cells** | **Stem cells of gastric glands** |
| **3** | **Parietal**[^ety-parietal] **cells** | • **HCl acid** • **Intrinsic Factor (IF) of Castle**[^ety-castle]: for **Vit B₁₂ absorption** |
| **4** | **Enterochromaffin-like cells (ECL)**[^ety-enterochromaffin] | **Histamine**[^ety-histamine] → ↑ acid secretion |
| **5** | **Chief cells** | **Pepsinogen**[^ety-pepsinogen][^suf-ogen] enzyme |
| **6** | **Antral**[^ety-antrum] **gastric glands** | **G cells → Gastrin**[^ety-gastrin] → ↑ acid secretion |

> [!danger] Note: In pernicious anaemia
> ```
> Autoantibodies to parietal cells ──► Destruction of parietal cells ─┬─► Achlorhydria (↓ acid secretion)
>                                                ↓                    └─► Anemia (↓ vit B12 absorption)
>                                     Atrophic gastric mucosa
> ```
> In **pernicious**[^ety-pernicious] **anaemia**[^ety-anaemia]: **autoantibodies to parietal cells** → destruction of parietal cells → **achlorhydria**[^ety-achlorhydria] (↓ acid secretion) + **anaemia** (↓ vit B₁₂ absorption, because intrinsic factor is lost) → **atrophic**[^ety-atrophy][^suf-trophy] **gastric mucosa**.[^8]

### Acid production in parietal cell

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - acid production in parietal cell.png]]
*Parietal cell: CO₂ + H₂O → (carbonic anhydrase, CA) → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. H⁺ is pumped into the glandular lumen by the H⁺/K⁺ ATPase (a) in exchange for K⁺; Cl⁻ follows through the Cl⁻ channel (c) → HCl. HCO₃⁻ leaves into the blood through the Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger (b) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Label | Transporter |
|---|---|
| **a** | **H⁺/K⁺ ATPase**[^ety-atpase] **proton pump** (apical) |
| **b** | **Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger** (basolateral) |
| **c** | **Cl⁻ channel** (apical) |
| **CA** | **Carbonic anhydrase**[^ety-carbonic-anhydrase] |

```
CO2 + H2O ──CA──► H2CO3 ──► H+  ──(a: H+/K+ ATPase)──► lumen  + Cl- (via c)  ═══►  HCl
                        └─► HCO3- ──(b: Cl-/HCO3- exchanger)──► blood   (Cl- enters the cell in return)
```

**Post-prandial**[^ety-prandial][^pre-post] **alkaline tide:**

```
↑ in HCl production after meal
            ↓
↑ HCO3- to blood  (post-prandial alkaline tide)
```

- **↑ in HCl production after meal → ↑ HCO₃⁻ to blood (post-prandial alkaline tide).**[^9]

### Regulation of gastric acid secretion

**Factors that increase:**

| Substance | Source | Acts on (Receptor[^ety-receptor]) |
|---|---|---|
| **Histamine** | **ECL cells** | **H₂** |
| **Gastrin** | **Antral G-cells** | **Cholecystokinin-B (CCK-B)** |
| **Acetyl choline**[^ety-acetylcholine] | **Vagal**[^ety-vagus] **synapses** | **M₃**[^ety-muscarinic] |
| Coffee | Ingestion | – |
| Alcohol | Ingestion | – |
| Ca²⁺ | Ingestion | – |

**Factors that decrease:**
- **Excess acid: negative feedback.**
- **Somatostatin**[^ety-somatostatin][^suf-statin]**: universal inhibitor.**
- **Prostaglandin**[^ety-prostaglandin].
- **Cholecystokinin**[^ety-cholecystokinin][^pre-chole].
- **Secretin**[^ety-secretin].
- **Calcitonin**[^ety-calcitonin].[^10]

### Functions of gastric secretion

| Function → **Digestion of** | Carbohydrate | Fats | Proteins |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Enzyme involved** | **No enzymes** | **Gastric lipases** | • **Pepsinogen —(activated by HCl)→ Pepsin** • **Rennin**[^ety-rennin] (in cows) |

| Function | Details |
|---|---|
| **Protection** | **↑ acidity → microorganism killed** |

### Phases of gastric acid secretion

| | **Cephalic**[^ety-cephalic] **phase** | **Gastric phase** | **Intestinal phase** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **% of gastric acid secretion** | 20–30 % | **60–70 %** | 10 % |
| **Gastric secretion occurs** | **In absence of food** | When food enters stomach | D/t products of **protein digestion** |
| **Initiated by** | Sight, smell or thought of food | Antral G-cells → gastrin → ↑ acid secretion | Intestinal G-cells → gastrin → ↑ acid secretion |

🧠 *In plain words:* the brain starts the acid before food arrives (cephalic, vagal), the stomach makes most of it once food stretches it and protein reaches the antral G cells (gastric), and a little more comes when protein products reach the duodenum (intestinal).[^11]

## 🥞 Exocrine Pancreatic Secretion

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - exocrine pancreas.png]]
*Pancreas — head (in the duodenal loop), tail, lobules, the pancreatic duct joining the common bile duct; the enlarged lobule shows acinar cells that secrete digestive enzymes and exocrine cells that secrete pancreatic juice into the pancreatic duct. The page labels the pale islet clusters at left "pancreatic duct cells — secrete hormones" (label masked here) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- Labels on the page: **common bile duct**, **pancreatic duct**, **head of pancreas**, **tail of pancreas**, **lobules**, **acinar cells secrete digestive enzymes**, **pancreatic duct cells — secrete hormones**[^12], **exocrine cells secrete pancreatic juice**.
- **Pancreatic juice:**
  - **Can digest all nutrients.**
  - **1500 mL.**
  - **pH: 8.3 (↑ HCO₃⁻).**

### Function

```
                               Digestion of
            ┌───────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────┐
     Carbohydrates                 Fats                         Proteins
   Pancreatic amylase      Pancreatic lipase,          • Zymogens (inactive enzymes)
                         assisted by co-lipase         • Trypsinogen
                                                       • Chymotrypsinogen
                                                       • Procarboxypeptidase
```

- **Carbohydrates → pancreatic amylase.**
- **Fats → pancreatic lipase, assisted by co-lipase**[^ety-colipase][^pre-co].
- **Proteins → zymogens**[^ety-zymogen] **(inactive enzymes):** **trypsinogen**[^ety-trypsin], **chymotrypsinogen**[^ety-chymotrypsin], **procarboxypeptidase**[^ety-carboxypeptidase][^pre-pro].

### Activation of zymogens in small intestine

```
                               Trypsinogen
                                    │  Enterokinase
                                    ▼
 Chymotrypsinogen ◄──(+)────────  Trypsin  ────────(+)──► Procarboxypeptidase
        │                           │                            │
        ▼                           │                            ▼
   Chymotrypsin                     │                     Carboxypeptidase
        └──────────────────►  Protein digestion  ◄───────────────┘
```

- **Trypsinogen —(enterokinase**[^ety-enterokinase]**)→ trypsin.**
- **Trypsin (+)** activates **chymotrypsinogen → chymotrypsin** and **procarboxypeptidase → carboxypeptidase**.
- All three → **protein digestion**.[^13]

> [!danger] Note
> **Inflammatory**[^ety-inflammation] **process → premature activation of zymogens → Acute pancreatitis**[^ety-pancreatitis][^suf-itis] **(auto-digestion).**

### Regulation

**Hormonal regulation:**

| **Cholecystokinin–pancreozymin (CCK-PZ)**[^ety-pancreozymin] | **Secretin (Nature's antacid)** |
|---|---|
| • **Constriction of gall bladder.** | Released from **S-cells** in intestine on stimulation by **acid chyme**[^ety-chyme] |
| • **↑ pancreatic enzymes in secretion.** | ↓ |
| | **↑ HCO₃⁻ secretion into pancreatic juice** |
| | ↓ |
| | **Alkalinity of pancreatic secretion for acid neutralisation** |

> [!note] Note — Secretin
> - **First hormone to be discovered.**
> - **↓↓ enzyme concentration** (it adds a large volume of bicarbonate-rich water, so the enzymes are diluted).[^14]

### Mechanism of HCO₃⁻ secretion

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - HCO3 secretion in pancreatic duct cell.png]]
*Pancreatic duct cell: CO₂ + H₂O → (CA) → H₂CO₃ → HCO₃⁻ + H⁺. HCO₃⁻ leaves into the lumen through the Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger (a), and the Cl⁻ it takes in is recycled back to the lumen through CFTR (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- **a: Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger.**
- **CFTR: Cystic fibrosis**[^ety-cystic-fibrosis][^suf-osis] **transmembrane**[^pre-trans] **conductance**[^ety-conductance] **regulator → chloride channel opening (mutation: cystic fibrosis).**

🧠 *In plain words:* the exchanger can only push HCO₃⁻ out if Cl⁻ is waiting in the lumen to swap with it. CFTR keeps returning that Cl⁻. A faulty CFTR (cystic fibrosis) → no luminal Cl⁻ → no HCO₃⁻ and no water → thick secretions plug the pancreatic ducts.[^15]

## 🟢 Biliary Secretion

```
Liver : Synthesis of bile (97 % water)  ──►  Gall bladder : Storage of concentrated bile (87 % water)
```

- **Liver: synthesis of bile (97 % water)** → **Gall bladder: storage of concentrated bile (87 % water).**[^16]

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - biliary secretion liver and gall bladder.png]]
*Biliary secretion — bile is produced by the liver (green branching bile ducts) and stored in the gall bladder (cropped from the handwritten page).*

### Functions of bile[^ety-bile]
- **Digestion and absorption of lipids.**
- **Excretion of:**
  - **Bile pigments.**
  - **Cholesterol.**

### Constituents of bile

```
Primary bile acids (in liver)  ──Intestinal flora──►  Secondary bile acids (in intestine)
 • Cholic acid                                         (eg: Deoxycholic acid, Lithocholic acid)
 • Chenodeoxycholic acid                                            ↓
                                                Conjugation in liver (with glycine / taurines)
                                                                    ↓ + Na+
                                                       Bile salts (Sodium salts)
                                                   (eg: Sodium glycocholate salts)
```

- **Primary bile acids (in liver):** **cholic acid**[^ety-cholic], **chenodeoxycholic acid**[^ety-chenodeoxycholic].
- **Intestinal flora** → **secondary bile acids (in intestine)** — eg: **deoxycholic acid**[^pre-de], **lithocholic acid**[^ety-lithocholic].
- **Conjugation**[^ety-conjugation] **in liver** (with **glycine**[^ety-glycine] / **taurines**[^ety-taurine]) → **+ Na⁺** → **bile salts (sodium salts)** — eg: **sodium glycocholate salts**.[^17]

### Enterohepatic[^ety-enterohepatic][^pre-hepato] circulation

**Purpose: minimize energy consumption during bile synthesis.**

![[Gastrointestinal Secretions - enterohepatic circulation.png]]
*Enterohepatic circulation — bile in the liver and gall bladder → intestine → reabsorbed at the terminal ileum → back to the liver in the portal vein, 6–8 times a day (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
Bile in liver and gall bladder ───────────────►  Terminal ileum
           ▲                                          │
           └─────────────── PORTAL VEIN ◄─────────────┘
                         (6–8 times / day)
```

- Bile salts secreted into the gut are **reabsorbed in the terminal ileum** and returned to the liver by the **portal**[^ety-portal] **vein** — the same pool is reused **6–8 times/day**, so the liver need not make new bile salts for every meal.

| **Choleretic**[^ety-choleretic] **(↑ bile synthesis)** | **Cholagogues**[^ety-cholagogue][^suf-gogue] **(↑ contraction of gall bladder)** |
|---|---|
| – **Bile salt** | – **CCK** |
| – Secretin | |
| – Vagal stimulation | |

## 🧫 Intestinal Secretions

- **AKA succus entericus**[^ety-succus].
- **Site: Crypts of Lieberkühn**[^ety-crypt][^ety-lieberkuhn].

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Every GI juice is a **plasma-like fluid made by an acinus or gland and then edited by a transporter** — and the transporter decides its character. The parietal cell's H⁺/K⁺ ATPase makes gastric juice the most acidic fluid in the body; the pancreatic duct's Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger (fed by CFTR) makes pancreatic juice the most alkaline; the salivary duct's Na⁺/Cl⁻ reabsorption makes saliva hypotonic. When a transporter fails (CFTR in cystic fibrosis) or its cell is destroyed (parietal cells in pernicious anaemia), the juice loses its defining property.
>
> Secretion is then **matched to the meal**: nerves start it (vagus → cephalic phase, parasympathetic → watery saliva), hormones fit it to what arrived (gastrin for protein, secretin for acid, CCK for fat), and the end product turns it off (excess acid, somatostatin). Enzymes that could digest the gland itself are made as zymogens and switched on only in the lumen.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> About 7 L of juice is secreted daily (saliva 1.5, stomach 2.5, pancreas 1.5, bile 0.5, intestine 1 L) and, with 2 L ingested, 8.8 of the 9 L are reabsorbed (jejunum most). Acid, enzymes and bicarbonate are released in phase with the meal, zymogens are activated only by enterokinase in the duodenum, and bile salts are recycled 6–8 times a day.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A partial loss is masked: dry mouth from drugs or early Sjögren syndrome is helped by sipping water; early autoimmune gastritis reduces acid, but B₁₂ stores (years' worth) hide the deficiency; a pancreas with mild CFTR dysfunction still digests because its reserve is huge. The only clues are subtle — achlorhydria raises gastrin, or a high sweat chloride.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Reserve is exhausted: xerostomia brings caries and dysphagia; parietal cell destruction gives pernicious anaemia with atrophic mucosa; premature intrapancreatic trypsin activation causes acute pancreatitis (auto-digestion); CF ducts plug and the gland fibroses (steatorrhoea); ileal resection breaks the enterohepatic circulation → bile-salt loss → fat malabsorption. After parotid surgery, misdirected regeneration gives gustatory sweating or crocodile tears.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → loss of a secreting cell or transporter (autoimmunity, mutation, surgery, inflammation) → reserve and stores compensate → reserve exhausted → overt deficiency (acid, B₁₂, HCO₃⁻, enzymes, bile salts) → clinical syndrome
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Replacing what the juice can no longer supply is the treatment: parenteral B₁₂ (oral fails without intrinsic factor), pancreatic enzyme capsules, proton-pump inhibitors that block the H⁺/K⁺ ATPase when acid is the problem, and saliva substitutes. Knowing which cell makes what tells you what to replace.

>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Pernicious anaemia:** "Autoantibodies to parietal cells", "achlorhydria", "atrophic gastric mucosa", "megaloblastic anaemia" → Parietal cells make both HCl and intrinsic factor of Castle, so one autoimmune attack removes acid and B₁₂ absorption together.
>

>
> **Cystic fibrosis:** "CFTR mutation", "chloride channel", "thick secretions", "↓ HCO₃⁻ in pancreatic juice" → Without luminal Cl⁻ the duct's Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchanger stalls.
>
> **Xerostomia:** "Dry mouth", "mumps", "Sjögren syndrome" → ↓ salivary secretion; absent secretion = aptyalism.
>
> **Crocodile tears / gustatory lacrimation:** "Tears while eating after facial nerve injury" → Regenerating salivary fibres innervate the lacrimal gland; into sweat glands → gustatory sweating.
>

>

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "Sympathetic stimulation stops salivation"**
>
> **What students think:** Sympathetic = dry mouth, so it must inhibit the glands.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Both divisions *stimulate* salivary secretion. The sympathetic gives a small volume of **thick**, protein-rich saliva; the parasympathetic gives a **profound** flow of **thin, watery** saliva. The dry mouth of fear comes from vasoconstriction and the absence of parasympathetic drive, not from direct inhibition.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Both say yes — PNS shouts (watery), SNS whispers (thick)."
>
> **Trap 2: Mixing up Meissner and Auerbach**
>
> **What students think:** The myenteric plexus controls secretion because it is "in the wall".
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The **submucosal (Meissner's)** plexus lies next to the mucosa and **regulates secretion**; the **myenteric (Auerbach's)** plexus lies between the circular and longitudinal coats and controls **motility**. The muscularis mucosa alters surface area and has **no role in motility**.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** **M**eissner–**M**ucosa–secretion; **A**uerbach–**A**ction (muscle).
>
> **Trap 3: Saliva is hypertonic at high flow**
>
> **What students think:** Fast flow means more ductal processing, so saliva becomes more dilute.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The duct reabsorbs Na⁺ and Cl⁻ with little water; that takes time. At high flow there is **less time for reabsorption**, so saliva has **more Na⁺ and Cl⁻** and its tonicity rises towards (but stays below) plasma. At low flow saliva is most hypotonic.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Rushing saliva is salty saliva."
>
> **Trap 4: Choleretic vs cholagogue**
>
> **What students think:** CCK increases bile production.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** CCK is a **cholagogue** — it **contracts the gall bladder** (and relaxes the sphincter of Oddi) to *release* stored bile. The strongest **choleretic** (↑ bile synthesis/secretion by the liver) is **bile salt** itself returning in the enterohepatic circulation; secretin and vagal stimulation are choleretics too.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** -gogue = "leader" (leads bile out of the gall bladder); -retic = "flow" (makes the liver pour more).
>
> **Trap 5: Which phase secretes the most acid**
>
> **What students think:** The cephalic phase, because it starts first.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The cephalic phase gives 20–30 %, the **gastric phase 60–70 %**, the intestinal phase about 10 %. Only the cephalic phase occurs in the **absence of food**.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Food in the stomach = most acid in the stomach."

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Gastrointestinal Hormones]]**
> Gastrin, CCK-PZ, secretin, somatostatin and GIP are the hormones that switch these juices on and off; the enteroendocrine cells that make them sit in the same glands described here.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Digestion and Absorption]]**
> The enzymes of this chapter (amylase, lipase + colipase, trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase) and bile-salt micelles are put to work there; intrinsic factor returns as the key to B₁₂ absorption in the terminal ileum.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Gastrointestinal Motility]]**
> Secretion and motility are the two requirements for digestion and share the same regulators (enteric plexuses, gastrin, CCK, secretin).
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Acid Base Balance]]**
> The post-prandial alkaline tide (parietal HCO₃⁻ into blood) and the loss of HCO₃⁻-rich pancreatic or intestinal juice in diarrhoea (metabolic acidosis) and of HCl in vomiting (metabolic alkalosis) link GI secretion to acid–base disorders.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Membrane Transport]]**
> H⁺/K⁺ ATPase (primary active transport), Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchange (antiport) and CFTR (an ATP-gated Cl⁻ channel) are textbook transporters seen in action in this chapter.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Hematopoiesis and Blood Groups]]**
> Vitamin B₁₂, which depends on intrinsic factor from parietal cells, is essential for DNA synthesis in erythropoiesis — its lack gives megaloblastic (pernicious) anaemia.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Proton-pump inhibitors, H₂ blockers and anticholinergics target the three acid stimulants (ACh, gastrin, histamine) at their receptors; pancreatic enzyme replacement, parenteral B₁₂, bile-acid sequestrants and saliva substitutes each replace one juice. Parotid surgery puts the facial nerve at risk and can leave gustatory sweating or crocodile tears.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Favourite one-liners: most acidic juice (gastric), most alkaline (pancreatic), largest salivary gland (parotid), lingual lipase from von Ebner's glands, IF of Castle from parietal cells, enterokinase activates trypsinogen, secretin = first hormone discovered / nature's antacid, maximum water absorption in jejunum, maximum K⁺ concentration in colonic secretion.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Digestion needs **secretions + motility**, both regulated by the **enteric nervous system and GI hormones**. **Submucosal (Meissner's) plexus → secretion**; **myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus → motility**; **muscularis mucosa alters surface area, no role in motility**.
> 2. Water: **9000 mL** enters (2000 ingested + saliva 1500, stomach 2500, pancreas 1500, bile 500, intestine 1000) → **8800 reabsorbed** (**jejunum 5500**, ileum 2000, colon 1300) → **200 mL in stool**.
> 3. Saliva: **pH 7, hypotonic**; **parotid = largest**; **Wharton's duct** = submandibular; minor glands **Weber's (tonsils)** and **von Ebner's (base of tongue → lingual lipase)**.
> 4. Saliva digests carbohydrate (**amylase/ptyalin**) and fat (**lingual lipase**), **no protein enzyme**; protection by **secretory IgA, lactoferrin, lysozymes**.
> 5. **Salivon**: acinus → plasma-like 1° secretion; duct → reabsorbs Na⁺/Cl⁻, secretes K⁺/HCO₃⁻; **high flow → ↑ Na⁺ and Cl⁻**.
> 6. **PNS → profound ↑ thin watery saliva**; SNS → ↑ thick saliva; **CN IX → otic ganglion → parotid**; **CN VII → submandibular ganglion → submandibular & sublingual**.
> 7. **Aptyalism** (absent), **xerostomia** (↓ — mumps, Sjögren), **crocodile tears / gustatory lacrimation** after facial nerve injury.
> 8. Gastric juice: **2.5 L/day, pH 1.5–3 (most acidic)**; **parietal → HCl + IF of Castle**; **chief → pepsinogen**; **ECL → histamine**; **neck cells = stem cells**; **G cells → gastrin**.
> 9. **Pernicious anaemia**: autoantibodies to parietal cells → **achlorhydria + ↓ B₁₂ absorption**, atrophic mucosa.
> 10. Acid stimulants: **histamine (H₂), gastrin (CCK-B), ACh (M₃)**; inhibitors: **excess acid, somatostatin (universal inhibitor)**, PG, CCK, secretin, calcitonin. **Post-prandial alkaline tide** = ↑ HCO₃⁻ to blood after a meal.
> 11. Phases: **cephalic 20–30 % (no food)**, **gastric 60–70 %**, intestinal 10 %.
> 12. Pancreatic juice: **1500 mL, pH 8.3**, digests all nutrients; **enterokinase: trypsinogen → trypsin**, which activates the rest; premature activation → **acute pancreatitis**. **CFTR** mutation → cystic fibrosis.
> 13. **Secretin** = **nature's antacid**, **first hormone discovered**, S cells, ↑ HCO₃⁻, ↓↓ enzyme concentration; **CCK-PZ** → gall-bladder contraction + enzymes.
> 14. Bile: liver **97 % water** → gall bladder **87 % water**; primary (cholic, chenodeoxycholic) → **secondary (deoxycholic, lithocholic) by flora**; **enterohepatic circulation 6–8 times/day** via terminal ileum and portal vein. **Choleretic = bile salt** (best), secretin, vagus; **cholagogue = CCK**.
> 15. **Succus entericus** from **crypts of Lieberkühn**; **Paneth cells (base): zinc, defensins, lysozymes**. **Max K⁺ secretion: saliva; max K⁺ concentration: colonic secretion.**

[^ety-secretion]: **secretion** — Latin *secernere*, "to separate, set apart" (*se-* "apart" + *cernere* "to sift") + *-ion*, the process → the process of separating a substance out of the blood and releasing it as a juice. **Differentiate:** *secretion* (a useful product released — saliva, HCl) · *excretion* (*ex-* + *cernere* — sifting *out* waste: urine, bile pigments) · *absorption* (taking in).
[^ety-digestion]: **digestion** — Latin *dis-* (*di-*), "apart" + *gerere*, "to carry" + *-ion* → carrying apart, breaking food into pieces small enough to absorb. **Differentiate:** *digestion* (chemical/mechanical breakdown in the lumen) · *absorption* (transfer across the wall) · *assimilation* (incorporating into the body's own tissue).
[^ety-absorption]: **absorption / reabsorption** — Latin *ab-*, "away, off" + *sorbere*, "to suck in" + *-tion* → sucking in; *re-* "again" → taking back what was secreted. **Differentiate:** *absorption* (into a substance — through it) · *adsorption* (*ad-*, onto a surface only) · *resorption* (dissolving and taking back, as in bone).
[^ety-enzyme]: **enzyme** — Greek *en*, "in" + *zymē*, "leaven, yeast" → "in yeast"; named by Wilhelm Kühne (1877). Enzyme names end in **-ase** (amylase, lipase); their inactive precursors end in **-ogen** (pepsinogen, trypsinogen).
[^ety-enteric]: **enteric** — Greek *enteron*, "intestine, gut" + *-ic* → of the intestine; the *enteric nervous system* is the gut's own nervous system in its wall (submucosal + myenteric plexuses).
[^ety-hormone]: **hormone** — Greek *hormōn*, "setting in motion, urging on" (*horman*, to excite); the word was coined by Ernest Starling in 1905 for secretin. **Differentiate:** *endocrine* (via blood) · *paracrine* (to neighbours, like histamine and somatostatin in the stomach) · *neurocrine* (from nerves).
[^ety-motility]: **motility** — Latin *movere*, "to move" → *motilis*, "able to move" + *-ity*, the quality → the ability of the gut to move its contents. **Differentiate:** *motility* (movement of the gut wall) · *mobility* (ability to be moved).
[^ety-serosa]: **serosa** — Latin *serum*, "whey, watery fluid" + *-osa*, "full of" → the membrane that produces serous (watery) fluid; the outermost visceral peritoneum on intraperitoneal gut. Where the gut is retroperitoneal (oesophagus, parts of duodenum, colon) the outer coat is *adventitia* instead.
[^ety-muscularis]: **muscularis propria / externa** — Latin *musculus*, "little mouse" (a muscle rippling under the skin looked like a mouse) + *-aris*, "of"; *propria*, "its own" → the gut's own muscle coat. **Differentiate:** *muscularis propria* (motility) vs *muscularis mucosae* (thin layer inside the mucosa that folds it — alters surface area).
[^ety-myenteric]: **myenteric** — Greek *mys*, "muscle" + *enteron*, "intestine" → in the muscle of the intestine.
[^ety-plexus]: **plexus** — Latin, "a braid, an interweaving" (*plectere*, to braid) → a network of interlacing nerves or vessels. Plural *plexuses* or *plexus*.
[^ety-auerbach]: **Auerbach's plexus** — Leopold Auerbach (1828–1897), German anatomist and neuropathologist, who described the myenteric plexus in 1862.
[^ety-meissner]: **Meissner's plexus** — Georg Meissner (1829–1905), German anatomist and physiologist, who described the submucosal plexus in 1857; also the eponym of Meissner's (touch) corpuscles in skin.
[^ety-mucosa]: **mucosa** — Latin *mucus*, "slime" + *-osa*, "full of" → the slimy (mucus-lined) inner membrane. **Differentiate:** *mucous* (adjective — mucous membrane) · *mucus* (the noun — the slime itself).
[^ety-exocrine]: **exocrine** — Greek *exō*, "outside" + *krinein*, "to separate, secrete" → secreting outward through a duct (pancreatic juice into the duodenum). **Differentiate:** *endocrine* (*endon*, within — into blood: insulin) · *paracrine* · *apocrine / holocrine / merocrine* (how the cell releases its product).
[^ety-jejunum]: **jejunum** — Latin *jejunus*, "fasting, empty" → anatomists found this part of the small intestine empty at autopsy because contents pass through it fast. Same root as *jejune*.
[^ety-ileum]: **ileum** — Latin, from Greek *eilein*, "to roll, twist" → the coiled last part of the small intestine. Don't confuse with **ilium** (the hip bone, Latin *ilia*, "flanks").
[^ety-parotid]: **parotid** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + *ous, ōtos*, "ear" + *-id* → the gland beside the ear.
[^ety-submandibular]: **submandibular** — Latin *sub-*, "under" + *mandibula*, "jaw" (*mandere*, to chew) + *-ar* → under the jaw; older name *submaxillary*.
[^ety-wharton]: **Wharton's duct** — Thomas Wharton (1614–1673), English physician and anatomist, who described the submandibular duct in *Adenographia* (1656); also eponym of Wharton's jelly of the umbilical cord. The parotid duct is *Stensen's duct*.
[^ety-sublingual]: **sublingual** — Latin *sub-*, "under" + *lingua*, "tongue" + *-al* → under the tongue; same idea as sublingual nitroglycerine.
[^ety-tonicity]: **hypotonic / tonicity** — Greek *tonos*, "tension, stretching" + *-ic*; *hypo-*, "under" → a solution with lower effective osmotic pressure than plasma, so water moves into cells. **Differentiate:** *hypotonic* · *isotonic* (*isos*, equal) · *hypertonic*; *tonicity* counts only non-penetrating solutes, *osmolality* counts all.
[^ety-weber]: **Weber's glands** — Moritz Ignatz Weber (1795–1875), German anatomist; mucous salivary glands at the lateral border of the tongue near the palatine tonsil (the page places them at the tonsils).
[^ety-ebner]: **von Ebner's glands** — Victor von Ebner (1842–1925), Austrian histologist; serous glands that open into the trenches of the circumvallate papillae at the back of the tongue and secrete **lingual lipase**.
[^ety-amylase]: **amylase** — Greek *amylon*, "starch" (*a-* "not" + *mylē* "mill" — fine flour not made in a mill) + *-ase*, enzyme → the starch-splitting enzyme; salivary and pancreatic forms.
[^ety-ptyalin]: **ptyalin** — Greek *ptyalon*, "spittle, saliva" (*ptyein*, to spit) + *-in* → salivary amylase; same root as *aptyalism* (no saliva) and *ptyalism* (excess saliva).
[^ety-lipase]: **lipase** — Greek *lipos*, "fat" + *-ase* → the fat-splitting enzyme (lingual, gastric, pancreatic lipase).
[^ety-lubrication]: **lubrication** — Latin *lubricus*, "slippery" + *-ation* → the process of making slippery so food slides without injuring the mucosa.
[^ety-buccal]: **buccal** — Latin *bucca*, "cheek" + *-al* → of the cheek or mouth cavity. Same root as *buccinator*.
[^ety-immunoglobulin]: **IgA (immunoglobulin A)** — Latin *immunis*, "exempt" (*in-* not + *munus* duty) + *globulus*, "little ball" → the protective globulin; *secretory* IgA is a dimer with a secretory component that survives in saliva, tears, milk and gut juice.
[^ety-lactoferrin]: **lactoferrin** — Latin *lac, lactis*, "milk" + *ferrum*, "iron" + *-in* → the iron-binding protein first found in milk; it starves bacteria of iron.
[^ety-lysozyme]: **lysozyme** — Greek *lysis*, "loosening, dissolving" + *zymē*, "leaven" → the enzyme that dissolves bacterial cell walls (peptidoglycan); discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1922 in nasal mucus.
[^ety-salivon]: **salivon** — Latin *saliva*, "spittle" + Greek-style *-on*, "unit" (as in *neuron, nephron*) → the functional unit of the salivary gland (acinus + its duct).
[^ety-acinus]: **acinus** — Latin, "berry, grape seed" → the berry-shaped blind end of a gland where secretion begins; plural *acini*, adjective *acinar*. Pancreatic acini make enzymes, salivary acini the primary saliva.
[^ety-autonomic]: **autonomic** — Greek *autos*, "self" + *nomos*, "law" + *-ic* → self-governing; the part of the nervous system that works without voluntary control.
[^ety-sympathetic]: **sympathetic / parasympathetic** — Greek *syn-* (*sym-*), "together" + *pathos*, "feeling" → feeling together (Galen's idea that organs "sympathise"); *para-*, "beside" → the system beside it (craniosacral outflow).
[^ety-bradykinin]: **bradykinin** — Greek *bradys*, "slow" + *kinein*, "to move" + *-in* → "slow mover", because it contracts gut smooth muscle slowly (named by Rocha e Silva, 1949). In salivary glands it is formed by kallikrein and dilates vessels.
[^ety-vasodilation]: **vasodilation** — Latin *vas*, "vessel" + *dilatare*, "to spread wide" (*dis-* apart + *latus* wide) + *-ion* → the process of widening a vessel. **Differentiate:** *vasodilation* (lumen widens → ↑ flow) · *vasoconstriction* (narrows) · *dilatation* (a structure stretched, e.g. a dilated stomach).
[^ety-glossopharyngeal]: **glossopharyngeal (CN IX)** — Greek *glōssa*, "tongue" + *pharynx*, "throat" + *-eal* → the nerve of tongue and pharynx; its parasympathetic fibres reach the parotid via the otic ganglion.
[^ety-otic]: **otic** — Greek *ous, ōtos*, "ear" + *-ic* → of the ear; the otic ganglion lies just below the foramen ovale, medial to the mandibular nerve — near the ear.
[^ety-ganglion]: **ganglion** — Greek *ganglion*, "a knot, a swelling under the skin" → a knot-like collection of nerve-cell bodies outside the CNS; plural *ganglia*. **Differentiate:** *ganglion* (outside the CNS) · *nucleus* (inside the CNS); the "ganglion" of the wrist is an unrelated cystic swelling.
[^ety-facial]: **facial nerve (CN VII)** — Latin *facies*, "face, form" + *-al* → the nerve of the face; it carries the parasympathetic fibres for the submandibular, sublingual and lacrimal glands and passes through the parotid gland.
[^ety-aptyalism]: **aptyalism** — Greek *a-*, "not, without" + *ptyalon*, "saliva" + *-ism*, state → a state of no saliva (absent secretion). **Differentiate:** *aptyalism / asialia* (none) · *xerostomia* (dry mouth, ↓ saliva) · *ptyalism / sialorrhoea* (excess).
[^ety-xerostomia]: **xerostomia** — Greek *xēros*, "dry" + *stoma*, "mouth" + *-ia*, condition → dry mouth. Same *xero-* as *xerophthalmia* (dry eye, vitamin A deficiency) and *xeroderma*.
[^ety-mumps]: **mumps** — from an old English verb *mump*, "to grimace, to mumble" → the swollen-faced, mumbling look of parotitis; medical name *epidemic parotitis* (paramyxovirus).
[^ety-sjogren]: **Sjögren syndrome** — Henrik Sjögren (1899–1986), Swedish ophthalmologist, who described the combination of dry eyes and dry mouth with arthritis in 1933 — autoimmune destruction of exocrine glands (anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB).
[^ety-regeneration]: **regeneration** — Latin *re-*, "again" + *generare*, "to beget, produce" + *-ation* → the process of growing again. **Differentiate:** *regeneration* (lost tissue regrows as the same tissue — here nerve fibres regrow, but to the wrong target) · *repair* (healing by scar) · *reinnervation* (nerve reaching a target again).
[^ety-lacrimal]: **lacrimal / lacrimation** — Latin *lacrima*, "tear" + *-al* / *-ation* → of tears; the process of shedding tears. *Gustatory* (Latin *gustare*, to taste) lacrimation = tears when tasting food.
[^ety-neutralisation]: **neutralisation** — Latin *neuter*, "neither" (*ne-* not + *uter* either) + *-ize* + *-ation* → the process of making a solution neither acid nor base (pH 7) — here HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → H₂CO₃ → CO₂ + H₂O.
[^ety-trefoil]: **trefoil peptides** — Latin *trifolium*, "three-leaved" (*tri-* three + *folium* leaf) → small peptides whose three disulphide loops look like a clover leaf; they help mucosal repair and protection (TFF1–3).
[^ety-parietal]: **parietal** — Latin *paries, parietis*, "wall" + *-al* → wall cells, because they sit in the wall of the gland, bulging outward; also called *oxyntic* (Greek *oxynein*, to make acid) cells. Same root as the *parietal* bone and *parietal* peritoneum (lining the body wall).
[^ety-castle]: **Intrinsic factor of Castle** — William Bosworth Castle (1897–1990), American haematologist, who showed in 1928–29 that normal gastric juice contains an "intrinsic factor" needed to absorb the "extrinsic factor" (vitamin B₁₂) from food. *Intrinsic* = Latin *intrinsecus*, "on the inside".
[^ety-enterochromaffin]: **enterochromaffin-like (ECL) cell** — Greek *enteron*, "intestine" + Latin *chroma*, "colour" + *affinis*, "related" → intestinal cells with an affinity for chromium salts (they stain brown); ECL cells *look like* these but make histamine instead of serotonin.
[^ety-histamine]: **histamine** — Greek *histos*, "tissue, web" + *amine* (an ammonia derivative) → the amine found in tissues; acts on H₂ receptors of parietal cells (blocked by ranitidine, famotidine).
[^ety-pepsinogen]: **pepsin / pepsinogen** — Greek *pepsis*, "digestion, cooking" (*peptein*, to cook, digest) + *-in*; *-ogen* "producer of" → the precursor that produces pepsin when HCl cuts it. Same root as *dyspepsia* and *peptic* ulcer.
[^ety-antrum]: **antrum / antral** — Greek *antron*, "cave" → a cavity; the pyloric antrum is the lower, cave-like part of the stomach where G cells live.
[^ety-gastrin]: **gastrin** — Greek *gastēr*, "stomach, belly" + *-in* → the stomach hormone; named by John Edkins (1905), who extracted it from the antral mucosa.
[^ety-pernicious]: **pernicious** — Latin *perniciosus*, "destructive, deadly" (*per-* thoroughly + *nex, necis* death) → this anaemia was invariably fatal until liver therapy (Minot & Murphy, 1926) and then B₁₂.
[^ety-anaemia]: **anaemia** — Greek *an-*, "without" + *haima*, "blood" + *-ia*, condition → "without blood": ↓ haemoglobin / RBC mass. **Differentiate:** *anaemia* · *haemorrhage* (blood bursting out) · *ischaemia* (*ischein* to hold back — blood supply held back).
[^ety-achlorhydria]: **achlorhydria** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *chlōros* (chlorine, from "pale green") + *hydōr*, "water" (hydrochloric) + *-ia* → absence of hydrochloric acid in gastric juice. **Differentiate:** *achlorhydria* (none) · *hypochlorhydria* (↓) · *hyperchlorhydria* (↑, Zollinger–Ellison).
[^ety-atrophy]: **atrophic / atrophy** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *trophē*, "nourishment" + *-y* → wasting of a tissue that was once normal. **Differentiate:** *atrophy* (shrinks after being normal) · *hypoplasia* (never grew fully) · *aplasia* (never formed) · *hypertrophy* (cells enlarge).
[^ety-atpase]: **ATPase / proton pump** — *ATP* (adenosine triphosphate) + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that splits ATP and uses its energy to move ions; the gastric H⁺/K⁺ ATPase is blocked irreversibly by proton-pump inhibitors (omeprazole).
[^ety-carbonic-anhydrase]: **carbonic anhydrase** — Latin *carbo*, "coal" (carbon) + Greek *an-*, "without" + *hydōr*, "water" + *-ase* → the enzyme that adds or removes water from CO₂ (CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃); a zinc enzyme, blocked by acetazolamide.
[^ety-prandial]: **post-prandial** — Latin *post-*, "after" + *prandium*, "late breakfast, meal" + *-al* → after a meal. **Differentiate:** *pre-prandial* (before a meal) · *post-prandial* (after) · *fasting*.
[^ety-receptor]: **receptor** — Latin *recipere*, "to take back, receive" (*re-* + *capere*) + *-tor*, the agent → the protein that receives a chemical signal (H₂, M₃, CCK-B).
[^ety-acetylcholine]: **acetylcholine** — Latin *acetum*, "vinegar" (acetyl group) + Greek *cholē*, "bile" (choline was first isolated from bile) + *-ine* → acetylated choline, the vagal transmitter.
[^ety-vagus]: **vagus / vagal** — Latin *vagus*, "wandering" → the 10th cranial nerve that wanders from the brainstem to the abdomen; carries the cephalic-phase drive for acid. Same root as *vagabond*.
[^ety-muscarinic]: **muscarinic (M₃)** — Latin *musca*, "fly" → *Amanita muscaria*, the fly agaric mushroom whose alkaloid *muscarine* stimulates these ACh receptors. **Differentiate:** *muscarinic* (G-protein receptors, M₁–M₅; M₃ on parietal cells) · *nicotinic* (ion channels — named from nicotine).
[^ety-somatostatin]: **somatostatin** — Greek *sōma*, "body" + *statos*, "standing, stopped" + *-in* → the body(-growth)-stopping hormone, first found to inhibit growth hormone (Brazeau & Guillemin, 1973); from D cells it inhibits almost every GI secretion — "universal inhibitor".
[^ety-prostaglandin]: **prostaglandin** — named in 1935 by Ulf von Euler from *prostate gland*, where it was thought to come from (it actually came from seminal vesicles) → lipid mediators made from arachidonic acid; PGE₂ inhibits acid and stimulates mucus and HCO₃⁻.
[^ety-cholecystokinin]: **cholecystokinin (CCK)** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" + *kystis*, "bladder" + *kinein*, "to move" + *-in* → the hormone that moves the gall bladder (Ivy & Oldberg, 1928). Its receptors: CCK-A (alimentary — gall bladder, pancreas) and CCK-B (brain — also the gastrin receptor of parietal cells).
[^ety-secretin]: **secretin** — Latin *secernere*, "to separate, secrete" + *-in* → the substance that excites secretion; Bayliss and Starling (1902) — the first hormone discovered.
[^ety-calcitonin]: **calcitonin** — Latin *calx, calcis*, "lime, calcium" + Greek *tonos*, "tone, tension" + *-in* → the hormone that regulates the "tone" (level) of calcium in blood; from thyroid parafollicular C cells.
[^ety-rennin]: **rennin (chymosin)** — Old English *rennet*, "that which makes milk run together (curdle)" + *-in* → the milk-curdling enzyme of calves' stomachs. Don't confuse with **renin** (Latin *ren*, kidney — the BP enzyme).
[^ety-cephalic]: **cephalic** — Greek *kephalē*, "head" + *-ic* → of the head; the cephalic phase is driven from the brain (sight, smell, thought of food) through the vagus.
[^ety-colipase]: **colipase** — Latin *co-*, "together with" + *lipase* → the pancreatic cofactor that anchors lipase to the bile-salt-coated surface of fat droplets so it can work.
[^ety-zymogen]: **zymogen** — Greek *zymē*, "leaven, ferment" + *-gen*, "producer" → a ferment-producer: an inactive enzyme precursor (proenzyme) that becomes active when a piece is cut off.
[^ety-trypsin]: **trypsin / trypsinogen** — Greek *tripsis*, "rubbing" (*tribein*, to rub) + *-in* → named by Kühne (1876) because he obtained it by rubbing pancreas with glycerine; *-ogen* → its precursor.
[^ety-chymotrypsin]: **chymotrypsin** — Greek *chymos*, "juice" (chyme) + *trypsin* → the trypsin-like enzyme that curdles milk (chyme); an endopeptidase that cuts after aromatic amino acids.
[^ety-carboxypeptidase]: **carboxypeptidase** — *carboxyl* (the –COOH end of a protein) + Greek *peptos*, "digested" + *-ase* → the enzyme that removes amino acids one by one from the carboxyl end (an exopeptidase); *pro-* → its inactive precursor.
[^ety-enterokinase]: **enterokinase (enteropeptidase)** — Greek *enteron*, "intestine" + *kinein*, "to move, activate" + *-ase* → the intestinal (duodenal brush-border) enzyme that activates trypsinogen; Pavlov's laboratory (Schepovalnikow, 1899) found it. Its congenital deficiency causes severe protein malabsorption.
[^ety-inflammation]: **inflammation / inflammatory** — Latin *inflammare*, "to set on fire" (*in-* + *flamma* flame) + *-ation* → the process of "burning": redness, heat, swelling, pain (Celsus). **Differentiate:** *inflammation* (tissue response, -itis) · *infection* (invasion by microbes — may or may not inflame).
[^ety-pancreatitis]: **pancreatitis / pancreas** — Greek *pan*, "all" + *kreas*, "flesh" → "all flesh" (it has no bone or cartilage) + *-itis*, inflammation → inflammation of the pancreas, in the acute form by auto-digestion.
[^ety-pancreozymin]: **pancreozymin (PZ)** — *pancreas* + Greek *zymē*, "ferment, enzyme" + *-in* → the factor that makes the pancreas release enzymes (Harper & Raper, 1943); later proved to be the same molecule as cholecystokinin → **CCK-PZ**.
[^ety-chyme]: **chyme** — Greek *chymos*, "juice" → the semi-liquid, acidic mass of partly digested food leaving the stomach. **Differentiate:** *chyme* (stomach output) · *chyle* (Greek *chylos*, juice — milky lymph full of chylomicrons in lacteals).
[^ety-cystic-fibrosis]: **cystic fibrosis** — Greek *kystis*, "bladder, sac" + *-ic*; Latin *fibra*, "fibre" + *-osis*, condition → a disease with cysts and fibrous scarring of the pancreas (Dorothy Andersen, 1938, "cystic fibrosis of the pancreas"); the gene CFTR was identified in 1989.
[^ety-conductance]: **conductance** — Latin *conducere*, "to lead together" (*con-* + *ducere*, to lead) + *-ance* → how easily ions are led (conducted) through a channel; the inverse of resistance.
[^ety-bile]: **bile** — Latin *bilis*, "bile, anger" (one of the four humours; Greek *cholē* gives *chole-* words). **Differentiate:** *bile acids* (cholic, chenodeoxycholic) · *bile salts* (their conjugated sodium salts — the detergents) · *bile pigments* (bilirubin — excretory, no digestive role).
[^ety-cholic]: **cholic acid** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" + *-ic* → the acid of bile; a primary bile acid with three OH groups (3α, 7α, 12α).
[^ety-chenodeoxycholic]: **chenodeoxycholic acid** — Greek *chēn*, "goose" (first isolated from goose bile) + *de-* "removed" + *oxy-* "oxygen" + *cholic* → goose cholic acid lacking one oxygen (one OH fewer than cholic acid).
[^ety-lithocholic]: **lithocholic acid** — Greek *lithos*, "stone" + *cholic* → the bile acid first found in gallstones; a toxic secondary bile acid made by bacterial 7α-dehydroxylation of chenodeoxycholic acid. Same *litho-* as *cholelithiasis*.
[^ety-conjugation]: **conjugation** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *jugum*, "yoke" + *-ation* → the process of yoking two molecules together (bile acid + glycine/taurine; bilirubin + glucuronic acid) to make them water-soluble. **Differentiate:** *conjugation* (chemical joining) · *emulsification* (physical dispersion of fat).
[^ety-glycine]: **glycine** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" + *-ine* → the sweet-tasting simplest amino acid; conjugated with cholic acid → glycocholic acid.
[^ety-taurine]: **taurine** — Latin *taurus*, "bull" → first isolated from ox bile (1827) → taurocholic acid when conjugated.
[^ety-portal]: **portal** — Latin *porta*, "gate" → the vein that enters the liver at its gate (porta hepatis); a portal system carries blood between two capillary beds (gut → liver).
[^ety-enterohepatic]: **enterohepatic** — Greek *enteron*, "intestine" + *hēpar, hēpatos*, "liver" + *-ic* → between intestine and liver; the enterohepatic circulation recycles bile salts (and some drugs) through the portal vein.
[^ety-choleretic]: **choleretic** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" + *hairesis*, "taking", or *rhein*, "to flow" (origin debated) + *-tic* → an agent that increases the flow of bile from the liver. **Differentiate:** *choleretic* (↑ hepatic bile secretion — bile salts, secretin, vagus) · *cholagogue* (empties the gall bladder — CCK) · *hydrocholeretic* (↑ only the watery part — secretin).
[^ety-cholagogue]: **cholagogue** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" + *agōgos*, "leading, drawing forth" → a substance that leads bile out (contracts the gall bladder); CCK is the physiological cholagogue.
[^ety-succus]: **succus entericus** — Latin *succus*, "juice, sap" + Greek *enterikos*, "of the intestine" → intestinal juice (~1–1.5 L/day, slightly alkaline, almost enzyme-free; the brush-border enzymes stay on the cells).
[^ety-crypt]: **crypt** — Greek *kryptē*, "a hidden place, vault" (*kryptein*, to hide) → the tubular gland hidden between the villi. Same root as *cryptorchidism* (hidden testis).
[^ety-lieberkuhn]: **crypts of Lieberkühn** — Johann Nathanael Lieberkühn (1711–1756), German anatomist and physician, who described the intestinal glands in 1745.
[^ety-goblet]: **goblet cell** — Old French *gobelet*, "a drinking cup" → a mucus-filled cell shaped like a wine glass, wide at the top.
[^ety-epithelium]: **epithelium / epithelial** — Greek *epi-*, "upon" + *thēlē*, "nipple, teat" + *-ium* → originally the thin covering of the nipple; now any cell sheet covering a surface.
[^ety-paneth]: **Paneth cell** — Joseph Paneth (1857–1890), Austrian physiologist, who described these granular cells at the base of the crypts in 1888.
[^ety-defensin]: **defensin** — Latin *defendere*, "to ward off" + *-in* → small antimicrobial peptides that punch holes in bacterial membranes (α-defensins from Paneth cells).
[^pre-di]: **di- / dis- / dia-** — Latin *dis-* "apart" (digestion, dissection) · Greek *di-* "two" (disaccharide) · Greek *dia-* "through" (diarrhoea, diaphragm). Three look-alikes — decide by the root's language.
[^pre-ab]: **ab-** — away from: absorption, abduction, abnormal. Don't confuse with **ad-** towards (adsorption, adduction) · **a- / an-** without (aptyalism, anaemia).
[^suf-osa]: **-osa / -ose / -ous** — full of: serosa, mucosa, adipose, mucous. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition (fibrosis) · **-ose** a sugar (glucose, lactose).
[^pre-myo]: **my- / myo-** — muscle: myenteric, myocardium, myosin. Don't confuse with **myel- / myelo-** marrow, spinal cord (myeloid, myelin) · **myc- / myco-** fungus.
[^pre-sub]: **sub-** — under, below: submucosa, submandibular, sublingual. Don't confuse with **supra- / super-** above · **hypo-** under (Greek twin of *sub-*) · **infra-** below (infraorbital).
[^pre-exo]: **exo-** — outside, outward: exocrine, exopeptidase, exocytosis. Don't confuse with **ecto-** outer layer (ectoderm) · **ex-** out of (excretion) · **endo-** within.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: reabsorption, regeneration, receptor, resorption. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards, behind (retroperitoneal, retropulsion) · **pre-** before.
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, alongside: parotid (beside the ear), parasympathetic, paracrine. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypotonic, hypochlorhydria, hypokalaemia. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over, excessive · **sub-** (Latin twin) · **hypno-** sleep.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme, named for its substrate or action: amylase (starch), lipase (fat), carboxypeptidase, carbonic anhydrase, ATPase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (lactose) · **-in** an active peptide or protein (trypsin, pepsin — older enzyme names).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: anti-bacterial, antibody, antacid. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (antenatal) · **antero-** front (anterior).
[^pre-auto]: **auto-** — self: autonomic, autoantibodies, auto-digestion, autocatalysis. Don't confuse with **allo-** other (alloantibody) · **iso-** equal.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together, with: sympathetic, synapse, syndrome. Don't confuse with **semi-** half · **sin-** (Latin *sinister*, left).
[^pre-brady]: **brady-** — slow: bradykinin, bradycardia. Don't confuse with **tachy-** fast · **brachy-** short (brachydactyly) · **baro-** pressure.
[^suf-kinin]: **-kinin / -kinase / -kine** — Greek *kinein*, "to move, set in motion": bradykinin, cholecystokinin, enterokinase, cytokine. Don't confuse with **-kinesis** movement of cells/organs (karyokinesis) · **cine-** (film — cineradiography, same root).
[^pre-vaso]: **vaso-** — vessel (or duct): vasodilation, vasoactive, vasopressin, vas deferens. Don't confuse with **vago-** of the vagus nerve · **vesico-** bladder.
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — not, without: aptyalism, achlorhydria, anaemia, anhydrase. Don't confuse with **ab-** away · **ad-** towards · **ana-** up, again (anabolism).
[^suf-ism]: **-ism** — a state, condition or doctrine: aptyalism, ptyalism, metabolism. Don't confuse with **-osis** a disease condition (fibrosis) · **-itis** inflammation.
[^pre-xero]: **xero-** — dry: xerostomia, xerophthalmia, xeroderma. Don't confuse with **xeno-** foreign (xenograft) · **sclero-** hard.
[^pre-entero]: **entero-** — intestine: enteric, enterochromaffin, enterokinase, enterohepatic. Don't confuse with **gastro-** stomach · **colo-** colon · **intra-** within (intra- also starts with *in-t*, but means inside, not gut).
[^suf-ogen]: **-ogen / -gen** — producer of, precursor: pepsinogen, trypsinogen, zymogen, fibrinogen, antigen. Don't confuse with **-genic / -genesis** origin, formation (pathogenic, adipogenesis) · **-gen** in *oxygen, hydrogen* (producer of acid / water).
[^suf-trophy]: **-trophy / -trophic** — nourishment, growth: atrophy, hypertrophy, trophic (gastrin's trophic action). Don't confuse with **-tropic** turning towards, acting on (gonadotropic) · **-plasia** formation of cells (hyperplasia).
[^pre-post]: **post-** — after, behind: post-prandial, postpartum, posterior. Don't confuse with **pre-** before · **para-** beside.
[^suf-statin]: **-statin** — "stopper", from Greek *statos*, standing still: somatostatin. Don't confuse with **statins** the cholesterol drugs (from HMG-CoA reductase in*hibitors*, same "stop" idea) · **-stasis** (cholestasis) · **-static** (bacteriostatic).
[^pre-chole]: **chol- / chole- / cholecyst-** — bile, gall bladder: cholecystokinin, choleretic, cholagogue, cholic acid, cholesterol. Don't confuse with **col- / colo-** colon · **chol-** in *cholera* (same root, "bile-flow" disease) · **choledocho-** common bile duct.
[^pre-co]: **co- / con- / com-** — together, with: colipase, conjugation, conductance, coagulation. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** opposite (countercurrent).
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, precursor; forward: procarboxypeptidase, proenzyme, prothrombin, prolapse. Don't confuse with **pre-** before (in time or place) · **post-** after.
[^suf-itis]: **-itis** — inflammation: pancreatitis, gastritis, parotitis. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition, often degenerative (fibrosis) · **-ism** a state.
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a condition, process or disease state (often non-inflammatory): fibrosis, thrombosis, cirrhosis; *-iasis* is its cousin for a disease state (cholelithiasis). Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation · **-oma** tumour · **-osa** full of (mucosa).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: transmembrane, transport, transferrin. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **intra-** within · **per-** through, thoroughly.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — removal, down from: deoxycholic (one oxygen removed), deconjugate, dehydroxylation. Don't confuse with **di-** two / apart · **dys-** bad, faulty (dyspepsia).
[^pre-hepato]: **hepat- / hepato-** — liver: enterohepatic, hepatocyte, hepatitis. Don't confuse with **hepta-** seven · **hemi-** half.
[^suf-gogue]: **-gogue / -agogue** — leader, that which draws out: cholagogue, galactagogue (milk), sialagogue (saliva), emmenagogue (menses). Don't confuse with **-retic** flow (choleretic, diuretic) · **-gogy** teaching (pedagogy).
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, over: epithelium, epiglottis, epigastrium. Don't confuse with **endo-** within · **ecto-** outer · **meta-** after, change.
