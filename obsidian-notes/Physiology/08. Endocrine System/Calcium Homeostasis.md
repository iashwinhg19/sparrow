# 🦴 Calcium Homeostasis

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> **Calcium**[^ety-calcium] **homeostasis**[^ety-homeostasis][^pre-homeo][^suf-stasis] is the control of plasma calcium — **normal 8.7–10.2 mg/dL** — by **three hormones** (parathormone, vitamin D, calcitonin) acting on **three organs** (bone, GIT, kidneys). Only the **free (ionic) calcium** acts, so every question in the chapter is one of these:
> - ✅ **How much of the calcium is free**, and what shifts it (pH, albumin) → tetany?
> - ✅ **What does each hormone do** at bone, gut and kidney — and so to serum Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻?
> - ✅ **Which drug, marker or disease** follows from it (teriparatide, denosumab, salcatonin, CaSR mutation, PTHrP, bone markers)?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — Bone Is the Bank, PTH Is the Cashier

Think of calcium as money. **Bone is the bank** (99 % of the body's calcium is stored there), the **gut is income** and the **kidney is the spending/saving desk**. **PTH** is the cashier who withdraws from the bank and tells the kidney to save Ca²⁺ but spend phosphate; **vitamin D** brings in new income from the gut (both Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻); **calcitonin** locks the bank when the till is overflowing.

```
            ↓ serum Ca²⁺                                  ↑ serum Ca²⁺
                 ↓                                              ↓
       PTH (parathyroid chief cells)                 CALCITONIN (thyroid C cells)
   BONE: ↑ resorption (via osteoblast RANKL)      BONE: ⊣ osteoclasts → ↓ resorption
   KIDNEY: ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption (DCT)              GIT: ↓ Ca²⁺ absorption
           ↑ PO₄³⁻ excretion (PCT)                KIDNEY: ↓ Ca²⁺ reabsorption
   GIT: indirect → ↑ vitamin D (1α-hydroxylase)
                 ↓                                              ↓
       ↑ S.Ca²⁺   ↓ S.PO₄³⁻                               ↓ S.Ca²⁺
VITAMIN D (calcitriol): ↑ Ca²⁺ AND ↑ PO₄³⁻ — gut absorption + renal reabsorption (+ bone both ways)
```

### Applied aspect — Hypocalcemic tetany[^ety-hypocalcemia][^pre-hypo][^suf-emia][^ety-tetany]

- **Concept:** the **ratio of free to bound calcium** is determined by:
  - **Blood pH**[^ety-ph].
  - **Albumin** (binds both **H⁺** & **calcium** — the two compete for the same sites).
- **Mechanism:**

```
↓ Ca²⁺ → Na⁺ channels open more frequently → ↑ depolarisation → ↑ frequency of action potentials
       → Hyperexcitability → Hypocalcemic tetany
```

Low free Ca²⁺ makes nerve **Na⁺ channels** leaky, so the membrane **depolarisation**[^ety-depolarisation][^pre-de] reaches threshold by itself and nerves fire again and again — **hyperexcitability**[^ety-excitability][^pre-hyper][^pre-ex] — which shows as muscle spasm.[^3]

- **Signs** — both **d/t hyperexcitability:**
  1. **Trousseau's sign**[^ety-trousseau]**:** eliciting **carpopedal spasm**[^ety-carpopedal][^ety-spasm] by inflating the cuff **↑ 20 mm Hg above SBP for 3 min.**[^4]
  2. **Chvostek's sign**[^ety-chvostek]**:** **facial muscle twitching on tapping facial nerve at angle of jaw.**

![Trousseau's sign of latent tetany — carpal spasm after inflating a BP cuff](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Trousseau's_Sign_of_Latent_Tetany.jpg/800px-Trousseau's_Sign_of_Latent_Tetany.jpg)
*Trousseau's sign — a positive response pointing to hypocalcemia: flexed wrist and metacarpophalangeal joints with extended fingers (the page's photograph shows the same hand) (recreation by Tmdswan, Wikimedia Commons — free licence, see file page).*

- **Eg: Respiratory alkalosis**[^ety-alkalosis][^suf-osis] → **↓ H⁺ levels** → **↑ binding of Ca²⁺ to albumin** → **↓ free Ca²⁺** — tetany with a *normal total* calcium (e.g. hysterical **hyperventilation**[^ety-hyperventilation]).[^5]

## 🧪 Hormones Regulating Ca²⁺ Homeostasis

### Three hormone three organ model

```
                         Three hormone three organ model
               ┌───────────────────────┴───────────────────────┐
         Three hormones:                                 Three organs:
         • Parathormone ┐                                • Bone (bank for Ca²⁺)
         • Vitamin D    ┘ (↑ Ca²⁺)                       • GIT (absorption of dietary Ca²⁺)
         • Calcitonin     (↓ Ca²⁺)                       • Kidneys
```

- **Hormones that raise Ca²⁺:** **parathormone**[^ety-parathormone][^pre-para] and **vitamin D**[^ety-vitamin].
- **Hormone that lowers Ca²⁺:** **calcitonin**[^ety-calcitonin].
- **Organs:** **bone** (bank for Ca²⁺) · **GIT** (absorption of dietary Ca²⁺) · **kidneys**.[^7]

- **Oxyphill cells**[^ety-oxyphil].[^8]

**Structure:** **84 amino acids**[^ety-amino-acid] **(large protein).**[^9]

#### Actions

**1. Actions on bone**

```
                                                          Denosumab (used in Rx of osteoporosis)
                                                                 │ ⊖
PTH ──acts on──→ PTH receptor ──activates──→ RANKL ──induces differentiation──→ Osteoclast ──→ Releases enzymes
                 on osteoblasts                                                                     ↓
                                                     Bone resorption (dissolves hydroxyapatite crystals)
                                                                         ↓
                                                     Release of Ca²⁺ & PO₄³⁻ from bones
```

PTH does **not** act on the osteoclast directly — it binds its receptor on the **osteoblast**[^ety-osteoblast][^pre-osteo][^suf-blast], which then shows **RANKL**[^ety-rankl] on its surface; RANKL makes precursor cells **differentiate**[^ety-differentiation] into **osteoclasts**[^ety-osteoclast][^suf-clast], whose enzymes dissolve the **hydroxyapatite**[^ety-hydroxyapatite] crystals (**bone resorption**[^ety-resorption][^pre-re]) and release Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻. **Denosumab**[^ety-denosumab] blocks RANKL (⊖) — used in Rx of **osteoporosis**[^ety-osteoporosis].[^10]

**Applied aspect:**
- **Concept:**
  - **Osteoblast: bone formation.**
  - **Osteoclast: bone resorption.**
- **Use — Teriparatide**[^ety-teriparatide]**: drug form of PTH.**
  - **Continuous administration**[^ety-administration] **→ bone resorption.**
  - **Intermittent**[^ety-intermittent][^pre-inter] **administration → bone formation.**[^11]

![[Calcium Homeostasis - parathormone and teriparatide amino acid chain.png]]
*Parathormone (84 amino acids, H₂N → COOH) with the first 34 amino acids bracketed as teriparatide (34 AA's) — the active N-terminal fragment used as the drug (cropped from the handwritten page).*

**2. Action on kidneys**

```
                    Action on kidneys
           ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
          PCT                           DCT
   ⊣ Na⁺–PO₄³⁻ cotransporter       ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption
   → PO₄³⁻ excreted in urine
   → ↓ S.PO₄³⁻ (phosphaturia)
```

- **PCT** — PTH inhibits (⊖) the **Na⁺ PO₄³⁻ cotransporter**[^ety-cotransporter][^pre-co] on the luminal side of the PCT cell, so phosphate stays in the lumen → **excreted in urine** → **↓ S. PO₄³⁻**. **PTH (AKA phosphatonin**[^ety-phosphatonin]**) → phosphaturia**[^ety-phosphaturia][^suf-uria].
- **DCT** — **↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption**[^ety-reabsorption].[^12]

![[Calcium Homeostasis - PTH action on the PCT cell.png]]
*PTH on the PCT cell — the Na⁺–PO₄³⁻ cotransporter carries Na⁺ and PO₄³⁻ from the lumen towards the peritubular capillary; PTH (⊖) blocks it, so PO₄³⁻ is excreted in urine and serum PO₄³⁻ falls (cropped from the handwritten page).*

The **peritubular**[^ety-peritubular][^pre-peri] capillary on the left of the figure is where reabsorbed solutes go; blocking the cotransporter diverts phosphate to the urine instead.

> [!info] **Overall action of PTH**
> - **↑ S. Ca²⁺**
> - **↓ S. PO₄³⁻**

**3. Action on GIT**

```
PTH ──→ ↑ Vit D ──→ ↑↑ Ca²⁺ absorption from GIT (indirect action)
```

#### Calcium sensing receptors (CaSR)

The chief cell carries a **CaSR**[^ety-sensing] that reads the blood calcium itself: **high calcium ⊖** the receptor (PTH held back), **low calcium ⊕** it (PTH released).

![[Calcium Homeostasis - calcium sensing receptor on the chief cell.png]]
*Calcium sensing receptor on the parathyroid chief cell — high calcium inhibits (⊖) and low calcium stimulates (⊕) the release of stored PTH (cropped from the handwritten page).*

**Applied aspect:**

```
Mutation of CaSR → PTH not suppressed → PTH ↑↑↑ → Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia
```

A **mutation**[^ety-mutation] that blinds the CaSR makes the gland "think" calcium is low, so PTH keeps coming → **familial**[^ety-familial] **hypocalciuric**[^ety-hypocalciuric] **hypercalcemia**[^ety-hypercalcemia] (FHH).[^13]

### ☀️ Vitamin D

#### Synthesis

```
7-Dehydrocholesterol (skin)
        │  UV-B rays (280–320 nm)
        ↓
Vit D3 or Cholecalciferol
        │  25-hydroxylase in liver
        ↓
25-Hydroxycholecalciferol / Calcidiol   ← Lab investigation for Vit D status
        │  1α-Hydroxylase in kidney      ← RATE LIMITING STEP
        ↓
1,25-Dihydroxycholecalciferol / Calcitriol   ← MOST ACTIVE FORM
```

- **7-Dehydrocholesterol**[^ety-dehydrocholesterol] in the **skin** is converted by **UV-B rays (280–320 nm)**[^ety-ultraviolet][^pre-ultra] into **Vit D3 or cholecalciferol**[^ety-cholecalciferol][^pre-chole].[^14]
- **25-hydroxylase**[^ety-hydroxylase][^suf-ase] **in liver** → **25-hydroxycholecalciferol / calcidiol**[^ety-calcidiol][^suf-ol] — the **lab investigation for Vit D status**.[^15]
- **1α-Hydroxylase in kidney** — the **rate limiting step** → **1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol / calcitriol**[^ety-calcitriol] — the **most active form**.

#### Actions

| 1. On GIT | 2. On Bone | 3. On Kidneys |
|---|---|---|
| **Ca²⁺ absorption from duodenum** ↓ | **Dual action** | **↑ resorption of Ca²⁺ & PO₄³⁻**[^16] |
| • ↑ **Calbindin**[^ety-calbindin] | • **Low calcium states:** **bone resorption** (vitamin D receptors on **osteoblast** → action similar to PTH) | ↓ |
| • ↑ expression of **Ca²⁺ ATPase**[^ety-atpase] | • **High calcium states:** **bone formation** | **↑ S.Ca²⁺ & ↑ S.PO₄³⁻** |
| • ↑ **Alkaline phosphatase**[^ety-phosphatase] | — | — |

Calcitriol builds the gut's calcium-carrying machinery (calbindin inside the cell, the Ca²⁺ pump at the base, alkaline phosphatase on the brush border), so its main job is **more income**; at bone it withdraws when calcium is low and deposits when calcium is high.[^17]

## 📋 Summary

|  | **PTH** | **Vitamin D** | **Calcitonin** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stimulus**[^ety-stimulus] | ↓ in serum Ca²⁺ | • ↓ in serum Ca²⁺ · • ↓ in serum PO₄³⁻ · • ↑ PTH | ↑ in serum Ca²⁺ |
| **Bone** | ↑ bone resorption | Both bone formation & resorption | Inhibits bone resorption |

| **Kidney** | • ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption · • ↑ PO₄³⁻ excretion[^ety-excretion] | ↑ Ca²⁺ & ↑ PO₄³⁻ reabsorption | ↑ Ca²⁺ excretion |

> [!tip] One way to remember the phosphate column
> PTH and vitamin D both raise calcium; they differ only in **phosphate**. PTH "**P**hosphate **T**rashing **H**ormone" lowers it (phosphaturia); vitamin D raises it (absorbs and reabsorbs both).

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Only **free (ionised) calcium** acts, and it is held in a narrow band by one fast sensor — the **CaSR on parathyroid chief cells**. A fall in free Ca²⁺ releases PTH within minutes; PTH then raises Ca²⁺ by three routes (withdraw from bone via the osteoblast's RANKL, save Ca²⁺ at the DCT, and switch on renal 1α-hydroxylase so calcitriol pulls more from the gut) while dumping phosphate at the PCT so the Ca × PO₄ product does not precipitate.
>
> Everything clinical follows from where the chain breaks: a pH or albumin shift changes the *free* fraction without changing the total (alkalosis tetany); a blind CaSR resets the set-point (FHH); a tumour peptide mimics PTH (PTHrP); and the *pattern* of administration flips PTH from resorptive (continuous) to anabolic (intermittent teriparatide).

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Total calcium 8.7–10.2 mg/dL, about 50 % free, 41 % albumin-bound, 9 % complexed. The CaSR keeps PTH just high enough; calcidiol (the storage form) is converted to calcitriol as needed; bone formation (osteoblasts, osteocalcin, ALP, P1NP) balances resorption (osteoclasts, TRAP, cathepsin K, NTX/CTX).
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Vitamin D deficiency or low dietary calcium lowers absorption; free Ca²⁺ dips, the CaSR releases PTH, and serum calcium is held near normal at the cost of bone (resorption ↑, resorption markers ↑) and phosphate (phosphaturia ↓ S.PO₄³⁻) — secondary hyperparathyroidism. Pregnancy and lactation draw on the bone reserve, with calcitonin limiting the loss.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> When the PTH response cannot keep up (hypoparathyroidism, severe deficiency) or the free fraction falls acutely (alkalosis), neuromuscular hyperexcitability appears: paraesthesia, carpopedal spasm, Trousseau's and Chvostek's signs, laryngospasm, seizures. In the opposite direction, PTH or PTHrP excess produces hypercalcemia with stones, bone pain and osteoporosis.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → trigger (↓ intake / ↓ vitamin D / alkalosis / parathyroid loss / tumour PTHrP) → ↓ free Ca²⁺ → CaSR → ↑ PTH (compensation, bone loss, ↓ PO₄³⁻) → decompensation → tetany (low) or hypercalcemic syndrome (high)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Always interpret calcium with albumin and pH — a "normal" total calcium can hide symptomatic low ionised calcium in alkalosis. Treatment follows the hormone: calcium + calcitriol for hypoparathyroidism; denosumab or bisphosphonates and calcitonin for resorption; intermittent teriparatide to build bone.

>

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "PTH acts directly on osteoclasts"**
>
> **What students think:** PTH binds osteoclasts to make them resorb bone.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Osteoclasts have no PTH receptor. PTH binds osteoblasts, which express RANKL (and less osteoprotegerin); RANKL drives osteoclast differentiation. This is why denosumab (anti-RANKL) blocks PTH-driven resorption, and why calcitonin — not PTH — is the hormone with a receptor on the osteoclast.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "PTH talks to the builder (osteoblast), the builder tells the breaker (osteoclast)."
>
> **Trap 2: Thinking PTH always destroys bone**
>
> **What students think:** A PTH drug would worsen osteoporosis.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The effect depends on the pattern: continuous PTH → resorption; intermittent (once-daily teriparatide) → net bone formation, because the brief pulse stimulates osteoblasts before the resorptive signal builds up.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Continuous = consumes bone; intermittent = invests in bone."
>
> **Trap 3: Measuring the wrong vitamin D**
>
> **What students think:** Calcitriol, being most active, is the test for vitamin D status.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Calcitriol has a short half-life and is tightly regulated by PTH, so it can be normal or even high in deficiency. Calcidiol (25-OH D) is the circulating storage form and reflects stores — the lab investigation for vitamin D status.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Status = 25 (storage); activity = 1,25 (active)."
>
> **Trap 4: Ignoring albumin and pH**
>
> **What students think:** A normal total calcium excludes hypocalcemic tetany.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Only free Ca²⁺ acts. In alkalosis albumin gives up H⁺ and binds more Ca²⁺, lowering the free fraction with no change in total; conversely hypoalbuminaemia lowers total calcium while free calcium stays normal (no tetany).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Correct for albumin and check pH (or measure ionised calcium) before calling calcium normal or low.
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing up the phosphate effects**
>
> **What students think:** All calcium-raising hormones raise phosphate too.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** PTH lowers serum phosphate by inhibiting the Na⁺–PO₄³⁻ cotransporter in the PCT (phosphaturia), whereas vitamin D raises both calcium and phosphate (gut absorption and renal reabsorption). The summary table's "overall effect" row is the most tested line.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "PTH: Ca up, P down. Vitamin D: both up. Calcitonin: Ca down."

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Thyroid Gland]]**
> Parafollicular C cells sit between the thyroid follicles; calcitonin is the C-cell product and the tumour marker of medullary carcinoma.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Proximal Convoluted Tubule]]**
> The Na⁺–PO₄³⁻ cotransporter that PTH inhibits, and the 1α-hydroxylase that makes calcitriol, both live in the PCT.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule]]**
> PTH-stimulated Ca²⁺ reabsorption in the DCT and the CaSR of the thick ascending limb complete the renal side of calcium handling.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Characteristics of Nerve Fibres]]**
> Low extracellular Ca²⁺ lowers the threshold of Na⁺ channels — the membrane basis of hypocalcemic tetany.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Acid Base Balance]]**
> Alkalosis increases albumin binding of Ca²⁺; acidosis frees it — the pH link in the "ratio of free to bound calcium".
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Digestion and Absorption]]**
> Ca²⁺ absorption in the duodenum and proximal jejunum, its dependence on gastric acid and calbindin, and its blockage by phytates, oxalates and fatty acids.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Post-thyroidectomy tetany (parathyroid injury), hyperventilation tetany, hypercalcemia of malignancy, FHH vs primary hyperparathyroidism, vitamin D deficiency rickets/osteomalacia and osteoporosis drugs (denosumab, teriparatide, salmon calcitonin) all apply this chapter.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Normal calcium **8.7–10.2 mg/dL**: **free 50 %** (ionic, **most active**), **bound 41 %** (albumin, storage, not active), **complex 9 %** (citrate, phosphate).
> 2. Free/bound ratio set by **blood pH** and **albumin**; **respiratory alkalosis → ↓ H⁺ → ↑ Ca²⁺–albumin binding → ↓ free Ca²⁺** → tetany.
> 3. **↓ Ca²⁺ → Na⁺ channels open more frequently → hyperexcitability → tetany**; **Trousseau's** (cuff 20 mmHg above SBP × 3 min → carpopedal spasm) and **Chvostek's** (tap facial nerve at angle of jaw).
> 4. Absorption: **duodenum & proximal jejunum**; ↑ by **gastric acid, protein-rich diet**; ↓ by **phytates, oxalates, fatty acids** (insoluble complexes).
> 5. Three hormones (PTH, vitamin D ↑ Ca²⁺; calcitonin ↓ Ca²⁺) × three organs (bone = bank, GIT, kidneys).
> 6. PTH: **chief cells**, **84 amino acids**; acts on **osteoblast PTH receptor → RANKL → osteoclast differentiation → bone resorption**; **denosumab** blocks RANKL (osteoporosis).
> 7. **Teriparatide** = PTH (1–34): **continuous → resorption, intermittent → formation**.
> 8. PTH kidney: **PCT ⊣ Na⁺–PO₄³⁻ cotransporter (phosphaturia, "phosphatonin")**, **DCT ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption**; overall **↑ S.Ca²⁺, ↓ S.PO₄³⁻**; GIT action is **indirect via vitamin D**.
> 9. **CaSR** on chief cells: high Ca ⊖, low Ca ⊕; **CaSR mutation → familial hypocalciuric hypercalcemia**.
> 10. Vitamin D: 7-dehydrocholesterol (skin) → **UV-B 280–320 nm** → cholecalciferol → **25-hydroxylase (liver)** → **calcidiol = lab test for status** → **1α-hydroxylase (kidney) = rate limiting** → **calcitriol = most active**.
> 11. Calcitriol: gut ↑ calbindin, Ca²⁺ ATPase, ALP; bone **dual action**; kidney ↑ Ca²⁺ & PO₄³⁻ → **↑ S.Ca²⁺ & ↑ S.PO₄³⁻**.
> 12. **Calcitonin**: **parafollicular C cells**, **tumour marker of medullary carcinoma thyroid**; ⊣ osteoclast, ↓ gut absorption, ↓ renal reabsorption; protects bone in **pregnancy & lactation**; analogue from **salmon = salcatonin**.
> 13. **PTHrP → hypercalcemia of malignancy** (squamous cell, small cell lung carcinoma on the page). Bone formation markers: **osteocalcin, ALP, P1NP**; resorption: **TRAP, MMP, cathepsin K (blood), NTX, CTX (urine)**.

[^1]: Textbook ranges are 8.5–10.5 mg/dL (2.2–2.6 mmol/L) total; ionised calcium 4.5–5.3 mg/dL (1.1–1.3 mmol/L). About 99 % of body calcium (≈ 1 kg) is in bone, 1 % in cells and only 0.1 % in the ECF — which is why bone is called the bank.
[^2]: Guyton gives ~50 % ionised, ~40 % protein-bound (mostly albumin) and ~10 % complexed with anions — the page's 50 / 41 / 9. Free Ca²⁺ triggers excitation–contraction coupling, exocytosis of transmitters, hormones and exocrine granules, and acts as a cofactor (e.g. clotting factor IV, calmodulin-dependent enzymes).
[^3]: Ca²⁺ ions bind the outer surface of voltage-gated Na⁺ channels and stabilise them ("membrane-stabilising"); when extracellular Ca²⁺ falls the channels open at a less negative voltage, so the threshold drops and peripheral nerves fire spontaneously — paraesthesia, carpopedal spasm, laryngospasm and seizures. Hypercalcemia does the opposite (weakness, constipation, short QT).
[^4]: Trousseau's sign: the cuff is kept above systolic pressure for 3 minutes; ischaemia of the nerves of the forearm unmasks latent tetany as *main d'accoucheur* (flexion at wrist and metacarpophalangeal joints, extension of the fingers, adducted thumb). It is more specific than Chvostek's sign, which is positive in ~10–25 % of normal people.
[^5]: Albumin's binding sites take H⁺ and Ca²⁺ competitively: in alkalosis H⁺ leaves albumin and Ca²⁺ takes its place, so ionised Ca²⁺ falls (≈ 0.05 mmol/L per 0.1 rise in pH); acidosis raises ionised Ca²⁺. Correct total calcium for albumin as: corrected Ca = measured Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 − albumin g/dL).
[^6]: Two routes: an active, vitamin D-dependent transcellular route (TRPV6 channel → calbindin → PMCA pump) mostly in the duodenum, and a passive paracellular route along the whole small intestine (the jejunum and ileum absorb more in total because chyme stays there longer). Only ~25–35 % of dietary calcium is absorbed.
[^7]: Modern texts add a fourth hormone, **FGF-23** from osteocytes, which causes phosphaturia and inhibits 1α-hydroxylase; together with PTH and calcitriol it forms the bone–kidney–parathyroid axis of phosphate control.
[^8]: Usually four parathyroid glands (two superior from the 4th, two inferior from the 3rd pharyngeal pouch) on the back of the thyroid. Chief (principal) cells make PTH; oxyphil cells (large, eosinophilic, mitochondria-rich) appear after puberty and increase with age, function uncertain.
[^9]: PTH is a single-chain polypeptide of 84 amino acids (MW ≈ 9500), made as pre-pro-PTH (115) → pro-PTH (90) → PTH (84); the biological activity lies in the N-terminal 1–34. "Large protein" on the page means larger than its 34-residue active fragment; strictly it is a polypeptide. Plasma t½ ≈ 2–4 min.
[^10]: PTH increases RANKL and decreases **osteoprotegerin** (OPG, a decoy receptor) on osteoblasts; RANKL binds RANK on osteoclast precursors (with M-CSF) → mature, active osteoclasts that secrete H⁺ and cathepsin K. Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody against RANKL given 60 mg subcutaneously every 6 months — it acts like OPG.
[^11]: Teriparatide = recombinant human PTH 1–34, 20 µg subcutaneously once daily, for up to 2 years in severe osteoporosis. A daily pulse stimulates osteoblasts (and lining-cell activation, ↓ sclerostin) more than osteoclasts — the "anabolic window". Abaloparatide is the PTHrP-analogue counterpart.
[^12]: PTH also stimulates **1α-hydroxylase** in the PCT (the link to vitamin D), inhibits NHE3-mediated HCO₃⁻ reabsorption (mild hyperchloraemic acidosis in hyperparathyroidism), and increases Ca²⁺ reabsorption in the DCT via TRPV5 channels and in the thick ascending limb. The page calls PTH "phosphatonin"; in current usage *phosphatonin* names FGF-23 and related phosphaturic factors, but PTH is equally phosphaturic.
[^13]: The CaSR is a G-protein-coupled receptor on parathyroid chief cells and in the thick ascending limb. FHH is an autosomal dominant inactivating CaSR mutation: mild lifelong hypercalcemia, **low urinary calcium** (Ca/Cr clearance ratio < 0.01) and a normal or mildly raised (inappropriately unsuppressed) PTH — benign, no surgery. Homozygous mutation → neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism. Cinacalcet (a calcimimetic) activates the CaSR.
[^14]: Many texts give the effective UV-B band as 290–315 nm; the page's 280–320 nm is kept in the body. UV-B converts 7-dehydrocholesterol to pre-vitamin D3, which isomerises with heat to D3; D2 (ergocalciferol) comes from plants and fungi.
[^15]: 25-OH D (calcidiol) has a half-life of ~2–3 weeks and reflects body stores (deficiency < 20 ng/mL); renal 1α-hydroxylase (CYP27B1) is stimulated by PTH, low phosphate and low calcium and inhibited by FGF-23 and calcitriol itself; 24-hydroxylase inactivates both forms.
[^16]: The page writes "↑ resorption of Ca²⁺ & PO₄³⁻" for the kidney — meaning tubular **reabsorption**; *resorption* is usually reserved for bone.
[^17]: In the enterocyte calcitriol induces the apical TRPV6 channel, the carrier protein **calbindin-D9k**, and the basolateral Ca²⁺ ATPase (PMCA1b); it also raises brush-border alkaline phosphatase and phosphate absorption (NaPi-IIb). At bone, its physiological role is to supply Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ for mineralisation (deficiency → rickets/osteomalacia); at high levels it mobilises calcium through osteoblast RANKL like PTH.
[^18]: Medullary carcinoma of thyroid arises from the neural-crest C cells; it occurs sporadically or in MEN 2A/2B (RET proto-oncogene), shows amyloid stroma derived from calcitonin, and is followed with serum calcitonin and CEA. Despite very high calcitonin, patients are not hypocalcemic — calcitonin's role in adult humans is minor.
[^19]: Calcitonin is a 32-amino-acid peptide released when plasma Ca²⁺ rises (and by gastrin); its effect is rapid but transient (osteoclasts escape within days). Thyroidectomy does not cause hypercalcemia — another sign that its role in adults is small; it matters more in children, pregnancy and lactation.
[^20]: About 25–30 g of calcium passes to the fetus, mostly in the third trimester. Maternal calcitonin, calcitriol (also made by the placenta) and intestinal absorption all rise; in lactation PTHrP from the breast mobilises bone, and calcitonin is thought to limit maternal bone loss.
[^21]: Salmon calcitonin is ~40–50 × more potent than human calcitonin (it binds the receptor more tightly and is cleared more slowly). "Salcatonin" is its British approved name; uses: hypercalcemia, Paget's disease, painful osteoporotic fractures (nasal spray / injection); tachyphylaxis limits it.
[^22]: PTHrP shares its first 13 amino acids with PTH and acts on the same PTH1 receptor, causing *humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy* with **suppressed** PTH. It is classically produced by **squamous cell** carcinomas (lung, head and neck, oesophagus, cervix), renal and breast cancers and HTLV-1 lymphoma. Standard texts link **small cell** lung carcinoma to ectopic ACTH and SIADH rather than PTHrP; the page lists both, and the page is kept in the body.
[^23]: Osteocalcin (bone Gla protein, vitamin K-dependent) is made by osteoblasts; bone-specific ALP and the procollagen type I propeptides (P1NP, P1CP) are cut off when osteoblasts lay down collagen. TRAP 5b and cathepsin K come from osteoclasts; NTX and CTX are the cross-linked end pieces released when collagen is broken down (CTX is usually measured in serum, NTX in urine). Markers are used to monitor osteoporosis treatment.
[^ety-calcium]: **calcium** — Latin *calx, calcis*, "lime, limestone" + *-ium* (the suffix for a metallic element) → the metal of lime; isolated and named by Humphry Davy (1808). Same root as *calculus* (a little stone) and *calcification*.
[^ety-homeostasis]: **homeostasis** — Greek *homoios*, "similar" + *stasis*, "standing still" → staying similar within limits; coined by Walter Cannon (1926). Calcium is one of the most tightly held values in the body.
[^ety-distribution]: **distribution** — Latin *dis-*, "apart" + *tribuere*, "to assign, allot" + *-ion* → allotting apart: how a substance is shared among compartments (free, bound, complexed). **Differentiate:** *distribution* (where it sits) vs *absorption* (how it gets in) vs *excretion* (how it leaves).
[^ety-ion]: **ion / ionic** — Greek *ion*, "going" (present participle of *ienai*, to go) — named by Faraday (1834) for particles that go towards an electrode → a charged atom; *ionic calcium* = Ca²⁺ free in solution. **Differentiate:** *cation* (+, goes to the cathode — Ca²⁺) vs *anion* (–, goes to the anode — PO₄³⁻).
[^ety-albumin]: **albumin** — Latin *albumen*, "white of egg" (*albus*, white) + *-in*, protein → the main plasma protein, first known from egg white; it carries Ca²⁺, bilirubin, fatty acids and drugs, and sets oncotic pressure.
[^ety-citrate]: **citrate** — Latin *citrus*, "citron tree" + *-ate*, a salt → a salt of citric acid (first crystallised from lemon juice). Citrate chelates Ca²⁺ — the basis of citrate anticoagulant in stored blood (massive transfusion → hypocalcemia).
[^ety-phosphate]: **phosphate** — Greek *phōs*, "light" + *pherein*, "to carry" → *phosphorus*, "light-bearer" (it glows in air) + *-ate*, a salt → a salt of phosphoric acid (PO₄³⁻). Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ together form hydroxyapatite.
[^ety-contraction]: **contraction** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *trahere*, "to draw, pull" + *-ion* → drawing together: shortening or tension of muscle. **Differentiate:** *contraction* (active shortening) · *contracture* (fixed shortening) · *spasm* (involuntary contraction) · *tetany* (sustained spasms from hyperexcitable nerves).
[^ety-neurotransmitter]: **neurotransmitter** — Greek *neuron*, "nerve" + Latin *trans-*, "across" + *mittere*, "to send" + *-er* → a chemical sent across (the synapse) by a nerve. Its release is triggered by Ca²⁺ entry into the terminal.
[^ety-secretion]: **secretion / exocrine** — Latin *se-*, "apart" + *cernere*, "to sift, separate" + *-ion* → separating out a useful product; *exocrine* = Greek *exō*, "outside" + *krinein*, "to secrete" → secreting outward into a duct. **Differentiate:** *secretion* (useful product released) vs *excretion* (waste removed); *exocrine* (duct) vs *endocrine* (blood).
[^ety-hypocalcemia]: **hypocalcemia / hypocalcemic** — Greek *hypo-*, "under" + Latin *calx*, lime (calcium) + *-emia*, "blood condition" → low blood calcium (< 8.5 mg/dL total, < 4.5 mg/dL ionised); *-ic* = "of or caused by".
[^ety-tetany]: **tetany** — Greek *tetanos*, "stretched, rigid, convulsive tension" (*teinein*, to stretch) → intermittent tonic spasms of muscles from hyperexcitable nerves. **Differentiate:** *tetany* (hypocalcemia, alkalosis) vs *tetanus* (the disease — *Clostridium tetani* toxin) vs *tetanic contraction* (a fused, sustained muscle contraction from high-frequency stimulation).
[^ety-ph]: **pH** — "potential (power) of hydrogen", introduced by Søren Sørensen (1909); *p* is usually read as German *Potenz* or French *puissance* — origin disputed. pH = −log [H⁺]; normal 7.35–7.45.
[^ety-depolarisation]: **depolarisation** — Latin *de-*, "away, reversing" + *polaris* (Greek *polos*, pivot, pole) + *-isation*, the process → removing the polarity: the inside of the membrane becoming less negative. **Differentiate:** *depolarisation* (towards zero / positive) · *repolarisation* (back to resting) · *hyperpolarisation* (more negative than resting).
[^ety-excitability]: **excitability / hyperexcitability** — Latin *ex-*, "out" + *citare*, "to set in motion, rouse" + *-ability* → the capacity to be roused (to fire an action potential); *hyper-* = excessive. **Differentiate:** *excitability* (can it fire?) vs *conductivity* (can it propagate?) vs *irritability* (older synonym for excitability).
[^ety-trousseau]: **Trousseau's sign** — Armand Trousseau (1801–1867), French physician at the Hôtel-Dieu, Paris, who described carpal spasm on compressing the arm in latent tetany (1861). **Differentiate:** *Trousseau's sign of latent tetany* (hypocalcemia) vs *Trousseau's sign of malignancy* (migratory thrombophlebitis in visceral cancer — he diagnosed it in himself).
[^ety-carpopedal]: **carpopedal** — Greek *karpos*, "wrist" + Latin *pes, pedis*, "foot" + *-al* → of the wrists and feet: carpopedal spasm affects both hands and feet in tetany.
[^ety-spasm]: **spasm** — Greek *spasmos*, "a drawing, convulsion" (*span*, to draw, pull) → an involuntary muscle contraction. **Differentiate:** *spasm* (involuntary contraction) vs *spasticity* (velocity-dependent ↑ tone from an upper motor neuron lesion) vs *cramp* (painful spasm).
[^ety-chvostek]: **Chvostek's sign** — František Chvostek (1835–1884), Austrian (Moravian-born) surgeon in Vienna, who described facial twitching on tapping the facial nerve in tetany (1876).
[^ety-alkalosis]: **alkalosis** — Arabic *al-qaly*, "the ashes (of saltwort)" → *alkali* + Greek *-ōsis*, a condition → a condition of excess base (pH > 7.45). *Respiratory* alkalosis = from blowing off CO₂ (hyperventilation); *metabolic* = from ↑ HCO₃⁻.
[^ety-hyperventilation]: **hyperventilation** — Greek *hyper-*, "over" + Latin *ventilare*, "to fan, air" (*ventus*, wind) + *-ation* → breathing more than metabolism needs → ↓ PaCO₂ → respiratory alkalosis. **Differentiate:** *hyperventilation* (beyond need) vs *hyperpnoea* (deeper, matched to need — exercise) vs *tachypnoea* (faster).
[^ety-absorption]: **absorption** — Latin *ab-*, "away, from" + *sorbere*, "to suck in" + *-ion* → sucking in from (the gut lumen into the blood). **Differentiate:** *absorption* (gut → blood) · *reabsorption* (tubular fluid → blood) · *resorption* (bone dissolved → blood) · *adsorption* (sticking to a surface).
[^ety-duodenum]: **duodenum** — Latin *duodeni*, "twelve each" → *intestinum duodenum digitorum*, "the intestine twelve fingers long" (Herophilus' measure, ~25 cm). Main site of active Ca²⁺ and iron absorption.
[^ety-jejunum]: **jejunum** — Latin *jejunus*, "empty, fasting" → it was usually found empty at autopsy (rapid transit). *Proximal* = nearer the stomach.
[^ety-phytate]: **phytate** — Greek *phyton*, "plant" + *-ate*, a salt → a salt of phytic acid (inositol hexaphosphate), the phosphorus store of cereals and legumes; it binds Ca²⁺, Fe²⁺ and Zn²⁺ into insoluble complexes.
[^ety-protein]: **protein** — Greek *prōteios*, "of the first rank" (*prōtos*, first) → the primary substance of living matter (Mulder/Berzelius, 1838). Amino acids such as lysine and arginine keep Ca²⁺ soluble and favour its absorption.
[^ety-oxalate]: **oxalate** — Greek *oxalis*, "sorrel" (from *oxys*, sour) + *-ate* → a salt of oxalic acid, first isolated from wood sorrel; it forms insoluble calcium oxalate — in the gut (less absorption) and in the kidney (the commonest stone).
[^ety-parathormone]: **parathormone** — *para(thyroid)* + *hormone* (Greek *hormōn*, "setting in motion") → the hormone of the parathyroid; also called parathyroid hormone (PTH) or parathyrin.
[^ety-vitamin]: **vitamin** — Latin *vita*, "life" + *amine* → "vital amine", coined by Casimir Funk (1912) when he thought all such factors were amines (the *-e* was later dropped). Vitamin D is really a steroid **hormone** (secosteroid) made in the skin.
[^ety-calcitonin]: **calcitonin** — Latin *calx*, "calcium" + Greek *tonos*, "tension, tone" + *-in* → the hormone that regulates the "tone" (level) of calcium; named by D. Harold Copp (1962), who discovered it.
[^ety-parathyroid]: **parathyroid** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + *thyreoeidēs*, "shield-shaped" (*thyreos*, an oblong door-shaped shield + *-eidēs*, like) → the glands beside the thyroid; described by Ivar Sandström (1880), the last major organ to be discovered in humans.
[^ety-oxyphil]: **oxyphil** — Greek *oxys*, "sharp, acid" + *philos*, "loving" → acid-dye-loving: cells whose mitochondria-packed cytoplasm takes eosin strongly (pink). The page spells it "oxyphill". **Differentiate:** *oxyphil* (= eosinophilic, acidophilic) vs *basophil* (blue) vs *chromophobe* (pale).
[^ety-amino-acid]: **amino acid** — *amine* (from *ammonia*, named after the temple of Ammon in Libya where ammonium salts were gathered) + *acid* (Latin *acidus*, sour) → a molecule with an amino (–NH₂) group and an acid (–COOH) group; PTH is a chain of 84 of them.
[^ety-osteoblast]: **osteoblast** — Greek *osteon*, "bone" + *blastos*, "germ, bud" → the bone-building cell; it carries the PTH receptor and RANKL. **Differentiate:** *osteoblast* (builds) · *osteocyte* (mature, embedded, senses strain, makes FGF-23 and sclerostin) · *osteoclast* (breaks).
[^ety-rankl]: **RANKL** — **R**eceptor **A**ctivator of **N**uclear factor **κ**B **L**igand; *ligand* is Latin *ligare*, "to bind" → the molecule that binds the RANK receptor on osteoclast precursors. **Differentiate:** *RANK* (the receptor on the osteoclast) · *RANKL* (the signal on the osteoblast) · *OPG* (osteoprotegerin, "bone protector" decoy).
[^ety-differentiation]: **differentiation** — Latin *dis-*, "apart" + *ferre*, "to carry" → *differre*, "to carry apart, to differ" + *-ation* → the process by which a precursor becomes a specialised cell. **Differentiate:** *differentiation* (becoming specialised) vs *proliferation* (dividing) vs *maturation* (finishing development).
[^ety-osteoclast]: **osteoclast** — Greek *osteon*, "bone" + *klastos*, "broken" (*klan*, to break) → the bone-breaking cell: a multinucleated macrophage relative that seals onto bone and dissolves it with acid and cathepsin K. It carries the calcitonin receptor, not the PTH receptor.
[^ety-hydroxyapatite]: **hydroxyapatite** — *hydroxy-* (–OH) + Greek *apatē*, "deceit" + *-ite*, a mineral → the "deceiving" mineral (apatite was easily mistaken for other gems); Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂ — the crystal of bone and enamel.
[^ety-resorption]: **resorption** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *sorbere*, "to suck in" + *-ion* → sucking back: osteoclasts dissolving bone and returning its mineral to the blood. **Differentiate:** *bone resorption* (osteoclast) vs *bone formation* (osteoblast); *resorption* (bone) vs *reabsorption* (tubule).
[^ety-denosumab]: **denosumab** — a coined name: *-mab* = **m**onoclonal **a**nti**b**ody, *-u-* = fully human (*-umab*), *-os-* = the bone target stem, *den-* = a distinctive prefix → a fully human monoclonal antibody acting on bone (anti-RANKL).
[^ety-osteoporosis]: **osteoporosis** — Greek *osteon*, "bone" + *poros*, "pore" + *-ōsis* → porous bone: too little bone mass, normally mineralised. **Differentiate:** *osteoporosis* (less bone) vs *osteomalacia* (soft, poorly mineralised bone — vitamin D deficiency in adults) vs *rickets* (the same in children, with growth-plate changes).
[^ety-teriparatide]: **teriparatide** — a coined drug name (INN): the stem *-paratide* marks a parathyroid-hormone peptide (as in abaloparatide), and *teri-* is a distinguishing syllable of uncertain origin → recombinant PTH 1–34. Remember the *para(thyroid)* hidden inside it.
[^ety-administration]: **administration** — Latin *ad-*, "to" + *ministrare*, "to serve" + *-ation* → serving to (a patient): giving a drug. **Differentiate:** *continuous* (constant level — resorption) vs *intermittent / pulsatile* (brief daily peaks — formation) — the same idea as continuous vs pulsatile GnRH.
[^ety-intermittent]: **intermittent** — Latin *inter-*, "between" + *mittere*, "to send, let go" → letting go in between: given in pulses with gaps. **Differentiate:** *intermittent* (on–off) vs *continuous* (unbroken) vs *pulsatile* (rhythmic bursts).
[^ety-cotransporter]: **cotransporter** — Latin *co-*, "together" + *trans-*, "across" + *portare*, "to carry" + *-er* → a protein that carries two solutes across together in the same direction (Na⁺ with PO₄³⁻; symport). **Differentiate:** *cotransport / symport* (same direction) vs *countertransport / antiport* (opposite directions — Cl⁻–HCO₃⁻ exchanger) vs *uniport* (one solute).
[^ety-phosphatonin]: **phosphatonin** — *phosphate* + Greek *tonos*, "tension, regulation" + *-in* → a hormone that regulates phosphate (causes phosphaturia). The page uses it for PTH; in current literature the name belongs chiefly to FGF-23.
[^ety-phosphaturia]: **phosphaturia** — *phosphate* + Greek *ouron*, "urine" + *-ia* → phosphate in (excess in) the urine. **Differentiate:** *phosphaturia* (urine) vs *hypophosphatemia* (low blood phosphate — its result).
[^ety-reabsorption]: **reabsorption** — Latin *re-*, "back, again" + *ab-*, "from" + *sorbere*, "to suck in" + *-ion* → sucking back again: moving filtered solute from the tubular fluid back into blood. **Differentiate:** *reabsorption* (tubule → blood) vs *secretion* (blood → tubule) vs *excretion* (what ends in urine).
[^ety-peritubular]: **peritubular** — Greek *peri-*, "around" + Latin *tubulus*, "little tube" + *-ar* → around the tubules: the capillary network around the PCT and DCT that takes up what the tubule reabsorbs.
[^ety-sensing]: **sensing (calcium-sensing receptor)** — Latin *sentire*, "to feel, perceive" → a receptor that perceives the extracellular Ca²⁺ concentration itself. **Differentiate:** *sensor* receptors like CaSR (ion as the ligand) vs hormone receptors (a hormone as ligand).
[^ety-mutation]: **mutation** — Latin *mutare*, "to change" + *-ation* → a change in the DNA sequence. **Differentiate:** *loss-of-function* (inactivating — FHH, CaSR blind to Ca²⁺) vs *gain-of-function* (activating — autosomal dominant hypocalcemia, CaSR over-sensitive).
[^ety-familial]: **familial** — Latin *familia*, "household" + *-al* → running in families. **Differentiate:** *familial* (clusters in families) vs *congenital* (present at birth) vs *hereditary* (inherited through genes).
[^ety-hypocalciuric]: **hypocalciuric** — Greek *hypo-*, "under" + Latin *calx*, calcium + Greek *ouron*, urine + *-ic* → with low urinary calcium. In FHH the kidney's CaSR is also blind, so the thick ascending limb reabsorbs calcium avidly.
[^ety-hypercalcemia]: **hypercalcemia** — Greek *hyper-*, "over" + Latin *calx* + *-emia* → raised blood calcium (> 10.5 mg/dL): "stones, bones, groans, moans and psychiatric overtones".
[^ety-dehydrocholesterol]: **7-dehydrocholesterol** — Latin *de-*, "removing" + *hydro-*, hydrogen + *cholesterol* (Greek *cholē* bile + *stereos* solid + *-ol*) → cholesterol with two hydrogens removed (an extra double bond at C-7) — the skin's provitamin D3.
[^ety-ultraviolet]: **ultraviolet (UV-B)** — Latin *ultra*, "beyond" + *violet* → light beyond the violet end of the visible spectrum. **Differentiate:** *UV-A* (320–400 nm, tanning, ageing) · *UV-B* (≈ 280–320 nm, vitamin D synthesis and sunburn) · *UV-C* (< 280 nm, blocked by ozone, germicidal).
[^ety-cholecalciferol]: **cholecalciferol** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" (from chole-sterol) + Latin *calx*, "calcium" + *ferre*, "to carry" + *-ol*, alcohol → the cholesterol-derived calcium-carrier: vitamin D3. **Differentiate:** *cholecalciferol* (D3, animal / skin) vs *ergocalciferol* (D2, from ergosterol of fungi/plants).
[^ety-hydroxylase]: **hydroxylase** — *hydroxyl* (–OH: *hydr-* water + *oxy-* oxygen + *-yl*) + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that adds an –OH group at a numbered carbon: 25-hydroxylase (liver), 1α-hydroxylase (kidney), 24-hydroxylase (inactivating).
[^ety-calcidiol]: **calcidiol** — *calci-* (calciferol, vitamin D) + *di-*, "two" + *-ol*, alcohol → vitamin D with two –OH groups (C-3 and C-25) = 25-hydroxycholecalciferol, the circulating store.
[^ety-calcitriol]: **calcitriol** — *calci-* (calciferol) + *tri-*, "three" + *-ol* → vitamin D with three –OH groups (C-1, C-3, C-25) = 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol, the active hormone. **Memory:** di-ol → tri-ol: one more OH (from the kidney) makes it active.
[^ety-calbindin]: **calbindin** — *cal(cium)* + *binding* + *-in*, protein → the vitamin D-induced protein that binds and ferries Ca²⁺ across the enterocyte (calbindin-D9k) and renal DCT cell (calbindin-D28k).
[^ety-atpase]: **ATPase** — *ATP* (adenosine triphosphate: *tri-* three + *phosphate*) + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that splits ATP to power a pump; the Ca²⁺ ATPase pushes Ca²⁺ out of the cell into the blood against its gradient.
[^ety-phosphatase]: **alkaline phosphatase** — Arabic *al-qaly*, "ashes" → *alkaline* (works at high pH, ~ 10) + *phosphate* + *-ase* → an enzyme that removes phosphate groups at alkaline pH; from osteoblasts (bone ALP), liver, gut and placenta. **Differentiate:** *alkaline phosphatase* (bone formation) vs *acid phosphatase* (TRAP — osteoclasts; prostate).
[^ety-parafollicular]: **parafollicular** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + Latin *folliculus*, "little bag" (*follis*, bag + *-culus*, small) + *-ar* → beside the follicles: the C cells (C for calcitonin, or "clear" cells) lie between the thyroid follicles. **Differentiate:** *follicular cells* (T₃/T₄) vs *parafollicular C cells* (calcitonin).
[^ety-thyroid]: **thyroid** — Greek *thyreos*, "large oblong shield" + *-eidēs*, "like" → shield-shaped; named by Thomas Wharton (1656) after the shield-shaped thyroid cartilage it lies under.
[^ety-carcinoma]: **carcinoma** — Greek *karkinos*, "crab" + *-ōma*, tumour → a malignant epithelial tumour (Hippocrates likened the swollen veins of a breast cancer to a crab's legs). *Medullary* = Latin *medulla*, "marrow" — soft, marrow-like on the cut surface. **Differentiate:** *carcinoma* (epithelial) vs *sarcoma* (connective tissue) vs *lymphoma*.
[^ety-pregnancy]: **pregnancy** — Latin *prae-*, "before" + *gnasci*, "to be born" + *-ancy*, state → the state before birth. **Differentiate:** *gravid* (pregnant) · *gravida* (number of pregnancies) · *para* (number of deliveries).
[^ety-lactation]: **lactation** — Latin *lac, lactis*, "milk" + *-ation*, the process → making milk. **Differentiate:** *lactation* (milk production — prolactin) vs *let-down / milk ejection* (oxytocin) vs *galactorrhoea* (milk flow outside breastfeeding).
[^ety-analogue]: **analogue** — Greek *ana-*, "according to" + *logos*, "ratio, proportion" → in proportion, similar: a drug similar in structure and action to a natural hormone. **Differentiate:** *analogue* (similar molecule acting the same way) vs *agonist* (any molecule activating the receptor) vs *antagonist* (blocks it).
[^ety-salcatonin]: **salcatonin** — *sal(mon)* + *(cal)catonin* → salmon calcitonin, the British approved name; Latin *salmo*, "salmon", possibly from *salire*, "to leap" (origin uncertain).
[^ety-paraneoplastic]: **paraneoplastic** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + *neos*, "new" + *plasma*, "something formed" + *-tic* → beside the new growth: effects of a cancer at a distance, through hormones or antibodies, not by local spread or metastasis. **Differentiate:** *paraneoplastic* (remote, humoral) vs *metastatic* (by spread) vs *local* (direct invasion).
[^ety-syndrome]: **syndrome** — Greek *syn-*, "together" + *dromos*, "a running" → features that run together. **Differentiate:** *syndrome* (a recognised cluster, many causes) vs *disease* (one defined cause).
[^ety-malignancy]: **malignancy** — Latin *malignus*, "evil-natured, wicked" (*malus*, bad + *gignere*, to beget) + *-ancy*, state → the evil-natured state: a cancer that invades and spreads. **Differentiate:** *malignant* vs *benign* (*bene*, well + *gignere*: good-natured).
[^ety-peptide]: **peptide** — Greek *peptos*, "digested" (*peptein*, to digest) + *-ide* → a short chain of amino acids. PTHrP (parathyroid hormone-related peptide) mimics PTH at the PTH1 receptor.
[^ety-squamous]: **squamous** — Latin *squama*, "scale" + *-ous*, "full of" → scaly: flat, scale-like epithelial cells. **Differentiate:** *squamous* (flat) · *cuboidal* (cube) · *columnar* (column) epithelium; squamous cell carcinoma of lung is central and classically secretes PTHrP.
[^ety-osteocalcin]: **osteocalcin** — Greek *osteon*, "bone" + Latin *calx*, calcium + *-in*, protein → the bone calcium-binding protein (bone Gla protein) secreted by osteoblasts — a formation marker.
[^ety-collagen]: **collagen** — Greek *kolla*, "glue" + *-gen*, "producing" → glue-producing (boiled collagen gives gelatin); type I collagen is 90 % of the organic bone matrix. Its *pro*-peptides are clipped off at formation; its *telo*-peptides are released at resorption.
[^ety-tartrate]: **tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP)** — Medieval Latin *tartarum*, the crust ("tartar") inside wine casks → *tartrate*, a salt of tartaric acid + *resistant* → an acid phosphatase isoform (5b) that is not inhibited by tartrate — a marker of osteoclasts (and hairy-cell leukaemia).
[^ety-metalloproteinase]: **matrix metalloproteinase** — Latin *matrix*, "womb, source" (the extracellular scaffold) + Greek *metallon*, "metal" (zinc-dependent) + *protein* + *-ase* → zinc-requiring enzymes that digest matrix proteins such as collagen.
[^ety-cathepsin]: **cathepsin K** — Greek *kathepsein*, "to digest down, boil down" (*kata-* down + *hepsein* to boil) + *-in* → a lysosomal protease; K is the osteoclast form that digests type I collagen in the resorption pit.
[^ety-telopeptide]: **telopeptide** — Greek *telos*, "end" + *peptide* → the non-helical end pieces of a collagen molecule, joined by cross-links; N- and C-terminal telopeptides (NTX, CTX) appear in blood and urine when collagen is broken down.
[^ety-stimulus]: **stimulus** — Latin, "a goad, a pointed stick for driving cattle" → the change that prods a gland into secreting (↓ Ca²⁺ for PTH, ↑ Ca²⁺ for calcitonin).
[^ety-excretion]: **excretion** — Latin *ex-*, "out" + *cernere*, "to sift" + *-ion* → sifting out: what finally leaves in urine. **Differentiate:** *excretion* (out of the body) vs *secretion* (into the tubule or a duct) vs *reabsorption* (back into blood).
[^pre-homeo]: **homeo- / homoeo-** — similar, like: homeostasis. Don't confuse with **homo-** same, identical (homologous) · **hetero-** different.
[^suf-stasis]: **-stasis** — standing still, stopping: homeostasis, haemostasis, cholestasis. Don't confuse with **metastasis** (a change of place — *meta-* + *stasis*) · **-static** (bacteriostatic — stops growth).
[^pre-dis]: **dis- (di-, dif-)** — apart, away, in different directions: distribution, differentiation. Don't confuse with **dys-** bad, abnormal (dysplasia) · **di-** two (calcidiol) · **dia-** through.
[^suf-in]: **-in / -ine** — a protein, peptide or chemical substance: albumin, calcitonin, calbindin, osteocalcin, cathepsin, globulin. Don't confuse with **-ine** an amine or alkaloid (epinephrine, dopamine) · **-ase** an enzyme.
[^suf-ate]: **-ate** — a salt or ester of an acid: citrate, phosphate, oxalate, phytate, tartrate. Don't confuse with **-ite** a salt of an "-ous" acid or a mineral (nitrite, apatite) · **-ide** a binary compound or peptide (chloride, teriparatide).
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with: contraction, cotransporter, complex, congenital. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** opposite (countertransport).
[^pre-neuro]: **neuro- / neur-** — nerve: neurotransmitter, neuromuscular. Don't confuse with **nephro-** kidney · **neo-** new (neoplastic).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: neurotransmitter, cotransporter, transport. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **trans-** with **tri-** three.
[^pre-exo]: **exo-** — outside, outward (Greek): exocrine (secreting outward into a duct), exocytosis. Don't confuse with **ex-** out of (Latin: excitability, excretion) · **endo-** within (endocrine) · **ecto-** outer layer.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypocalcemia, hypocalciuric, hypoparathyroidism. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep · **sub-** below (Latin).
[^suf-emia]: **-emia / -aemia** — a condition of the blood: hypocalcemia, hypercalcemia, hypophosphatemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** a condition of the urine (phosphaturia) · **-penia** a lack.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — away, removing, reversing: depolarisation, dehydrocholesterol. Don't confuse with **di-** two (calcidiol) · **dis-** apart (distribution) · **dia-** through.
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, above, excessive: hyperexcitability, hyperventilation, hypercalcemia. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra- / super-** above (Latin).
[^pre-ex]: **ex- (e-, ef-)** — out of, away (Latin): excitability (rousing out), excretion. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek: exocrine) · **extra-** beyond (extracellular).
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a condition, often abnormal: alkalosis, osteoporosis, acidosis. Don't confuse with **-iasis** a disease state (lithiasis) · **-esis** a process (diuresis) · **-itis** inflammation.
[^pre-ab]: **ab- (abs-)** — away, from: absorption (sucking in from the lumen). Don't confuse with **ad-** to, towards (administration) · **a- / an-** without.
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, alongside, abnormal: parathormone, parathyroid, parafollicular, paraneoplastic. Don't confuse with **peri-** around (peritubular) · **pre-** before · **para** as a noun (number of deliveries).
[^suf-oid]: **-oid** — like, resembling: thyroid (shield-like), parathyroid, steroid. Don't confuse with **-ode** a path (electrode) · **-ose** a sugar.
[^pre-osteo]: **osteo- / oste-** — bone: osteoblast, osteoclast, osteocalcin, osteoporosis. Don't confuse with **ostium** an opening · **oto-** ear.
[^suf-blast]: **-blast** — bud, germ, building cell: osteoblast, fibroblast. Don't confuse with **-clast** breaker (osteoclast) · **-cyte** mature cell (osteocyte).
[^suf-clast]: **-clast** — breaker: osteoclast, (iconoclast). Don't confuse with **-blast** builder · **-plast** formed thing (chloroplast) · **-lysis** dissolving.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: resorption, reabsorption, receptor, related. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards · **pre-** before.
[^pre-inter]: **inter-** — between: intermittent, interstitial. Don't confuse with **intra-** within (intracellular) · **infra-** below.
[^pre-co]: **co- (con-, com-)** — together: cotransporter (two solutes carried together), cofactor. Don't confuse with **counter-** opposite (countertransport) · **contra-** against.
[^suf-uria]: **-uria** — a condition of the urine: phosphaturia, hypocalciuric, hypercalciuria. Don't confuse with **-emia** blood · **-uresis** urination (diuresis).
[^pre-peri]: **peri-** — around: peritubular, periosteum. Don't confuse with **para-** beside (parathyroid) · **pre-** before.
[^pre-ultra]: **ultra-** — beyond: ultraviolet, ultrasound, ultrafiltration. Don't confuse with **infra-** below (infrared) · **supra-** above.
[^pre-chole]: **chole- / chol-** — bile: cholecalciferol, cholesterol. Don't confuse with **chondro-** cartilage · **chloro-** green / chlorine.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: hydroxylase, ATPase, phosphatase, metalloproteinase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar · **-ate** a salt.
[^suf-ol]: **-ol** — an alcohol (–OH): calcidiol, calcitriol, cholecalciferol, cholesterol. Don't confuse with **-one** a ketone · **-ole / -ule** small.
[^suf-ule]: **-ule / -iculus / -icle** — small: follicle (little bag) → parafollicular, tubule → peritubular. Don't confuse with **-ole** (arteriole — also small) with **-ol** an alcohol.
[^suf-oma]: **-oma** — a tumour: carcinoma, adenoma, lymphoma. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition · **-stomy** a surgical opening.
[^pre-pre]: **pre- (prae-)** — before: pregnancy (before birth), precursor. Don't confuse with **pro-** forward, before (pro-peptide) · **post-** after · **peri-** around.
[^pre-ana]: **ana-** — up, again, according to: analogue (according to proportion), anabolic. Don't confuse with **an- / a-** without · **ante-** before · **anti-** against.
[^pre-neo]: **neo-** — new: paraneoplastic, neoplasm. Don't confuse with **neuro-** nerve · **nephro-** kidney.
[^suf-plasm]: **-plasm / -plasia / -plastic** — formed substance / formation: neoplasm, paraneoplastic, cytoplasm. Don't confuse with **-plasty** surgical repair · **-plast** a formed body (osteoblast is *-blast*, not *-plast*).
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together: syndrome (running together), synthesis. Don't confuse with **sin-** without (Latin *sine*) · **semi-** half.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, forward, precursor: pro-peptide, pro-PTH, procollagen. Don't confuse with **pre-** before (pre-pro-PTH carries both) · **post-** after · **proto-** first.
[^pre-telo]: **telo- / tele-** — end (*telos*) or far (*tēle*): telopeptide (end peptide), telomere. Don't confuse the two roots: **tele-** far (telemetry) · **telo-** end · **tela** a web (tela choroidea).
