# Chemical Regulation of Respiration

## Chemoreceptors
- Sense chemical changes of metabolism → O2 ↓, CO2 ↑, H+ ↑

### Central chemoreceptors
- Location → ventral surface of medulla (floor of 4th ventricle per page)
- Stimulated by → ↑ PCO2 (blood), ↑ H+ (CSF), hypotension
- MOA
  - ↑↑ CO2 in blood → crosses into CSF
  - H+ in blood → cannot cross
  - CO2 + H2O → carbonic anhydrase → H2CO3
  - H2CO3 → H+ + HCO3−
  - H+ → stimulates central chemoreceptors
- Applied → central respiratory depression / COPD
  - O2 therapy C/I
  - Hypoxia → only stimulus for respiration
  - O2 therapy → abolishes respiratory drive

### Peripheral chemoreceptors
- Location (neck)
  - Carotid bodies → carotid bifurcation
  - Aortic bodies (> 2) → arch of aorta
  - Together → glomus
- Types
  - Type I → glomus cells (O2 sensors)
  - Type II → glia-like supporting cells
- Stimulated by → ↓ O2 (blood), ↑ H+ (blood), hypotension
- MOA (glomus cell)
  - 1 Hypoxia (↓ PO2)
  - 2 O2-sensing K+ channel closes
  - 3 Depolarisation
  - 4 Ca2+ channel opens
  - 5 Influx of Ca2+
  - 6 Exocytosis of dopamine
  - 7 Stimulates respiratory centre
- Carotid body → dopamine (D2) → CN IX
- Aortic body → dopamine (D2) → CN X
- Applied → carotid body transplant tried for Parkinson's

### Asphyxia
- Fall in PO2 (hypoxia)
- Rise in PCO2 (hypercarbia)
- Rise in H+ (acidosis)
- → central + peripheral chemoreceptors activated
- → chemoreflex → hyperventilation
  - ↑ rate → ↓ PCO2, ↓ H+
  - ↑ depth → ↑ PO2

## Hypoxia
- O2 transport
  - Dissolved form
  - O2–Hb (reduced O2 content)

### Hypoxic hypoxia
- Seen in → high altitude, COPD
- ↓ dissolved O2 → ↓ PO2
- → stimulates peripheral chemoreceptor
- Rx → O2 therapy

### Anaemic hypoxia
- Seen in → anaemia, CO poisoning
- ↓ O2–Hb
- CO → ↑ affinity to Hb → defective O2 release
- → left shift of O2 dissociation curve
- Peripheral chemoreceptor → not stimulated

### Stagnant / ischaemic hypoxia
- Stasis of venous blood
- → ↑ O2 utilisation at tissue
- → ↑ arteriovenous O2 (AVO2) difference
- Peripheral chemoreceptor → not stimulated

### Histotoxic hypoxia
- Seen in → cyanide poisoning
- Cyanide → inhibits cytochrome oxidase
- → defective O2 utilisation → ↓ AVO2 difference
- Peripheral chemoreceptor → stimulated
  - Cyanide → most powerful stimulator
