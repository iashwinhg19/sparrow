# 🌊 Gastrointestinal Motility

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> GI **motility**[^ety-motility] is the movement of the gut wall that **mixes** food with the juices and **propels** it from mouth to anus. It is driven by a pacemaker — the **interstitial cells of Cajal**[^ety-cajal] with their **basal electrical rhythm (BER)** — and shaped by the autonomic nerves, gut hormones and local reflexes. The chapter answers:
> - ✅ **What sets the rhythm** (ICC, BER) and **what turns rhythm into contraction** (spikes)?
> - ✅ **How does each segment move food in the fed state** — deglutition, peristalsis, gastric emptying, segmentation, haustration, mass movement, defecation?
> - ✅ **What sweeps the empty gut in the fasting state** — the migrating motor complex (MMC)?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Metronome, a Conductor and a Street-Sweeper

The ICC are a **metronome**: they tick all the time (BER) but a tick alone moves nothing. Only when a tick crosses threshold does a **spike** fire and the muscle contract — and the autonomic nerves are the **conductor**, adding spikes (parasympathetic, ACh) or removing them (sympathetic, NE). After a meal each segment plays its own tune (swallow, peristalse, grind, segment, mass-move, defecate); between meals a **street-sweeper** (MMC, every 90 min) clears the whole tube.

```
ICC (pacemaker) → BER −60 ↔ −40 mV (no contraction) ── crosses −40 → SPIKES (Ca2+ in, K+ out) → CONTRACTION
                                                     PNS / ACh ↑ spikes ↑ force     SNS / NE ↓ spikes ↓ force
FED STATE   mouth: deglutition (oral voluntary → pharyngeal → oesophageal involuntary)
            oesophagus: peristalsis (ACh/SP contract behind · NO/VIP relax ahead) · LES: ACh = NO
            stomach: receptive relaxation → retropulsion → gastric emptying
            small gut: segmentation · colon: haustration + mass movement · rectum: defecation reflex
FASTING     MMC: stomach → colon, once / 90 min, 5 cm/min, motilin (Mo cells) · phase I quiet · II irregular · III regular
```

### Basal electrical rhythm (BER)

| **BER** | **Action potential / spikes** |
|---|---|
| • **Basal potential oscillating b/w −60 to −40.** | • **BER on crossing threshold (−40).** |
| • **No role in muscle contraction.** | • **Phases:** – **Depolarization**[^ety-depolarisation][^pre-de] **(D) d/t Ca²⁺ influx**[^ety-influx][^pre-in]. – **Repolarization**[^pre-re] **(R) d/t K⁺ efflux**[^pre-ex]. |
| — | • **Cause smooth muscle contraction.** |

![[Gastrointestinal Motility - BER and spike potentials.png]]
*BER oscillating between −60 and −40 mV ("restless membrane potential"); where a wave crosses the threshold (−40) an action potential fires — depolarisation (D) and repolarisation (R) (cropped from the handwritten page).*

🧠 *In plain words:* the BER is a slow wave that rolls through the muscle all the time. A wave that peaks below −40 mV does nothing; a wave that reaches −40 mV fires spikes (Ca²⁺ entry), and only spikes make the muscle contract. More spikes per wave → stronger contraction.

### Effect of Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)

![[Gastrointestinal Motility - effect of ANS on spikes.png]]
*Parasympathetic nerves (acetylcholine) add spikes on the BER → ↑ force of contraction and tension; sympathetic nerves (norepinephrine) reduce spikes → ↓ force and tension (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| **Parasympathetic**[^ety-sympathetic][^pre-para] **nervous system (PNS)** — **Acetylcholine**[^ety-acetylcholine] | **Sympathetic nervous system (SNS)** — **Norepinephrine**[^ety-norepinephrine][^pre-epi] |
|---|---|
| • **Increase in spikes.** – **↑ force of contraction.** – **↑ tension generated.** | • **Decrease in spikes.** – **↓ force of contraction.** – **↓ tension generated.** |

**Frequency of BER (oscillations/min):**

| Site | Frequency |
|---|---|
| **Duodenum**[^ety-duodenum] | **12** |
| **Ileum**[^ety-ileum] | **8** |
| **Stomach** | **4** |
| **Caecum**[^ety-caecum] | **2** |

- Highest in the duodenum, lowest in the caecum/colon; it falls from duodenum to ileum (the gradient that helps push contents aborally).[^3]

### FOOD IN ESOPHAGUS

**Peristalsis**[^ety-peristalsis][^pre-peri][^suf-stalsis]**:**

```
Mechanism:  Esophageal stretch ──► Release serotonin ──► Activates myenteric plexus ──► Starts peristalsis
```

- **Mechanism: esophageal stretch → release serotonin**[^ety-serotonin] **→ activates myenteric plexus**[^ety-myenteric][^pre-myo] **→ starts peristalsis.**

![[Gastrointestinal Motility - peristalsis physiologic and reverse.png]]
*Physiologic peristalsis (law of peristalsis, oral → anal): contraction behind the bolus pushes food forward (acetylcholine & substance P); relaxation ahead of it receives food (nitric oxide & VIP). Pathologic: reverse peristalsis (anal → oral) seen in vomiting (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| **Physiologic** — **Law of peristalsis**: oral → anal | **Pathologic** |
|---|---|
| **Contraction** (push food forward) **d/t acetylcholine (ACh) & substance P**[^5] | **Reverse peristalsis** (anal → oral) |
| **Relaxation** (receive food) **d/t nitric oxide (NO) & vasoactive**[^pre-vaso] **intestinal peptide (VIP)** | **Seen in vomiting** |

🧠 *In plain words:* the "law of the intestine" — squeeze behind, relax in front. Excitatory nerves (ACh, substance P) close the tube behind the bolus while inhibitory nerves (NO, VIP) open it ahead, so the bolus always moves towards the anus.

**Types:**

| Types | Movement | Seen in |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | **Progressive** | **Physiologic** (follows a swallow) |
| **Secondary** | **Progressive** | **Esophageal irritation** (food left behind distends the oesophagus) |
| **Tertiary** | **Non progressive** | **Esophageal spasm**[^ety-spasm] |

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Gut muscle contracts only when **spikes ride on the slow wave**: the ICC set the **frequency** (BER — duodenum 12, ileum 8, stomach 4, caecum 2 per minute) and the nerves and hormones set the **force** by adding or removing spikes. Every motor pattern is then built from one local rule — **contract behind, relax ahead** (ACh/substance P behind; NO/VIP ahead) — so the direction is oral → anal unless the pattern is reversed (vomiting).
>
> Speed is matched to the job: fast where food only passes (oesophagus 2–3 s), slow where it must be digested and absorbed (segmentation, haustration — hours). Feedback from the next segment holds back the previous one (the duodenum slows gastric emptying), and sphincters (LES, pylorus, IAS/EAS) open only when the balance of excitation and inhibition allows it.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> ICC pace slow waves; PNS adds spikes after a meal. Swallowing is voluntary then reflex (NTS); primary peristalsis carries the bolus in seconds; LES opens because ACh = NO; the stomach relaxes, grinds and empties under duodenal feedback; segmentation and haustration slow transit for absorption; mass movements fill the rectum and the defecation reflex empties it under voluntary control; MMCs sweep every 90 minutes between meals.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A partial loss is offset: early loss of oesophageal NO neurons is masked by gravity and secondary peristalsis; diabetic vagal neuropathy slows emptying but MMCs (motilin) still clear the stomach intermittently; low-fibre diet slows colonic transit but the patient adapts with straining.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Complete loss gives a syndrome: achalasia cardia (no NO → LES fails to relax → dysphagia to solids and liquids, regurgitation, "bird's-beak" oesophagus); diffuse oesophageal spasm (tertiary non-progressive contractions); gastroparesis (vomiting of undigested food); loss of the MMC → small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth; low fibre → raised colonic pressure → diverticular disease; rectal pressure > 55 mmHg → loss of voluntary control → soiling.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → loss of a pacemaker, nerve or transmitter (NO neurons, vagus, ICC) → compensation by gravity, secondary peristalsis or hormones → compensation fails → stasis or uncontrolled contraction → clinical syndrome
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Treatment reverses the missing half of the balance: Botox (blocks ACh) or myotomy/dilatation for achalasia, erythromycin (motilin agonist) or metoclopramide for gastroparesis, fibre for constipation and diverticular disease, imatinib for GIST (a KIT-driven ICC tumour).

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "The BER causes contraction"**
>
> **What students think:** Slow waves are the contractions.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The BER (−60 to −40 mV) has **no role in muscle contraction** by itself; only when it crosses threshold (−40 mV) do **spikes** fire (Ca²⁺ influx), and the spikes cause contraction. The BER sets the **maximum frequency** of contraction; spikes set its **force**.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Waves time it, spikes do it."
>
> **Trap 2: Achalasia is a spasm caused by too much ACh**
>
> **What students think:** The primary problem is excess cholinergic drive.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The primary lesion is **loss of the inhibitory NO/VIP neurons** of the myenteric plexus; the **ACh overactivity** is relative — unopposed. That is why Botox (blocking ACh release) helps by restoring the balance [ACh] = [NO].
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Start the chain where the page starts it: "absence of nitric oxide".
>
> **Trap 3: Which peristalsis is pathological**
>
> **What students think:** Secondary peristalsis is abnormal.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Primary (after a swallow) and secondary (triggered by oesophageal distension/irritation) are both **progressive** and protective. Only **tertiary** contractions are **non-progressive** — seen in oesophageal spasm and the elderly.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Progressive = useful; non-progressive = tertiary = spasm."
>
> **Trap 4: Mixing up gastrocolic, gastroileal and enterogastric reflexes**
>
> **What students think:** All "gastro-" reflexes speed things up.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Gastrocolic** (stomach → colon: ↑ colonic motility, defecation after meals) and **gastroileal** (stomach → ileum: ↑ ileal motility, ileocaecal valve opens) speed transit downstream; the **enterogastric** (page: "enteric gastro") reflex is the brake — duodenal acid, fat, protein or hyperosmolar content **inhibits** gastric emptying.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Read the name as "from → to": entero→gastric = intestine tells the stomach to wait.
>
> **Trap 5: The internal sphincter is under voluntary control**
>
> **What students think:** We control both anal sphincters.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The **IAS** is **smooth muscle, involuntary** — the PNS relaxes it, the SNS contracts it. Only the **EAS** is **skeletal, voluntary**, supplied by the **pudendal nerve**. Defecation proceeds only when the reflex relaxes the IAS **and** we choose to relax the EAS.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Internal = Involuntary; External = Elective."

> The myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus controls motility while the submucosal (Meissner's) plexus controls secretion; secretion and motility together make digestion possible.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Spinal Reflexes]]**
> The defecation reflex (sacral cord, pelvic parasympathetic outflow, pudendal nerve) parallels the micturition reflex; spinal cord lesions disturb both.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Characteristics of Nerve Fibres]]**
> Depolarisation by Ca²⁺ influx and repolarisation by K⁺ efflux, threshold and spikes are the general excitability concepts used for the BER.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Digestion and Absorption]]**
> Segmentation and haustration give the time needed for digestion and for absorption of nutrients, water and electrolytes; fibre gives SCFAs.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Achalasia, diffuse oesophageal spasm, GERD (LES tone), gastroparesis, GIST, constipation, diverticular disease and faecal incontinence are motility disorders explained by this chapter.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Classic one-liners: pacemaker of the gut (ICC), BER frequency highest in duodenum (12/min), ICC location in stomach (mid body along greater curvature), achalasia mechanism (absent NO) and treatment (Botox), MMC every 90 min regulated by motilin, phase III = regular maximal activity, EAS nerve (pudendal), oesophageal transit 2–3 s.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **ICC = pacemaker of GIT**, with a "restless" membrane potential (BER); in the stomach located in the **mid body along the greater curvature**; **GIST arises from ICC**.
> 2. **BER oscillates −60 to −40 mV, no role in contraction**; crossing threshold (−40) → **spikes: depolarisation by Ca²⁺ influx, repolarisation by K⁺ efflux → contraction**.
> 3. **PNS (ACh) ↑ spikes → ↑ force & tension; SNS (NE) ↓ spikes → ↓ force & tension**.
> 4. **BER frequency: duodenum 12, ileum 8, stomach 4, caecum 2 per min**.
> 5. **Deglutition**: oral (**voluntary**) → pharyngeal → oesophageal (**involuntary**); reflex: **CN V, IX, X afferent → NTS (medulla) → CN V, VII, XII efferent**.
> 6. **Peristalsis**: oesophageal stretch → **serotonin** → myenteric plexus; **contraction by ACh & substance P, relaxation by NO & VIP**; reverse peristalsis in **vomiting**.
> 7. **Primary & secondary peristalsis progressive; tertiary non-progressive (oesophageal spasm)**.
> 8. **Achalasia cardia: absence of NO → ACh overactivity → sustained LES contraction; Rx botulinum toxin**; normal LES: **[ACh] = [NO]**.
> 9. Stomach: **receptive relaxation (storage) → retropulsion (mixing & grinding) → gastric emptying**; GE ↑ by volume, gastrin, motilin, ACh, substance P, serotonin; ↓ by duodenal nutrients/acid/hyperosmolarity (**enterogastric reflex**), NO, VIP, **CCK**, dopamine, enkephalin.
> 10. **Gastrocolic reflex** (defecation after feeds in newborns), **amplified by gastrin**.
> 11. **Segmentation** slows food for digestion & absorption; small-gut motility ↑ by PNS, motilin, gastrin, CCK, serotonin, gastroileal reflex; ↓ by **SNS and secretin**.
> 12. **Haustrations** absorb water & electrolytes; **mass movement** → rectal distension → defecation.
> 13. **IAS smooth, involuntary (PNS relaxes, SNS contracts); EAS skeletal, voluntary, pudendal nerve**; rectal pressure **18 mmHg first urge, 55 mmHg voluntary control lost**.
> 14. **Transit: oesophagus 2–3 s**, stomach 2–5 h, small intestine 3–6 h, sigmoid 12 h; **fibre** (lignin, pectin, cellulose, hemicellulose) adds bulk, slows glucose absorption, lowers LDL, makes SCFAs, prevents diverticula.
> 15. **MMC: fasting, stomach → colon, once in 90 min (5 cm/min), motilin (Mo cells), clears debris & bacteria; phases I quiescent, II irregular, III regular maximal; erythromycin for diabetic gastroparesis**.

[^1]: The gastric pacemaker region lies high on the greater curvature in the mid-body, where ICC generate the fastest gastric slow waves (~3/min); waves spread distally towards the pylorus. ICC are found throughout the gut — between the circular and longitudinal muscle (myenteric ICC, the pacemakers) and within the muscle layers.
[^2]: GIST is the commonest mesenchymal tumour of the GI tract (stomach ~60 %); ~95 % express KIT (CD117) and most carry activating KIT or PDGFRA mutations — the same receptor that marks ICC. It responds to imatinib, a tyrosine-kinase inhibitor.
[^3]: Guyton gives slow-wave rates of about 3/min in the body of the stomach, 12/min in the duodenum and 8–9/min in the terminal ileum; colonic rates are 2–10/min. The page's stomach value (4/min) is kept in the body.
[^4]: Standard texts give the afferents of the swallowing reflex as V, IX and X (with the superior laryngeal branch of X) to the NTS and the swallowing centre of the medulla/lower pons, and the efferents as V, IX, X and XII (plus some VII). The page's efferent list "V, VII, XII" is kept in the body.
[^5]: Substance P (an 11-amino-acid tachykinin, von Euler & Gaddum, 1931 — "P" for the *powder* preparation) is co-released with ACh by excitatory motor neurons; NO (from nNOS) and VIP are the inhibitory transmitters. The "law of the intestine" (contract above, relax below) was described by Bayliss and Starling in 1899.
[^6]: Achalasia is caused by degeneration of the inhibitory (NO/VIP) neurons of the oesophageal myenteric plexus — idiopathic or in Chagas disease (*Trypanosoma cruzi*). The LES fails to relax and the body loses peristalsis; barium swallow shows a "bird's-beak". Botulinum toxin blocks ACh release (cleaves SNAP-25) and lasts months; Heller myotomy, pneumatic dilatation or POEM are definitive.
[^7]: The page writes "enteric gastro reflex"; the standard name is the **enterogastric reflex** — duodenal distension, acid (pH < 3.5), fat, protein breakdown products and hyperosmolarity inhibit gastric emptying through the enteric plexus, the vagus and prevertebral sympathetic ganglia; CCK, secretin and GIP add a hormonal brake.
[^8]: The intrinsic reflex (myenteric plexus) is weak; the strong parasympathetic defecation reflex runs through the sacral cord (S2–S4, pelvic splanchnic nerves). The EAS and puborectalis are supplied by the pudendal nerve (S2–S4). Straining (Valsalva) raises abdominal pressure. Spinal cord injury above the sacral segments leaves the reflex intact but voluntary control lost.
[^9]: Recommended fibre intake is about 25–30 g/day. Soluble fibre (pectin, gums) forms gels that slow glucose absorption and binds bile acids (↓ LDL); insoluble fibre (cellulose, lignin) retains water and adds bulk. Low-fibre diets raise intracolonic pressure and are linked to diverticulosis of the sigmoid colon.
[^10]: The MMC cycle lasts about 90–120 minutes in humans: phase I (40–60 % of the cycle) quiescent, phase II (20–30 %) intermittent contractions, phase III (5–10 min) regular contractions at the maximal rate for that segment (3/min in the stomach, 11–12/min in the duodenum). It is interrupted by feeding. Loss of the MMC predisposes to small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth.

[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — not, without: achalasia, atony, anaemia. Don't confuse with **ab-** away · **ad-** towards · **ana-** up, again · *anal* (Latin *anus*, ring — no prefix at all).
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — across, through: diaphragm, diarrhoea, diabetes, dialysis. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis-** apart · **dys-** bad.
[^pre-retro]: **retro-** — backwards, behind: retropulsion, retroperitoneal, retrograde. Don't confuse with **re-** again, back (repolarisation) · **pro-** forward (propulsion) · **post-** after.
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, above, excessive: hyperosmolar, hyperpolarisation, hypertonic. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra- / super-** above (Latin twins).
[^pre-en]: **en- / em-** — Greek "in": enkephalin (in the head), endemic, embolus. Don't confuse with **endo-** within (a longer, separate prefix) · **in-** (Latin "into" or "not").
[^pre-gastro]: **gastro- / gastr-** — stomach: gastrocolic, gastroileal, gastroparesis, gastrin. Don't confuse with **entero-** intestine · **gastrocnemius** (calf muscle — "belly of the leg", same root).
[^pre-hemi]: **hemi-** — half: hemicellulose (a "half-cellulose", shorter branched polysaccharides), hemiplegia, hemisphere. Don't confuse with **haem- / hem-** blood · **semi-** half (Latin twin) · **demi-** half (French).
[^suf-ulum]: **-ulum / -ulus / -ule** — small (diminutive): diverticulum, ventriculum, globule, tubule. Don't confuse with **-ium** a structure (epithelium) · **-oma** a tumour.
[^pre-inter]: **inter-** — between: interdigestive (between meals), interstitial, intercostal. Don't confuse with **intra-** within (intracellular) · **infra-** below · **intero-** inward (interoceptor).
[^suf-paresis]: **-paresis** — partial paralysis, weakness: gastroparesis, hemiparesis. Don't confuse with **-plegia** complete paralysis (hemiplegia) · **paraesthesia** abnormal sensation.
