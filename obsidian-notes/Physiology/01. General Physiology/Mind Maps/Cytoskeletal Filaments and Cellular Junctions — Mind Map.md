# Cytoskeletal Filaments and Cellular Junctions

## Cytoskeletal filaments
### Introduction
- Cyto = cell, skeleton = bone
- Support system → movement of cells, gives strength
- Types by size (diameter)
  - Microtubules → big
  - Microfilaments → small
  - Intermediate filaments → most abundant

### Microtubules
- Proteins
  - Kinesin
  - Dynein
  - Tubulin
- Axonal transport (AT)
  - Forward AT
    - Cell body → synapse (anterograde / antegrade)
    - Protein → kinesin
    - Fast 400 mm/day, slow 0.5–10 mm/day
  - Reverse AT
    - Synapse → cell body
    - Protein → dynein
    - Fast 200 mm/day
    - Invasion of microorganisms → rabies virus
    - Tetanus toxin → tetanospasmin
- Ciliary motility
  - Lung → clearing sputum (protective)
  - Embryo → rotation of internal organs
  - Structure → 9 + 2 microtubule arrangement
  - Dynein arms → cilia, sperm, fallopian tube motility
  - Kartagener (immotile cilia) syndrome → absent dynein
    - Bronchiectasis → sputum accumulates → infections
    - Infertility → no sperm / fallopian tube motility
    - Situs inversus → no rotation of organs
- Chromosomal movement in cell division
  - Tubulin in MTOC (microtubule organising centre)
  - Moves chromosomes to daughter cells in mitosis
  - Microtubule inhibitor drugs
    - Anticancer → vinca alkaloids (vincristine, vinblastine)
    - Anticancer → taxanes (paclitaxel)
    - Anti-gout → colchicine

### Microfilaments
- Proteins
  - Actin
  - Myosin
- Functions
  - Skeletal muscle contraction and relaxation → sliding filament theory
  - Cell motility → actin polymerization enhances motility
- Listeria
  - Internalins (A/B) → enters cell
  - Engulfed into vacuole (to contain spread)
  - Listeriolysin-O (LLO) → vacuole broken down
  - Act-A → actin polymerization
  - → comet tail pattern (polymerized actin)
  - → tumbling motility → infects nearby cells

### Intermediate filaments
- Cell specific → tumour markers
- Keratin (cytokeratin)
  - Epithelial cells
  - Squamous cell carcinoma marker
  - Mallory–Denk bodies → alcoholic liver disease
- Desmin
  - Muscle
  - Sarcoma → rhabdomyosarcoma
- Vimentin
  - Connective tissue → fibroblast
  - Tumours of mesenchymal origin
- GFAP (glial fibrillary acidic protein)
  - Astrocytes (star-shaped glial cells)
  - Glioma → astrocytoma, glioblastoma multiforme
- Lamin
  - Nucleus of cells
  - Progeria → premature ageing (Werner syndrome)

## Cellular junctions
### Types
- Cell–cell junction
  - Tight junction
  - Cell adhesive junctions → zonula adherens, desmosomes
  - Gap junctions
- Cell–basal lamina junction
  - Hemidesmosomes
  - Focal adhesions

### Cell adhesive junctions
- Cell adhesive molecules (CAMs)
  - Cadherins
  - Integrin
  - Selectin
- Structure
  - Primary protein
  - Linker protein
  - Cytoskeletal filaments
- Zonula adherens
  - Primary → E-cadherin
  - Linker → β-catenin
  - Filament → actin
  - Listeria internalins → recognise E-cadherin → invade cells
- Desmosomes
  - Primary → desmoglein, desmocolin
  - Linker → desmoplakin
  - Filament → tonofilaments (intermediate filament)
  - Abundant where mechanical stress → heart, skin, uterine cervix
  - Pemphigus → skin blistering
  - Autoantibodies against desmoglein

### Tight junction
- AKA zona occludens
- Proteins → occludin, claudin
- Location → blood–brain barrier, GIT, kidneys
- Claudin-16 mutation (kidney)
  - Familial hypomagnesemia hypercalciuria
  - ↑ Ca²⁺ excretion → renal stones
- Regulates paracellular movement (sideways / lateral)
  - Transcellular route → through the cell
  - Paracellular route → between cells via tight junction

### Gap junction
- Cells needing rapid communication → gap of 3–4 nm
  - Heart (cardiac muscle) → abundant
  - Nervous (nerve fibres)
- Proteins
  - Integral protein → connexon
  - Subunits → connexin
- Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease
  - Connexin-32 mutation
  - Neurons and nerve fibres → peripheral sensory neuropathy
- Idiopathic atrial fibrillation
  - Connexin-40 mutation
  - Affects heart

### Cell to basal lamina junction
- Hemidesmosomes → attached to intermediate filaments
- Focal adhesions → attached to actin
- Metastasis → cancer cells disrupt these junctions
