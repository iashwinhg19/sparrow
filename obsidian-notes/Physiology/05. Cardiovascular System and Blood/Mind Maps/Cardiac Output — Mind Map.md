# Cardiac Output

## Cardiac output
- CO = HR × SV
- Sympathetic stimulation → ↑HR → ↑CO
- Parasympathetic stimulation → ↓HR → ↓CO
- Circuits
  - Right heart → pulmonary circuit → lungs → oxygenation
  - Left heart → systemic circuit → body → venous return

## Determinants of stroke volume
### Factors affecting stroke volume (CAP)
- Preload
  - Determined by → EDV, venous return
  - Best marker → EDV
  - Factors ↑VR → calf muscle contraction, one-way valve, deep fascia
  - Deep fascia → prevents collapse of veins
  - Relation → ∝ CO
- Contractility (inotropy)
  - Determined by → ejection fraction
  - Best marker → EF
  - Frank–Starling law → length–tension relationship
  - ↑EDV → ↑↑ force within physiological limits → plateau
  - Relation → ∝ CO
  - Digoxin → positive inotrope → ↑contractility → ↑EF
  - Digoxin → used in Rx of heart failure
- Afterload
  - Determined by → TPR, aortic pressure
  - Best marker → TPR
  - Relation → ∝ 1/CO
  - Heart & kidney in parallel → kidney removed → ↑TPR → ↓CO

### Factors affecting force of contraction
- Bowditch effect / force–frequency / treppe
  - ↑HR → ↑ force of contraction
- Anrep effect
  - Abrupt ↑ afterload → ↑ force of contraction

## Measurement & distribution
### Measurement
- 1. Fick's principle
  - O₂ consumption = A–V O₂ difference × blood flow
  - CO = O₂ consumption / A–V O₂ difference = 5 L/min
  - Heart → highest A–V O₂ difference = 114 mL/L
- 2. Echocardiography → m/c, clinically used
- 3. Thermodilution → theoretical (page)
- 4. Dye dilution → theoretical (page)

### Distribution & O₂ consumption
- Liver → 1500 mL/min (highest) · O₂ 51 mL/min (highest)
- Brain → 750 mL/min · O₂ 46 mL/min
- Kidney → 1250 mL/min · 420 mL/100 g/min (highest per g)
- Resting skeletal muscle → 3 mL/100 g/min
- Heart → 250 mL/min (5 % CO) · O₂ 10 mL/100 g/min (highest per g)
- Cardiac index → CO per m² BSA → normal 3.2 L/min/m²

## Coronary circulation
- Heart gets most blood during diastole
- Subendocardium → most prone to ischaemia
- Myocardial O₂ demand ∝ HR, duration of systole, systolic BP
### Regulation of coronary blood flow
- 1. Nervous control → minimal role
- 2. Local factors → adenosine, NO, H⁺, metabolic end products → ↑ flow
- 3. Postural change → supine to immediate standing
  - Transient fall in BP (BP = CO × TPR)
  - VR ↓, CO ↓, CVP ↓
  - TPR ↑, HR ↑
  - BP maintained by baroreceptor reflex
  - ↑TPR → counters ↓CO

## Shock
### Types
- Hypovolaemic (m/c) → RTA → CO ↓
- Neurogenic → general / spinal anaesthesia → CO ↓
  - Sympathetic inhibition → vasodilation → ↓TPR
- Cardiogenic → cardiac disorders, eg tamponade → CO ↓
- Anaphylactic → bee sting → CO ↓
  - Histamine → ↑ capillary permeability → vasodilation
- Septic → bacterial infection → CO ↑
  - Septicaemia → ↑ body temperature → SA node activation

### Hypovolaemic shock stages
- 1. Non-progressive → compensatory mechanisms present
  - Sympathetic activation → norepinephrine release
  - Heart → ↑HR, ↑contractility, ↑CO, ↑BP
  - Kidney → afferent arteriole constriction → blood to heart & brain
  - ADH release → ↑ water reabsorption → ↑ blood volume
- 2. Progressive → without Rx → death
  - ↓ coronary blood flow
  - Cardiac deterioration → positive feedback
  - Clots in small vessels → ↓ blood flow to heart
- 3. Irreversible → death inevitable
  - Loss of energy sources
  - ATP → adenosine regeneration slow
  - Creatine (page: creatinine) phosphate
