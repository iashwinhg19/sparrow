# ⚡ Cardiac Action Potentials

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The heart makes **two kinds of action potential**[^ety-potential]. The **SA nodal (pacemaker[^ety-pacemaker]) potential** has no stable resting value — it drifts up by itself and fires, so it sets the **heart rate (chronotropy)**. The **ventricular[^ety-ventricle] action potential** rests at **−90 mV**, fires fast and then holds a long **plateau**, so it drives **contraction (inotropy)**. The **conduction system** carries each beat SA node → AV node → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres in about **0.22 s**.
> - ✅ Which cell fires on its own, and why? (funny current, T-type Ca²⁺)
> - ✅ Which channel carries each phase? (Na⁺, K⁺ₜ, Ca²⁺ₗ, K⁺ᴅʀ)
> - ✅ Where is conduction slowest and fastest? (AV node 0.04 m/s vs Purkinje 4 m/s)

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Dripping Tap and a Camera Flash with a Long Hold

The **SA node** is a tap that keeps dripping into a cup: the cup (membrane potential) fills by itself until it tips over (threshold −40 mV) and empties (fires), then starts filling again — that self-filling is the **prepotential**, and its speed is the heart rate. The **ventricular muscle cell** is a camera flash: it stays charged at −90 mV until something triggers it, then flashes (Na⁺) and holds the light on for a long time (Ca²⁺ plateau) before switching off (K⁺).

```
SA NODE  (pacemaker, CHRONOTROPY)          VENTRICLE  (worker, INOTROPY)
RMP: "restless" −60 → −40 mV               RMP: resting −90 mV
Ph 4  prepotential: Ca²⁺T in, Na⁺f in,     Ph 0  Na⁺ in        → fast upstroke
      ↓ K⁺ out   → self-depolarises        Ph 1  K⁺T out       → notch
Ph 0  Ca²⁺L in   → slow upstroke           Ph 2  Ca²⁺L in ≈ K⁺ out → PLATEAU
Ph 3  K⁺ out     → repolarises             Ph 3  K⁺DR out      → repolarises
(no phase 1, no phase 2)                   Ph 4  Na⁺–K⁺ pump   → back to −90 mV

CONDUCTION:  SA node (100/min) → AV node (delay 0.1 s, 0.04 m/s, slowest)
             → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres (4 m/s, fastest)  — total 0.22 s
```

## 💓 SA Nodal & Ventricular Action Potential

**Comparison b/w SA nodal[^ety-sinoatrial][^ety-node] & ventricular action potential:**

| Feature | **SA nodal potential (pacemaker)** | **Ventricular action potential** |
|---|---|---|
| **RMP** | **Restless membrane[^ety-membrane] potential = −60 to −40 mV**[^1] | Resting membrane potential = **−90 mV** |
| **Property** | **Chronotropy**[^ety-chronotropy][^suf-tropy] (heart rate generation) | **Inotropy**[^ety-inotropy] (contractility[^ety-contractility][^pre-con][^suf-ity]) |
| **Action potential** | Phase 4 → 0 → 3 (drawing below) | Phase 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 (drawing below) |

### 🔁 SA nodal potential — phase by phase

🧠 *The SA node never rests: as soon as it has repolarised, three currents start pushing it up again. That slow climb is what makes it a pacemaker.*

| Phase | What happens | Channel / current on the page |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 4** — **prepotential**[^ety-prepotential][^pre-pre] (−60 → −40 mV) | Slow spontaneous **depolarization**[^ety-depolarisation][^pre-de][^suf-ation] up to threshold | • **Transient**[^ety-transient][^pre-trans] **Ca²⁺ channel (Ca²⁺ₜ)** — **influx**[^ety-influx][^pre-in] · • **Funny current**[^ety-funny] **(Na⁺f)** — influx · • **↓ in K⁺ efflux**[^pre-ex] |
| **Phase 0** — depolarization | Upstroke | **Ca²⁺ₗ : long lasting Ca²⁺ channel** |
| **Phase 3** — **repolarization**[^ety-repolarisation][^pre-re] | Back down to −60 mV | **K⁺** (efflux) |

**Prepotential phase d/t:**
- **Transient Ca²⁺ channel (Ca²⁺ₜ) — influx**
- **Funny current (Na⁺f) — influx**
- **↓ in K⁺ efflux**

> [!info] Why only three phases?
> The SA nodal cell has **no phase 1 and no phase 2** — no fast Na⁺ spike and no plateau. Its upstroke is carried by the slow **L-type Ca²⁺** channel, so the action potential looks like a slow triangle, not a square.[^2]

![Pacemaker potential — the slow phase 4 drift to threshold, Ca²⁺ upstroke and K⁺ repolarisation (Wikimedia Commons)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7e/Pacemaker_potential.svg/800px-Pacemaker_potential.svg.png)
*Pacemaker (SA nodal) potential with its main ion movements — the same three phases (4, 0, 3) as the page's drawing (Diberri / Silvia3, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0).*

### 🔋 Ventricular action potential — phase by phase

🧠 *The ventricular cell is a "fast-response" cell: Na⁺ throws it up in a millisecond, then Ca²⁺ coming in balances K⁺ going out, so the voltage hangs flat for ~0.2 s — the plateau that makes cardiac muscle impossible to tetanise.*

| Phase | Name on the page | Current on the page |
|---|---|---|
| **Phase 0** | **Early depolarization** | **Na⁺** influx (fast) |
| **Phase 1** | **Early repolarization** | **K⁺ₜ : transient K⁺ channel** (efflux) |
| **Phase 2** | **Plateau**[^ety-plateau] | **Ca²⁺ₗ** influx in, **K⁺** efflux out — balanced |
| **Phase 3** | **Late / proper repolarization** | **K⁺ᴅʀ : delayed rectifier**[^ety-rectifier] **K⁺ channel** |
| **Phase 4** | **Resting membrane potential (−90 mV)** | **Na⁺–K⁺ pump** (Na⁺ out ↑, K⁺ in ↓)[^3] |

![Phases of the ventricular myocyte action potential — 0 upstroke, 1 notch, 2 plateau, 3 repolarisation, 4 rest (Wikimedia Commons)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Ventricular_myocyte_action_potential.svg/800px-Ventricular_myocyte_action_potential.svg.png)
*Ventricular myocyte action potential: the plateau (phase 2) is held up by voltage-sensitive Ca²⁺ channels, matching the page's Ca²⁺ₗ label (Wikimedia Commons; licence on the file page).[^4]*

### 🌀 Funny currents / hyperpolarized cyclic nucleotide channels (HCN)

🧠 *"Funny" because, unlike almost every other channel, it opens when the membrane becomes more negative (hyperpolarised) — exactly the moment after each beat, so the next beat starts automatically.*

- **Location:** Eye, Heart (SA node).
- **Opened by:** Cyclic GMP.[^5]
- **Function:** Vision, heart rate generation.
- **Applied aspect:**
  - **Ivabradine**[^ety-ivabradine][^pre-brady] **: Funny current blocker.**
    - Used in Rx of **arrhythmia**[^ety-arrhythmia][^pre-a][^suf-ia].[^6]
    - **S/E: ↓ visual acuity**[^ety-acuity].[^7]

### 🎛️ Effect of ANS on prepotential phase

| Division of the ANS[^ety-autonomic][^pre-auto] | Effect on the prepotential slope | Heart rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Sympathetic**[^ety-sympathetic][^pre-syn] **(+)** | Slope **steeper** → threshold reached sooner | **↑ HR : (+) chronotropy** |
| **Parasympathetic**[^pre-para] **(PNS) (−)** | Slope **flatter** → threshold reached later | **↓ HR : (−) chronotropic** |

## 🔌 Conduction System of Heart

**Dromotropy**[^ety-dromotropy] **: conduction**[^ety-conduction] **of impulses**[^ety-impulse].

![[Cardiac Action Potentials - conduction system impulse time.png]]
*Conduction system of the heart — SA node → AV node → bundle of His → Purkinje fibres; impulse time from SA node to the last ventricular fibre = 0.22 s (cropped from the handwritten page).[^9]*

### 1. SA node
- **Dominant pacemaker.**
- **Impulse: 100/min (highest rate).**[^10]
  - **Resting vagal**[^ety-vagus] **tone —(−)→ SA node → HR : 72/min.**
  - **Atropine**[^ety-atropine][^suf-ine] **(PNS blocker) —(+)→ SA node → HR : 150 bpm.**
  - **Heart transplant**[^ety-transplant] **(denervated**[^ety-denervation] **heart) —No effect→ SA node → HR : 100 bpm.**
- **Overdrive suppression**[^ety-suppression][^pre-sub] **:** suppresses impulses generated by other pacemakers in the heart.

🧠 *The SA node's own rate is 100/min, but at rest the vagus keeps braking it to 72. Remove the brake with atropine and it races (150); cut every nerve in a transplant and it settles at its own 100. Because it fires fastest, it keeps "resetting" every slower pacemaker before they can fire — overdrive suppression.*

### 2. AV node
- **Gatekeeper of heart.**
- **Physiological delay of impulse conduction : 0.1 sec.**[^11]
  - For adequate ventricular relaxation[^ety-relaxation].
- **Conduction velocity : 0.04 m/s – slowest (D/t ↓ gap junctions[^ety-junction]).**

### 3. Bundle of His[^ety-his]

### 4. Purkinje fibres[^ety-purkinje]
- **Innervates**[^ety-innervation] **every single ventricular myocardial**[^ety-myocardium][^pre-myo][^suf-ium] **cell.**
  - **Functional syncytium**[^ety-syncytium] **:** synchronised contractions.
- **Conduction velocity : 4 m/s – highest (due to ↑ gap junctions at intercalated**[^ety-intercalated][^pre-inter] **disc).**[^12]

```
SA node ──────► AV node ─────────► Bundle of His ──► Purkinje fibres ──► every ventricular cell
100/min         delay 0.1 s                          4 m/s (FASTEST)        (functional syncytium)
dominant        0.04 m/s (SLOWEST)
pacemaker       gatekeeper
        └──────────────────────── impulse time 0.22 s ─────────────────────────┘
```

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Every property of a cardiac cell follows from **which channels it owns**. The SA node owns **funny (Na⁺f) and T-type Ca²⁺ channels and few K⁺ leak channels**, so it cannot sit still — it drifts up every cycle and becomes the pacemaker; whatever changes the **slope of that drift** (sympathetic ↑, vagus ↓, ivabradine ↓) changes the heart rate. The ventricular cell owns **fast Na⁺ channels and L-type Ca²⁺ channels**, so it fires fast and then **holds a Ca²⁺ plateau** that both triggers contraction and makes the refractory period as long as the contraction itself — the reason the heart cannot be tetanised.
>
> Conduction speed follows **gap-junction density**: few in the AV node (0.04 m/s — the 0.1 s delay that lets the ventricles fill), many in the Purkinje system (4 m/s — so the whole ventricle contracts together as a functional syncytium).

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> The SA node fires at its intrinsic 100/min, held to ~72/min by resting vagal tone; it overdrive-suppresses all slower pacemakers. Each impulse is delayed 0.1 s in the AV node and then spreads through the Purkinje system so that every ventricular cell is activated within 0.22 s.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Mild disturbances change rate or conduction without symptoms: high vagal tone (athletes, sleep) flattens the prepotential → sinus bradycardia; sympathetic drive or fever steepens it → sinus tachycardia; slowed AV conduction (drugs, ageing) lengthens the PR interval but every beat still gets through.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> When the SA node fails (sick sinus) a slower latent pacemaker escapes (AV junction 40–60/min, Purkinje 15–40/min); when the AV node blocks completely the ventricles beat at their own slow escape rate — syncope (Stokes–Adams attack). Excess or abnormal currents (hypokalaemia, ischaemia) create ectopic firing and re-entry → tachyarrhythmias.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → altered autonomic tone / drugs / ischaemia → rate or PR change (compensated) → nodal failure or block → escape rhythm or tachyarrhythmia → low cardiac output, syncope
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Rate is treated at the channel that sets it: atropine removes vagal braking in bradycardia; β-blockers and ivabradine flatten the prepotential to slow the rate; a pacemaker replaces the SA node or bypasses a blocked AV node.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **SA nodal potential:** "Restless membrane potential", "prepotential", "no plateau", "automaticity" → phase 4 driven by Ca²⁺ₜ, Na⁺f (funny current) and ↓K⁺ efflux; upstroke by Ca²⁺ₗ.
>
> **Ventricular potential:** "Plateau", "−90 mV", "delayed rectifier", "transient outward K⁺" → phase 2 Ca²⁺ₗ vs K⁺; phase 1 K⁺ₜ; phase 3 K⁺ᴅʀ.
>
> **Funny current / HCN:** "Opens on hyperpolarisation", "eye and heart", "ivabradine", "phosphenes / ↓ visual acuity" → HCN channel block slows the SA node only.
>
> **Denervated (transplanted) heart:** "HR 100 at rest", "no response to atropine" → intrinsic SA nodal rate with no vagal tone.
>
> **AV node:** "Gatekeeper", "0.1 s delay", "0.04 m/s slowest" → few gap junctions; the PR segment.
>
> **Purkinje fibres:** "4 m/s fastest", "functional syncytium", "intercalated disc" → many gap junctions.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> A triangular potential without a plateau, rising slowly from −60 mV = SA nodal; a square potential with a long flat top from −90 mV = ventricular.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Escape rhythm rate tells the level: 40–60/min = AV junctional; < 40/min = ventricular (Purkinje) — the lower the pacemaker, the slower and less reliable the rhythm.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "The SA nodal upstroke is carried by Na⁺"**
>
> **What students think:** Every action potential rises because fast Na⁺ channels open.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The SA nodal cell sits at −60 to −40 mV, where fast Na⁺ channels are inactivated. Its phase 0 is carried by L-type (long-lasting) Ca²⁺ channels, which is why the upstroke is slow and why Ca²⁺-channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem) slow the SA and AV nodes. Na⁺ enters in phase 4 only through the funny current.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Node = Ca²⁺ upstroke; muscle = Na⁺ upstroke."
>
> **Trap 2: Confusing the two Ca²⁺ channels**
>
> **What students think:** "The Ca²⁺ channel" does one job.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Ca²⁺ₜ (transient) opens in the late prepotential and helps reach threshold; Ca²⁺ₗ (long-lasting) carries the nodal upstroke and the ventricular plateau. Questions ask which channel is responsible for which phase.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** T = Threshold (phase 4); L = Long (plateau, phase 0 of node).
>
> **Trap 3: Thinking the resting heart rate equals the SA node's intrinsic rate**
>
> **What students think:** The SA node fires at 72/min by itself.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Its intrinsic rate is ~100/min; resting vagal tone slows it to 72. That is why atropine raises the rate (to ~150) and why a denervated transplanted heart beats at ~100 and does not respond to atropine.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "72 = 100 minus the vagal brake."
>
> **Trap 4: Thinking the AV node is fast because it is important**
>
> **What students think:** The gatekeeper must conduct quickly.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** It is the slowest tissue (0.04 m/s) on purpose — few gap junctions create the 0.1 s delay so the atria finish emptying before the ventricles contract. The fastest tissue is the Purkinje system (4 m/s), which spreads the impulse to all ventricular cells at once.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Slowest = AV node (gatekeeper); fastest = Purkinje (syncytium).

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Membrane Potentials]]**
> The −90 mV of the ventricular cell is close to the K⁺ equilibrium potential; the Nernst and Goldman equations explain why the SA node, with fewer K⁺ leak channels, sits less negative.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Characteristics of Nerve Fibres]]**
> The nerve action potential has no plateau; comparing it with the ventricular plateau explains why nerve can be tetanised in effect (high firing rates) and heart cannot.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Cardiac and Smooth Muscles]]**
> The plateau Ca²⁺ triggers Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum; intercalated discs and gap junctions make the myocardium a functional syncytium.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[ECG and JVP]]**
> The surface ECG is the sum of these potentials: P = atrial depolarisation, PR segment = AV nodal delay, QRS = ventricular phase 0, ST = plateau, T = phase 3.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Cardiac Cycle]]**
> The AV nodal delay and the Purkinje spread time the mechanical events — atrial kick before isovolumetric contraction.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Regulation of Blood Pressure]]**
> The baroreflex acts on the SA node's prepotential through sympathetic and vagal outflow to change heart rate.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Antiarrhythmic drug classes map onto the phases: class I block Na⁺ (phase 0), class II (β-blockers) flatten phase 4, class III block K⁺ (prolong phase 3), class IV block Ca²⁺ₗ (nodal phase 0). Ivabradine blocks the funny current; atropine and isoprenaline speed the node.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Typical questions: the RMP of SA node vs ventricle; the ion of phase 0/2/3/4; the "funny current" and its blocker; HR after atropine vs after transplant; slowest and fastest conduction velocities; the AV nodal delay value.

[^1]: The SA node has no true resting potential: its most negative value (maximum diastolic potential, about −60 to −65 mV) is reached just after each beat and it immediately starts drifting up to threshold at about −40 mV — hence the page's word **restless**. Fewer K⁺ leak channels and a Na⁺ leak keep it less negative than ventricular muscle.
[^2]: At −60 to −40 mV most fast Na⁺ channels are inactivated, so the nodal upstroke depends on L-type Ca²⁺ channels — slower (about 1–10 V/s vs 200–300 V/s in ventricular muscle). That is why the SA and AV nodes are slowed by Ca²⁺-channel blockers (verapamil, diltiazem).
[^3]: Standard texts attribute the stable phase 4 (−90 mV) chiefly to the inward-rectifier K⁺ current (I_K1), which keeps the membrane near the K⁺ equilibrium potential; the Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase restores the ion gradients after each beat. The page labels phase 4 with the Na⁺–K⁺ pump.
[^4]: The plateau lasts ~0.2 s. Ca²⁺ entering through L-type (dihydropyridine) channels triggers Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, and the long plateau makes the absolute refractory period last almost as long as the contraction — so cardiac muscle cannot be tetanised.
[^5]: The page reads **cyclic GMP**. Standard texts: HCN channels open when the membrane is **hyperpolarised** and their opening is **facilitated by cAMP** binding (which is how sympathetic β₁ stimulation speeds the funny current); cGMP gates the related CNG channels of retinal photoreceptors. HCN channels also exist in the retina (I_h), which is why ivabradine affects vision.
[^6]: Ivabradine slows the SA node without lowering contractility or BP. Licensed uses: stable angina and chronic heart failure with sinus rhythm and resting HR ≥ 70–75/min (with or instead of β-blockers); also used for inappropriate sinus tachycardia. It is useless in atrial fibrillation (no sinus rhythm to slow).
[^7]: The typical visual side effect is **phosphenes** — transient brightness in part of the visual field — caused by block of retinal I_h (HCN) channels; usually mild and reversible.
[^8]: Noradrenaline (β₁ → ↑cAMP) increases the funny current and Ca²⁺ currents → steeper phase 4 → threshold reached sooner. Acetylcholine (M₂) opens K⁺_ACh channels (hyperpolarising the cell) and lowers cAMP → flatter phase 4 → threshold reached later.
[^9]: Guyton: the impulse leaves the SA node and reaches the last ventricular epicardial fibre about 0.22 s later; ~0.16 s of this is spent before the impulse enters the ventricular septum (atrial spread + AV delay).
[^10]: Intrinsic rates of the latent pacemakers (standard texts): SA node 90–100/min · AV node / junction 40–60/min · Purkinje fibres 15–40/min. Only the fastest one leads; the others are overdrive-suppressed.
[^11]: Guyton gives the total AV delay as ~0.13 s (≈ 0.03 s in the transitional fibres, 0.09 s in the AV node, 0.04 s in the penetrating bundle). The delay lets the atria empty into the ventricles before ventricular contraction — the page's "for adequate ventricular relaxation" means adequate ventricular **filling** while the ventricle is still relaxed.
[^12]: Conduction velocities in standard texts: atrial muscle ~0.3 m/s · internodal pathways ~1 m/s · AV node 0.02–0.05 m/s · bundle of His and Purkinje fibres 1.5–4 m/s · ventricular muscle 0.3–0.5 m/s.
[^ety-potential]: **potential** — Late Latin *potentialis*, from *potentia*, "power, ability" (*posse*, to be able) → stored ability to do work; the *membrane potential* is the voltage stored across the membrane. **Differentiate:** *action potential* (the brief all-or-none spike) · *resting potential* (the steady value between spikes) · *pacemaker potential / prepotential* (the slow drift towards threshold).
[^ety-pacemaker]: **pacemaker** — English *pace* (Latin *passus*, "a step") + *maker* → the tissue that sets the step (rate) of the beat; also the name of the artificial device that does the same job.
[^ety-ventricle]: **ventricle** — Latin *ventriculus*, "little belly" (diminutive of *venter*, belly) → a small hollow chamber; used for the pumping chambers of the heart and the cavities of the brain. **Differentiate:** *ventricle* (pumping chamber) vs *atrium* (receiving "entrance hall").
[^ety-sinoatrial]: **sinoatrial (SA)** — Latin *sinus*, "a curve, bay, hollow" (here the *sinus venosus*, the embryonic venous chamber absorbed into the right atrium) + *atrium*, "entrance hall" + *-al* → the node at the junction of the sinus venosus and the right atrium. Discovered by Arthur Keith and Martin Flack (1907).
[^ety-node]: **node** — Latin *nodus*, "knot" → a small knot-like mass of specialised tissue (SA node, AV node, lymph node, node of Ranvier). **Differentiate:** *node* (a knot of tissue) · *nodule* (a small node, *-ule* = little) · *ganglion* (a knot of nerve cell bodies).
[^ety-membrane]: **membrane** — Latin *membrana*, "the skin covering a limb", from *membrum*, "limb" → a thin covering layer; the cell membrane across which the potential is measured.
[^ety-chronotropy]: **chronotropy** — Greek *chronos*, "time" + *tropos*, "a turn, change" + *-y* → change in timing, i.e. heart **rate**. **Differentiate the cardiac "-tropy" family:** *chronotropy* (rate) · *inotropy* (force) · *dromotropy* (conduction speed) · *bathmotropy* (excitability, *bathmos* threshold) · *lusitropy* (relaxation, *lusis* loosening).
[^ety-inotropy]: **inotropy** — Greek *is, inos*, "fibre, sinew, muscle" + *tropos*, "turn, change" → change in the strength of the muscle fibre, i.e. **contractility**. *Positive inotropes* (digoxin, dobutamine) strengthen contraction; *negative inotropes* (verapamil, β-blockers) weaken it. Not related to *ion*.
[^ety-contractility]: **contractility** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *trahere*, "to draw, pull" (*contractus*, drawn together) + *-ility*, the capacity → the capacity to draw together, i.e. the intrinsic strength of contraction at a given length. **Differentiate:** *contraction* (the act) · *contractility* (the capacity, independent of preload and afterload) · *contracture* (a fixed shortening).
[^ety-prepotential]: **prepotential** — Latin *prae-*, "before" + *potential* → the potential that comes before the action potential: the slow phase 4 drift of pacemaker cells. Synonym: *pacemaker potential*, *diastolic depolarisation*.
[^ety-depolarisation]: **depolarisation** — Latin *de-*, "away, removing" + Greek *polos*, "pole, axis" + *-ise* + *-ation*, the process → the process of removing the polarity (the negative inside) of the membrane, so the inside becomes less negative. **Differentiate:** *depolarisation* (towards zero / positive) · *repolarisation* (back to the resting negative value) · *hyperpolarisation* (more negative than rest).
[^ety-transient]: **transient** — Latin *transire*, "to go across, pass by" (*trans-* + *ire*, to go) → passing quickly; the T-type Ca²⁺ channel opens briefly ("transiently"), unlike the L-type (long-lasting) channel. Also the *transient outward* K⁺ current (K⁺ₜ / I_to) of phase 1.
[^ety-influx]: **influx / efflux** — Latin *in-*, "into" + *fluere*, "to flow" → a flowing in; *ex-* (*ef-*), "out of" + *fluere* → a flowing out. Inward (cation) influx depolarises; outward K⁺ efflux repolarises. **Differentiate** from *reflux* (*re-*, back — flowing backwards, as in gastro-oesophageal reflux).
[^ety-funny]: **funny current (I_f)** — named "funny" by Dario DiFrancesco and colleagues (1979) because it behaved unusually: a mixed Na⁺/K⁺ inward current that is **activated by hyperpolarisation**, the opposite of most voltage-gated channels. Its channel is the **HCN** (hyperpolarisation-activated cyclic nucleotide-gated) channel.
[^ety-repolarisation]: **repolarisation** — Latin *re-*, "back, again" + Greek *polos*, "pole" + *-isation*, the process → the process of restoring the resting polarity (negative inside). Phase 1 = early (transient K⁺), phase 3 = late/proper (delayed rectifier K⁺). Contrast *depolarisation* and *hyperpolarisation*.
[^ety-plateau]: **plateau** — French, "a flat tray, tableland" (Old French *plat*, flat) → the flat top of the ventricular action potential (phase 2), where Ca²⁺ influx balances K⁺ efflux.
[^ety-rectifier]: **rectifier** — Latin *rectus*, "straight, right" + *facere*, "to make" → "to make straight"; in electricity a device that lets current pass more easily in one direction. *Delayed rectifier* K⁺ channels open late (delayed) and pass outward current — the main repolarising current of phase 3. **Differentiate:** *delayed rectifier* (outward, phase 3) · *inward rectifier* (I_K1 — holds phase 4 at −90 mV).
[^ety-ivabradine]: **ivabradine** — a drug name built on the INN stem **-bradine**, "bradycardic agents" (from Greek *bradys*, slow): the group of drugs that slow the heart by blocking the funny current.
[^ety-arrhythmia]: **arrhythmia** — Greek *a-*, "without" + *rhythmos*, "measured flow, rhythm" + *-ia*, condition → a condition without normal rhythm. Strictly *dysrhythmia* (abnormal rhythm) is more accurate, since most "arrhythmias" still have a rhythm. **Differentiate:** *tachyarrhythmia* (fast) · *bradyarrhythmia* (slow) · *ectopic beat* (from outside the SA node).
[^ety-acuity]: **acuity** — Latin *acuere*, "to sharpen" (*acus*, needle) + *-ity*, state → sharpness; *visual acuity* = sharpness of vision. Same root as *acute* and *acupuncture*.
[^ety-autonomic]: **autonomic** — Greek *autos*, "self" + *nomos*, "law" + *-ic* → self-governing; the autonomic nervous system works without voluntary control. **Differentiate:** *autonomic* (self-governing nerves) · *automaticity* (*automatos*, self-moving — a cell's ability to fire by itself, as the SA node does).
[^ety-sympathetic]: **sympathetic / parasympathetic** — Greek *syn-* (*sym-* before *p*), "together" + *pathos*, "feeling" → feeling together (Galen believed organs "sympathised" through these nerves); *para-*, "beside" → the system beside the sympathetic (craniosacral outflow, vagus to the heart).
[^ety-dromotropy]: **dromotropy** — Greek *dromos*, "running, a course" (as in *hippodrome*, *syndrome* = running together) + *tropos*, "change" → change in the speed of conduction through the heart. *Positive dromotropy* (sympathetic) speeds AV conduction; *negative dromotropy* (vagus, adenosine) slows it.
[^ety-conduction]: **conduction** — Latin *con-*, "together, with" + *ducere*, "to lead" + *-ion*, the process → the process of leading (an impulse) along. **Differentiate:** *conduction* (spread through tissue) · *conductance* (the ease of ion flow through a channel, the reciprocal of resistance) · *convection* (heat or mass carried by moving fluid).
[^ety-impulse]: **impulse** — Latin *in-*, "into, on" + *pellere*, "to drive, push" → a push driven onwards; here the wave of depolarisation travelling through the heart. Same root as *propulsion* and *expel*.
[^ety-vagus]: **vagus / vagal** — Latin *vagus*, "wandering" → the tenth cranial nerve "wanders" from the brainstem down to the abdomen; *vagal tone* = its constant resting braking action on the SA node. Same root as *vagabond* and *vague*.
[^ety-atropine]: **atropine** — from the plant *Atropa belladonna* (deadly nightshade), named after **Atropos**, the Greek Fate "who cannot be turned" (*a-*, not + *trepein*, to turn) and cuts the thread of life; *belladonna*, Italian "beautiful lady" (it dilates the pupils). A muscarinic (PNS) blocker → removes vagal tone → ↑HR.
[^ety-transplant]: **transplant** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *plantare*, "to plant" → to plant across (from donor to recipient). A transplanted heart is **denervated** — its autonomic nerves are cut.
[^ety-denervation]: **denervation** — Latin *de-*, "removing" + *nervus*, "sinew, nerve" + *-ation*, the process → the process of removing the nerve supply. **Differentiate:** *denervation* (nerves cut) · *innervation* (nerves supplied) · *enervation* (weakening — despite the spelling, not about nerves supply).
[^ety-suppression]: **suppression** — Latin *sub-* (assimilated to *sup-*), "under" + *premere*, "to press" + *-ion* → pressing under, holding down. *Overdrive suppression*: the fastest pacemaker keeps the slower ones from reaching threshold. **Differentiate:** *suppression* (held down, recovers when the driver stops) · *inhibition* (*in-* + *habere*, held in — blocked by a signal).
[^ety-relaxation]: **relaxation** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *laxare*, "to loosen" (*laxus*, loose) + *-ation*, the process → loosening back; the ventricle relaxes and fills during diastole. Cardiac relaxation is *lusitropy*.
[^ety-junction]: **gap junction** — *gap*, an opening + Latin *iungere*, "to join" + *-tion* → a joint with an opening: channels (connexons, built of connexins) that link the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells so current passes directly. **Differentiate:** *gap junction* (electrical coupling) · *desmosome* (mechanical anchoring) · *tight junction* (seal) — all three meet in the intercalated disc.
[^ety-his]: **bundle of His** — **Wilhelm His Jr.** (1863–1934), Swiss cardiologist and anatomist, who described the atrioventricular bundle in 1893; not to be confused with his father, the embryologist Wilhelm His Sr.
[^ety-purkinje]: **Purkinje fibres** — **Jan Evangelista Purkyně** (1787–1869), Czech physiologist, who described these large subendocardial conducting fibres in 1839; the same man gave his name to the Purkinje cells of the cerebellum and the Purkinje shift in vision.
[^ety-innervation]: **innervation** — Latin *in-*, "into" + *nervus*, "nerve" + *-ation*, the process → supplying with nerves; the page uses it loosely: the Purkinje network reaches (activates) every ventricular cell.
[^ety-myocardium]: **myocardium / myocardial** — Greek *mys, myos*, "muscle" (literally "mouse", for a muscle rippling under the skin) + *kardia*, "heart" + *-ium*, a tissue/structure → the heart muscle. **Differentiate the layers:** *endocardium* (*endo-*, inner lining) · *myocardium* (muscle) · *epicardium* (*epi-*, upon — outer layer) · *pericardium* (*peri-*, around — the sac).
[^ety-syncytium]: **syncytium** — Greek *syn-*, "together" + *kytos*, "hollow vessel, cell" + *-ium* → cells together as one; a true syncytium is a multinucleate mass with no cell borders (syncytiotrophoblast, skeletal muscle fibre). The myocardium is a **functional** syncytium: separate cells that behave as one because gap junctions couple them.
[^ety-intercalated]: **intercalated disc** — Latin *inter-*, "between" + *calare*, "to call out, proclaim" → *intercalare*, "to insert (an extra day) into the calendar" → something inserted between; the disc is inserted between two cardiac muscle cells, holding desmosomes and gap junctions. Greek *diskos*, a flat round plate.
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with, thoroughly: contractility, conduction, contraction. Don't confuse with **contra-** against (contraindication) · **counter-** against (countercurrent).
[^suf-tropy]: **-tropy / -tropic** — turning, change, affecting: chronotropy, inotropy, dromotropy; *-tropic* hormones act on (turn towards) a gland (thyrotropic). Don't confuse with **-trophy / -trophic** nourishment, growth (hypertrophy, trophic ulcer) · **-tropism** turning towards (phototropism).
[^suf-ity]: **-ity / -ility** — the state or capacity of: contractility, acuity, excitability, automaticity. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation (myocarditis) · **-ism** a state or doctrine.
[^pre-pre]: **pre- / prae-** — before (in time or place): prepotential, preload, presynaptic. Don't confuse with **pro-** forward, in favour of (prophylaxis) · **post-** after · **per-** through.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, away, removing, reversing: depolarisation, denervated. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart (dissection) · **dys-** bad, abnormal (dysrhythmia) · **di-** two.
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -isation / -ion** — the process or result of an action: depolarisation, repolarisation, conduction, relaxation. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition or state (fibrosis) · **-ism** a state or doctrine (metabolism).

[^pre-in]: **in- (Latin, "into")** — into, on: influx, impulse, innervation. Don't confuse with **in- (Latin, "not")** — inactivation, insufficiency, involuntary · **intra-** within · **en- / em-** in (Greek: endemic).
[^pre-ex]: **ex- (ef-, e-)** — out of, away: efflux, excitation, ejection. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek: exocytosis) · **extra-** beyond (extracellular).
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: repolarisation, relaxation, refractory, reabsorption (but not *rectifier*, where *rect-* is the root *rectus*, straight). Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards, behind (retrograde) · **pre-** before.
[^pre-brady]: **brady-** — slow: ivabradine (-bradine = bradycardic agents), bradycardia, bradykinesia. Don't confuse with **tachy-** fast · **brachy-** short (brachydactyly) · **baro-** pressure.
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — without, not: arrhythmia, anaemia, anoxia. Don't confuse with **ad- (a- before sc, sp, st)** towards (ascend) · **ab-** away from (abduction) · **ana-** up, again (anaphylaxis).
[^suf-ia]: **-ia** — a condition or state: arrhythmia, ischaemia, tachycardia, anaemia. Don't confuse with **-iasis** a disease process or infestation (lithiasis) · **-ium** a structure or tissue (myocardium).

[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, alongside, abnormal: parasympathetic, paracrine, paraesthesia. Don't confuse with **peri-** around (pericardium) · **pre-** before.
[^suf-ine]: **-ine** — chemistry suffix for alkaloids, amines and many drugs: atropine, ivabradine, adrenaline, histamine. Don't confuse with **-in** proteins and glycosides (insulin, digoxin) · **-ase** enzymes (ATPase).
