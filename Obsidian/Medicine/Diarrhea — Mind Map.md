# Diarrhea

## Definition and Physiology of Absorption
- Stool water > 200 mL/24 h
- Bristol stool chart
  - Type 1 & 2 → constipation
  - Type 3 & 4 → normal
  - Type 5 → lacking fibre
  - Type 6 & 7 → diarrhea
- Stages of absorption
  - Luminal
  - Mucosal → small intestine, maximum absorption
  - Post mucosal
- Macronutrients
  - Fat → most calorie dense; most specific nutrient affected in malabsorption
  - Carbohydrates
  - Proteins

## Frequency Classification
### Duration
- Acute → < 2 weeks (nuisance symptom)
- Persistent → > 2 weeks
- Chronic → > 4 weeks
### Acute diarrhea
- Infectious → viral m/c
  - Adults → norovirus
  - Children → rotavirus
  - Self-limiting
### Chronic diarrhea
- Osmotic → malabsorption
- Secretory → tumor, toxin
- Inflammatory → inflammatory bowel disease
- Malassimilation = maldigestion + malabsorption

## Pathological Classification
### Osmotic diarrhea
- Most consistent symptom of malabsorption
- Steatorrhea → hallmark of malabsorption
  - Pale, bulky, fatty, malodorous, greasy stools
  - Presents as diarrhea (most consistent finding)
- Pathology
  - ↓ fat absorption in small intestine
  - Unabsorbed macronutrients in colon → ↑ stool osmolality
  - Stool draws water from colonic epithelium → osmotic diarrhea
- Investigations
  - 72-hour fecal fat test → gold standard
    - Fat input 100 g/day
    - Normal < 7 g/day
    - Steatorrhea > 7 g/day for 3 days (D3, D4, D5) or excretion ≥ 7 %
  - Qualitative stool fat → Sudan III, Sudan IV, Oil red O
### Secretory diarrhea
- Toxins
  - ETEC, V. cholera → heat labile toxin
  - Enteropathogenic virus → rotavirus
- Tumours → VIPoma → WDHA syndrome
  - Watery Diarrhoea
  - Hypokalemia
  - Acidosis
- No structural damage
### Osmotic vs secretory
- Stool osmotic gap → increased > 100 vs normal/decreased < 50 (↑ stool Na + K)
- Response to fasting → improvement vs no change
- Stool pH → < 5.5 (fermentation → acids) vs > 6.0
- Osmotic gap = measured − calculated
  - Calculated = 290 − 2 (stool Na + K)
  - Normal 50–100 mOsm/kg
### Factitious diarrhoea
- Adolescent female, chronic diarrhea, fatigue, weight loss
- Unsupervised stool (mixed with water) → osmolality ~16 mOsm/kg
- Supervised stool → normal ~279

## Anatomical Classification
### Small intestinal diarrhea
- Etiology → osmotic; secretory (ETEC, Vibrio cholera)
- Large volume (↓ nutrient absorption), watery
- Frequency/urgency ↓
- Pus/blood/mucus, tenesmus, dyschezia, pain, fever → absent
- Cramps, bloating → present
- Weight loss → present if persistent
- Vomiting → maybe present
- Steatorrhoea → present
### Large intestinal diarrhea
- Etiology → invasive infection (Shigella); inflammation (ulcerative colitis)
- Small volume, watery
- Frequency/urgency ↑
- Pus/blood/mucus, tenesmus, dyschezia, pain, fever → present
- Cramps, bloating → present
- Weight loss, vomiting → rare
- Steatorrhoea → absent

## Approach to Diarrhea
### < 2 weeks → infection
- Small intestine (large volume) → viral m/c, ETEC, cholera → self limiting
- Large intestine (fever, pain, blood, mucus) → invasive organisms
  - Shigella
  - Non typhoid salmonella
  - Campylobacter
### > 4 weeks
- Small intestine
  - Osmotic → ↑ stool osmotic gap; improves on fasting
  - Secretory → VIPoma
- Large intestine → inflammatory → ulcerative colitis
