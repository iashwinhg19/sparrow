> [!abstract] Core Definition
> Embryology[^ety-embryo] is **geography plus history**: every adult structure is explained by **where its cells came from** (germ layer / mesoderm subdivision / neural crest), **how far they migrated or rotated**, and **what regressed on the way**. The anomalies in this note are all failures of one of those three steps.

## 🧠 Mental Model — Origin → Migration → Regression

```
WHERE FROM?           HOW DID IT MOVE?              WHAT SHOULD HAVE DISAPPEARED?
ectoderm / mesoderm   neural crest migrates         cervical sinus → branchial cyst
(paraxial /           midgut rotates 270° CCW       pleuroperitoneal canal → CDH
intermediate /        diaphragm descends C3–C5      right dorsal aorta → aberrant subclavian
lateral plate) /      arch arteries remodel         processus / umbilical ring → omphalocele
endoderm / NCC        ducts fuse (Müllerian)        Wolffian duct in ♀ → Gartner's duct
```

### Mesoderm — the tree in your notes

| Subdivision | Divides into | Gives rise to |
|---|---|---|
| **Paraxial mesoderm**[^ety-mesoderm] (somites)[^ety-somite] | **Dermatome**[^ety-tomes] | Dermis of the back |
| | **Myotome** | Skeletal muscle of trunk and limbs (except the exceptions below) |
| | **Sclerotome** | Vertebrae, ribs, annulus fibrosus |
| **Intermediate mesoderm** | **Urogenital system** | Kidneys (pronephros → mesonephros → metanephros), gonads, genital ducts, **adrenal cortex** ⚠ verify |
| **Lateral plate mesoderm** | **Somatopleuric** (next to ectoderm) | Body wall, limb bones and connective tissue, parietal pleura/peritoneum |
| | **Splanchnopleuric** (next to endoderm) | **Cardiac muscle**, **smooth muscle** of gut and vessels, visceral pleura/peritoneum, blood vessels and blood |

### Exceptions to "smooth muscle comes from mesoderm"

| Smooth muscle | Derived from |
|---|---|
| Sphincter pupillae, dilator pupillae and ciliary muscle (muscles of the iris) | **Neural crest cells** (via the optic cup — neuroectoderm)[^1] |
| Myoepithelial cells of sweat glands and mammary gland | **Ectoderm** |
| Smooth muscle of the gut, vessels, bladder | Mesoderm / mesenchyme (the rule, not the exception) |

## 🧠 Neural Tube

### Formation
Formed by **invagination of the ectoderm (neural plate) induced by the notochord**[^ety-notochord] → neural groove → neural folds fuse in the midline → **neural tube**, which sinks beneath the surface ectoderm. Fusion starts at the level of the 4th–5th somite and spreads cranially and caudally; the **anterior neuropore closes on day 25** and the **posterior neuropore on day 27–28**.[^2]

![Neural crest formation during neurulation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/Neural_Crest.png)
*Neural crest cells (green) arise at the crest of the neural folds — the junction of the neural tube and surface ectoderm — and migrate away as the tube closes.*

### Remnants of the notochord
1. **Apical ligament of the dens**
2. **Nucleus pulposus** (central part of the intervertebral disc)
3. **Tectorial membrane** (continuation of the posterior longitudinal ligament) ⚠ verify

> [!note] Intervertebral disc = 2 parts
> - **Outer:** annulus fibrosus — from **sclerotome**
> - **Central:** nucleus pulposus — from the **notochord**

### Derivatives — forms CNS structures
- Brain and spinal cord
- **Oligodendrocytes** (myelination of the CNS)
- **Astrocytes** (blood–brain barrier)
- **Ependymal cells** (lining of the ventricles)
- Retina and retinal pigment epithelium, posterior pituitary, pineal gland[^3]

## ⭐ Neural Crest[^ety-crest] Cells (NCC)

> [!info] Present at the **junction of the neural tube and surface ectoderm**. They migrate widely, which is why their derivatives are scattered through the body.

![Cranial neural crest cell migration](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e1/Cranial_Neural_Crest_Cells_-_migration.jpg)
*Cranial neural crest cells: where they originate, how they migrate into the pharyngeal arches and face, and what they become.*

| PNS structures | Derivatives of head & neck |
|---|---|
| Pia and arachnoid mater (leptomeninges) | Skull bones of the face (membranous) |
| Enteric plexus (Meissner's and Auerbach's) | **Dentine** (odontoblasts) |
| Schwann cells | **Pharyngeal arch cartilages** |
| Adrenal medulla (chromaffin cells) | Dermis of the head and neck |
| **Parafollicular C cells** of the thyroid | Ciliary muscle, sphincter and dilator pupillae ⚠ verify |
| **Melanocytes** | — |
| Dorsal root, cranial sensory and autonomic ganglia[^4] | |

> [!warning] Note: **Adrenal cortex → derivative of intermediate mesoderm** (the *medulla* is neural crest). Likewise the **dura mater is mesodermal**, only pia–arachnoid are neural crest.

## 🫁 Development of the Diaphragm

| | Structure | Derivative in the adult diaphragm |
|---|---|---|
| a | **Septum transversum**[^ety-septum] | **Central tendon** |
| b | **Pleuroperitoneal membranes**[^ety-pleuroperitoneal] | Posterolateral (dome) parts — the part that fails in CDH |
| c | **Dorsal mesentery of the oesophagus** | **Crura** of the diaphragm |
| d | **Body wall mesoderm** (muscular ingrowth from the lateral body wall) | Peripheral muscular rim |

![Diaphragm development in the embryo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0d/Diaphragma-embryo.png)
*The four embryonic contributions to the diaphragm seen from below: septum transversum in front, pleuroperitoneal membranes posterolaterally, oesophageal mesentery in the midline behind, body-wall muscle around the rim.*

> [!tip] Why is the phrenic nerve C3, C4, C5?
> The septum transversum forms opposite cervical segments 3–5 and then **descends** to the level of L1 as the embryo folds, dragging its nerve supply with it — "C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive".

### Applied anatomy — Congenital diaphragmatic hernia
- Occurs due to **absence (non-closure) of the pleuroperitoneal membrane**
- ↓
- **Persistence of the pleuroperitoneal canal** (**Bochdalek foramen**[^ety-bochdalek], posterolateral)
- ↓
- **Intestinal herniation** into the thorax
  - **Left > right** (the right canal closes earlier and the liver buttresses it)
  - Complication: **lung hypoplasia** (the main cause of death), scaphoid abdomen, bowel sounds in the chest[^5]

## 🐟 Pharyngeal[^ety-pharyngeal] Arches

> [!abstract] Each arch is a **core of mesoderm (with neural crest mesenchyme), lined by ectoderm on the outside and endoderm on the inside**. The outside groove between arches is a **cleft**; the inside pocket is a **pouch**; where they meet is a **membrane**.

![Scheme of the pharyngeal arch (redrawn from Gray's)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/56/PharyngealArchHuman.jpg)
*Pharyngeal arch scheme: pouch (endoderm) inside, cleft (ectoderm) outside, mesoderm core — the picture in your notes with "Pouch → Endoderm / Mesoderm / Ectoderm / Cleft".*

![Pharyngeal arches in embryonic development](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/04/Pharyngeal_arches.jpg)
*Pharyngeal arches of the embryo (Journal of Anatomy 2023, CC BY 4.0).*

### Pharyngeal clefts (ectoderm)
- **1st cleft** — the **only cleft that persists**
  - Derivatives: **external auditory meatus** and the **outer (epithelial) layer of the tympanic membrane**
- **Cervical sinus**[^ety-cervical-sinus]
  - Formed by overgrowth of the **2nd arch** ectoderm, which buries the 2nd, 3rd and 4th clefts
  - Normally **obliterates** the 2nd–4th clefts

> [!danger] Applied anatomy — persistence of the cervical sinus
> **Branchial cyst / fistula**, classically along the **anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid** (at the junction of the upper ⅓ and lower ⅔ for a cyst; a fistula opens lower, at the junction of the middle and lower thirds, and tracks up between the internal and external carotid arteries to the tonsillar fossa — the 2nd pouch).[^6]

![Branchial cleft cyst in the neck](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/95/Right_Branchial_Cleft_Cyst_prior_to_excision.jpg)
*Right branchial cleft cyst before excision — a smooth swelling along the anterior border of sternocleidomastoid (Wikimedia Commons, own work of the uploader).*

### Pharyngeal pouches (endoderm)

| Pouch | Derivatives |
|---|---|
| **1** | Inner layer of the tympanic membrane · **tympanic cavity** (middle ear) · **auditory (Eustachian) tube** · mastoid antrum |
| **2** | **Palatine tonsil** (crypts; the lymphoid tissue is mesenchymal) |
| **3** | **Thymus**[^ety-thymus] · **Inferior parathyroid**[^ety-parathyroid] |
| **4** | **Superior parathyroid** |
| Remnant of 5th pouch (ultimobranchial body)[^ety-ultimobranchial] | **Ultimobranchial body** → parafollicular C cells |

> [!note] C cells are derived from **neural crest cells that migrate into the ultimobranchial body**, not from the endoderm itself.

> [!danger] Applied anatomy — DiGeorge syndrome[^ety-digeorge]
> - Anomaly of the **3rd and 4th pouches**
> - **22q11.2 deletion** syndrome (CATCH-22: **C**ardiac defects, **A**bnormal facies, **T**hymic aplasia, **C**left palate, **H**ypocalcaemia)
> - **Absent thymus** (T-cell deficiency) and **hypoparathyroidism** (hypocalcaemia, tetany)[^7]

![DiGeorge syndrome — characteristic facies](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cb/DiGeorge_syndrome1.jpg)
*DiGeorge syndrome: low-set ears, hypertelorism, short philtrum and micrognathia (image: Prof Victor Grech, free licence).*

### Pharyngeal arch cartilages

| Arch | Cartilage | Derived structures |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Meckel's cartilage**[^ety-meckel] | **Malleus**, **incus** · anterior ligament of malleus · sphenomandibular ligament · template for the **mandible** (which ossifies in membrane around it) |
| **2** | **Reichert's cartilage**[^ety-reichert] | **Stapes** · **styloid process** · **stylohyoid ligament** · **lesser cornu** and upper part of the body of the hyoid |
| **3** | — | **Greater cornu** and lower part of the **body of the hyoid** |
| **4 & 6** | — | **Laryngeal cartilages** (thyroid, cricoid, arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform)[^8] |

> [!tip] Arch → nerve → muscles (the companion table your notes imply)
> 1 → **V3** (muscles of mastication, tensors, mylohyoid, anterior belly of digastric) · 2 → **VII** (facial expression, stapedius, stylohyoid, posterior belly of digastric) · 3 → **IX** (stylopharyngeus) · 4 → **superior laryngeal branch of X** (cricothyroid, pharyngeal constrictors, levator veli palatini) · 6 → **recurrent laryngeal branch of X** (all other intrinsic laryngeal muscles).

### Aortic arch anomalies

| Anomaly | Embryological error | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Aberrant (retro-oesophageal) right subclavian artery** | Obliteration of the **right 4th arch artery** → persistence of the caudal part of the **right dorsal aorta** | Right subclavian arises last from the arch and passes **behind the oesophagus** → **dysphagia lusoria**[^ety-lusoria]; the right RLN is **non-recurrent** |
| **Double aortic arch** | Persistence of **both 4th arch arteries and both dorsal aortae** | **Vascular ring** around trachea and oesophagus → stridor, dysphagia |
| Right aortic arch | Left dorsal aorta regresses instead of the right | Arch over the right bronchus; ± vascular ring with left ligamentum arteriosum |

## 🔄 Midgut

> [!info] Normal rotation: the midgut loop herniates into the umbilical cord in week 6, rotates **270° counter-clockwise around the superior mesenteric artery** (90° outside, 180° on return in week 10) and the caecum descends to the right iliac fossa.

![Intestinal rotation and physiological herniation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2e/Intestinal_rotation_and_herniation.jpg)
*Physiological herniation and rotation of the midgut loop around the superior mesenteric artery.*

### Rotation anomalies

| Anomaly | Defect in rotation | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-rotation** | Loop returns **without rotating** beyond the first 90° | **Small intestine on the right**, **large intestine on the left** ("left-sided colon"); narrow mesenteric base → volvulus |
| **Reversed rotation** | Normal first rotation then **clockwise** (−90°) instead of counter-clockwise | **Transverse colon passes behind the superior mesenteric artery** (duodenum in front) ⚠ verify |
| **Mixed rotation (malrotation)** | Rotation stops at 180°; caecum fails to descend | **Subhepatic caecum**; **Ladd's bands**[^ety-ladd] from the caecum cross the duodenum → obstruction; narrow base → volvulus |
| **Incomplete rotation** | Caecum does not complete its descent | **Subhepatic caecum**, appendix under the liver ("subhepatic appendix") |

## 👶 Omphalocele vs Gastroschisis

| | Omphalocele[^ety-omphalocele] | Gastroschisis[^ety-gastroschisis] |
|---|---|---|
| **Defect** | **Failure of reduction of the physiological hernia** (midgut does not return by week 10) — herniation **through the umbilical ring** | **Defect of the anterior abdominal wall lateral (usually right) to the umbilicus** — incomplete lateral folding / involution of the right umbilical vein ⚠ verify |
| **Covering** | **Covered** by amnion + peritoneum (membrane); cord inserts on the sac | **No covering** — bowel free in amniotic fluid, thickened and matted |
| **Associations** | **Associated with multiple anomalies** (cardiac, chromosomal — trisomy 13/18, Beckwith–Wiedemann) → **high mortality** | Few anomalies; main problem is **intestinal obstruction / atresia** and fluid loss; better survival |
| **AFP** | Raised | Raised (higher) |

*Gastroschisis: uncovered loops of bowel to the right of an intact umbilical cord (CDC, public domain).*

## ⚧ Genital Duct Derivatives

> [!info] All from **intermediate mesoderm**: the **mesonephric (Wolffian) duct**[^ety-wolffian], the **paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct**[^ety-mullerian] and the **genital ridge**.

![Gray's Anatomy — primitive urogenital organs before sexual differentiation](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6d/Gray1109.png)
*Gray's Anatomy (1918): the indifferent stage — genital ridge, mesonephros, mesonephric (Wolffian) duct and paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct side by side.*

![Relation of the Wolffian duct and the duct of Müller in the female embryo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7a/Relation_of_the_Wolffian_Duct_and_the_Duct_of_M%C3%BCller_in_the_Female_Embryo.jpg)
*Female embryo: the Müllerian ducts fuse in the midline to form the uterus while the Wolffian ducts regress (Kollmann, 1901).*

| | Males | Females |
|---|---|---|
| **Mesonephric (Wolffian) duct** | **Epididymis**, **vas deferens**, **seminal vesicle**, **ejaculatory duct**; **ureteric bud** → ureter, pelvis, calyces, collecting ducts; **trigone** of the bladder ⚠ verify | **Remnants:** **Gartner's duct**[^ety-gartner] (cyst in the anterolateral vaginal wall), **epoophoron**, **paroophoron**; ureteric bud and trigone as in the male |
| | **Remnant:** appendix of the epididymis ⚠ verify | |
| **Paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct** | **Remnants:** **appendix of the testis**, **prostatic utricle** | **Fallopian tubes**, **uterus**, **cervix**, **upper ⅓ of the vagina**[^11] |
| **Genital ridge** | **Testis** | **Ovary** |

> [!tip] The switch
> **SRY → testis → Sertoli cells make AMH (MIS) → Müllerian ducts regress; Leydig cells make testosterone → Wolffian ducts persist.** No testis → no AMH, no testosterone → Müllerian ducts persist, Wolffian ducts regress (the female pattern is the default).

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Every structure in this note is explained by three questions: **where did the cells come from, how far did they travel, and what was supposed to regress?** Origin fixes identity (paraxial mesoderm → vertebrae, neural crest → everything that migrates: melanocytes, C cells, arch cartilages, adrenal medulla), migration and rotation fix position (the diaphragm's C3–C5 nerve, the 270° midgut turn, the recurrent laryngeal nerves hooked under the last arch artery that survived), and programmed regression fixes patency (cervical sinus, pleuroperitoneal canal, right dorsal aorta, Wolffian ducts in the female, the umbilical hernia).
>
> Clinical anomalies are therefore never random: a branchial cyst is a sinus that did not close, CDH is a canal that did not close, dysphagia lusoria is an arch that did not disappear, malrotation is a turn that stopped early, and Gartner's cyst is a duct that did not regress. Ask which of the three steps failed and the whole presentation, side-predilection and associated findings fall out.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Weeks 3–4: the trilaminar disc folds, the notochord induces the neural tube, neural crest leaves the folds, mesoderm splits into paraxial/intermediate/lateral plate, and the pharyngeal arches, arch arteries, paired veins and septum transversum all form in their original cranial positions. Weeks 5–10: descent, rotation, fusion and selective regression convert this symmetric plan into the adult asymmetric anatomy.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A single step fails but the embryo compensates: the cervical sinus stays as a silent epithelial pocket; a Bochdalek defect lets a little bowel sit in the chest while the lung still grows; a persistent right dorsal aorta leaves a subclavian running behind the oesophagus that carries blood normally; a midgut that stopped at 180° drains and absorbs normally on a narrow stalk. These patients are often asymptomatic at birth; the anomaly is found incidentally (a neck lump in adolescence, a tracheal indentation on a barium swallow, an unusual position of the caecum at surgery).
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> The anatomical flaw meets a physiological load: the buried sinus fills and infects (branchial cyst/fistula); herniated bowel compresses the developing lung until it is hypoplastic and the newborn cannot oxygenate (CDH with respiratory distress, scaphoid abdomen); a solid bolus catches on the retro-oesophageal artery (dysphagia lusoria) or a double arch rings the trachea (stridor); the narrow-based midgut twists (volvulus) or Ladd's bands obstruct the duodenum (bilious vomiting on day 1–5); a 3rd/4th pouch failure declares itself as neonatal tetany and recurrent infections (DiGeorge).
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal plan (wk 3–4) → failed closure / regression / rotation (wk 5–10) → silent anatomical variant → load or infection → clinical syndrome (neonatal or adolescent)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Recognising the embryological step tells you the side (CDH and sliding structures favour the left; aberrant subclavian is right-sided), the associated lesions (omphalocele brings chromosomal and cardiac defects, gastroschisis brings bowel atresia) and the operation (a branchial fistula must be traced between the carotids to the tonsil; a malrotation needs Ladd's procedure, not just detorsion; CDH repair must wait for lung stabilisation).

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Branchial cyst / fistula:** "Painless cystic swelling at the anterior border of sternocleidomastoid in a young adult; fistula tracking between internal and external carotid to the tonsillar fossa" → persistent cervical sinus (2nd arch overgrowth failed to obliterate the 2nd–4th clefts); the fistula follows the 2nd cleft/pouch path.
>
> **DiGeorge syndrome:** "Neonatal tetany + recurrent viral/fungal infections + conotruncal heart defect + abnormal facies" → 3rd and 4th pouch failure (no thymus, no parathyroids), 22q11.2 deletion; CATCH-22.
>
> **Congenital diaphragmatic hernia:** "Newborn with respiratory distress, scaphoid abdomen, bowel sounds in the left chest, mediastinal shift to the right" → persistent pleuroperitoneal canal (Bochdalek, posterolateral, left); death is from lung hypoplasia.
>
> **Dysphagia lusoria:** "Dysphagia to solids, barium swallow shows an oblique posterior indentation of the oesophagus" → aberrant right subclavian artery from persistent right dorsal aorta; also a non-recurrent right laryngeal nerve.
>
> **Double aortic arch:** "Infant with stridor and dysphagia, vascular ring on CT" → both 4th arches and dorsal aortae persisted.
>
> **Malrotation with volvulus:** "Bilious vomiting in the first week, 'corkscrew' duodenum on upper GI contrast, caecum under the liver, Ladd's bands" → midgut rotation stopped at 180°.
>
> **Omphalocele:** "Midline sac with cord inserting at its apex, liver inside, trisomy 18 / Beckwith–Wiedemann" → failure of the physiological hernia to return.
>
> **Gastroschisis:** "Right para-umbilical defect, uncovered matted bowel, intact cord, isolated anomaly" → abdominal wall defect, not a hernia.
>
> **Gartner's duct cyst:** "Cyst in the anterolateral vaginal wall" → Wolffian duct remnant in the female.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Psammoma-free "Schwann cell, melanocyte, chromaffin cell" tumours (schwannoma, melanoma, phaeochromocytoma) share a neural crest origin. CXR: bowel gas in the left hemithorax with a nasogastric tube curving into the chest → CDH. Upper GI series: duodenojejunal flexure right of midline → malrotation. Lateral neck: a fistula opening low on the anterior border of SCM → 2nd branchial fistula.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> CDH: liver-up position and lung-to-head ratio < 1 on antenatal scan = worst prognosis. Omphalocele: giant (> 5 cm, liver-containing) and chromosomal association = highest mortality; gastroschisis survival > 90%. Malrotation: bilious vomiting = volvulus until proven otherwise → emergency laparotomy.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "Everything in the adrenal is neural crest"**
>
> **What students think:** The adrenal gland is one organ, so it has one origin.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The adrenal **medulla** (chromaffin cells) is neural crest, like the sympathetic ganglia it resembles; the adrenal **cortex** is intermediate mesoderm, like the gonads and kidneys next to it. The same split applies to the thyroid: follicular cells are endoderm (thyroglossal duct), parafollicular C cells are neural crest via the ultimobranchial body.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Medulla migrates (crest), cortex stays (mesoderm)." Any cell that secretes catecholamines, pigment or calcitonin is a migrant — neural crest.
>
> **Trap 2: Swapping the inferior and superior parathyroids**
>
> **What students think:** The 3rd pouch is higher than the 4th, so it must give the superior parathyroid.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The 3rd pouch also gives the thymus, which descends into the mediastinum and drags its parathyroid down below the 4th pouch's gland. So 3rd → **inferior** parathyroid, 4th → **superior** parathyroid. This is also why the inferior parathyroids are the variable ones (ectopic, intrathymic).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "3 goes down with the thymus; 4 stays up."
>
> **Trap 3: Thinking the left recurrent laryngeal nerve is long because of the heart**
>
> **What students think:** The nerve loops under the aorta "because the heart is on the left".
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Both recurrent laryngeal nerves are the nerves of the 6th arch and originally loop under the 6th arch artery. On the right, the distal 6th arch and the right dorsal aorta disappear, so the nerve slides up to the next surviving arch — the 4th (subclavian). On the left the 6th arch persists as the ductus arteriosus, so the nerve stays trapped under the arch of the aorta.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Right 6 vanishes → hook on 4 (subclavian); left 6 persists as the ductus → hook on the arch." Corollary: with an aberrant right subclavian (right 4th gone too) the right nerve is non-recurrent.
>
> **Trap 4: Calling gastroschisis a hernia and omphalocele a wall defect**
>
> **What students think:** Both are "bowel outside the abdomen", so the terms are interchangeable.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Omphalocele is a true persistent physiological **hernia** through the umbilical ring, covered by amnion and peritoneum, with the cord on the sac and a high rate of chromosomal/cardiac anomalies. Gastroschisis is a full-thickness **wall defect** beside (usually right of) a normal cord, with no covering, matted bowel and few other anomalies.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "O = Omphalos = cord On the sac, Other anomalies; G = Gap in the wall, Gut bare, Good prognosis."
>
> **Trap 5: Assuming the nucleus pulposus is sclerotome**
>
> **What students think:** The whole vertebral column, disc included, comes from the sclerotome.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The sclerotome forms the vertebral bodies and the annulus fibrosus, but the notochord is squeezed out of the bodies and survives only inside the discs as the nucleus pulposus (plus the apical ligament of the dens). Tumours of notochord remnants — chordomas — therefore arise in the midline at the clivus and sacrum.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Notochord → Nucleus; Sclerotome → Shell (annulus and bodies)."

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Neural Crest Tumours and Neurocutaneous Syndromes]]**
> Phaeochromocytoma, neuroblastoma, medullary thyroid carcinoma, melanoma, schwannoma and the MEN 2 syndromes are all diseases of neural crest derivatives; knowing the derivative list predicts the tumour list and the shared markers (chromogranin, synaptophysin, S-100).
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Congenital Heart Disease — Conotruncal Defects]]**
> Cardiac neural crest migrates through arches 3, 4 and 6 to septate the outflow tract, which is why DiGeorge (22q11) combines pouch defects with tetralogy of Fallot, truncus arteriosus and interrupted aortic arch.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Thyroid and Parathyroid Surgery]]**
> The recurrent laryngeal nerve's asymmetric course, the non-recurrent nerve with an aberrant subclavian, the descent of the inferior parathyroids with the thymus and the ultimobranchial C-cell origin of medullary carcinoma are all surgical consequences of this embryology.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Hernia and Hernia Surgeries]]**
> The processus vaginalis (indirect inguinal hernia, hydrocele) and the physiological midgut hernia (omphalocele) are the same lesson — a connection that should have closed — and congenital diaphragmatic hernia belongs to the same family.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Intestinal Obstruction in the Newborn]]**
> Malrotation with volvulus, duodenal atresia and the atresias that accompany gastroschisis share the presentation of bilious vomiting; the embryology decides the imaging sign and the operation (Ladd's procedure).
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Uterine Anomalies and Disorders of Sexual Development]]**
> Müllerian fusion failures (bicornuate, septate, didelphys), Müllerian agenesis (MRKH) and persistent Müllerian duct syndrome follow directly from the duct table; AMH and testosterone decide which duct survives.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> This note is used in paediatric surgery (CDH, omphalocele, gastroschisis, malrotation), ENT (branchial cyst), endocrinology and genetics (DiGeorge, MEN), cardiothoracic surgery (vascular rings), thyroid surgery (RLN, parathyroids), gynaecology (Müllerian anomalies, Gartner's cyst) and neurology/neurosurgery (neural tube defects, chordoma).
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Examiners pair a derivative with its clinical lesion: "C cells are derived from…" (neural crest), "inferior parathyroid comes from…" (3rd pouch), "the nerve of the 6th arch is…" (recurrent laryngeal), "left-sided posterolateral diaphragmatic hernia is…" (Bochdalek), "structure passing behind the oesophagus in dysphagia lusoria is…" (aberrant right subclavian), "Gartner's duct is a remnant of…" (mesonephric duct).

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **Notochord remnants:** nucleus pulposus, apical ligament of dens (± tectorial membrane). Annulus fibrosus = sclerotome.
> 2. **Neural crest** = everything that migrates: pia–arachnoid, enteric plexus, Schwann cells, adrenal medulla, C cells, melanocytes, facial bones, dentine, arch cartilages, iris muscles. **Adrenal cortex = intermediate mesoderm.**
> 3. **Diaphragm:** septum transversum → central tendon; pleuroperitoneal membranes → posterolateral; oesophageal mesentery → crura; body wall → rim. **CDH = Bochdalek, left, lung hypoplasia.**
> 4. **1st cleft** is the only one that persists (external auditory meatus); the **cervical sinus** buries clefts 2–4 → branchial cyst/fistula if it persists.
> 5. **Pouches:** 1 middle ear/auditory tube, 2 palatine tonsil, 3 thymus + **inferior** parathyroid, 4 **superior** parathyroid + ultimobranchial body (C cells from neural crest). **DiGeorge = 3rd + 4th pouch, 22q11.**
> 6. **Cartilages:** 1 Meckel (malleus, incus), 2 Reichert (stapes, styloid, stylohyoid ligament, lesser cornu), 3 greater cornu + body of hyoid, 4 & 6 laryngeal cartilages.
> 7. **Arch arteries:** 1 maxillary, 2 stapedial/hyoid, 3 carotids, 4 arch of aorta (L) / proximal right subclavian (R), 5 degenerates, 6 pulmonary arteries + ductus arteriosus (L).
> 8. **RLN:** left under the ductus (6th arch) → long; right under the subclavian (4th arch) → short; aberrant right subclavian → non-recurrent right RLN + dysphagia lusoria.
> 9. **Veins:** right anterior + common cardinal → SVC; subcardinal → renal/gonadal; supracardinal → azygos + infrarenal IVC; vitelline → portal vein + hepatic IVC; left umbilical → ductus venosus → ligamentum teres/venosum.
> 10. **Mesogastrium:** ventral → falciform, lesser omentum, coronary, triangular; dorsal → greater omentum, gastrosplenic, lienorenal, gastrophrenic.
> 11. **Midgut:** 270° counter-clockwise around the SMA; non-rotation = left-sided colon; mixed = subhepatic caecum + Ladd's bands; reversed = transverse colon behind the SMA.
> 12. **Omphalocele** = covered, cord on sac, many anomalies; **gastroschisis** = right of cord, uncovered, bowel problems only.
> 13. **Wolffian** → epididymis, vas, seminal vesicle, ejaculatory duct (♀ remnant Gartner's duct); **Müllerian** → tubes, uterus, upper vagina (♂ remnants appendix testis, prostatic utricle).

[^ety-embryo]: **Etymology — embryo:** Greek *embryon*, "that which grows within" (*en*, in + *bryein*, to swell, to grow).
[^ety-mesoderm]: **Etymology — mesoderm / ectoderm / endoderm:** Greek *derma*, "skin" with *mesos* "middle", *ektos* "outside", *endon* "within" — the middle, outer and inner "skins" of the trilaminar disc. *Paraxial* = *para*, beside + *axis*.
[^ety-somite]: **Etymology — somite:** Greek *sōma*, "body" + *-ite* — a body segment; the paired blocks of paraxial mesoderm.
[^ety-tomes]: **Etymology — dermatome / myotome / sclerotome:** Greek *tomos*, "a cut, a segment" with *derma* "skin", *mys* "muscle", *sklēros* "hard" — the skin-, muscle- and hard-tissue (bone) segments of each somite.
[^ety-notochord]: **Etymology — notochord:** Greek *nōton*, "the back" + *chordē*, "string, gut-string" — the string along the back; the first axial skeleton.
[^ety-crest]: **Etymology — neural crest:** Latin *crista*, "a tuft, a ridge" (as on a helmet) — the ridge at the crest of each neural fold where these cells arise.
[^ety-septum]: **Etymology — septum transversum:** Latin *septum*, "a partition, a fence" + *transversum*, "lying across" — the transverse partition between the pericardial cavity and the yolk sac.
[^ety-pleuroperitoneal]: **Etymology — pleuroperitoneal:** Greek *pleura*, "the side, a rib" + *peritonaion*, "stretched around" — the membrane between the pleural and peritoneal cavities.
[^ety-bochdalek]: **Etymology — Bochdalek foramen:** Vincenz Alexander Bochdalek (1801–1883), Czech anatomist in Prague, who described the posterolateral defect in 1848. The anterior, retrosternal hernia is named after Giovanni Battista Morgagni (1682–1771).
[^ety-pharyngeal]: **Etymology — pharyngeal / branchial:** Greek *pharynx*, "the throat"; the older term *branchial* is from Greek *branchia*, "gills", because the arches of a fish become its gill bars.
[^ety-cervical-sinus]: **Etymology — cervical sinus:** Latin *cervix*, "neck" + *sinus*, "a hollow, a bay" — the pocket in the neck left when the second arch grows over the clefts.
[^ety-thymus]: **Etymology — thymus:** Greek *thymos*, "a warty growth", also the thyme plant — the gland was likened to a bunch of thyme.
[^ety-parathyroid]: **Etymology — parathyroid:** Greek *para*, "beside" + *thyreos*, "an oblong shield" + *eidos*, "form" — the glands beside the shield-shaped (thyroid) cartilage and gland.
[^ety-ultimobranchial]: **Etymology — ultimobranchial body:** Latin *ultimus*, "last" + *branchial*, "of the gill arches" — the body from the last pharyngeal pouch.
[^ety-digeorge]: **Etymology — DiGeorge syndrome:** Angelo M. DiGeorge (1921–2009), American paediatric endocrinologist in Philadelphia, who described the thymic–parathyroid syndrome in 1965.
[^ety-meckel]: **Etymology — Meckel's cartilage:** Johann Friedrich Meckel the Younger (1781–1833), German anatomist at Halle — also the Meckel of Meckel's diverticulum.
[^ety-reichert]: **Etymology — Reichert's cartilage:** Karl Bogislaus Reichert (1811–1883), German anatomist and embryologist, who described the second-arch cartilage in 1837.
[^ety-cardinal]: **Etymology — cardinal veins:** Latin *cardo*, *cardinis*, "a hinge" — the principal veins on which the embryo's venous drainage "hinges".
[^ety-vitelline]: **Etymology — vitelline / umbilical:** Latin *vitellus*, "yolk" — the veins of the yolk sac; *umbilicus*, "the navel".
[^ety-azygos]: **Etymology — azygos / hemiazygos:** Greek *a-*, "without" + *zygon*, "a yoke, a pair" — the unpaired vein; *hemi-*, "half".
[^ety-aorta]: **Etymology — aorta:** Greek *aortē*, "that which is hung up" (*aeirein*, to lift) — Aristotle's name for the vessel from which the heart seemed to hang.
[^ety-ductus]: **Etymology — ductus arteriosus:** Latin, "the arterial duct"; also called the duct of Botallo after Leonardo Botallo (1519–1588), Italian surgeon.
[^ety-lusoria]: **Etymology — dysphagia lusoria:** Greek *dys-*, "bad" + *phagein*, "to eat" + Latin *lusus naturae*, "a trick (sport) of nature" — David Bayford's 1787 name for swallowing difficulty caused by an aberrant subclavian artery.
[^ety-mesogastrium]: **Etymology — mesogastrium / mesentery:** Greek *mesos*, "middle" + *gastēr*, "belly" / *enteron*, "intestine" — the membrane in the middle, suspending the stomach / the gut.
[^ety-ladd]: **Etymology — Ladd's bands:** William Edwards Ladd (1880–1967), Boston surgeon and founder of paediatric surgery, who described the bands and the operation for malrotation in 1932–36.
[^ety-omphalocele]: **Etymology — omphalocele:** Greek *omphalos*, "navel" + *kēlē*, "hernia" — a hernia at the navel.
[^ety-gastroschisis]: **Etymology — gastroschisis:** Greek *gastēr*, "belly" + *schisis*, "a splitting" — a split belly wall.
[^ety-wolffian]: **Etymology — mesonephric (Wolffian) duct:** Greek *mesos*, "middle" + *nephros*, "kidney" — the duct of the middle kidney; named for Caspar Friedrich Wolff (1733–1794), German embryologist.
[^ety-mullerian]: **Etymology — paramesonephric (Müllerian) duct:** Greek *para*, "beside" + mesonephric — the duct beside the mesonephric duct; named for Johannes Peter Müller (1801–1858), German physiologist, who described it in 1830.
[^ety-gartner]: **Etymology — Gartner's duct:** Hermann Treschow Gartner (1785–1827), Danish surgeon and anatomist, who described the Wolffian duct remnant in the cow (1822) before it was found in women.
