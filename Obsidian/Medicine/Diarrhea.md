# 💧 Diarrhea

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> Diarrhea[^ety-diarrhea][^pre-dia][^suf-rrhoea] is stool water content above 200 mL in 24 hours, or Bristol types 6 and 7. The page classifies it three ways — by *time* (acute, persistent, chronic), by *mechanism* (osmotic, secretory, inflammatory, factitious) and by *site* (small versus large intestine) — and the whole approach is those three questions asked in order.
> How long? What mechanism (does fasting stop it, what is the osmotic gap)? Which bowel (large-volume watery versus small-volume bloody with tenesmus)?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — Time, mechanism, site
Under two weeks it is almost always an infection and self-limiting. Over four weeks you must find a mechanism: osmotic diarrhea is unabsorbed solute pulling water in (it stops when you stop eating, the osmotic gap is wide, the stool is acid); secretory diarrhea is the epithelium actively pumping out salt and water under a toxin or hormone (it does not stop with fasting, the gap is narrow, no structural damage); inflammatory diarrhea is a damaged large-bowel mucosa (blood, mucus, pus, fever, tenesmus). The site follows from the picture: large volume, watery, no blood = small bowel; small volume, frequent, bloody, painful = large bowel.

```
                       DURATION
          < 2 weeks ──────────────── > 4 weeks
              │                          │
          INFECTION              MECHANISM + SITE
         ┌────┴────┐            ┌─────────┴──────────┐
   small bowel   large bowel   small bowel        large bowel
   large volume  fever, blood  osmotic (↑ gap,     inflammatory
   viral m/c     Shigella,     improves fasting)   ulcerative colitis
   ETEC, cholera Salmonella,   secretory (VIPoma)
   self-limiting Campylobacter
```

## 📖 Definition and Physiology of Absorption

**Definition**
- Stool water content > 200 mL/24 hours.
- According to Bristol stool chart:[^ety-bristol]
  - Type 1 & 2: Constipation[^ety-constipation][^pre-con].
  - Type 6 & 7: Diarrhea.

![Bristol stool chart — seven stool types from separate hard lumps to entirely liquid](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/BristolStoolChart.png/800px-BristolStoolChart.png)
*Bristol stool chart as printed on the page: Type 1 separate hard lumps (severe constipation) · Type 2 lumpy and sausage-like (mild constipation) · Type 3 sausage with cracks (normal) · Type 4 smooth soft sausage or snake (normal) · Type 5 soft blobs with clear-cut edges (lacking fibre) · Type 6 mushy with ragged edges (mild diarrhea) · Type 7 liquid, no solid pieces (severe diarrhea). **Physiology of absorption**[^ety-absorption][^pre-ab][^suf-tion]
1. Luminal[^ety-lumen].
2. **Mucosal**:[^ety-mucosa] Small intestine (majority) · Maximum absorption.
3. Post mucosal[^pre-post].

**Macronutrients for absorption**
- Fat: Most calorie dense nutrient · Most specific nutrient affected in malabsorption[^ety-malabsorption][^pre-mal].
- Carbohydrates.
- Proteins.

## 🔬 Pathological Classification

### 1. OSMOTIC DIARRHEA
**Most consistent** clinical symptom of malabsorption.

**Steatorrhea**[^ety-steatorrhea][^pre-steato]
- **Hallmark** of malabsorption.
- Definition: Passing of pale, bulky, fatty, malodorous, greasy stools.
- Clinical presentation: Diarrhea (most consistent clinical finding).

Pathology:
```
 ↓ fat absorption in small intestine
          │ in colon
          ▼
 Unabsorbed macronutrients ↑ stool osmolality (osmotically active)
          │
          ▼
 Stool draws water from large intestinal epithelial cells
          │
          ▼
 Osmotic diarrhea
```

Investigations:
1. 72 hour fecal fat test: **Gold standard**.[^1]

![[Diarrhea - 72 hour fecal fat test.png]]
*72-hour fecal fat test as drawn on the page: fat input 100 g/day, fat absorbed along the small intestine; what comes out is measured (cropped from the handwritten page).*

```
 Fat input = 100 g/day ──► small intestine (fat absorption) ──► stool
        ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
   Normal: < 7 g/day                    • > 7 g/day for 3 days (D3, D4, D5)  } Steatorrhea
                                        • Stool fat excretion ≥ 7 %
```

2. Qualitative stool fat: **Sudan III**[^ety-sudan] · Sudan IV · Oil red O.

### 2. SECRETORY DIARRHEA
Etiology:
- a. Toxins:[^ety-toxin] ETEC, V. cholera[^ety-cholera] (heat labile toxin) · Enteropathogenic virus (rotavirus).
- b. Tumours: VIPoma[^ety-vipoma][^suf-oma] → **WDHA** syndrome.

![[Diarrhea - WDHA syndrome triangle.png]]
*WDHA syndrome as the page draws it — Watery Diarrhoea, Hypokalemia[^pre-hypo][^suf-emia], Acidosis[^suf-osis] at the three corners of a triangle (cropped from the handwritten page).*

> [!note] Note
> No structural damage seen.

| | Osmotic diarrhoea | Secretory diarrhoea |
|---|---|---|
| Stool osmotic gap | **Increased** (> 100 mOsm/kg) | Normal/**decreased** (< 50 mOsm/kg) · ↑ stool Na+ + K+ (by toxins) |
| Response to fasting | Improvement | No change |
| Stool pH | < 5.5 (Fermentation[^ety-fermentation] of unabsorbed carbohydrates → Acids) | > 6.0 |

> [!info] Note — Stool osmotic gap
> - Measured osmolality (osmometer) − calculated osmolality
> - Calculated osmolality = 290 − 2 (Stool Na+ + K+)[^2]
> - Normal: 50–100 mOsm/kg.

### 3. FACTITIOUS DIARRHOEA[^ety-factitious]
Clinical presentation:
- Adolescent female.
- Chronic diarrhea.
- Fatigue.
- Weight loss.

Findings:

| Stool collection | Stool osmolality |
|---|---|
| Unsupervised (mixed with water) | Very low (~ 16 mOsm/kg) |
| Supervised | Normal (~ 279) |

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Diarrhea is water that the bowel either could not absorb or chose to secrete. Unabsorbed solute (osmotic) drags water passively and therefore stops when the solute stops coming — fasting improves it, the osmotic gap is wide because the unmeasured solute is there, and the stool is acid because colonic bacteria ferment it. A toxin or hormone (secretory) drives the enterocyte's own chloride pump, so the stool is made of measured electrolytes — the gap is narrow, fasting changes nothing, and the mucosa is structurally normal.
>
> The site is read from the stool itself: the small bowel handles litres, so its diarrhea is large-volume, watery and painless, with steatorrhea if absorption fails; the colon handles the last few hundred millilitres, so its diarrhea is small, frequent, urgent, bloody and painful, with fever when the mucosa is invaded.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> The small intestine absorbs the great majority of the roughly nine litres of fluid that enter it each day, and the colon salvages most of the rest, leaving under 200 mL of water in stool (Bristol types 3–4). Fat, the most calorie-dense nutrient, is absorbed in the proximal small bowel; its appearance in stool is the earliest and most specific marker of failure.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A viral infection or a small osmotic load exceeds small-bowel capacity, but the colon's reserve absorbs the excess: loose stools for a few days, large volume, no blood, no fever — the "nuisance symptom" of acute diarrhea under two weeks that settles on its own. In early malabsorption the only sign may be bulkier, paler stools and fat on a Sudan III smear.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Beyond four weeks the mechanism declares itself. Osmotic: frank steatorrhea with > 7 g fat/day, osmotic gap > 100, pH < 5.5, relief on fasting, weight loss. Secretory: VIPoma's watery diarrhea with hypokalemia and acidosis, continuing through fasting, gap < 50. Inflammatory: ulcerative colitis with blood, mucus, pus, tenesmus, dyschezia, fever and abdominal pain.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → [infection / malabsorption / toxin or tumour / mucosal inflammation] → loose large-volume stool with colonic compensation → persistent (> 2 weeks) → chronic (> 4 weeks) with the osmotic, secretory or inflammatory signature → dehydration, electrolyte loss, weight loss, steatorrhea
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Duration decides whether to investigate at all; the fasting response and osmotic gap decide between a diet or enzyme problem and a tumour or toxin; the stool character decides between a small-bowel work-up (fecal fat, breath tests) and a colonoscopy. A supervised stool collection unmasks factitious diarrhea before an invasive work-up begins.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Osmotic diarrhea / malabsorption:** "improves on fasting, osmotic gap > 100, stool pH < 5.5, pale bulky greasy stool" → unabsorbed solute, fermented in the colon to acid; steatorrhea is the hallmark and 72-hour fecal fat the gold standard.
>
> **Secretory diarrhea:** "continues during fasting, osmotic gap < 50, high stool Na+ and K+, no structural damage" → cholera or ETEC heat-labile toxin, rotavirus, or a VIPoma.
>
> **VIPoma:** "watery diarrhea + hypokalemia + achlorhydria/acidosis" → WDHA syndrome, the page's triangle.
>
> **Factitious diarrhea:** "adolescent female, chronic diarrhea, fatigue, weight loss, stool osmolality ~16 mOsm/kg that normalises (~279) when collected under supervision" → stool diluted with water.
>
> **Large-bowel / inflammatory diarrhea:** "small volume, frequent, blood, mucus, pus, tenesmus, dyschezia, fever, abdominal pain" → Shigella, non-typhoid Salmonella, Campylobacter, or ulcerative colitis.
>
> **Small-bowel diarrhea:** "large volume, watery, painless, no blood, weight loss if persistent, steatorrhea" → viral (norovirus in adults, rotavirus in children), ETEC, cholera, or malabsorption.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Bristol types 1–2 = constipation, 6–7 = diarrhea. Orange-red globules on a Sudan III stool smear = fat. Pale, bulky, floating, greasy stool = steatorrhea.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Stool water > 200 mL/24 h. Acute < 2 weeks, persistent > 2 weeks, chronic > 4 weeks. Osmotic gap normal 50–100, > 100 osmotic, < 50 secretory (calculated as 290 − 2[Na+ + K+]). Stool pH < 5.5 osmotic, > 6.0 secretory. Fecal fat normal < 7 g/day; steatorrhea > 7 g/day on days 3–5 or ≥ 7 % of intake.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Reading a low stool osmolality as a low osmotic gap**
>
> **What students think:** A stool osmolality far below plasma means secretory diarrhea.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Stool in the colon equilibrates with plasma, so true stool osmolality is always close to 290; that is why the gap is *calculated* from 290, not from the measured value. A measured osmolality of ~16 mOsm/kg is physically impossible for real stool and means the sample was diluted with water — factitious diarrhea — which the page proves by a supervised collection returning ~279.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Gap = 290 − 2(Na+ + K+); a measured osmolality far below 290 is a contaminated sample, not a mechanism.
>
> **Trap 2: Assuming rotavirus means osmotic diarrhea because it is an infection of the small bowel**
>
> **What students think:** Viral gastroenteritis damages villi, so it must be osmotic.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The page lists rotavirus under secretory toxins: its NSP4 enterotoxin drives chloride secretion, so the diarrhea behaves as secretory (continues with fasting) even though the villi are also injured. Cholera and ETEC are the purer models — heat-labile toxin, no structural damage at all.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Classify by the dominant mechanism the page gives, not by the organ: toxins and VIPoma are secretory; malabsorption is osmotic; IBD is inflammatory.
>
> **Trap 3: Letting cramps or bloating decide the site**
>
> **What students think:** Abdominal cramps and bloating mean large-bowel disease.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In the page's table cramps and bloating are *present in both* small- and large-intestinal diarrhea, so they discriminate nothing. The discriminators are volume (large vs small), frequency/urgency, blood/pus/mucus, tenesmus, dyschezia, abdominal pain, fever (large bowel) and steatorrhea, vomiting, weight loss (small bowel).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Use the rows that differ; ignore the two rows that read "Present / Present".
>
> **Trap 4: Calling every two-week diarrhea "chronic"**
>
> **What students think:** Diarrhea lasting more than two weeks is chronic and needs a full work-up.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The page's thresholds are acute < 2 weeks, persistent > 2 weeks, chronic > 4 weeks. Persistent diarrhea is still often post-infectious and self-limiting; the mechanism-and-site algorithm belongs to the > 4-week group.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Three bins, two cut-offs: 2 weeks and 4 weeks.
>
> **Trap 5: Equating steatorrhea with fat intolerance only**
>
> **What students think:** Steatorrhea is a problem of fat, so it causes greasy stool and nothing more.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Unabsorbed fat is osmotically active in the colon and draws water, so steatorrhea presents *as diarrhea* — the page calls diarrhea the most consistent clinical finding of steatorrhea. Fat is also the most specific nutrient affected in malabsorption, which is why the fecal fat test is the gold standard and why fat is the macronutrient to measure.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Steatorrhea = osmotic diarrhea with fat as the solute; confirm with 72-hour fecal fat.

>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>

> The three stages of absorption (luminal, mucosal, post-mucosal) on this page are expanded there; every osmotic diarrhea of malabsorption is a failure at one of them.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Clinical Features and Tests for Malabsorption]]**
> The 72-hour fecal fat gold standard, Sudan III, D-xylose and the breath tests are the investigation ladder for the osmotic branch of the approach.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Inflammatory Bowel Disease]]**
> Ulcerative colitis is the page's model of inflammatory, large-bowel diarrhea: blood, mucus, tenesmus, fever, abdominal pain.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Cholera and Enterotoxigenic E. coli]]**
> The heat-labile toxin raises cAMP and opens the CFTR chloride channel — the mechanism behind "secretory, no structural damage, no change on fasting".
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Pancreatic Neuroendocrine Tumours]]**
> VIPoma (WDHA) sits beside gastrinoma, insulinoma and glucagonoma; its diarrhea is the secretory type with hypokalemia and acidosis.
>

> Secretory diarrhea loses bicarbonate and potassium, giving the normal-anion-gap metabolic acidosis and hypokalemia of WDHA and cholera.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Ask duration first; under two weeks, rehydrate and wait. Over four weeks, characterise the stool (volume, blood, fat), measure the osmotic gap and pH, and try a fast: improvement sends you to the malabsorption work-up, no change to toxin and tumour (VIP level), blood and fever to colonoscopy. A supervised collection comes before any of this in a young woman with an implausibly dilute stool.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> The numbers carry the questions: 200 mL, 2 and 4 weeks, > 100 vs < 50 mOsm/kg, 290 − 2(Na+ + K+), 50–100 normal, pH 5.5 and 6.0, 7 g/day and 7 %, ~16 vs ~279. The most-commons: viral overall, norovirus in adults, rotavirus in children. The hallmark (steatorrhea) and the gold standard (72-hour fecal fat).

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)
> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Diarrhea = stool water > 200 mL/24 h; Bristol types 6–7 (types 1–2 = constipation).
> 2. Absorption is luminal → **mucosal** (small intestine, maximum) → post-mucosal; fat is the most calorie-dense nutrient and the most specific one affected in malabsorption.
> 3. Duration: acute < 2 weeks (nuisance), **persistent > 2 weeks**, **chronic > 4 weeks**.
> 4. Acute diarrhea is **viral (m/c)** and self-limiting: **norovirus in adults, rotavirus in children**.
> 5. Chronic diarrhea D/d: osmotic (malabsorption), secretory (tumor, toxin), inflammatory (IBD); malassimilation = maldigestion + malabsorption.
> 6. Osmotic diarrhea is the **most consistent symptom** of malabsorption; **steatorrhea is the hallmark** — pale, bulky, fatty, malodorous, greasy stools.
> 7. **72-hour fecal fat = gold standard**: normal < 7 g/day; steatorrhea > 7 g/day for 3 days (D3–D5) or excretion ≥ 7 %; qualitative = **Sudan III**, Sudan IV, Oil red O.
> 8. Secretory diarrhea: toxins (ETEC, V. cholera heat-labile toxin, rotavirus) and VIPoma → **WDHA** (watery diarrhoea, hypokalemia, acidosis); no structural damage.
> 9. Osmotic vs secretory: gap **> 100** vs **< 50** mOsm/kg; fasting improves vs no change; stool pH < 5.5 (fermentation) vs > 6.0.
> 10. Stool osmotic gap = measured − calculated, calculated = **290 − 2 (Na+ + K+)**; normal **50–100 mOsm/kg**.
> 11. Factitious diarrhoea: adolescent female, chronic diarrhea, fatigue, weight loss; unsupervised stool osmolality ~16, supervised ~279.
> 12. Small-bowel diarrhea: large volume, watery, ↓ frequency, no blood/pus/mucus, no tenesmus/dyschezia, no pain/fever, steatorrhea present, vomiting maybe, weight loss if persistent.
> 13. Large-bowel diarrhea: small volume, ↑ frequency/urgency, blood/pus/mucus, tenesmus, dyschezia, pain, fever; Shigella, ulcerative colitis.
> 14. Approach: < 2 weeks = infection (small bowel viral/ETEC/cholera self-limiting; large bowel invasive Shigella, non-typhoid Salmonella, Campylobacter); > 4 weeks = small bowel osmotic (↑ gap, improves on fasting) or secretory (VIPoma), large bowel inflammatory (ulcerative colitis).

## 🔁 One-Line Recall
```
> 200 mL/day or Bristol 6–7
  → how long? < 2 wk infection (viral m/c, self-limiting) · > 2 wk persistent · > 4 wk chronic
    → fasting helps + gap > 100 + pH < 5.5 → OSMOTIC (malabsorption; steatorrhea hallmark; 72-h fecal fat > 7 g)
    → fasting no help + gap < 50 + pH > 6 → SECRETORY (toxin: cholera/ETEC/rotavirus; tumour: VIPoma = WDHA)

```

[^1]: Textbook method: the patient eats about 100 g fat/day for 5 days and all stool from days 3–5 is collected; normal excretion is < 7 g/day (≤ 7 % of intake). The page's "> 7 g/day for 3 days (D3, D4, D5)" is this protocol.
[^2]: The factor 2 accounts for the anions that accompany sodium and potassium; 290 mOsm/kg is taken as the luminal osmolality because stool in the colon equilibrates with plasma. Some texts use 290 − 2(Na+ + K+) exactly as the page does; others quote the normal gap as < 50 with > 125 as clearly osmotic — the page's 50–100 normal, > 100 osmotic, < 50 secretory is the Harrison's convention.

[^ety-diarrhea]: **diarrhea / diarrhoea** — Greek *dia-*, "through" + *rhein*, "to flow" + *-ia* → a flowing-through of the bowel; defined here as stool water > 200 mL/24 h. **Differentiate:** *diarrhoea* (↑ water) vs *steatorrhoea* (↑ fat) vs *dysentery* (bloody, mucoid, with tenesmus — the large-bowel inflammatory picture).
[^ety-bristol]: **Bristol stool chart** — devised at the University of Bristol, England, by Ken Heaton and Stephen Lewis and published in 1997 as a seven-type scale of stool form that tracks intestinal transit time.
[^ety-constipation]: **constipation** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *stipare*, "to press, cram" + *-tion* → stool pressed together into hard lumps (Bristol types 1–2). **Differentiate:** *constipation* (infrequent, hard) vs *obstipation* (complete failure to pass stool or gas, as in obstruction) vs *dyschezia* (painful or difficult defecation).
[^ety-absorption]: **absorption** — Latin *ab-*, "away" + *sorbēre*, "to suck in" + *-tion* → sucking nutrients away from the lumen into the mucosa. **Differentiate:** *digestion* (luminal breakdown) → *absorption* (mucosal uptake) → *assimilation* (use by tissues); the page's *malassimilation* = maldigestion + malabsorption.
[^ety-lumen]: **lumen** — Latin *lumen*, "light, opening" (the "light" seen through a tube) → the inside of the bowel; *luminal* digestion happens there before anything touches the mucosa.
[^ety-mucosa]: **mucosa** — Latin *mucosus*, "slimy" → the mucus-coated inner lining of the gut where most absorption happens; the *mucosal* stage of the page.
[^ety-malabsorption]: **malabsorption** — Latin *male*, "badly" + *absorption* → defective uptake across the mucosa. **Differentiate:** *maldigestion* (lumen: enzymes or bile missing) vs *malabsorption* (mucosa or beyond); together *malassimilation*.
[^ety-acute]: **acute** — Latin *acutus*, "sharp, pointed" (from *acuere*, "to sharpen") → sharp in onset and short in course; here < 2 weeks. **Differentiate:** *acute* (< 2 weeks) → *persistent* (> 2 weeks) → *chronic* (> 4 weeks).
[^ety-persistent]: **persistent** — Latin *per-*, "through, thoroughly" + *sistere*, "to stand" → standing through, continuing beyond the expected time; the page's > 2-week bin between acute and chronic.
[^ety-chronic]: **chronic** — Greek *chronos*, "time" + *-ikos* → lasting a long time; here > 4 weeks. Don't confuse with **chronic** meaning severe — it means long, not bad.
[^ety-etiology]: **etiology** — Greek *aitia*, "cause" + *-logia*, "study" → the cause of the disease. **Differentiate:** *etiology* (cause) → *pathogenesis* (mechanism) → *pathology* (structural change).
[^ety-norovirus]: **norovirus** — named after Norwalk, Ohio, USA, where a 1968 school outbreak of gastroenteritis gave the "Norwalk agent" (identified 1972); the m/c cause of acute viral diarrhea in adults.
[^ety-rotavirus]: **rotavirus** — Latin *rota*, "wheel", for the wheel-like appearance of the virion on electron microscopy (Flewett, 1974); the m/c cause of acute diarrhea in children. Its NSP4 enterotoxin is why the page lists it under secretory causes.
[^ety-osmotic]: **osmotic** — Greek *ōsmos*, "a pushing" (Thomas Graham, 1854) + *-ic* → driven by solute concentration; *osmotic diarrhea* = water pulled into the lumen by unabsorbed solute. **Differentiate:** *osmotic* (gap > 100, improves on fasting, pH < 5.5) vs *secretory* (gap < 50, no change on fasting, pH > 6.0) vs *inflammatory* (blood, pus, mucus, fever).
[^ety-secretory]: **secretory** — Latin *se-*, "apart" + *cernere*, "to sift, separate" + *-ory* → set apart and released by the cell; *secretory diarrhea* = the enterocyte actively secreting chloride and water under a toxin or hormone, with no structural damage. **Differentiate:** *secretion* (useful product released by a cell) vs *excretion* (waste put out of the body) vs *exudation* (fluid oozing from damaged vessels, the inflammatory pattern).
[^ety-inflammatory]: **inflammatory** — Latin *in-*, "in" + *flamma*, "flame" + *-ation* + *-ory* → "set on fire within"; the four cardinal signs of Celsus (redness, heat, swelling, pain). **Differentiate:** *inflammation* (the host response, sterile or infective: ulcerative colitis) vs *infection* (invasion by an organism: Shigella) — an infection causes inflammation, but inflammation need not be infection.
[^ety-malassimilation]: **malassimilation** — Latin *male*, "badly" + *ad-*, "to" + *similis*, "like" + *-ation* → failure to make nutrients "like" the body, i.e. to take them up and use them; the page's umbrella for maldigestion + malabsorption.
[^ety-steatorrhea]: **steatorrhea** — Greek *stear, steat-*, "tallow, fat" + *rhoia*, "flow" → fat flowing out in the stool: pale, bulky, greasy, malodorous; > 7 g/day on the 72-hour test; the hallmark of malabsorption.
[^ety-sudan]: **Sudan III** — a red azo dye of the "Sudan" series, named by its German makers in the 1890s after the then much-discussed region; it dissolves in neutral fat and stains fat globules orange-red on a stool smear — the qualitative test. Sudan IV and Oil Red O are related lipid dyes.
[^ety-toxin]: **toxin** — Greek *toxikon (pharmakon)*, "(poison) for arrows", from *toxon*, "bow" → a poison made by a living organism. **Differentiate:** *enterotoxin* (acts on the gut lining: cholera toxin, ETEC heat-labile toxin, rotavirus NSP4 — secretory, no damage) vs *cytotoxin* (kills cells: Shiga toxin — inflammatory, bloody) vs *endotoxin* (lipopolysaccharide of the Gram-negative wall).
[^ety-cholera]: **cholera** — Greek *cholera*, from *cholē*, "bile" (ancient physicians blamed a flux of bile) → the secretory diarrhea of *Vibrio cholerae*, whose heat-labile toxin raises cAMP and opens chloride channels; *Vibrio* from Latin *vibrare*, "to quiver", for the organism's darting motility.
[^ety-vipoma]: **VIPoma** — *VIP*, vasoactive intestinal peptide (isolated by Said and Mutt, 1970) + *-oma*, "tumour" → a pancreatic neuroendocrine tumour secreting VIP; described by Verner and Morrison in 1958, so also Verner–Morrison syndrome or pancreatic cholera. Its triad is the page's WDHA: watery diarrhoea, hypokalemia, achlorhydria/acidosis.
[^ety-fermentation]: **fermentation** — Latin *fervēre*, "to boil, seethe" + *-mentum* + *-ation* → the bubbling breakdown of sugars by microbes; colonic bacteria ferment unabsorbed carbohydrate to short-chain fatty acids and gas, dropping stool pH below 5.5 in osmotic diarrhea.
[^ety-factitious]: **factitious** — Latin *facticius*, "made by art, artificial", from *facere*, "to make" → a disorder manufactured by the patient; here stool diluted with water. **Differentiate:** *factitious disorder* (symptoms produced deliberately for the sick role, no external gain) vs *malingering* (produced for an external gain) vs *functional* disorder (real symptoms without structural cause, not produced on purpose).
[^ety-shigella]: **Shigella** — named for Kiyoshi Shiga (1871–1957), Japanese bacteriologist, who isolated the bacillus during the 1897 dysentery epidemic in Japan; an invasive large-bowel organism producing bloody, mucoid, febrile diarrhea.
[^ety-colitis]: **colitis** — Greek *kolon*, "large intestine" + *-itis*, "inflammation" → inflammation of the colon; *ulcerative colitis* adds Latin *ulcus*, "sore", for the continuous mucosal ulceration that begins in the rectum. **Differentiate:** *colitis* (colon) vs *enteritis* (small intestine) vs *enterocolitis* (both) vs *proctitis* (rectum).
[^ety-tenesmus]: **tenesmus** — Greek *teinesmos*, "straining", from *teinein*, "to stretch, strain" → a painful, ineffective urge to defecate from an inflamed rectum; a large-bowel sign. **Differentiate:** *tenesmus* (urge with little or nothing passed) vs *urgency* (urge with stool passed) vs *dyschezia* (painful or difficult passage).
[^ety-dyschezia]: **dyschezia** — Greek *dys-*, "bad, difficult" + *chezein*, "to defecate" + *-ia* → painful or difficult defecation; present in large-bowel, absent in small-bowel diarrhea.
[^ety-campylobacter]: **Campylobacter** — Greek *kampylos*, "curved" + *baktron*, "rod, staff" → the curved, gull-wing-shaped rod (separated from *Vibrio* in 1963); an invasive cause of febrile, bloody large-bowel diarrhea and the classic trigger of Guillain–Barré syndrome.
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, across: diarrhea (flowing through), diagnosis. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis-** apart · **dys-** bad, difficult (dyschezia).
[^suf-rrhoea]: **-rrhoea / -rrhea** — Greek *rhoia*, flow: diarrhoea, steatorrhoea. Don't confuse with **-rrhage** bursting out of blood (haemorrhage) · **-rrhaphy** suturing · **-rrhexis** rupture.
[^pre-con]: **con- / com-** — together, with: constipation (pressed together), consistency. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **co-** merely "jointly" (co-transporter).
[^pre-ab]: **ab-** — away from: absorption. Don't confuse with **ad-** toward (adsorption, assimilation = *ad-* + *similis*) · **a-/an-** without.
[^suf-tion]: **-tion / -ation** — the act or process: absorption, constipation, fermentation, inflammation, malassimilation. The noun form of a process; the verb drops it (absorb, ferment, inflame).
[^pre-post]: **post-** — after, behind: post-mucosal (beyond the mucosa), post-prandial. Don't confuse with **pre-** before · **per-** through (persistent) · **peri-** around.
[^pre-mal]: **mal-** — bad, badly: malabsorption, maldigestion, malassimilation, malodorous. Don't confuse with **dys-** difficult, disordered (dyschezia) · **a-** absent.
[^pre-per]: **per-** — through, thoroughly: persistent (standing through). Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before · **post-** after.
[^suf-ation]: **-ation in "inflammation"** — Latin *inflammatio*; the process noun of *inflame*. The matching adjective is **-atory** (inflammatory, secretory); the matching agent noun is **-ator**. Don't confuse with **-itis**, the Greek suffix that names inflammation *of a named organ* (colitis).
[^pre-steato]: **steato-** — Greek *stear, steat-*, fat: steatorrhoea, steatocrit, steatosis. Don't confuse with **lipo-** fat in general (lipase, lipolysis) · **adipo-** fat tissue · **chol-** bile.
[^suf-oma]: **-oma** — tumour, swelling: VIPoma, gastrinoma, insulinoma. Don't confuse with **-osis** condition (acidosis) · **-itis** inflammation (colitis); benign vs malignant is not encoded (lipoma vs lymphoma).
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, deficient: hypokalemia (low potassium in blood, the K of WDHA). Don't confuse with **hyper-** excessive · **sub-** beneath in position.
[^suf-emia]: **-emia / -aemia** — in the blood: hypokalemia (*kalium*, potassium). Don't confuse with **-uria** in the urine · **-rrhoea** flow in stool.
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — condition, abnormal state: acidosis (the A of WDHA), steatosis. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation (colitis) · **-iasis** infestation or formation.
[^suf-itis]: **-itis** — inflammation: colitis, ulcerative colitis, enteritis, gastroenteritis. Don't confuse with **-osis** non-inflammatory condition (acidosis) · **-oma** tumour (VIPoma).
[^pre-dys]: **dys-** — bad, difficult, disordered: dyschezia (difficult defecation), dysentery (*dys-* + *enteron*, "bad bowel"), dyspepsia. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart · **mal-** badly (malabsorption) · **a-** absent.
