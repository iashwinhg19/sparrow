# Alveolar Ventilation and Pulmonary Circulation

## Ventilation
### Useful ventilation
- Environmental air
  - Dry
  - Pressure 760 mmHg
  - N2 79 %, O2 21 % → PO2 160 mmHg
- Inspired air
  - Moist
  - PO2 ↓ → dilution by water vapour 47 mmHg
  - Humidified tracheal PO2 150 mmHg
- Alveolar air
  - Alveolar gas equation → PAO2 = PiO2 − (PACO2 / RER)
  - PiO2 = FiO2 × (Pb − PH2O)
  - RER = CO2 produced ÷ O2 consumed = 0.8
  - PAO2 100, PACO2 40 (d/t gas exchange)
  - A–a O2 gradient = P(A–a)O2
- Blood
  - Systemic arterial 100 / 40
  - Mixed venous 40 / 46
- Worked example (severe RDS on ventilator)
  - FiO2 0.5, Pb 747, PCO2 40, RER 0.8
  - PAO2 = 0.5 × (747 − 47) − 40/0.8 = 300 mmHg
  - A–a gradient = 140 mmHg
- Mouth-to-mouth breathing → provides 16 % O2
- Expired air → PEO2 120 mmHg

### Dead space ventilation
- Formulas
  - VT = VD + VA → 500 = 150 + 350 ml
  - Alveolar ventilation = (VT − VD) × RR → 4.2 L/min
  - VD / VT = 150 / 500 = 30 %
  - Minute ventilation = VT × RR → 6 L/min
- Anatomical dead space
  - Conducting airways → generations 0–16
  - Measured by single-breath N2 / Fowler's method
  - Phase I → dead space air
  - Phase II → dead space + alveolar air
  - Phase III → alveolar air (plateau)
  - Phase IV → dependent alveoli close (closing volume)
  - RV → residual volume
  - Dead space air = start of expiration → midpoint of phase II
- Alveolar dead space
  - Normal → nil
  - Pathological → dead space occurs
- Total physiological dead space
  - Normal → = anatomical
  - Pathological → alveolar + anatomical
  - Bohr's equation → VD = VT × (PACO2 − PECO2) / PACO2

## Perfusion
### Zones of perfusion
- Zone I (apex)
  - PA > Pa > Pv
  - Compression of blood vessel → no flow
- Zone II (middle)
  - Pa > PA > Pv
  - Intermittent flow → waterfall effect
- Zone III (base)
  - Pa > Pv > PA
  - Continuous blood flow
- PA alveolar · Pa arterial · Pv venous pressure

### Properties of pulmonary circulation
- = Right ventricular output = 5 L
- Highly distensible
- Low pressure

### Response to hypoxia in pulmonary arteries
- Blood diverted from hypoxic to well-ventilated alveoli
- Mechanism of vasoconstriction
  - Hypoxia
  - → O2-sensitive K+ channel closes
  - → K+ accumulates
  - → depolarization
  - → Ca2+ channel opens in vascular smooth muscle
  - → vasoconstriction
- Systemic circulation → hypoxia causes vasodilation
  - Eg kidney, heart
