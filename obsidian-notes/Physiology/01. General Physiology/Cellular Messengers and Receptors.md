# 📡 Cellular Messengers and Receptors

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> **Hormones**[^ety-hormone] **act on cells by receptors**[^ety-receptor][^pre-re]. Whether a hormone can cross the lipid cell membrane decides *where* its receptor sits — and that one fact decides everything else in the chapter:
> - ✅ **Water-soluble hormones** (peptides, catecholamines) **cannot cross** → **extracellular (cell-membrane) receptors** → act through a **second messenger**[^ety-messenger] or a kinase → fast.
> - ✅ **Lipid-soluble hormones** (steroids, vitamins A and D, thyroid hormone) **cross the membrane** → **intracellular (cytoplasmic or nuclear) receptors** → switch genes on → slow.

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — The Doorbell and the House Key

A water-soluble hormone is a visitor without a key: it **rings the doorbell** (membrane receptor) and someone inside (the **second messenger**) runs the message through the house. A lipid-soluble hormone **has the key**: it walks straight in and talks to the boss in the study (the **nucleus**), who rewrites the work orders (gene transcription).

```
WATER-SOLUBLE (peptides, catecholamines) → cannot cross → CELL-MEMBRANE receptor → 2nd messenger / kinase → FAST
   GPCR (7-TM; Gs ↑cAMP · Gi ↓cAMP · Gq ↑Ca²⁺ · Gt ↓cGMP)
   Tyrosine kinase (insulin)  ·  JAK-STAT (GH, PRL, EPO, leptin)  ·  Serine kinase (activins, AMH)
LIPID-SOLUBLE (steroids, vit A/D, thyroid) → cross → CYTOPLASMIC or NUCLEAR receptor → DNA → transcription → SLOW
SAFETY VALVE against overactivity: internalisation · desensitisation · inactivation of receptors
```

## 📖 Introduction

- **Hormones act on cells by receptors.**
- Receptors can be:
  - **Extracellular**[^ety-extracellular][^pre-extra] — on the cell membrane.
  - **Intracellular**[^pre-intra] — inside the cell (cytoplasm or nucleus).

### Classification of hormones

| Class | Types | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Amino acid**[^ety-amino-acid] **derivatives** | **Tyrosine**[^ety-tyrosine][^suf-ine]: Epinephrine[^ety-epinephrine][^pre-epi], Norepinephrine[^ety-norepinephrine], Dopamine[^ety-dopamine] → **catecholamines**[^ety-catecholamine]; **Thyroid**[^ety-thyroid][^suf-oid] **hormones**[^1] · **Tryptophan**[^ety-tryptophan]: Serotonin[^ety-serotonin], Melatonin[^ety-melatonin] · **Histidine: Histamine**[^ety-histamine] | Cannot cross the cell membrane → **extracellular receptors** |
| **Peptide**[^ety-peptide] **& protein**[^ety-protein] **hormones** | **3 AAs:** Thyrotropin releasing hormone (**TRH**)[^ety-thyrotropin][^suf-tropic] · **51 AAs:** Insulin[^ety-insulin] · **84 AAs:** Parathormone[^ety-parathormone][^pre-para][^2] | **Cannot cross the cell membrane → extracellular receptors** |

🧠 *Read the table from right to left:* the "Features" column is just a solubility test. Charged, water-loving molecules (amines, peptides) bounce off the fatty membrane and need a doorbell; the flat, greasy steroid ring and the fat-soluble vitamins slip straight through.

*Where the receptors sit: the hormone is the **1st messenger**; it binds an **extracellular / cell-membrane receptor**, which releases a **2nd messenger** inside the cell. ### G-protein coupled receptor

- **Transmembrane**[^pre-trans] protein.
- AKA **serpentine**[^ety-serpentine] **receptors / 7 transmembrane receptors** — the single protein chain weaves through the membrane seven times.

![Schematic of a seven-transmembrane G protein-coupled receptor (Wikimedia Commons)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/GPCR_structure_and_receptor.svg/800px-GPCR_structure_and_receptor.svg.png)
*A G protein-coupled receptor: one chain crossing the membrane seven times ("serpentine"), the hormone-binding site outside and the G protein docked inside (Wikimedia Commons; vector version of "GPCR.png" — licence on the file page).*

#### Subunits of the receptor

```
                    Subunits
        ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
    Alpha (α)       Beta (β)      Gamma (γ)
        │
 Has GTPase activity
```

- **Alpha (α)** — **has GTPase**[^ety-gtpase] **activity** (it carries GDP/GTP and switches itself off).
- **Beta (β)** and **Gamma (γ)** — stay together as a βγ pair.[^3]

#### Mechanism of action

```
Hormone binds to GPCR
        ↓
α-subunit gets separated
        ↓
Hydrolyses GTP to GDP
        ↓
Energy liberated
        ↓
2nd messengers are sent inside the cell
```

🧠 The G protein[^ety-g-protein] is a **molecular switch**: GDP-bound = OFF, GTP-bound = ON. The hormone does not enter; it only flips the switch, and the α-subunit carries the "ON" signal to an enzyme that makes the second messenger. The α-subunit's own GTPase hydrolysis[^ety-hydrolysis][^pre-hydro][^suf-lysis] is the built-in timer that turns it back off.[^4]

![[Cellular Messengers and Receptors - GPCR inactive and activated.png]]
*Inactive vs activated receptor — left, the empty receptor with its G protein (α-GDP + β + γ) inactive; right, the ligand bound, α now carrying GTP and separated from βγ → cell response (cropped from the handwritten page).*

#### 2nd messengers

- **Enter the cell & influence cellular function.**
- **Types:**
  - **Cyclic**[^ety-cyclic] **AMP (c-AMP)**
  - **Ca²⁺ (IP3–DAG–Ca²⁺)**[^5]
  - **Cyclic GMP (c-GMP)**
- All three → **activate protein kinases → regulate downstream reactions.**

#### GPCR can use any 2nd messenger system

| G protein | Action | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **a. Gs** (Stimulatory) | Stimulates **adenylyl cyclase**[^ety-adenylyl-cyclase] | **↑ cAMP** |
| **b. Gi** (Inhibitory) | Inhibits adenylyl cyclase | **↓ cAMP** |
| **c. Gq** | — | **↑ Ca²⁺** |
| **d. Gt** | Acts by **transducin**[^ety-transducin] in **retinal rods** → activates **PDE enzymes**[^ety-phosphodiesterase][^pre-di] | **↓ cGMP**[^6] |

🧠 *Memory:* **s** = **s**timulates, **i** = **i**nhibits, **q** = Ca²⁺ (think "**q**uick calcium"), **t** = **t**ransducin in the eye.

## 🧬 Intracellular Receptors

**Hormones that enter the cell act on intracellular receptors.**

### Cytoplasmic receptors

![[Cellular Messengers and Receptors - cytoplasmic receptor.png]]
*Cytoplasmic receptor — the **hormone** enters the cell; the **inactive receptor** is held by **heat shock proteins** (the three small circles; the page's label for them is overwritten by a watermark and has been blanked); the hormone frees the receptor to form the **hormone–receptor complex (active)**, which moves into the **nucleus** and binds DNA (cropped from the handwritten page).*

🧠 In words: hormone in → it binds the receptor sitting in the cytoplasm → the heat shock[^ety-heat-shock] proteins fall off → the **hormone–receptor complex** walks into the nucleus → binds DNA → new proteins made. That is why steroid effects take hours, not seconds.

## 🛡️ Homeostasis of Hormones & Neurotransmitter Receptors

### Safety mechanism for hormone overactivity

1. **Internalization**[^ety-internalization] of receptors.
2. **Desensitization**[^ety-desensitization] of receptors: receptors are modified to be less responsive.
3. **Inactivation**[^ety-inactivation][^pre-in] of receptors.[^16]

🧠 Too much hormone for too long → the cell protects itself: it **pulls receptors inside** (fewer doorbells), **muffles** the ones left (desensitisation), or **switches them off**. This is why chronic β-agonist inhalers lose effect (tachyphylaxis) and why GnRH given continuously *suppresses* the pituitary.

### Neurotransmitter receptors

**Types:**

| Type | How it works | Speed | Eg |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ligand gated receptors** | **Activates ion channels** | **Fast responses** | **Nicotinic**[^ety-nicotinic] **ACh receptors**, **GABA**[^ety-gaba] **A**, **GABA C**[^17] |
| **GPCR / metabotropic**[^ety-metabotropic][^pre-meta] **receptors** | **Act by 2nd messengers** | **Slow response** | **Muscarinic**[^ety-muscarinic] **acetylcholine receptor**, **GABA B** |

![[Cellular Messengers and Receptors - ligand gated receptor.png]]
*Ligand gated receptor — two ACh molecules bind the channel protein, which opens a central pore: Na⁺ (and Ca²⁺) rush into the cytosol and K⁺ leaves — the ion movement itself is the response, so it is fast (cropped from the handwritten page).*

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> **Solubility decides the receptor, the receptor decides the speed.** A hormone that cannot cross the lipid membrane (catecholamines, peptides) must use a membrane receptor and a relay — a G protein flipping on adenylyl cyclase (cAMP), phospholipase C (Ca²⁺) or a phosphodiesterase (cGMP), or the receptor's own kinase (tyrosine, JAK, serine) — and acts in seconds to minutes. A hormone that crosses (steroids, vitamins A and D, thyroid hormone) binds a cytoplasmic or nuclear receptor that is itself a transcription factor, and acts over hours to days.
>
> Every disease in this chapter is a switch stuck in one position: cholera toxin and the McCune–Albright mutation jam Gs **on**, pertussis toxin jams the brake Gi **off**, and BCR-ABL is a tyrosine kinase that never switches off. Identify the stuck switch and the clinical picture writes itself.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> A hormone (1st messenger) binds its receptor only while it is present; the G protein's own GTPase, phosphodiesterases breaking down cAMP/cGMP, and phosphatases removing phosphates terminate every signal within seconds. Receptor number and sensitivity are adjusted (internalisation, desensitisation) when hormone levels stay high.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A switch becomes partly stuck — an activating Gsα mutation in only some cells (mosaic), or a toxin acting on a limited patch of gut. Compensatory desensitisation and receptor internalisation blunt the response, so signs may be patchy: a few café-au-lait macules, mild watery stools, an early leucocytosis in CML with few symptoms.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> The stuck switch overwhelms the brakes: cholera's ↑↑ cAMP in enterocytes drives litres of isotonic "rice-water" stool → dehydration and death; pertussis gives paroxysmal whooping cough; McCune–Albright shows precocious puberty, polyostotic fibrous dysplasia and café-au-lait spots; BCR-ABL drives uncontrolled myeloid proliferation (CML with massive splenomegaly).
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → Switch mutated or poisoned (toxin, GNAS mutation, t(9;22)) → Early Changes (↑ second messenger / kinase activity) → Compensation (desensitisation, internalisation) → Decompensation (signal runs unchecked) → Full Clinical Syndrome
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Treatment either replaces what is lost (ORS and fluids in cholera) or blocks the stuck switch at its source (imatinib on BCR-ABL, JAK inhibitors on JAK2-driven disease). Knowing which second messenger a hormone uses also predicts drug interactions — e.g. desensitisation explains why continuous agonists stop working.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Cholera:** "Rice-water stools", "painless profuse watery diarrhoea", "stimulates Gs" → cholera toxin locks Gsα on → ↑↑ cAMP in intestinal crypt cells → Cl⁻ (and water) secretion; death is from dehydration, not tissue invasion.
>
> **Pertussis:** "Whooping cough", "paroxysms with inspiratory whoop", "inhibits Gi" → the brake on adenylyl cyclase is removed → ↑↑ cAMP; lymphocytosis is a classic accompaniment.
>
> **McCune–Albright syndrome:** "Precocious puberty + café-au-lait spots + bone lesions", "gain of function of Gsα" → mosaic activating GNAS mutation; endocrine glands behave as if constantly stimulated.
>
> **CML:** "Philadelphia chromosome", "BCR-ABL", "t(9;22)", "imatinib" → constitutively active tyrosine kinase → pathological growth and differentiation.
>
> **JAK-STAT:** "Growth hormone, prolactin, erythropoietin, leptin", "cytokine receptor" → receptors without their own kinase that borrow a Janus kinase.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> A seven-loop "snake" crossing the membrane = GPCR; a Y- or U-shaped dimer with phosphorylated tails = receptor tyrosine kinase; a bar labelled A/B–C–D–E–F = nuclear receptor; a channel opened by ACh = ligand-gated receptor.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Cholera dehydration grading drives fluid therapy (stool losses can exceed 1 L/hour); in CML the Philadelphia chromosome / BCR-ABL transcript level is followed to judge response to imatinib.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "All amino acid-derived hormones use membrane receptors"**
>
> **What students think:** Thyroid hormone is a tyrosine derivative, so like adrenaline it binds a membrane receptor.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Thyroid hormones (T3/T4) are lipophilic iodinated tyrosines; they enter the cell (via transporters) and bind a **nuclear** receptor that heterodimerises with RXR and changes transcription. That is why the page writes "Thyroid hormones" in colour in the amino-acid row and lists "Thyroid hormone receptor" among nuclear receptors.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Catecholamines → membrane (GPCR); thyroid hormones → nucleus. "Same parent (tyrosine), opposite address."
>
> **Trap 2: Mixing up cholera and pertussis toxins**
>
> **What students think:** Since both raise cAMP, both must stimulate Gs.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Cholera toxin keeps the accelerator (Gsα) pressed; pertussis toxin cuts the brake (Gi). Both end in ↑↑ cAMP, but by opposite actions on two different G proteins.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**C**holera = **C**an't stop going (Gs on); **P**ertussis = **P**aralysed brake (Gi off)."
>
> **Trap 3: Thinking insulin acts through a G protein or JAK**
>
> **What students think:** Insulin, being a peptide, must use a GPCR and cAMP.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Insulin's receptor is itself a tyrosine kinase (receptor tyrosine kinase); GH, prolactin, erythropoietin and leptin use JAK-STAT; glucagon uses a GPCR (Gs, cAMP). Three peptide hormones, three different receptor families.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Insulin = **TK**; "GH-PRL-EPO-Leptin" = **JAK**; activins/AMH = **serine kinase**; everything else peptide = mostly GPCR.
>
> **Trap 4: Vasopressin has one receptor and one messenger**
>
> **What students think:** ADH always works through cAMP.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** V2 receptors in the collecting duct use Gs → cAMP → aquaporin-2 (water reabsorption); V1 receptors on vascular smooth muscle use Gq → Ca²⁺ (vasoconstriction). The page lists vasopressin in both the cAMP and the Ca²⁺ trees for this reason.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "V**2** = **2**nd messenger cAMP in the kidney; V**1** = vessels, calcium."
>
> **Trap 5: cGMP behaves like Ca²⁺**
>
> **What students think:** All second messengers make muscle contract.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** cGMP is the **opposite** of Ca²⁺ — nitric oxide and natriuretic peptides raise cGMP, which relaxes smooth muscle and dilates vessels (the basis of nitrates and sildenafil). In the retina, light *lowers* cGMP via transducin and PDE.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Ca²⁺ contracts, cGMP relaxes."

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **Amino acid derivatives & peptides cannot cross** the membrane → **extracellular receptors**; **steroids and vitamins A/D cross** → **intracellular receptors**.
> 2. **Catecholamines** = epinephrine, norepinephrine, dopamine (from tyrosine); **thyroid hormones** are the tyrosine derivatives that act on **nuclear receptors**.
> 3. Peptide sizes: **TRH = 3 AAs**, **insulin = 51 AAs**, **parathormone = 84 AAs**.
> 4. Extracellular receptor types: **GPCR**, **tyrosine kinase**, **Janus kinase**, **serine kinase**.
> 5. GPCR = **serpentine / 7-transmembrane** receptor; G protein **α subunit has GTPase activity**.
> 6. **Gs ↑ cAMP, Gi ↓ cAMP, Gq ↑ Ca²⁺, Gt (transducin, rods) → PDE → ↓ cGMP**.
> 7. cAMP effects: **glycogenolysis** (glucagon, epinephrine), **↑ HCO₃⁻** (secretin = **nature's antacid**), **↑ aquaporin-2** (vasopressin V2).
> 8. **Cholera toxin stimulates Gs** → diarrhoea, dehydration, death; **pertussis toxin inhibits Gi** → whooping cough — both ↑↑ cAMP.
> 9. **Gain of function of Gsα → McCune Albright**: precocious puberty, skeletal malformations, café au lait spots.
> 10. Ca²⁺ effects: **uterine contraction (oxytocin)**, **vasoconstriction (V1, angiotensin)**, **exocytosis (TRH, CCK)**; **cGMP = opposite → relaxation & vasodilatation (NO, ANP)**.
> 11. **Insulin → TK receptor**; **BCR-ABL (Philadelphia) → CML → imatinib mesylate**.
> 12. **JAK-STAT: GH, prolactin, erythropoietin, leptin**; **serine kinase: activins, AMH** (reproduction).
> 13. Nuclear receptor: **A/B (AF-1) · C = DNA binding · D = hinge · E = ligand binding · F (AF-2)**; cytoplasmic = **MR, GR, AR, vit D**; nuclear = **thyroid, vit A, estrogen, progesterone**.
> 14. Safety against overactivity: **internalisation, desensitisation, inactivation**; **ligand-gated = fast (nicotinic, GABA A/C)**, **metabotropic = slow (muscarinic, GABA B)**.

[^1]: Thyroid hormones (T3, T4) are written in colour in the amino-acid row because they are the exception: though made from tyrosine, they are lipophilic, enter cells through transporters (e.g. MCT8) and act on a **nuclear** receptor — the page lists "Thyroid hormone receptor" among the nuclear receptors further down.
[^2]: TRH is a tripeptide (pyroGlu–His–Pro-NH₂); insulin's 51 amino acids form an A chain (21) and a B chain (30) joined by two disulfide bonds — the two chains drawn in the page's figure; PTH is a single chain of 84 amino acids whose first 34 carry full activity (the basis of teriparatide).
[^3]: Strictly, α, β and γ are the subunits of the **heterotrimeric G protein** that sits on the inner face of the receptor, not of the seven-transmembrane receptor itself; the page's heading "Subunits of the receptor" refers to this G protein.
[^4]: Textbook order: hormone binds → the receptor makes Gα release GDP and take up GTP → α-GTP separates from βγ and activates an effector enzyme (adenylyl cyclase, phospholipase C or phosphodiesterase) → second messenger made → α's own GTPase hydrolyses GTP to GDP, which **ends** the signal and lets α rejoin βγ. The page's simplified chain is kept in the body.
[^5]: The Gq route: Gq activates **phospholipase C**, which splits membrane PIP₂ into **IP₃** (opens Ca²⁺ channels of the endoplasmic reticulum → ↑ cytosolic Ca²⁺) and **DAG** (activates protein kinase C) — hence "IP3–DAG–Ca²⁺".
[^6]: In rods, light converts rhodopsin, which activates transducin (Gt); transducin activates phosphodiesterase-6, cGMP falls, cGMP-gated Na⁺ channels close and the photoreceptor hyperpolarises — the only sense organ where the stimulus *hyperpolarises* the receptor cell.
[^7]: Vasopressin on **V2** receptors of collecting-duct principal cells → Gs → cAMP → protein kinase A → insertion of **aquaporin-2** water channels in the luminal membrane. Its vascular **V1** receptors use Gq/Ca²⁺ instead (see the Calcium tree).
[^8]: ⚠ The subscripts after "Stimulates G" and "Inhibits G" are blurred on the page; standard texts give **cholera toxin → ADP-ribosylates Gsα** (keeps it permanently ON, its GTPase blocked) and **pertussis toxin → ADP-ribosylates Giα** (keeps the inhibitory brake OFF), so both raise cAMP. In the gut, cAMP opens CFTR Cl⁻ channels → Cl⁻, Na⁺ and water pour into the lumen → "rice-water" stools. "Ac" = adenylyl cyclase.
[^9]: McCune–Albright syndrome: a **post-zygotic (mosaic) activating mutation of GNAS** (the gene for Gsα) → cells carrying it behave as if permanently stimulated: polyostotic fibrous dysplasia, café-au-lait macules with jagged "coast of Maine" borders, and endocrine hyperfunction (precocious puberty, hyperthyroidism, GH excess, Cushing's).
[^10]: Nitric oxide raises cGMP by activating **soluble** guanylyl cyclase in smooth muscle (the target of nitrates); natriuretic peptides bind a **membrane guanylyl cyclase receptor** (NPR-A). Sildenafil prolongs cGMP action by blocking PDE-5.
[^11]: The insulin receptor is a pre-formed α₂β₂ tetramer; insulin binding makes the β chains phosphorylate each other (autophosphorylation) and then insulin-receptor substrates (IRS-1/2), leading to GLUT-4 translocation, glycogen synthesis and growth effects.
[^12]: t(9;22) fuses BCR (chromosome 22) with ABL (chromosome 9); the BCR-ABL protein is a constitutively active **cytoplasmic (non-receptor)** tyrosine kinase. Imatinib (Gleevec) occupies its ATP-binding pocket. The page's wording "blocks TK receptor" is kept.
[^13]: Serine (threonine) kinase receptors belong to the **TGF-β superfamily** — TGF-β, activins, inhibins, AMH and bone morphogenetic proteins; they signal through SMAD proteins. AMH from Sertoli cells makes the Müllerian (paramesonephric) ducts regress in the male fetus.
[^14]: The DNA-binding domain (C) holds two **zinc fingers** that recognise a specific DNA sequence, the hormone-response element; this domain is the most conserved part of the whole nuclear-receptor family.
[^15]: The page places the **vitamin D receptor** with the cytoplasmic group; many textbooks classify VDR as nuclear (it pairs with RXR on DNA), alongside thyroid hormone, retinoic acid (vitamin A) and oestrogen receptors. In general, unliganded MR, GR and AR sit in the cytoplasm bound to heat-shock protein 90, while the TR/RXR family sits on DNA in the nucleus.
[^16]: Mechanisms in practice: **desensitisation** = receptor phosphorylation by G protein-coupled receptor kinases followed by β-arrestin binding (minutes); **internalisation / down-regulation** = endocytosis of receptors, which are then recycled or destroyed (hours). [^ety-receptor]: **receptor** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *capere*, "to take" → *recipere*, "to receive" + *-tor*, the agent → the receiver; a protein that receives a specific chemical (ligand) and turns it into a cell response.
[^ety-messenger]: **messenger** — Old French *messager*, from Latin *missus*, "sent" (*mittere*, to send). [^ety-extracellular]: **extracellular / intracellular** — Latin *extra*, "outside" / *intra*, "within" + *cellula*, "a small room" (diminutive of *cella*, a storeroom) + *-ar*, "relating to" → outside / inside the cell. **Differentiate:** *intracellular* (inside a cell) · *intercellular* (*inter-*, between cells) · *transcellular* (across a cell).
[^ety-amino-acid]: **amino acid** — *amine* (from *ammonia*, named after the salt found near the temple of the Egyptian god Ammon) + Latin *acidus*, "sour" → a molecule carrying both an amine (–NH₂) and an acid (–COOH) group, the building block of peptides.
[^ety-tyrosine]: **tyrosine** — Greek *tyros*, "cheese" + *-ine* → first isolated by Justus von Liebig (1846) from casein, the protein of cheese; the parent of catecholamines and thyroid hormones.
[^ety-epinephrine]: **epinephrine** — Greek *epi-*, "upon" + *nephros*, "kidney" + *-ine* → the substance from the gland upon the kidney; *adrenaline* is the same idea in Latin (*ad-*, "at" + *renes*, "kidneys").
[^ety-norepinephrine]: **norepinephrine** — *nor-* + epinephrine; *nor-* is classically explained as German *N ohne Radikal*, "nitrogen without its (methyl) radical" — noradrenaline is adrenaline minus the methyl group on the nitrogen.
[^ety-dopamine]: **dopamine** — from **DOPA** (3,4-**d**ihydr**o**xy**p**henyl**a**lanine), its precursor, + *amine* → the amine made from DOPA; the first catecholamine in the chain tyrosine → DOPA → dopamine → noradrenaline → adrenaline.
[^ety-catecholamine]: **catecholamine** — *catechol* (from *catechu*, a resin of the Acacia catechu tree, Malay *kachu*) + *amine* → an amine attached to a catechol ring (a benzene ring with two neighbouring –OH groups): dopamine, noradrenaline, adrenaline.
[^ety-thyroid]: **thyroid** — Greek *thyreos*, "a large oblong shield (shaped like a door, *thyra*)" + *-eidēs*, "like" → shield-shaped; first named for the thyroid cartilage, then given to the gland by Thomas Wharton (1656).
[^ety-tryptophan]: **tryptophan** — *trypsin* + Greek *phainein*, "to show" → "shown by trypsin": isolated by Frederick Gowland Hopkins (1901) from a tryptic digest of protein; parent of serotonin and melatonin.
[^ety-serotonin]: **serotonin** — Latin *serum* + Greek *tonos*, "tension, tone" + *-in* → the serum factor that gives vessels tone (constricts them); named by Rapport, Green and Page (1948). Chemically 5-hydroxytryptamine (5-HT).
[^ety-melatonin]: **melatonin** — Greek *melas*, "black" (*melanin*) + *-tonin* (from serotonin) → the serotonin-like hormone that blanches the dark melanophores of frog skin; isolated from the pineal gland by Aaron Lerner (1958).
[^ety-histamine]: **histidine / histamine** — Greek *histos*, "web, tissue" + *-ine* / *amine* → the amino acid found in tissues and its decarboxylated amine (histidine → histamine, by histidine decarboxylase).
[^ety-peptide]: **peptide** — Greek *peptos*, "cooked, digested" (*peptein*, to digest) + *-ide* → the fragments obtained by digesting protein; now any chain of amino acids. **Differentiate:** *peptide* (short chain, roughly < 50 amino acids) · *polypeptide* · *protein* (long, folded chain).
[^ety-protein]: **protein** — Greek *prōteios*, "of the first rank, primary" (*prōtos*, first) → the "primary" substance of living matter; name suggested by Jöns Jacob Berzelius to Gerardus Mulder in 1838.
[^ety-thyrotropin]: **thyrotropin (TRH, TSH)** — *thyro-* (thyroid) + Greek *tropē*, "a turning" + *-in* → the hormone that "turns toward" (stimulates) the thyroid; TRH = the hypothalamic hormone that releases thyrotropin (TSH).
[^ety-insulin]: **insulin** — Latin *insula*, "island" + *-in* → the hormone of the islets of Langerhans; name proposed by Jean de Meyer (1909) and used by Banting and Best after its isolation (1921–22).
[^ety-parathormone]: **parathormone** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + *thyroid* + *hormone* → hormone of the glands beside the thyroid (parathyroid hormone, PTH).
[^ety-cholesterol]: **cholesterol** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" + *stereos*, "solid" + *-ol*, alcohol → the solid bile alcohol first crystallised from gallstones; the four-ring parent of all steroid hormones and vitamin D.
[^ety-steroid]: **steroid** — *sterol* (from *stereos*, solid + *-ol*) + Greek *-eidēs*, "like" → sterol-like: molecules built on the four-ring cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene nucleus.
[^ety-estrogen]: **estrogen (oestrogen)** — Greek *oistros*, "gadfly; frenzy, heat" + *-gen*, "producing" → that which produces oestrus (the period of sexual receptivity in animals).
[^ety-testosterone]: **testosterone** — Latin *testis* + *sterol* + *-one* (a ketone group) → the ketone steroid of the testis; isolated and named by Ernst Laqueur's group (1935).
[^ety-aldosterone]: **aldosterone** — *aldehyde* + *sterol* + *-one* → the steroid with an aldehyde group at carbon 18 — the feature that sets it apart from corticosterone.
[^ety-cortisol]: **cortisol** — Latin *cortex*, "bark, outer layer" + *-ol*, alcohol → the alcohol steroid of the adrenal cortex (hydrocortisone); **differentiate** *cortex* (outer layer) vs *medulla* (inner core, which makes adrenaline).
[^ety-vitamin]: **vitamin** — Latin *vita*, "life" + *amine* → Casimir Funk's (1912) "vital amine"; the final *-e* was dropped when most vitamins proved not to be amines. Vitamins A and D are fat-soluble and act like hormones on intracellular receptors.
[^ety-kinase]: **kinase** — Greek *kinein*, "to move" + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that moves (transfers) a phosphate group from ATP onto a protein (phosphorylation). **Differentiate:** *kinase* (adds phosphate) · *phosphatase* (removes it) · *phosphorylase* (splits a bond using phosphate, e.g. glycogen phosphorylase).
[^ety-janus]: **Janus kinase (JAK)** — named after **Janus**, the two-faced Roman god of doorways, because each JAK has two near-identical kinase domains facing opposite ways (one active, one "pseudokinase"); the acronym was first read jokingly as "just another kinase".
[^ety-serpentine]: **serpentine** — Latin *serpens*, "creeping, a snake" (*serpere*, to creep) + *-ine* → snake-like: the receptor chain snakes in and out of the membrane seven times.
[^ety-gtpase]: **GTPase** — **GTP** (guanosine triphosphate) + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that hydrolyses GTP to GDP; in Gα it is the self-timer that switches the G protein off.
[^ety-g-protein]: **G protein** — **g**uanine nucleotide-binding protein; *guanine* from *guano* (Quechua *wanu*, sea-bird droppings) where it was first isolated. Alfred Gilman and Martin Rodbell shared the 1994 Nobel Prize for discovering G proteins.
[^ety-hydrolysis]: **hydrolysis** — Greek *hydōr*, "water" + *lysis*, "loosening" → splitting a bond by adding water (GTP + H₂O → GDP + Pi). **Differentiate:** *hydrolysis* (splitting by water) · *hydration* (adding water without splitting) · *dehydration* (water loss).
[^ety-cyclic]: **cyclic AMP / GMP** — Greek *kyklos*, "circle" → the phosphate is bonded to two carbons of the ribose (3′ and 5′), closing a ring; *AMP* = adenosine monophosphate (*adenine* from Greek *adēn*, "gland" — first isolated from pancreas), *GMP* = guanosine monophosphate.
[^ety-adenylyl-cyclase]: **adenylyl cyclase** — *adenylyl* (the AMP group) + *cyclase* (Greek *kyklos*, circle + *-ase*) → the enzyme that turns ATP into the ring-shaped cyclic AMP. Older name *adenylate cyclase*.
[^ety-transducin]: **transducin** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *ducere*, "to lead" + *-in* → the protein that leads the light signal across from rhodopsin to phosphodiesterase. **Signal transduction** = converting one form of signal into another (light or hormone → chemical inside the cell).
[^ety-phosphodiesterase]: **phosphodiesterase (PDE)** — *phospho-* (phosphate) + *di-*, "two" + *ester* + *-ase* → the enzyme that cuts the phosphate bonded by two ester links in cAMP/cGMP, inactivating them (PDE-5 is blocked by sildenafil; PDE by caffeine/theophylline).
[^ety-glycogenolysis]: **glycogenolysis** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" + *-gen*, "producing" (*glycogen*, "sugar-maker") + *lysis*, "breaking down" → breakdown of glycogen to glucose. **Differentiate:** *glycogenesis* (making glycogen) · *glycogenolysis* (breaking glycogen) · *gluconeogenesis* (*neo-*, new — making new glucose from non-carbohydrates) · *glycolysis* (breaking glucose to pyruvate).
[^ety-glucagon]: **glucagon** — *gluc(ose)* + Greek *agōn*, "leading, driving" → the hormone that drives blood glucose up; named by Kimball and Murlin (1923).
[^ety-secretion]: **secretion / secretin** — Latin *se-*, "apart" + *cernere*, "to sift, separate" → *secernere*, to set apart + *-ion*, the process → making and releasing a useful product. **Differentiate:** *secretion* (useful product released) · *excretion* (*ex-*, out — waste removed from the body) · *exocytosis* (the cellular mechanism of release). *Secretin* = the first hormone discovered (Bayliss & Starling, 1902).
[^ety-reabsorption]: **reabsorption** — Latin *re-*, "back, again" + *ab-*, "away" + *sorbere*, "to suck" + *-ion* → sucking back again: water taken back from the tubular fluid into blood. **Differentiate:** *absorption* (gut lumen → blood) · *reabsorption* (tubule → blood) · *adsorption* (sticking onto a surface).
[^ety-vasopressin]: **vasopressin** — Latin *vas*, "vessel" + *pressus*, "pressed" (pressor) + *-in* → the hormone that raises vascular pressure; same hormone as **ADH** (*anti-* + *diuresis*), named for its kidney action.
[^ety-aquaporin]: **aquaporin** — Latin *aqua*, "water" + *porus*, "passage, pore" + *-in* → a water-channel protein; discovered by Peter Agre (Nobel Prize 2003). AQP-2 is the vasopressin-regulated one in the collecting duct.
[^ety-toxin]: **toxin** — Greek *toxikon (pharmakon)*, "(poison) for arrows", from *toxon*, "bow" → a poison of biological origin. **Differentiate:** *toxin* (biological poison) · *toxoid* (inactivated toxin used as a vaccine) · *venom* (toxin injected by an animal).
[^ety-cholera]: **cholera** — Greek *cholera*, from *cholē*, "bile" → a disease once thought to be a flux of bile; caused by *Vibrio cholerae*, whose toxin acts on Gs.
[^ety-diarrhoea]: **diarrhoea** — Greek *dia-*, "through" + *rhoia*, "a flow" (*rhein*, to flow) → a flowing through: frequent loose stools. **Differentiate:** *diarrhoea* (flow through the bowel) · *dysentery* (*dys-* bad + *entera* bowels — bloody, mucoid, painful stools from invasion).
[^ety-dehydration]: **dehydration** — Latin *de-*, "away, removal" + Greek *hydōr*, "water" + *-ation*, the process → removal of water from the body. **Differentiate:** *dehydration* (water loss) vs *volume depletion / hypovolaemia* (loss of salt and water from the ECF — the cholera picture).
[^ety-pertussis]: **pertussis** — Latin *per-*, "thoroughly, intensely" + *tussis*, "cough" → an intense cough (whooping cough), caused by *Bordetella pertussis*, whose toxin acts on Gi.
[^ety-precocious]: **precocious** — Latin *praecox*, "ripened early" (*prae-*, before + *coquere*, to cook, ripen) → developing before the usual time. **Precocious puberty** = signs of puberty before 8 years in girls or 9 in boys.
[^ety-puberty]: **puberty** — Latin *pubertas*, from *puber*, "adult; covered with (pubic) hair" → the stage of becoming sexually mature. Same root as *pubes* and *pubic*.
[^ety-cafe-au-lait]: **café au lait** — French, "coffee with milk" → the light-brown colour of these flat pigmented macules; in McCune–Albright they have jagged "coast of Maine" borders, in neurofibromatosis-1 smooth "coast of California" borders.
[^ety-mccune-albright]: **McCune–Albright syndrome** — Donovan James McCune, American paediatrician, described it in 1936 and Fuller Albright, American endocrinologist (1900–1969), in 1937: the triad of polyostotic fibrous dysplasia, café-au-lait spots and endocrine hyperfunction (precocious puberty).
[^ety-oxytocin]: **oxytocin** — Greek *oxys*, "swift, sharp" + *tokos*, "childbirth" → swift birth; named by Henry Dale (1906) for its effect on the uterus. Its receptor uses Gq → Ca²⁺ → uterine contraction.
[^ety-vasoconstriction]: **vasoconstriction / vasodilatation** — Latin *vas*, "vessel" + *constringere*, "to draw tight" (*con-* together + *stringere* to bind) / *dilatare*, "to spread wide" + *-ion* → narrowing / widening of a vessel. **Differentiate:** *vasodilatation* (active widening of a vessel) · *dilation* (general widening, e.g. of the cervix) · *distension* (stretching by internal pressure).
[^ety-angiotensin]: **angiotensin** — Greek *angeion*, "vessel" + Latin *tensio*, "stretching, tension" + *-in* → the substance that tenses (constricts) vessels; the name joined Braun-Menéndez's "hypertensin" and Page's "angiotonin" in 1958.
[^ety-exocytosis]: **exocytosis / endocytosis** — Greek *exō*, "outside" / *endon*, "within" + *kytos*, "hollow vessel, cell" + *-osis*, "process" → the process of moving material out of / into the cell in membrane-bound vesicles. **Differentiate:** *exocytosis* (out) · *endocytosis* (in) · *transcytosis* (in one side, out the other).
[^ety-cholecystokinin]: **cholecystokinin (CCK)** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" + *kystis*, "bladder" + *kinein*, "to move" + *-in* → the hormone that moves (contracts) the gall bladder; Ivy and Oldberg (1928).
[^ety-natriuretic]: **natriuretic** — Latin *natrium*, "sodium" (from Arabic *natrūn*, soda — hence the symbol Na) + Greek *ourēsis*, "urination" + *-ic* → promoting sodium loss in the urine. **Differentiate:** *natriuresis* (salt loss in urine) · *diuresis* (more urine volume) · *aquaresis* (water-only loss).
[^ety-translocation]: **translocation** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *locare*, "to place" + *-ion* → moving a piece of one chromosome to another; in CML a reciprocal t(9;22). **Differentiate:** *translocation* (piece moved between chromosomes) · *deletion* (piece lost) · *inversion* (piece flipped in place).
[^ety-philadelphia]: **Philadelphia chromosome** — named after the city where Peter Nowell and David Hungerford found the small chromosome 22 in CML cells in 1960 — the first chromosomal change linked to a specific cancer; Janet Rowley showed in 1973 that it is a 9;22 translocation.
[^ety-differentiation]: **differentiation** — Latin *differre*, "to carry apart, to differ" (*dis-*, apart + *ferre*, to carry) + *-ation* → the process by which a cell becomes specialised. **Differentiate:** *differentiation* (becoming specialised) · *proliferation* (*proles* offspring + *ferre* — multiplying) · *dedifferentiation / anaplasia* (losing specialisation, as in cancer).
[^ety-leukaemia]: **leukaemia (CML)** — Greek *leukos*, "white" + *haima*, "blood" + *-ia* → "white blood", Rudolf Virchow's name (1847) for blood crowded with white cells; **CML** = chronic myeloid (*myelos*, marrow) leukaemia.
[^ety-imatinib]: **imatinib** — a drug name built on the stem *-tinib*, which marks a **tyrosine kinase inhibitor** (imatinib, dasatinib, nilotinib); *mesylate* = methanesulfonate, the salt form. Approved in 2001 as the first designed kinase inhibitor.
[^ety-cytokine]: **cytokine** — Greek *kytos*, "hollow vessel, cell" + *kinein*, "to move" → "cell movers": small proteins that carry signals between (mainly immune) cells — interleukins, interferons, erythropoietin.
[^ety-transcription]: **transcription** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *scribere*, "to write" + *-ion* → copying across: the DNA code is copied into messenger RNA. **Differentiate:** *transcription* (DNA → RNA, in the nucleus) · *translation* (RNA → protein, on ribosomes) · *replication* (DNA → DNA).
[^ety-prolactin]: **prolactin** — Latin *pro-*, "for" + *lac*, "milk" + *-in* → the hormone for milk production (anterior pituitary); uses the JAK-STAT receptor like growth hormone.
[^ety-erythropoietin]: **erythropoietin (EPO)** — Greek *erythros*, "red" + *poiein*, "to make" + *-in* → the red-cell-making hormone, from the peritubular cells of the kidney.
[^ety-leptin]: **leptin** — Greek *leptos*, "thin, slender" + *-in* → the satiety hormone from adipose tissue that tells the hypothalamus fat stores are full; discovered by Jeffrey Friedman's group (1994) in *ob/ob* mice.
[^ety-activin]: **activin / inhibin** — named for their opposite actions on pituitary FSH: *activin* activates FSH release, *inhibin* (Latin *inhibere*, to hold back) inhibits it; both are gonadal peptides of the TGF-β family.
[^ety-mullerian]: **Müllerian (anti-Müllerian hormone, AMH)** — after **Johannes Peter Müller** (1801–1858), German physiologist and anatomist who described the paramesonephric ducts in 1830; AMH (Müllerian-inhibiting substance) from Sertoli cells makes these ducts regress in the male.
[^ety-heat-shock]: **heat shock proteins (HSP)** — named because their synthesis jumps when cells are heated (Ferruccio Ritossa, 1962, in *Drosophila*); they are **chaperones** (French, "a protective escort") that keep proteins folded — HSP90 holds steroid receptors inactive until the hormone arrives.
[^ety-ligand]: **ligand** — Latin *ligare*, "to bind" → any molecule that binds specifically to a receptor (hormone, neurotransmitter, drug). Same root as *ligament* and *ligature*.
[^ety-mineralocorticoid]: **mineralocorticoid / glucocorticoid** — *mineral* (Na⁺, K⁺ salts) or *gluc(ose)* + *cortic-* (adrenal cortex) + *-oid*, "like" → cortical steroids named for what they regulate: salt balance (aldosterone) or glucose (cortisol).
[^ety-androgen]: **androgen** — Greek *anēr, andros*, "man" + *-gen*, "producing" → that which produces male characteristics (testosterone, DHT).
[^ety-progesterone]: **progesterone** — Latin *pro-*, "for" + *gestare*, "to carry (in the womb)" + *sterol* + *-one* → the steroid that favours (maintains) pregnancy.
[^ety-internalization]: **internalization** — Latin *internus*, "inner" + *-ize*, "to make" + *-ation* → making something go inside: receptors are pulled into the cell by endocytosis so fewer remain on the surface (down-regulation).
[^ety-desensitization]: **desensitization** — Latin *de-*, "away, reversing" + *sentire*, "to feel" + *-ize* + *-ation* → taking away the ability to feel; the receptor is still present but responds less (e.g. phosphorylated and capped by β-arrestin). **Differentiate:** *desensitisation* (less response per receptor, fast) · *down-regulation / internalisation* (fewer receptors, slow) · *tachyphylaxis* (rapidly diminishing drug response) · *tolerance* (gradual need for higher doses).
[^ety-inactivation]: **inactivation** — Latin *in-*, "not" + *activus*, "active" (*agere*, to do) + *-ation* → the process of making not active; the receptor is switched off or destroyed.
[^ety-nicotinic]: **nicotinic** — from **nicotine**, named after **Jean Nicot** (1530–1600), French ambassador to Portugal who sent tobacco to Paris in 1560; nicotinic ACh receptors are the ones nicotine stimulates (ligand-gated cation channels at the NMJ and autonomic ganglia).
[^ety-gaba]: **GABA** — **g**amma-**a**mino**b**utyric **a**cid; *butyric* from Latin *butyrum*, "butter" (butyric acid smells of rancid butter); gamma = the amine sits on the third (γ) carbon. The main inhibitory transmitter of the brain.
[^ety-metabotropic]: **metabotropic / ionotropic** — Greek *metabolē*, "change" + *tropos*, "a turning" → a receptor that acts by turning on a chain of chemical (metabolic) changes inside the cell (G protein, 2nd messenger); *ionotropic* (*ion* + *tropos*) acts by opening an ion channel directly. **Differentiate** from cardiac *inotropic* (*is, inos*, fibre — force of contraction).
[^ety-muscarinic]: **muscarinic** — from **muscarine**, an alkaloid of the fly-agaric mushroom *Amanita muscaria* (Latin *musca*, "fly" — used to kill flies) → muscarinic ACh receptors are the G protein-coupled ones that muscarine stimulates (heart, glands, smooth muscle).
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: receptor (takes back), reabsorption, reflex. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards, behind (retroperitoneal) · **pre-** before.
[^pre-extra]: **extra-** — outside, beyond: extracellular, extrapyramidal, extrasystole. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek: exocytosis) · **ex-** out of (excretion) · **intra-** within.
[^pre-intra]: **intra-** — within, inside: intracellular, intravascular, intramuscular. Don't confuse with **inter-** between (intercellular, interstitial) · **intro-** inward · **endo-** within (Greek equivalent).
[^suf-ine]: **-ine** — chemical suffix for amino acids, alkaloids and amines: tyrosine, histidine, epinephrine, serotonin's relatives, nicotine, muscarine. Don't confuse with **-in** proteins and peptides (insulin, transducin, secretin) · **-ase** enzymes · **-ose** sugars (glucose).
[^pre-epi]: **epi-** — upon, above, over: epinephrine (on the kidney), epithelium, epidermis. Don't confuse with **endo-** within · **ecto-** outer · **hypo-** below.
[^suf-oid]: **-oid** — like, resembling: thyroid (shield-like), steroid (sterol-like), mineralocorticoid, toxoid. Don't confuse with **-ode** a path or electrode · **-ide** a chemical compound (peptide, chloride).
[^suf-tropic]: **-tropin / -tropic** — "turning toward", acting on: thyrotropin (TSH), corticotropin (ACTH), gonadotropin; also metabotropic and ionotropic receptors. Don't confuse with **-trophic / -trophin** nourishing, growth (*trophē*, food — e.g. somatotrophin, hypertrophy) — the two are often used interchangeably for pituitary hormones but mean different things.
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, alongside: parathormone (beside the thyroid), paracrine, paramesonephric. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before · **post-** after.
[^suf-gen]: **-gen / -genic** — producing, giving rise to: estrogen (produces oestrus), androgen, glycogen, antigen. Don't confuse with **-genesis** the process of formation (glycogenesis) · **-gnosis** knowledge (diagnosis).
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: kinase, GTPase, phosphodiesterase, ATPase, lipase (from *diastase*, the first enzyme named, 1833). Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (glucose, ribose) · **-osis** a process or condition.
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through, beyond: transmembrane, transducin, translocation, transcription, transcytosis. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **dia-** through (Greek equivalent: diarrhoea) · **per-** through, thoroughly.
[^pre-hydro]: **hydro- / hydr-** — water: hydrolysis, dehydration, hydrostatic, hydrophilic. Don't confuse with **hygro-** moisture (hygroscopic) · **hyper-** over · **hypo-** under.
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis / -lytic** — loosening, breaking down, dissolving: hydrolysis, glycogenolysis, haemolysis, thrombolysis. Don't confuse with **-lyte** a substance that can be dissolved (electrolyte) · **-lithiasis** stones.
[^pre-di]: **di-** — two, double: phosphodiesterase, dimer, disaccharide, diploid. Don't confuse with **dia-** through (diarrhoea, dialysis) · **dis- / dys-** apart / bad (dysentery).
[^pre-glyco]: **glyco- / gluco-** — sweet, sugar, glucose: glycogenolysis, glycogen, glucagon, glucocorticoid, glycosylation. Don't confuse with **glyc-** in *glycine* (named for its sweet taste, an amino acid) · **gly-** in *glyph* (carving).
[^pre-vaso]: **vaso-** — vessel (Latin *vas*): vasopressin, vasoconstriction, vasodilatation, vasa recta. Don't confuse with **vas deferens** (the duct) · **angio-** vessel (Greek equivalent: angiotensin) · **veno-** vein.
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, across, apart: diarrhoea (flow through), dialysis, diaphragm, diagnosis. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dys-** bad, difficult.
[^suf-rrhoea]: **-rrhoea / -rrhea** — flow, discharge: diarrhoea, rhinorrhoea, amenorrhoea, galactorrhoea. Don't confuse with **-rrhage** bursting, heavy bleeding (haemorrhage) · **-rrhaphy** suturing · **-rrhexis** rupture.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, away, removal, reversal: dehydration, desensitization, depolarisation, deglutition. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart · **di-** two · **dys-** bad.
[^pre-per]: **per-** — through, thoroughly, intensely: pertussis (intense cough), permeable, perfusion. Don't confuse with **peri-** around (Greek: pericardium) · **pre-** before · **para-** beside.
[^pre-prae]: **pre- / prae-** — before (in time or place): precocious, precursor, prevertebral. Don't confuse with **pro-** forward, for · **post-** after · **per-** through.
[^pre-exo]: **exo-** — outside, outward (Greek): exocytosis, exocrine, exogenous. Don't confuse with **ex-** out of (Latin: excretion) · **extra-** beyond · **ecto-** outer layer (ectoderm) · **endo-** within.
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a process, or a (usually abnormal) condition: exocytosis, endocytosis, fibrosis, thrombosis. Don't confuse with **-asis / -iasis** a diseased state (lithiasis) · **-itis** inflammation · **-ation** an action.
[^suf-aemia]: **-aemia / -emia** — a condition of the blood: leukaemia, anaemia, hyperkalaemia, hyponatraemia. Don't confuse with **haemo- / haemato-** blood as a prefix (haemolysis) · **-uria** a condition of the urine.
[^pre-cyto]: **cyto- / -cyte** — cell (Greek *kytos*, hollow vessel): cytokine, cytoplasm, cytoskeleton, erythrocyte. Don't confuse with **cysto-** bladder, sac (cholecystokinin, cystitis) · **histo-** tissue.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — for, forward, before: prolactin (for milk), progesterone (for gestation), prohormone. Don't confuse with **pre- / prae-** before (strictly in time) · **post-** after · **proto-** first.
[^pre-erythro]: **erythro-** — red: erythropoietin, erythrocyte, erythema. Don't confuse with **leuko-** white (leukaemia) · **xantho-** yellow · **cyano-** blue.
[^suf-poiesis]: **-poiesis / -poietin** — making, production: erythropoietin, haematopoiesis, thrombopoietin. Don't confuse with **-plasia** formation, growth of tissue (hyperplasia) · **-genesis** origin, formation · **-phoresis** carrying (electrophoresis).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against, opposite: anti-Müllerian hormone, antidiuretic, antacid, antibody. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (antenatal) · **contra-** against (Latin: contraindication).
[^pre-in]: **in- (im-, il-, ir-)** — two different Latin prefixes: (1) "not" — inactivation, insensible, impermeable; (2) "in, into" — inflow, infusion, insertion. Context decides; don't confuse with **intra-** within · **inter-** between.
[^pre-meta]: **meta-** — change, after, beyond: metabotropic, metabolism, metaplasia, metastasis. Don't confuse with **meso-** middle · **para-** beside · **peri-** around.
