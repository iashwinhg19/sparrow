# Glomerular Filtration Rate

## Glomerular Capillaries and GFR
### Renal corpuscle
- Afferent arteriole → more thicker
- Efferent arteriole → drains the glomerulus
- Glomerular capillaries
  - Only capillary in between 2 arterioles
- Bowman's capsule → receives plasma / filtrate
- Peritubular capillaries → around the tubule
- Four processes
  - Filtration
  - Reabsorption
  - Secretion
  - Excretion → urinary excretion
- Excretion = Filtration − Reabsorption + Secretion

### Nephron
- Glomerulus → group of blood vessels
- Functional unit of kidney
- 1 million / kidney
- Loss of nephron → other nephrons compensate

### Ultrafiltrate
- Filtered fluid = ultrafiltrate
  - Proteins not filtered, only plasma
- Normal GFR = 125 mL/min = 180 L/day

### Why proteins are not filtered
- Heparan sulphate
  - Lines glomerular capillaries
  - Negatively charged → repels protein anions
  - Renal disease → lost → proteins filtered
- Fenestrated endothelium
  - Small gap between cells (page: 4–8 mm; read nm)
  - Restricts protein passage
- Slit diaphragm
  - Foot process of podocytes → reinforce fenestrations
  - → smaller gaps → slit diaphragm
  - Restricts protein passage
- Integrity of slit diaphragm
  - Nephrin → congenital Finnish nephrotic syndrome (NPHS1)
  - Podocin → steroid resistant nephrotic syndrome
  - α-actinin → FSGS

### Filtration forces
- Starling forces
  - Pc = hydrostatic pressure in glomerular capillaries
  - πc = oncotic pressure in glomerular capillary
  - Pb = hydrostatic pressure in Bowman's capsule
- Favouring → Pc = 60 mm Hg
- Opposing → πc = 32 mm Hg, Pb = 18 mm Hg
- Net filtration pressure
  - = favouring − opposing
  - = Pc − (πc + Pb)
  - = 60 − (32 + 18) = 10 mm Hg
- Applied → renal calculi → ↑ Pb → ↓ GFR

### Measurement of GFR
- Criteria of the marker
  - Freely filtered
  - No reabsorption
  - No secretion
  - Non toxic
- Inulin
  - Clearance = 125 mL/min = normal GFR
  - Gold standard → inulin filtered = inulin excreted
  - IV inulin → plasma → filtered → kidneys → excreted → urine
  - GFR × P = U × V → GFR = UV/P
  - ↑ P → ↓ GFR
- Creatinine
  - Plasma creatinine → clinical estimation
  - ↑ plasma creatinine → ↓ GFR
- Cystatin C → newer marker
- Clearance vs GFR
  - Clearance = GFR → inulin
  - Clearance > GFR → PAH (net secretion)
  - Clearance < GFR → glucose (net reabsorption)

## Renal Circulation
### Flows
- RBF = 1250 mL/min
  - 20–25 % of cardiac output
- RPF = 55 % of RBF = 625 mL/min
  - Measured with PAH
  - Effective RPF = U(PAH) × V / P(PAH)
  - True RPF = effective RPF ÷ extraction ratio
  - = 625 ÷ 0.9 = 700 mL/min
  - Extraction ratio = % of PAH excreted = 90 %
- Filtration fraction
  - GFR ÷ RPF = 125 ÷ 625 = 20 %
  - 1/5th of renal plasma filtered at any time

### Regulation of GFR & RBF
- Constriction / dilatation of arterioles
  - Norepinephrine (shock, sympathetic)
    - Afferent constriction → ↓ Pc
    - ↓ GFR, ↓ RBF
  - Angiotensin II
    - Efferent constriction → biphasic
    - Initial (stronger) → pooling → ↑ Pc → ↑ GFR
    - Later → ↑ protein → ↑ πc → ↓ GFR
    - RBF ↓ in both phases
  - PGE2 (renal prostaglandin, from RMIC)
    - Afferent dilatation
    - ↑ cortical flow, ↓ medullary flow
    - ↑ GFR, ↑ RBF
  - ACE inhibitors
    - Efferent dilatation → blood leaves fast → ↓ Pc
    - ↓ GFR, ↑ RBF
  - Efferent constriction → ↑ πc in peritubular capillaries
- Autoregulation
  - Perfusion pressure changes → GFR & RBF unchanged
  - Between 80–180 mm Hg
  - No neural influence → works in denervated kidney
  - Myogenic (vascular)
    - ↑ RBF → wall stretch → Ca²⁺ channels open
    - Ca²⁺ influx → vasoconstriction → ↓ RBF
  - Tubuloglomerular feedback
    - Negative feedback
    - ↑ GFR → ↑ NaCl → macula densa (GFR sensor)
    - → adenosine → afferent constriction → ↓ GFR
    - Proteins need Na⁺ for absorption
    - High protein diet → ↑ GFR
- Mesangial cells
  - Contraction → ↓ GFR
    - Endothelin, angiotensin II, norepinephrine
    - ↓ blood flow to capillaries
  - Relaxation → ↑ RBF & ↑ GFR
    - NO, dopamine, cAMP, natriuretic peptide
    - Expansion of capillaries
