# Cell Organelles

## Endoplasmic reticulum
### Types
- Rough ER (RER)
  - Granular surface d/t presence of ribosomes
  - AKA granular ER
- Smooth ER (SER)
  - Smooth surface d/t absence of ribosomes
  - AKA agranular ER

### Rough ER functions
- Protein synthesis / translation
- Folding of proteins → chaperones / heat shock proteins (HSP)
- Quality control → unfolded protein response / ER stress response
  - ↑↑ misfolded proteins → halts protein synthesis
  - ↑↑ misfolded proteins → ERAD
- ERAD
  - Misfolded protein tagged to ubiquitin (kiss of death)
  - → protein–ubiquitin complex
  - → proteasome (graveyard of the cell)
  - → degradation
- Misfolded protein diseases
  - Alzheimer's disease → misfolded Aβ amyloid (more common)
  - Prion disease → PrPc (normal CNS) → PrPsc (misfolded)
  - Eg → Creutzfeldt–Jakob disease (very fatal)

### Smooth ER functions
- Steroid hormone synthesis
  - Adrenal cortex → aldosterone, cortisol
  - Testis → testosterone
  - Ovary → estrogen
- Ca²⁺ storage
  - Skeletal muscle SER = sarcoplasmic reticulum
- Drug detoxification (xenobiosis)
  - Major site → liver SER
  - Enzyme → cytochrome P450 (CYP450)

## Golgi apparatus
### Location
- Close to RER

### Post-translational modification
- RER protein → cis end of GA (receiving end)
- → glycosylation (carbohydrate side chain added)
- → glycoprotein (↑ stability)
- → stored in vesicles → stimulus → released from trans end (releasing end)
- Abundant in secretory cells
  - Antibody-secreting plasma cells
  - Goblet cells of GI tract

### Sorting of proteins
- GA decides where a protein is delivered
- Protein + phosphotransferase → protein + mannose-6-phosphate
  - → moves to lysosome → degraded

### I cell disease
- Absent phosphotransferase → M6P not added
- → lysosomal function affected → proteins not degraded
- → inclusion bodies accumulate in cell

## Lysosomes
### Role
- Recycle bin of the cell
- Acid-mediated destruction
  - H⁺ ATPase, acid hydrolase
  - Acid hydroxylase enzyme
- Acid can destroy the lysosome itself → suicidal bags / residual bodies
- Phagocytosis → engulfed particle digested

### Autophagy
- Destruction of cell's own part
- Late starvation → lysosomes engulf mitochondria
  - → proteins released → metabolism → energy for survival

### Clinical application
- Neutrophil → high content of lysosomes
- CHS1 gene → normal → LYST (lysosomal transport protein)
- CHS1 mutation → Chediak–Higashi syndrome
  - → more prone to bacterial infections

## Peroxisomes
### Structure
- AKA microbodies
- Lipid bilayer membrane
- Crystalline core → rich in proteins

### Functions
- Oxidation of VLCFAs → generates H₂O₂ (free radical)
- Degradation of H₂O₂ → catalase
  - Pseudocatalase → drug form of catalase
  - Used in free-radical disorders → vitiligo
- Products
  - Plasmalogens → myelin sheath of nerve fibres
  - Luciferase → glow in fireflies

### Peroxisomal storage disorders
- Zellweger syndrome
  - PEX gene mutation → no peroxins (peroxisome synthesis proteins)
  - VLCFA accumulate + ↓ plasmalogens → neurological impairment
- Refsum's disease
  - PAHX gene mutation
  - Defective phytanyl CoA α hydroxylase
  - Defective oxidation of phytanic acid
- Adrenoleukodystrophy
  - Defect in transport proteins
  - ↑↑ VLCFAs → neurological impairment

## Mitochondria
### Structure
- Powerhouse of the cell
- Outer membrane, inner membrane, intermembranous space
- Cristae → enzymes of oxidative phosphorylation → ATP synthesis

### Mitochondrial DNA
- Own DNA → endosymbiotic theory
- Source → ovum → maternal inheritance
- ds DNA, 16 500 base pairs
- 10× more prone to mutation than nuclear DNA
  - Repair of mtDNA mutations ineffective
- Cannot function without nuclear DNA (ATP synthesis enzymes)

### Mitochondrial disorders
- Hit organs with high metabolic requirement
  - Skeletal muscle → contraction and relaxation affected
  - CNS → stroke-like symptoms
  - GI tract affected
  - ↑ susceptibility to infection
  - Lactic acidosis common
- Mnemonic MELAS
  - Mitochondrial encephalopathy
  - Lactic acidosis
  - Stroke

## Nucleus
### Structure
- Nuclear envelope with nuclear pores
- Nucleoplasm, chromatin
- Nucleolus → contains RNA and ribosomes

### Features
- Controls all cellular functions
- Contains DNA (chromosomes)
  - DNA + histones → chromatin
  - Repeating unit of chromatin → nucleosome
- Entry / exit regulated by nuclear pore complex (NPC)
  - Nuclear localization sequence (NLS) → can cross NPC
- NPC proteins
  - Exportins → move substances out of nucleus
  - Importins → move substances into nucleus

## Marker enzymes
- Help identify cell organelles
- Cell membrane → Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase, adenylyl cyclase
- Endoplasmic reticulum → glucose-6-phosphatase
- Golgi apparatus → galactosyl transferase
- Lysosomes → acid phosphatase
- Peroxisomes → catalase, urate oxidase
- Mitochondria → ATP synthase, creatine kinase
- Nucleus → RNA polymerase, histone deacetylase
