# Regulation of Blood Pressure

## Mechanisms
- BP → single most important factor regulating the CVS
### Short term
- CNS controlled, rapid acting
- Baroreflex
- Chemoreflex
- CNS ischemic response
### Intermediate term
- Activation within hours
- Capillary fluid shift
  - Vasodilation → ↓ pressure
  - Vasoconstriction → ↑ pressure
- Stress relaxation
- Renin angiotensin aldosterone system (RAAS)
### Long term
- Activation within days
- Regulation by kidney → pressure diuresis
- Regulates fluid volume & urine output

## Short term regulation
### Baroreflex
- ↑ BP → baroreflex → ↓ BP (negative feedback)
- Stimulus → ↑ BP
- Receptor → baroreceptors (distension)
  - Carotid sinus → small dilation in internal carotid artery
  - Aortic arch receptors → aortic arch wall
- Afferent (buffer / sinus nerves)
  - CN IX → Hering nerve
  - CN X → Cyon's nerve
- Centre → nucleus tractus solitarius (medulla)
- Efferent
  - Glutamate → CVLM → GABA → RVLM → sympathetic inhibition
  - Glutamate → nucleus ambiguus → parasympathetic activation
- Effector → heart & blood vessel
- Response → ↓ BP, ↓ HR
### Marey's law
- ↑ BP → reflex ↓ HR
- BP ∝ 1/HR
### Experiments
- Occlusion of common carotid artery
  - ↓ flow to internal carotid → ↓ pressure → baroreflex inhibited
  - → ↑ BP & ↑ HR (sympathetic activation)
- Clamping below carotid sinus
  - Same → baroreflex inhibited → ↑ BP & ↑ HR
- Clamping above carotid sinus
  - ↑ pressure → baroreflex activated
  - → ↓ BP & ↓ HR (sympathetic inhibition)
### Clinical aspects
- Supine → standing
  - ↓ BP → baroreflex inhibited → SNS activated
  - Norepinephrine → ↑ HR
  - Vasoconstriction (α-receptors) → ↑ total peripheral resistance
  - SNS underactivity → postural hypotension
- Carotid sinus massage (Czermak-Hering test)
  - Activates baroreflex → ↓ HR
  - Rx of PSVT
### Other baroreceptors
- Atrial & pulmonary artery baroreceptors
- Low pressure (volume sensing) receptors
- Type A → atrial systole
- Type B → atrial diastole
### Regulation at different levels of BP
- Baroreceptors → 1st line
  - Stimulus → MAP 70–140 mmHg
  - Response → up to MAP 180 mmHg
- Chemoreceptors → 2nd line
  - Stimulus → MAP 40–70 mmHg
  - Direct effect → PNS → ↓ HR
  - Indirect effect → SNS → ↑ HR
  - Effect on HR → variable
- CNS ischemic response → last line / last ditch stand
  - Stimulus → MAP ≤ 40 mmHg
  - ↓ cerebral perfusion → RVLM (SNS) activated
  - ↑↑ MAP up to 250 mmHg → most powerful

## BP waves
- Mayer waves / vasomotor waves
  - Oscillation of nervous control mechanisms
- Traube-Hering waves
  - Fluctuations synchronised with respiration
  - Inspiration → ↓ systolic BP
  - Early expiration → ↑ arterial pressure

## Cardiac reflexes
### Bezold-Jarisch reflex
- Myocardial infarction → serotonin from injured tissue
- → bradycardia (↓ HR) & hypotension
### Cushing's reflex
- Head injury → ↑ ICT → bradycardia (↓ HR)
### Bainbridge reflex
- Fluid overload (congestive cardiac failure)
- → activates SA node (right atrium enlargement)
- → tachycardia (↑ HR)

## Chemical regulation
### Vasoconstrictors
- Mechanism → ↑ BP
- Urotensin → most potent
- Norepinephrine → α agonist
- Endothelin → from endothelial cells of lungs
  - ETA receptor → endothelin I overactivity
  - → pulmonary artery hypertension → Rx bosentan
  - ETB receptor
- Vasopressin (V1) → ↓ blood flow
  - Terlipressin → Rx of oesophageal varices
- Angiotensin II
### Vasodilators
- Mechanism → ↓ BP
- Used in treatment of hypertension
- CGRP → most potent
- Nitric oxide → 2nd messenger cGMP → Rx of angina
- Natriuretic peptides → ANP, BNP, CNP
  - 2nd messenger → cGMP
  - Nesiritide → BNP analogue
- Bradykinin
