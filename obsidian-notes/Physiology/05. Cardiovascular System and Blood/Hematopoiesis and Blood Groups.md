# 🩸 Hematopoiesis and Blood Groups

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> **Hematopoiesis**[^ety-hematopoiesis][^pre-haemo][^suf-poiesis] is the making of all blood cells from one **pluripotent hematopoietic stem cell (PHSC)** — in the yolk sac, then liver/spleen, then red bone marrow — steered by growth factors (**erythropoietin** for RBCs, **CSFs and interleukins** for WBCs, **thrombopoietin** for platelets). **Blood groups** are the antigens (agglutinogens) on the RBC surface — **ABO** sugars and the **Rh D** protein — and the antibodies (agglutinins) that react with them.
> - ✅ Where is blood made at each age, and what are the stages of erythropoiesis?
> - ✅ How does hypoxia switch on erythropoietin (HIF / VHL), and what drugs copy each growth factor?
> - ✅ What makes A, B, AB, O and Bombay, and why does Rh incompatibility cause kernicterus?

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — One Factory, Three Production Lines, Labels on the Product

The bone marrow is a **factory** with one master worker (the stem cell) who can start any of three **production lines** — red cells, white cells, platelets. Each line has its own **foreman hormone**: EPO for red (called in by the kidney when O₂ is short), CSFs/ILs for white, TPO for platelets. Every red cell leaving the factory carries **labels** (sugars A/B on an H base, and the Rh D protein); the immune system attacks any label it was not born with.

```
PHSC ──► RED line:   BFU-E → CFU-E → proerythroblast → baso → polychromato → orthochromatic → reticulocyte → RBC   (EPO, kidney, HIF)
     ├─► WHITE line: lymphoid → lymphocyte · CFU-GM → myeloblast → granulocytes / monoblast → monocyte (G-CSF, GM-CSF, IL-5, IL-3/4)
     └─► PLATELET line: megakaryocyte → platelets (TPO, IL-11)

LABELS: H base + NAG = A · H + galactose = B · both = AB · H only = O · no H = Bombay · D protein = Rh+
LANDSTEINER: antigen on RBC ⇒ matching antibody absent from serum
```

### 🔴 Erythropoiesis[^ety-erythropoiesis][^pre-erythro]

#### Stages of erythropoiesis

| Stage | Key point on the page |
|---|---|
| **Pluripotent**[^ety-pluripotent][^pre-pluri] **hematopoietic stem cell (PHSC)** | Driven towards the red line by **GATA**[^ety-gata] **and Friend of GATA (FOG) — transcription**[^ety-transcription][^pre-trans] **factors** |
| **Burst forming unit – erythrocyte (BFU-E)** | **Earliest committed progenitor**[^ety-progenitor][^pre-pro] |
| **Colony forming unit – erythrocyte (CFU-E)** | **Highest erythropoietin receptors** |
| **Proerythroblast**[^ety-erythroblast][^suf-blast] | **Hb synthesis begins** |
| **Basophilic**[^ety-basophilic][^suf-philic] **erythroblast** | — |
| **Polychromatophilic**[^ety-polychromatophilic][^pre-poly] **erythroblast** | **Hb appears** |
| **Orthochromatic**[^ety-orthochromatic][^pre-ortho] **erythroblast** | **Hb synthesis complete** |
| **Reticulocyte**[^ety-reticulocyte][^suf-cyte] | **Immature RBC** |
| **Erythrocyte** | **Mature RBC (size ~ 7.2 µ)**[^2] |

```
PHSC
  │  GATA + Friend of GATA (FOG)  = transcription factors
  ▼
BFU-E  (earliest committed progenitor)
  ▼
CFU-E  (highest erythropoietin receptors)
  ▼
Proerythroblast  (Hb synthesis begins)
  ▼
Basophilic erythroblast
  ▼
Polychromatophilic erythroblast  (Hb appears)
  ▼
Orthochromatic erythroblast  (Hb synthesis complete)
  ▼
Reticulocyte  (immature RBC)
  ▼
Erythrocyte  (mature RBC, ~7.2 µm)
```

🧠 *As the cell matures it gets smaller, its nucleus condenses and is pushed out, and its cytoplasm turns from blue (RNA, "basophilic") through a mixed grey ("polychromatophilic") to pink (haemoglobin, "orthochromatic" — the normal RBC colour).*

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> **Blood production is demand-driven, and each cell line has its own messenger.** Red cells are made in response to the one thing they carry — oxygen: when tissue O₂ falls, HIF escapes destruction by VHL, switches on the EPO gene in the kidney, and EPO acting through JAK2–STAT pushes CFU-E cells (the stage with the most EPO receptors) through the erythroblast stages to reticulocytes. White cells answer CSFs and interleukins, platelets answer thrombopoietin — and every one of these messengers now has a recombinant or mimetic drug.
>
> **Blood groups are labels the immune system reads.** The H sugar is the base; adding N-acetyl galactosamine makes A, galactose makes B; whatever label you lack, you make antibody against (Landsteiner's law). ABO antibodies are natural IgM; Rh antibodies are made only after exposure and are IgG, which is why Rh disease spares the first pregnancy and strikes the next.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Marrow in the adult axial skeleton produces RBCs (~7.2 µm, 120-day life) under EPO, WBCs under CSFs/ILs, platelets under TPO. Each person's serum carries antibodies only against the ABO antigens their RBCs lack.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> Chronic hypoxia (altitude, lung disease) raises EPO → reticulocytosis and secondary polycythaemia; blood loss raises EPO and reticulocytes within days. An Rh-negative mother exposed to Rh-positive fetal cells at her first delivery becomes sensitised without any harm to that baby.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Kidney failure → no EPO → anaemia of CKD (treated with epoetin/darbepoetin); a JAK2 gain-of-function mutation → polycythaemia vera with EPO-independent RBC overproduction; in the next Rh-positive pregnancy maternal IgG anti-D haemolyses fetal RBCs → erythroblastosis fetalis → unconjugated bilirubin crosses the immature blood–brain barrier → kernicterus.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → hypoxia / marrow or kidney disease / Rh sensitisation → compensatory EPO rise or silent antibody formation → overt anaemia, polycythaemia or haemolytic disease → organ damage (kernicterus, thrombosis)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> EPO analogues treat renal anaemia; JAK2 inhibitors treat PV; G-CSF/GM-CSF rescue neutropenia; TPO mimetics raise platelets; anti-D immunoglobulin to the Rh-negative mother prevents sensitisation — every step is a drug target or a prevention point.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "EPO is high in polycythaemia vera"**
>
> **What students think:** PV = more RBCs because of more EPO (as the page's arrow suggests).
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In PV the JAK2 V617F mutation keeps the EPO-receptor pathway switched on without EPO, so RBCs are made autonomously and the high haematocrit **suppresses** EPO — serum EPO is low or low-normal. High EPO with high RBCs means **secondary** polycythaemia (hypoxia, EPO-secreting tumour).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Primary (PV) → EPO low; secondary → EPO high."
>
> **Trap 2: BFU-E vs CFU-E**
>
> **What students think:** CFU-E is the earliest committed red progenitor.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** BFU-E comes first (earliest committed, responds mainly to IL-3/SCF); CFU-E comes next and has the most EPO receptors — it is the main EPO target.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "B before C" — Burst before Colony.
>
> **Trap 3: Universal donor and recipient**
>
> **What students think:** O can be given to anyone and AB can receive from anyone, including Bombay patients.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Bombay (hh) people lack H and make anti-H as well as anti-A and anti-B, so they react against O blood (which is rich in H); they can receive only Bombay blood. "Universal" labels also refer to red-cell transfusion only — for plasma, AB is the universal donor.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Bombay rejects even O."
>
> **Trap 4: Rh disease in the first pregnancy**
>
> **What students think:** The first Rh-positive baby of an Rh-negative mother is affected.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Sensitisation usually happens at the first delivery, when fetal cells enter the maternal circulation; the IgG anti-D then attacks the **subsequent** Rh-positive fetus. ABO incompatibility, in contrast, can affect the first baby but is mild.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Rh: first sensitises, second suffers."
>
> **Trap 5: Filgrastim is "CSF" — but which one?**
>
> **What students think:** Filgrastim and sargramostim are the same.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Filgrastim is recombinant **G-CSF** (neutrophils only); sargramostim is **GM-CSF** (granulocytes and monocytes). Both treat neutropenia; the INN stems tell them apart (-grastim vs -gramostim).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "-grastim = G; -gramostim = GM."

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Diffusion and Transport of Gases]]**
> Haemoglobin made during erythropoiesis carries O₂; the O₂ content of arterial blood is what HIF and EPO ultimately sense.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Environmental Physiology]]**
> High-altitude hypoxia → HIF → EPO → polycythaemia is the classic acclimatisation response (and the basis of altitude training and blood doping).
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Vascular Injury]]**
> Platelets from megakaryocytes (thrombopoiesis, TPO) are the first responders to vessel injury; thrombocytopenia drugs link the two chapters.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Glomerular Filtration Rate]]**
> The kidney's peritubular interstitial cells make EPO; chronic kidney disease therefore causes anaemia.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Male Reproduction]]** and **[[Female Reproduction]]**
> Testosterone promotes and oestrogen inhibits erythropoiesis — the reason men have higher RBC counts; Rh incompatibility and anti-D prophylaxis belong to pregnancy care.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Bone-marrow sampling from the iliac crest, renal anaemia treated with epoetin/darbepoetin, PV treated with phlebotomy and ruxolitinib, febrile neutropenia treated with filgrastim, ITP treated with eltrombopag/romiplostim, cross-matching (ABO, Rh, Bombay) before transfusion, anti-D prophylaxis in pregnancy.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Expect the timeline of haematopoietic sites, the erythropoiesis ladder with BFU-E/CFU-E, the HIF–VHL–EPO–JAK2 pathway, promoting vs inhibiting factors, the drug for each growth factor, ABO sugars and antibodies, Bombay phenotype, Landsteiner's law, antigen sites, the Rh-disease cascade, and Kell/P/Duffy associations.

> 2. **GATA + FOG** = transcription factors for the red line; **BFU-E = earliest committed progenitor**; **CFU-E = highest EPO receptors**.
> 3. Proerythroblast (**Hb synthesis begins**) → basophilic → polychromatophilic (**Hb appears**) → orthochromatic (**Hb complete**) → **reticulocyte (immature RBC)** → **erythrocyte (~7.2 µm)**.
> 4. **Hypoxia → HIF (transcription factor) → kidney interstitial cells (major source; also liver, brain) → ↑EPO (major regulator) → JAK2–STAT → erythropoiesis**; normoxia → **HIF–VHL complex → degradation**.
> 5. Promote erythropoiesis: **EPO, GH, cortisol, T3, testosterone**; inhibit: **oestrogen (↓RBC in females)**.
> 6. **Polycythaemia vera: JAK2 gain-of-function; Rx ruxolitinib.** **Epoetin / darbepoetin alfa** for anaemia of CKD and dialysis; **blood doping** in athletes.
> 7. **Filgrastim (G-CSF) and sargramostim (GM-CSF) → neutropenia**; **IL-5 → eosinophils**; **IL-3, 4 → basophils**; TNF.
> 8. **Thrombopoietin → eltrombopag, romiplostim; IL-11 → oprelvekin.**
> 9. ABO: antigens = **agglutinogens**, antibodies = **agglutinins**; **A = NAG on H; B = galactose on H; AB = co-dominance (universal recipient); O = H only (universal donor); Bombay = no H.**
> 10. **Landsteiner's law:** antigen on RBC → its antibody absent from serum.
> 11. Antigen sites: **RBC, saliva, amniotic fluid, semen, intestinal bacteria, food.**
> 12. **Rh antigens C, D, E; Rh+ = D present.** Rh− mother + Rh+ baby → maternal anti-D → haemolysis in **subsequent** pregnancy → **erythroblastosis fetalis → ↑ unconjugated bilirubin → crosses BBB → basal ganglia → kernicterus**; Rx anti-Rh antibodies.
> 13. **Kell absent → McLeod (acanthocytes, cardiac defects)** · **P → paroxysmal cold haemoglobinuria; receptor for parvovirus B19, E. coli** · **Duffy → receptor for P. vivax (malaria).**

[^1]: Textbook timeline: mesoblastic stage (yolk-sac blood islands) from the 3rd week; hepatic stage from ~6 weeks, peaking at 3–6 months (spleen and lymph nodes contribute); myeloid (bone-marrow) stage from ~5 months, the marrow being the main site from late fetal life onwards. After ~20 years the shafts of long bones fill with fat (yellow marrow); red marrow persists in the vertebrae, sternum, ribs, skull, pelvis and the proximal ends of the femur and humerus.
[^2]: Standard RBC diameter 7.2–7.8 µm (≈ 7.5 µm), thickness ~2 µm; life span ~120 days. The nucleus is extruded at the orthochromatic stage; the reticulocyte keeps residual RNA (stained as a reticulum), spends ~1–2 days in the marrow and ~1 day in blood, and forms ~1 % of circulating RBCs.
[^3]: In normoxia prolyl hydroxylases (which need O₂) hydroxylate HIF-α; the von Hippel–Lindau protein binds hydroxylated HIF-α and tags it with ubiquitin for proteasomal degradation. In hypoxia HIF-α is not hydroxylated, joins HIF-β and binds the hypoxia-response element of the EPO gene. ~85–90 % of EPO comes from peritubular interstitial (fibroblast-like) cells of the renal cortex, ~10–15 % from the liver (the main source in the fetus). The EPO receptor signals through **JAK2** and **STAT5**. The page's figure abbreviates this as "JAK 2 stat".
[^4]: The page writes "JAK2 receptor → ↑ EPO → ↑↑ RBCs". Standard texts: the JAK2 V617F mutation (~95 % of PV) makes the EPO-receptor pathway active **without** EPO, so red cells are made autonomously and serum EPO is **low or low-normal** (a WHO criterion for PV). Ruxolitinib inhibits JAK1 and JAK2.
[^5]: Darbepoetin alfa is a hyperglycosylated EPO analogue with more sialic acid, so its half-life is ~3 times longer (weekly or fortnightly dosing). Other indications: chemotherapy-induced anaemia, zidovudine-related anaemia in HIV, reducing transfusion before surgery.
[^6]: Filgrastim is recombinant **G-CSF** (pegfilgrastim is its long-acting form); sargramostim is recombinant **GM-CSF**. The page lists filgrastim against "colony stimulating factor (CSF)". Both shorten chemotherapy-induced neutropenia and mobilise stem cells for transplantation.
[^7]: The page writes "PHSC → GM-CSF thrombocyte → megakaryocyte → platelets". Standard texts: PHSC → common myeloid progenitor → megakaryocyte–erythroid progenitor → CFU-Meg → megakaryoblast → megakaryocyte (endomitosis, polyploid up to 64N) → each megakaryocyte sheds ~1000–3000 platelets.
[^8]: Thrombopoietin is made mainly by the liver (also kidney). Eltrombopag is an oral non-peptide TPO-receptor agonist; romiplostim is a subcutaneous "peptibody" TPO-receptor agonist — both used in chronic immune thrombocytopenia (ITP). Oprelvekin is recombinant IL-11 (for chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia), now rarely used because of fluid retention and arrhythmias.
[^9]: The page leaves the Bombay antibody cell blank ("—"). Standard texts: Bombay (hh, Oh) individuals have no H antigen and so make **anti-H, anti-A and anti-B**; they agglutinate group O red cells and can be transfused only with Bombay blood. First described by Bhende and colleagues in Bombay (Mumbai) in 1952.
[^10]: About 80 % of people are "secretors" (functional *FUT2* gene) and carry soluble A, B or H substances in saliva, semen, amniotic fluid and other secretions. Intestinal bacteria and foods carry A- and B-like carbohydrate antigens; exposure to them in infancy is why "natural" anti-A and anti-B (IgM) appear in the first months of life without any transfusion.
[^11]: The treatment on the page — anti-Rh (anti-D) antibodies — is **prophylaxis given to the Rh-negative mother** (anti-D immunoglobulin at ~28 weeks and within 72 h of delivering an Rh-positive baby, and after any sensitising event); it clears fetal D-positive cells before the mother's immune system can respond. An already affected baby is treated with phototherapy and exchange transfusion (intrauterine transfusion in severe cases).
[^12]: The McLeod phenotype is caused by absence of the Kx protein (X-linked *XK* gene), which leaves Kell antigens very weak; McLeod syndrome = acanthocytosis, myopathy with raised CK, chorea and other movement disorders, and cardiomyopathy with arrhythmias. The page summarises this as "Kell absent → acanthocytes, cardiac defects".
[^13]: Paroxysmal cold haemoglobinuria is caused by the Donath–Landsteiner antibody, a biphasic IgG anti-P that binds red cells in the cold and fixes complement on rewarming (classically after viral infections in children). Globoside (the P antigen) is the cellular receptor for parvovirus B19 (so rare p-null people are resistant), and P-fimbriated (uropathogenic) *E. coli* bind the P-system glycolipids of the urinary tract.
[^14]: The Duffy antigen (DARC — Duffy antigen receptor for chemokines) is the red-cell receptor for *Plasmodium vivax* (and *P. knowlesi*); the Duffy-negative phenotype Fy(a−b−), common in West Africa, protects against vivax malaria.

[^ety-intrauterine]: **intrauterine** — Latin *intra-*, "within" + *uterus*, "womb" + *-ine* → within the womb, i.e. fetal life. **Differentiate** *intrauterine* (within the uterus) from *extrauterine* (outside — ectopic).

[^ety-pluripotent]: **pluripotent** — Latin *plus, pluris*, "more, several" + *potens*, "able, powerful" → able to become many kinds of cell. **Differentiate:** *totipotent* (every cell including placenta — zygote) · *pluripotent* (all body cell types) · *multipotent* (several within one family — the haematopoietic stem cell is strictly multipotent) · *unipotent* (one type).
[^ety-gata]: **GATA (factors)** — named after the DNA sequence they bind, (A/T)**GATA**(A/G) — the letters are the bases guanine-adenine-thymine-adenine, not a word. *Friend of GATA (FOG-1)* is the co-factor that partners GATA-1 in red-cell and megakaryocyte development.
[^ety-transcription]: **transcription** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *scribere*, "to write" + *-ion* → writing across: copying DNA into RNA. A *transcription factor* (GATA, HIF) switches genes on. **Differentiate:** *transcription* (DNA → RNA) · *translation* (RNA → protein) · *replication* (DNA → DNA).
[^ety-progenitor]: **progenitor** — Latin *pro-*, "forward, before" + *gignere*, "to beget" + *-tor*, agent → one who begets before, an ancestor; a *committed progenitor* can form only one lineage. **Differentiate** from *precursor* (the recognisable blast stages that follow).
[^ety-erythroblast]: **erythroblast** — Greek *erythros*, "red" + *blastos*, "bud, germ, sprout" → a red-cell bud: a nucleated red-cell precursor. **Differentiate:** *-blast* (immature, nucleated, dividing — erythroblast, myeloblast) · *-cyte* (mature cell — erythrocyte) · *normoblast* (an older synonym for erythroblast).
[^ety-basophilic]: **basophilic** — Greek *basis*, "base" + *philos*, "loving" + *-ic* → base-loving: takes up basic (blue) dyes such as haematoxylin because of abundant RNA. **Differentiate:** *basophilic* (blue) · *acidophilic / eosinophilic* (red-pink, takes up eosin) · *polychromatophilic* (both) · *orthochromatic* (normal colour).
[^ety-polychromatophilic]: **polychromatophilic** — Greek *polys*, "many" + *chrōma*, "colour" + *philos*, "loving" + *-ic* → loving many colours: grey-blue, because RNA (blue) and haemoglobin (pink) are both present. *Polychromasia* on a smear = many reticulocytes.
[^ety-orthochromatic]: **orthochromatic** — Greek *orthos*, "straight, correct" + *chrōma*, "colour" + *-ic* → of the correct (normal red-cell) colour: the cytoplasm is full of haemoglobin and stains pink. Same *ortho-* as *orthopaedics* and *orthopnoea*.
[^ety-reticulocyte]: **reticulocyte** — Latin *reticulum*, "little net" (diminutive of *rete*, net) + Greek *kytos*, "hollow vessel, cell" → the cell with a little net: residual ribosomal RNA stains as a mesh with supravital dyes (new methylene blue). The reticulocyte count measures marrow output.
[^ety-hypoxia]: **hypoxia** — Greek *hypo-*, "under" + *oxys* (in *oxygen*, "acid-former") + *-ia* → too little oxygen in the tissues. **Differentiate:** *hypoxia* (low tissue O₂) · *hypoxaemia* (low arterial O₂) · *anoxia* (no O₂) · *normoxia* (normal O₂).
[^ety-vhl]: **von Hippel–Lindau** — **Eugen von Hippel** (1867–1939), German ophthalmologist, who described retinal angiomas (1904); and **Arvid Lindau** (1892–1958), Swedish pathologist, who linked them with cerebellar and spinal haemangioblastomas (1926). Loss of the VHL tumour-suppressor protein stabilises HIF → renal cell carcinoma, haemangioblastoma and secondary polycythaemia.
[^ety-interstitial]: **interstitial** — Latin *inter-*, "between" + *sistere*, "to stand, place" + *-al* → standing between; *interstitial cells* lie between the tubules of the renal cortex. Same root as *interstitium* (the tissue spaces).
[^ety-erythropoietin]: **erythropoietin (EPO)** — Greek *erythros*, "red" + *poiētēs*, "maker" + *-in*, protein → the red-maker protein; a glycoprotein hormone from the kidney. Drug names keep the stem *-poetin* (epoetin, darbepoetin).
[^ety-janus-kinase]: **Janus kinase (JAK)** — *Janus*, the two-faced Roman god of doorways, because the enzyme has two near-identical kinase-like domains facing opposite ways (one active, one regulatory pseudokinase); also nicknamed "just another kinase". *Kinase* — Greek *kinein*, "to move" + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that moves (transfers) a phosphate group.
[^ety-estrogen]: **estrogen / oestrogen** — Greek *oistros*, "gadfly, frenzy, mad desire" (the heat of animals in oestrus) + *-gen*, "producing" → the producer of oestrus. **Differentiate:** *oestrogen* (female sex hormone) · *androgen* (*anēr, andros*, man) · *progestogen* (*pro-* + *gestation* — favouring pregnancy).
[^ety-cortisol]: **cortisol** — Latin *cortex*, "bark, outer layer" (the adrenal cortex) + *-ol*, an alcohol (hydroxyl) group → the hydroxylated steroid of the adrenal cortex. **Differentiate:** *cortisol* (active) vs *cortisone* (*-one*, ketone — the inactive form).
[^ety-thyroid]: **thyroid** — Greek *thyreos*, "a large oblong shield" + *-eidēs*, "like" → shield-like (named by Thomas Wharton, 1656, after the shape of the thyroid cartilage). T3 = tri-iodothyronine.
[^ety-testosterone]: **testosterone** — Latin *testis*, "witness" (the testicles "bear witness" to virility) + *sterol* (Greek *stereos*, solid + *-ol*) + *-one*, a ketone → the ketone steroid from the testis; it stimulates EPO release and marrow directly — the reason men have higher Hb.
[^ety-polycythaemia]: **polycythaemia vera** — Greek *polys*, "many" + *kytos*, "cell" + *haima*, "blood" + *-ia* → too many cells in the blood; Latin *vera*, "true" → the true (primary) form, as opposed to secondary polycythaemia. **Differentiate:** *polycythaemia* (↑ red-cell mass) · *erythrocytosis* (↑ RBC count) · *relative polycythaemia* (↓ plasma volume).
[^ety-mutation]: **mutation** — Latin *mutare*, "to change" + *-ation* → a change in DNA. *Gain-of-function* mutations create a new or constitutive activity (JAK2 V617F — oncogene behaviour); *loss-of-function* mutations destroy it (VHL — tumour suppressor behaviour).
[^ety-ruxolitinib]: **ruxolitinib** — a drug name built on the INN stem **-tinib**, "tyrosine kinase inhibitors" (imatinib, ruxolitinib); ruxolitinib blocks JAK1/JAK2.
[^ety-anaemia]: **anaemia / anemia** — Greek *an-*, "without" + *haima*, "blood" + *-ia* → (literally) without blood: a low haemoglobin concentration. **Differentiate:** *anaemia* (↓Hb) · *ischaemia* (blood held back) · *hypovolaemia* (↓ blood volume).
[^ety-dialysis]: **dialysis** — Greek *dia-*, "through, apart" + *lysis*, "loosening" → loosening apart (separating) substances through a semipermeable membrane; haemodialysis replaces renal clearance but not the kidney's EPO production.
[^ety-doping]: **doping** — Dutch *doop*, "a thick sauce, dip" → in English slang *dope*, a drug mixture; *blood doping* = raising red-cell mass (EPO, transfusion) to enhance endurance.
[^ety-leucopoiesis]: **leucopoiesis / leukopoiesis** — Greek *leukos*, "white" + *poiēsis*, "making" → making of white blood cells. Same root as *leukaemia* (white blood) and *leukoplakia* (white patch).
[^ety-lymphocyte]: **lymphocyte** — Latin *lympha*, "clear water" (from Greek *nymphē*, a water goddess) + Greek *kytos*, "cell" → the cell of the clear fluid (lymph). **Differentiate:** *lymphocyte* (agranulocyte) · *lymphoblast* (its precursor) · *lymphoma* (a tumour).
[^ety-myeloblast]: **myeloblast** — Greek *myelos*, "marrow" + *blastos*, "bud" → the bud of the marrow (granulocyte) line; *promyelocyte* (*pro-*, before), *myelocyte* and *metamyelocyte* (*meta-*, after) follow. **Differentiate** *myelo-* (marrow, spinal cord) from *myo-* (muscle).
[^ety-eosinophil]: **eosinophil** — Greek *Ēōs*, the goddess of dawn (rosy) → the dye *eosin* (red-pink) + *philos*, "loving" → a cell whose granules love the red dye eosin. **Differentiate:** *eosinophil* (red granules — allergy, parasites; IL-5) · *basophil* (blue granules — histamine; IL-3) · *neutrophil* (neutral, lilac granules — bacteria; G-CSF).
[^ety-neutrophil]: **neutrophil** — Latin *neuter*, "neither" + Greek *philos*, "loving" → loving neither acidic nor basic dyes: its granules stain a neutral lilac. *Neutropenia* = too few neutrophils.
[^ety-granulocyte]: **granulocyte / agranulocyte** — Latin *granulum*, "little grain" + Greek *kytos*, "cell" → a cell with visible cytoplasmic granules (neutrophil, eosinophil, basophil); *a-*, "without" → agranulocyte (monocyte, lymphocyte) — not truly granule-free, only without specific granules. *Agranulocytosis* = severe neutropenia.
[^ety-filgrastim]: **filgrastim / sargramostim** — INN stems **-grastim** = granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) analogues (filgrastim, lenograstim) and **-gramostim** = granulocyte–macrophage CSF (GM-CSF) analogues (sargramostim, molgramostim).
[^ety-neutropenia]: **neutropenia** — Latin *neuter* (neutrophil) + Greek *penia*, "poverty, lack" → a lack of neutrophils (< 1500/µL). Same *-penia* as *thrombocytopenia* and *pancytopenia*.
[^ety-interleukin]: **interleukin (IL)** — Latin *inter-*, "between" + Greek *leukos*, "white" (leukocyte) + *-in*, protein → proteins that carry messages between white cells. IL-3 (multi-CSF, basophils/mast cells), IL-5 (eosinophils), IL-11 (platelets).
[^ety-necrosis]: **necrosis** — Greek *nekros*, "corpse, dead" + *-osis*, condition → death of cells in living tissue; *tumour necrosis factor* was named for its ability to kill tumour cells. **Differentiate:** *necrosis* (uncontrolled, inflammatory death) · *apoptosis* (*apo-*, off + *ptōsis*, falling — programmed, quiet death).
[^ety-thrombopoiesis]: **thrombopoiesis** — Greek *thrombos*, "clot, lump" + *poiēsis*, "making" → making of platelets (thrombocytes, "clot cells"). *Thrombopoietin* (TPO) is its hormone.
[^ety-megakaryocyte]: **megakaryocyte** — Greek *megas*, "large" + *karyon*, "nut, kernel, nucleus" + *kytos*, "cell" → the large cell with a large (polyploid, lobed) nucleus; it breaks up into platelets. **Differentiate:** *megakaryocyte* (platelet parent) · *megaloblast* (an abnormal large red precursor in B₁₂/folate deficiency).
[^ety-antigen]: **antigen** — *anti(body)* + Greek *-genēs*, "producing" → that which generates antibodies (coined by Ladislas Deutsch, 1899). **Differentiate:** *antigen* (the target) · *antibody* (the immunoglobulin that binds it) · *agglutinogen / agglutinin* (the same pair named for clumping red cells).
[^ety-oligosaccharide]: **oligosaccharide** — Greek *oligos*, "few, little" + *sakcharon*, "sugar" + *-ide* → a chain of a few sugars (3–10); the ABO antigens are oligosaccharides on glycolipids and glycoproteins. **Differentiate:** *monosaccharide* (one) · *disaccharide* (two) · *oligosaccharide* (few) · *polysaccharide* (many).
[^ety-agglutination]: **agglutinogen / agglutinin** — Latin *ad-* (*ag-* before *g*), "to" + *glutinare*, "to glue" (*gluten*, glue) → gluing together; *-gen* (producing) = the antigen that provokes clumping; *-in* (protein) = the antibody that clumps. **Differentiate:** *agglutination* (cells clumping by antibody) · *coagulation* (fibrin clot) · *aggregation* (platelets sticking).
[^ety-bombay]: **Bombay blood group** — named after the city of Bombay (now Mumbai), where Y. M. Bhende and colleagues first described the hh (Oh) phenotype in 1952; it is commonest in western India.
[^ety-codominance]: **co-dominance** — Latin *co-* (*con-*), "together" + *dominari*, "to rule" + *-ance* → ruling together: both alleles (A and B) are fully expressed in the heterozygote AB. **Differentiate:** *co-dominance* (both shown fully — AB) · *incomplete dominance* (a blend) · *dominance* (one masks the other — A over O).
[^ety-galactose]: **galactose / N-acetyl galactosamine** — Greek *gala, galaktos*, "milk" + *-ose*, sugar → milk sugar's half (lactose = glucose + galactose); *galactosamine* is galactose with an amine group, *N-acetyl* (Latin *acetum*, vinegar) adds an acetyl group to the nitrogen. NAG makes antigen A; galactose makes antigen B.
[^ety-landsteiner]: **Landsteiner's law** — **Karl Landsteiner** (1868–1943), Austrian-born (later American) immunologist, who discovered the ABO blood groups in 1900–1901, described the Donath–Landsteiner antibody (1904), co-discovered the Rh factor with Alexander Wiener (1940) and won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1930.
[^ety-semen]: **semen** — Latin *semen*, "seed" (*serere*, to sow) → the seminal fluid; secretors carry ABH substances in it (forensic use).
[^ety-amniotic]: **amniotic** — Greek *amnion*, "a bowl in which the blood of sacrifice was caught" (diminutive of *amnos*, lamb) + *-ic* → of the inner fetal membrane and its fluid.
[^ety-rh]: **Rh (Rhesus)** — named after the **rhesus macaque** (*Macaca mulatta*): Landsteiner and Wiener (1940) raised antibodies against rhesus-monkey red cells that also agglutinated most human red cells ("Rh-positive"). The monkey's name comes from Rhesus, a mythical king of Thrace in the *Iliad* (the zoological naming was arbitrary). The human antigen is now formally called RhD.
[^ety-haemolysis]: **haemolysis / hemolysis** — Greek *haima*, "blood" + *lysis*, "loosening, dissolution" → breaking open of red cells. **Differentiate:** *haemolysis* (RBCs burst) · *haemostasis* (bleeding stopped) · *haematolysis* (old synonym).
[^ety-erythroblastosis]: **erythroblastosis fetalis** — Greek *erythros*, "red" + *blastos*, "bud" + *-osis*, condition + Latin *fetalis*, "of the fetus" → a fetal condition with many erythroblasts in the blood: the marrow pours out immature red cells to replace those destroyed by maternal antibody. Synonym *haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN)*.
[^ety-incompatibility]: **incompatibility** — Latin *in-*, "not" + *com-*, "together" + *pati*, "to suffer, endure" + *-ibility* → not able to endure together: mother's antibodies and fetal antigens cannot coexist. Same root as *patient* and *passion*.
[^ety-bilirubin]: **bilirubin** — Latin *bilis*, "bile" + *ruber*, "red" + *-in* → the red(-orange) bile pigment from haem breakdown. **Differentiate:** *bilirubin* (yellow-orange) · *biliverdin* (*viridis*, green — its precursor) · *urobilinogen* (its gut product).
[^ety-unconjugated]: **unconjugated** — Old English *un-*, "not" + Latin *con-*, "together" + *iugum*, "yoke" + *-ated* → not yoked together: bilirubin not yet joined to glucuronic acid in the liver. Unconjugated (indirect) bilirubin is lipid-soluble, so it crosses the immature blood–brain barrier; conjugated (direct) bilirubin is water-soluble and does not.
[^ety-ganglia]: **basal ganglia** — Latin *basalis*, "at the base" + Greek *ganglion*, "a knot, a tumour-like swelling under the skin" → the knots of grey matter at the base of the cerebrum (globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus — the main sites of kernicterus). Strictly *basal nuclei*, since *ganglion* elsewhere means a cluster outside the CNS.
[^ety-kernicterus]: **kernicterus** — German *Kern*, "kernel, nucleus" + Greek *ikteros*, "jaundice" → jaundice of the (brain) nuclei: yellow staining of the basal ganglia by unconjugated bilirubin; coined by Christian Schmorl (1904). **Differentiate:** *kernicterus* (bilirubin brain damage) · *icterus* (jaundice) · *hydrops fetalis* (fetal oedema from severe anaemia).
[^ety-kell]: **Kell** — named after **Mrs Kelleher**, the patient in whose serum the antibody was found in 1946 (Coombs, Mourant and Race); the Kell system's antigens are highly immunogenic.
[^ety-mcleod]: **McLeod phenotype** — named after **Hugh McLeod**, a Harvard dental student in whom the weak-Kell, acanthocytic red-cell phenotype was first found (Allen and colleagues, 1961).
[^ety-acanthocyte]: **acanthocyte** — Greek *akantha*, "thorn" + *kytos*, "cell" → a thorny cell: a red cell with a few irregular spikes of varying length. **Differentiate:** *acanthocyte* (few irregular spikes — McLeod, abetalipoproteinaemia, liver disease) · *echinocyte / burr cell* (*echinos*, hedgehog — many regular short spikes, uraemia) · *spherocyte* (round, no pallor).
[^ety-paroxysmal]: **paroxysmal** — Greek *para-*, "beyond" + *oxynein*, "to sharpen, irritate" → *paroxysmos*, "irritation, a fit" → occurring in sudden attacks. *Paroxysmal cold haemoglobinuria* = sudden episodes of red urine after cold exposure. **Differentiate** from *paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria* (PNH — a complement-control defect, GPI anchor loss).
[^ety-haemoglobinuria]: **haemoglobinuria** — Greek *haima*, "blood" + Latin *globus*, "ball" (globin) + Greek *ouron*, "urine" + *-ia* → haemoglobin in the urine (from intravascular haemolysis). **Differentiate:** *haemoglobinuria* (free Hb, no RBCs) · *haematuria* (whole red cells) · *myoglobinuria* (muscle pigment).
[^ety-parvovirus]: **parvovirus** — Latin *parvus*, "small" + *virus*, "poison, slime" → the small virus (one of the smallest DNA viruses). Parvovirus B19 (named after sample B19 in a panel of sera) infects erythroid precursors via the P antigen → aplastic crisis, fifth disease, hydrops fetalis.
[^ety-escherichia]: **Escherichia coli** — named after **Theodor Escherich** (1857–1911), German-Austrian paediatrician who isolated it from infant faeces in 1885; Latin *coli*, "of the colon".
[^ety-duffy]: **Duffy** — named after **Richard Duffy**, a multiply-transfused haemophilia patient in whose serum the antibody (anti-Fyᵃ) was found in 1950 (Cutbush, Mollison and Parkin); *Fy* comes from the last two letters of the name.
[^ety-plasmodium]: **Plasmodium vivax** — Greek *plasma*, "something formed, moulded" + *-odium* (*eidos*, form) → a multinucleate amoeboid mass; Latin *vivax*, "long-lived, lively" (it relapses from dormant liver hypnozoites). It enters red cells through the Duffy antigen.
[^ety-malaria]: **malaria** — Italian *mala aria*, "bad air" → the old belief that the fever came from the foul air of marshes (where the *Anopheles* mosquito breeds).
[^pre-haemo]: **haemo- / hemo- / haemato-** — blood (Greek *haima*): haematopoiesis, haemolysis, haemoglobinuria. Don't confuse with **-aemia** blood condition as a suffix (anaemia) · **hemi-** half (hemiplegia).
[^suf-poiesis]: **-poiesis / -poietic / -poietin** — making, production: haematopoiesis, erythropoiesis, thrombopoietin. Don't confuse with **-plasia** formation, growth of tissue (hyperplasia) · **-genesis** origin, development · **-stasis** standing still.
[^pre-intra]: **intra-** — within: intrauterine, intracellular, intravascular. Don't confuse with **inter-** between · **infra-** below · **intro-** inwards.
[^pre-erythro]: **erythro-** — red: erythropoiesis, erythroblast, erythropoietin, erythrocyte. Don't confuse with **erythema** redness of the skin (same root, a sign) · **eryth-** vs **leuco-** white.
[^pre-pluri]: **pluri-** — several, more: pluripotent. Don't confuse with **poly-** many (Greek) · **multi-** many (Latin) · **toti-** all (totipotent).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: transcription, transfusion, transplacental. Don't confuse with **tri-** three · **inter-** between.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, forward: progenitor, proerythroblast, promyelocyte, promonocyte. Don't confuse with **proto-** first · **pre-** before · **post-** after.
[^suf-blast]: **-blast** — bud, immature germinal cell: erythroblast, myeloblast, lymphoblast, monoblast. Don't confuse with **-cyte** a mature cell · **-clast** breaker (osteoclast).
[^suf-philic]: **-philic / -phil** — loving, having affinity for: basophilic, polychromatophilic, eosinophil, neutrophil. Don't confuse with **-phobic** fearing, repelling (hydrophobic) · **-phage** eating (macrophage).
[^pre-poly]: **poly-** — many: polychromatophilic, polycythaemia, polymorph. Don't confuse with **pluri- / multi-** several (Latin) · **pan-** all.
[^pre-ortho]: **ortho-** — straight, correct, normal: orthochromatic, orthopnoea, orthopaedics. Don't confuse with **oto-** ear · **oro-** mouth.
[^suf-cyte]: **-cyte / cyto-** — cell (Greek *kytos*): reticulocyte, erythrocyte, megakaryocyte, acanthocyte. Don't confuse with **-blast** immature cell · **-cytosis** an increase in cells (lymphocytosis) · **-penia** a decrease.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, deficient: hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypochromic. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep.
[^pre-inter]: **inter-** — between: interstitial, interleukin, intercalated. Don't confuse with **intra-** within · **infra-** below.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: kinase, hydroxylase, ATPase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (galactose) · **-ase** vs **-in** (proteins that are not enzymes).
[^suf-gen]: **-gen / -genic** — producing, producer: oestrogen, antigen, agglutinogen, fibrinogen. Don't confuse with **-genous** originating in (endogenous) · **-gene** a unit of heredity.
[^suf-oid]: **-oid** — like, resembling (Greek *eidos*, form): thyroid (shield-like), steroid, lymphoid, myeloid. Don't confuse with **-ode** a way (electrode) · **-ose** a sugar.
[^suf-aemia]: **-aemia / -emia** — a condition of the blood: polycythaemia, anaemia, septicaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** a condition of the urine · **haem-** as a prefix.
[^pre-an]: **an- / a-** — without, not: anaemia, anoxia, agranulocyte. Don't confuse with **ana-** up, back (anaphylaxis) · **ante-** before · **anti-** against.
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, apart: dialysis, diapedesis, diastole. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dys-** bad.
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis / -lytic** — loosening, breaking down, dissolving: dialysis, haemolysis, fibrinolysis, glycolysis. Don't confuse with **-lysin** an antibody/protein that lyses (haemolysin) · **-stasis** stopping.
[^pre-leuco]: **leuco- / leuko-** — white: leucopoiesis, leukocyte, leukaemia, leukotriene. Don't confuse with **luteo-** yellow (luteinising) · **lympho-** lymph.
[^pre-myelo]: **myelo-** — marrow, spinal cord (Greek *myelos*): myeloblast, myeloid, myelocyte, myelin. Don't confuse with **myo-** muscle · **myco-** fungus.
[^pre-meta]: **meta-** — after, beyond, change: metamyelocyte (after the myelocyte), metabolism, metastasis. Don't confuse with **meso-** middle · **para-** beside.
[^pre-mono]: **mono-** — one, single: monoblast, monocyte (single rounded nucleus), monoclonal. Don't confuse with **uni-** one (Latin) · **mon-** vs **myo-** muscle.
[^pre-a]: **a- (before a consonant)** — without: agranulocyte, aplasia, apnoea. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from · **ad- / ag-** towards (agglutination).
[^suf-penia]: **-penia** — lack, deficiency (Greek *penia*, poverty): neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, osteopenia. Don't confuse with **-cytosis / -philia** an increase (neutrophilia) · **-plegia** paralysis.
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — an abnormal condition or process: necrosis, erythroblastosis, fibrosis. Don't confuse with **-sis** a neutral process (dialysis) · **-itis** inflammation.
[^pre-thrombo]: **thrombo-** — clot (Greek *thrombos*): thrombopoiesis, thrombocyte, thrombopoietin, thrombosis. Don't confuse with **thermo-** heat · **trophic** nourishing.
[^pre-mega]: **mega- / megalo-** — large: megakaryocyte, megaloblast, splenomegaly. Don't confuse with **micro-** small · **macro-** large (Greek *makros*, long — macrocyte).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: antigen (antibody-generator), antibody, antidiuretic. Don't confuse with **ante-** before · **ana-** up, again.
[^pre-oligo]: **oligo-** — few, little: oligosaccharide, oliguria, oligohydramnios. Don't confuse with **poly-** many · **olig-** vs **oleo-** oil.
[^pre-ad]: **ad- (ag-, ac-, af- …)** — to, towards; the *d* turns into the next consonant: agglutinin (*ad-* + *glutinare*), afferent, accommodation. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from · **a- / an-** without.
[^pre-co]: **co- / con- / com-** — together, with: co-dominance, conjugated, compatible. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** opposite.
[^pre-in]: **in- (not)** — not: incompatibility, inactive, insufficient. Don't confuse with **in- (into)** — infusion, influx · **un-** not (English/Germanic: unconjugated) · **intra-** within.
[^pre-un]: **un-** — not (Old English): unconjugated, unconscious. Don't confuse with **uni-** one · **in-** not (Latin equivalent: incompatible).
[^pre-auto]: **auto-** — self: autoantibodies (against one's own antigens), autoimmune, autonomic. Don't confuse with **allo-** other (alloantibody — against another person's antigen) · **iso-** equal.
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, beyond, abnormal: paroxysmal (*para-* + *oxynein*), paraesthesia, paracrine. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before.
[^suf-uria]: **-uria** — a condition of the urine: haemoglobinuria, haematuria, proteinuria, oliguria. Don't confuse with **-aemia** of the blood · **uro-** urine as a prefix (urobilinogen).
