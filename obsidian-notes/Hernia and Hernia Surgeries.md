# 🩺 Hernia and Hernia Surgeries

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> A hernia is the **protrusion of a viscus (or part of it) through a defect in the wall that normally contains it**, inside a peritoneal sac. Everything in this topic follows from two questions:
> - ✅ **Is the sac still freely connected to the abdomen?** (reducible → obstructed → strangulated)
> - ✅ **What is the defect, and how do we close it?** (herniotomy → herniorrhaphy → hernioplasty)

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Hernia Is a Clock

Think of every hernia as sitting on a **time axis**. It starts *reducible*, and as the neck tightens it becomes *obstructed* (contents trapped, blood supply intact) and finally *strangulated* (blood supply compromised). Each step removes one safe option.

```
REDUCIBLE ──────────→ OBSTRUCTED ──────────→ STRANGULATED
cough impulse ⊕        cough impulse ⊖          cough impulse ⊖
taxis possible         NO forceful taxis        NO forceful taxis
elective mesh repair   urgent surgery           emergency surgery
                       (no mesh)                (no mesh, ± resection)
```

## 📋 Types of Hernia

| Feature | Simple / uncomplicated | Obstructed | Strangulated |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hernia** | Reducible | Irreducible | Obstructed hernia **+ compromised blood supply** → overlying **skin inflamed** |
| **Cough impulse** | ⊕ | ⊖ | ⊖ |
| **Forceful taxis** (manual reduction) | Possible | **Contraindicated** — risk of *reduction en masse* | **Contraindicated** — risk of *reduction en masse* |

> [!danger] Reduction *en masse*
> Forceful taxis on an obstructed or strangulated hernia can push the **contents *and* the constricting ring** back into the abdomen together. The bowel is now "reduced" but is **still obstructed inside the ring**, so the patient keeps deteriorating with no lump to warn you.[^1]

| **Percussion note** | Dull | Tympanic / resonant |

## 🔪 Hernia Surgery — Herniotomy vs Herniorrhaphy vs Hernioplasty

| | Herniotomy | Herniorrhaphy | Hernioplasty |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Procedure** | Identify sac → cut open → reduce contents → excise sac | Same + | Same + |
| **Defect** | **Not repaired** | Edges **sutured together** (no mesh, because of infection risk) | Closed with **mesh** |
| **Recurrence rate** | Highest | — | **Least** |
| **Performed in** | **TOC in:** congenital inguinal hernia · inguinal hernia in children · congenital hydrocele | **Obstructed and strangulated** hernia (contaminated field → no synthetic mesh) | **All other hernias** |

> [!tip] Why no mesh in obstructed / strangulated hernia?
> The field is potentially contaminated (ischaemic bowel, possible resection). A synthetic mesh in an infected field becomes a chronic sinus. If a mesh is unavoidable, use a **biological** mesh (see below).

## 🕸 Mesh

### The ideal mesh
- **Low weight** → less **shrinkage**
- **Thin fibres**
- **Large pores** (macroporous — allows macrophage entry and tissue ingrowth)

### Placement
> [!warning] At least **2 cm overlap** all around the defect — to avoid recurrence.

### Mesh materials

| Synthetic | Biological |
|---|---|
| **Avoid in infection and strangulation** | **Can be used in infection** |
| e.g. **Prolene** (polypropylene), **Vypro** — *not used intraperitoneally* (bowel adhesions) | e.g. **Acellular human dermis** (AlloDerm) |
| **PTFE** (ePTFE) — the one synthetic that *can* go intraperitoneally | **Acellular porcine dermis** |

## 📍 Inguinal Hernia

> [!success] The "most common" chain — all point to the same answer
> - Most common type of **hernia** → inguinal
> - Most common type of **inguinal hernia** → **indirect**
> - Most common hernia **in females** → **indirect inguinal** (NOT femoral)
> - Femoral hernia is **F ≫ M**, but is still rarer than inguinal hernia in women.[^3]

### Clinical tests

| Test | Value |
|---|---|
| **Deep ring occlusion test** | **Single best test** — occlude the deep ring (midpoint between ASIS and pubic tubercle), ask the patient to cough: no bulge = indirect; bulge medial to your thumb = direct |
| Zieman's three-finger test | Low sensitivity |
| Ring invagination test | Low sensitivity |

### Types of indirect inguinal hernia

| Type | Extent of the sac |
|---|---|
| **Bubonocele** | Sac limited to the **inguinal canal** |
| **Funicular** | Sac comes through the superficial ring and reaches the **top of the testis** (testis still separate, palpable below the sac) |
| **Inguinoscrotal (complete)** | Sac reaches the **bottom of the scrotum**, surrounding the testis |

| Boundary | Structure |
|---|---|

> The inguinal ligament splits this one orifice into an upper (inguinal) and a lower (femoral) part. **A mesh placed over the whole orifice (preperitoneally) covers the defect of inguinal, femoral *and* obturator hernia at once** — the basis of laparoscopic repair.[^4]

## 🛠 Hernioplasty

### Open surgery
> [!info] **Lichtenstein's tension-free mesh hernioplasty** — the standard open repair (mesh sutured over the posterior wall, no tension on the tissues).[^5]

### Complications of open inguinal hernia surgery
1. Haemorrhage
2. Injury to the vas / cord structures
3. **Nerve injury** — the most common nerve injured:
   - **At the superficial ring → ilioinguinal nerve** (numbness of the upper medial thigh and scrotum / labium)
   - **Entrapped below the mesh → iliohypogastric nerve → chronic inguinal pain**[^6]
4. Recurrence
5. Wound infection

### Laparoscopic inguinal surgery

| | TEP | TAPP |
|---|---|---|
| **Full form** | **T**otally **E**xtra**p**eritoneal repair | **T**rans-**A**bdominal **P**re**p**eritoneal repair |
| **Peritoneum** | **Remains intact** — a balloon trocar creates the preperitoneal space from the umbilicus | **Breached** — enter the abdomen, cut a peritoneal flap, place the mesh, close the flap |
| **Character** | Technically **more challenging**, **better repair** (no peritoneal entry → no adhesions / bowel injury) | Easier to learn, larger working space, lets you see both groins |

## ⚠️ Structures Encountered During Laparoscopic Repair — The Danger Triangles

> [!note] Key from your diagram: **L** → lateral, **m** → medial. The vas deferens runs medially, the testicular vessels laterally, and they meet at the deep ring — the two triangles hang below that "V".

| | | Triangle of **Doom** | Triangle of **Pain** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boundaries** | Medial | Vas deferens | Testicular (gonadal) vessels |
| | Lateral | Testicular vessels | Peritoneal reflection |
| | Inferior | Peritoneal reflection | — |
| | Superior | — | Iliopubic tract |
| **Contents** | | a. External iliac artery b. External iliac vein c. Genital branch of genitofemoral nerve | a. Lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh b. Femoral nerve c. Femoral branch of genitofemoral nerve |
| **Complication of tacking here** | | **Torrential bleeding** if a stapler / tacker is applied | **Meralgia paraesthetica** — shooting pain along the lateral thigh from entrapment of the **lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh** (most common) |

> [!danger] Corona mortis — the "circle of death"
> An **abnormal communication between the obturator and external iliac (inferior epigastric) vessels** running over the superior pubic ramus. Injury → **torrential bleeding** that retracts into the pelvis.[^7]

## 🗂 Classification of Hernia

### European Hernia Society (EHS) classification — inguinal hernia
- Defect measured by the **size of the finger breadth** (1 = < 1 finger ≈ 1.5 cm; 2 = 1–2 fingers; 3 = > 2 fingers ≈ > 3 cm)[^8]
- Classified as:
  - **Primary (P) / Recurrent (R)**
  - **Location:** **L**ateral (indirect) · **M**edial (direct) · **F**emoral

| Lateral / indirect hernia (L) |
| Medial / direct hernia (M) |

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> A hernia is a viscus escaping through a defect, and every decision follows from **the state of the neck and the state of the field**. The neck decides urgency: while the sac slides freely (cough impulse ⊕) you have time for an elective, tension-free mesh repair; once the neck grips (impulse ⊖, irreducible) the clock is running and forceful reduction is forbidden; once blood supply fails (skin inflamed) it is an emergency. The field decides the repair: a clean groin gets a mesh over the whole myopectineal orifice (least recurrence), a contaminated groin gets sutures only (herniorrhaphy), and a child gets nothing but sac excision (herniotomy) because the defect is a patent process and not a weak wall.
>
> Hold the groin as one window — Fruchaud's myopectineal orifice — split by the inguinal ligament. Indirect hernias enter above through the deep ring lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels, direct hernias push through Hesselbach's triangle medial to them, femoral hernias drop below the ligament. One preperitoneal mesh covers all three, which is why the laparoscopic surgeon must know the two triangles of danger that lie in that same window.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> The inguinal canal is an oblique, valve-like passage: the deep ring (in fascia transversalis) and superficial ring (in external oblique aponeurosis) are offset, and the arching fibres of internal oblique / transversus abdominis (conjoint tendon) clamp the canal shut when intra-abdominal pressure rises. The processus vaginalis has obliterated, so nothing peritoneal reaches the scrotum.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A patent processus vaginalis (indirect) or a weakened posterior wall / Hesselbach's triangle (direct) lets peritoneum and later omentum or bowel slide out with straining and slide back on lying down. The patient has an intermittent lump with an expansile cough impulse and little pain; the deep ring occlusion test separates indirect from direct. The sac is reducible and this is the window for elective Lichtenstein or laparoscopic (TEP/TAPP) mesh repair.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Repeated herniation and oedema narrow the neck until contents can no longer return: the lump becomes irreducible, loses its cough impulse and, if bowel is inside, produces colicky pain, vomiting and distension (obstruction). Further pressure occludes the venous then arterial supply: the lump becomes tense, tender and the overlying skin inflamed (strangulation), with gangrene and perforation within hours. Forceful taxis now risks reduction en masse, and surgery must avoid synthetic mesh.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → patent processus / posterior wall weakness → intermittent reducible lump → irreducible (obstructed) → strangulated → gangrene / perforation
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> The stage dictates both timing and technique: reducible = elective hernioplasty with mesh and ≥ 2 cm overlap; obstructed or strangulated = urgent surgery, herniorrhaphy or biological mesh, resect non-viable bowel, never push it back by force. Recognising the loss of cough impulse is therefore the single most important bedside finding.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Indirect inguinal hernia:** "Lump controlled by pressure over the deep ring, lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels, may descend into the scrotum" → The sac follows the spermatic cord through a patent processus vaginalis, so occluding the deep ring stops it and it can reach the testis (funicular / complete). It is the most common hernia in both sexes.
>
> **Direct inguinal hernia:** "Elderly man, bulge appears despite deep ring occlusion, medial to the vessels, rarely reaches the scrotum" → The posterior wall bulges through Hesselbach's triangle; there is no cord to follow, so it stays a wide-necked, low-risk bulge.
>
> **Strangulated hernia:** "Irreducible, no cough impulse, tense, tender, red/dusky skin, toxic patient" → Venous then arterial compromise of the contents; the inflamed skin is the clue your notes highlight.
>
> **Reduction en masse:** "Hernia 'reduced' after forceful taxis but obstruction continues" → The constricting ring went in with the contents.
>
> **Omentocele vs enterocele:** "Doughy, dull, first part reduces easily" = omentum; "resonant, gurgles, first part hardest to reduce" = bowel.
>
> **Sliding hernia:** "Elderly man, large left-sided hernia, sigmoid colon forms the posterior wall of the sac" → Dissecting the 'sac' injures the colon.
>
> **Sportsman's hernia (Gilmore's groin):** "Athlete, severe groin pain on exertion, no palpable lump, MRI shows posterior wall tear" → Treat laparoscopically.
>
> **Meralgia paraesthetica after laparoscopic repair:** "Burning pain / numbness over the lateral thigh after TEP/TAPP" → Tack placed in the triangle of pain (lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh).
>
> **Chronic groin pain after open repair:** "Pain months after Lichtenstein" → Iliohypogastric nerve entrapped under the mesh (ilioinguinal if injured at the superficial ring).
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> "Bowel loop in the inguinal canal on CT with dilated proximal loops" → obstructed/incarcerated hernia. "Mesh with ≥ 2 cm overlap of the defect" → the operative standard. "Tear of the conjoint tendon / posterior wall on MRI in an athlete" → sportsman's hernia.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Cough impulse ⊕ (reducible, elective) → impulse ⊖ but soft and painless (irreducible, urgent) → impulse ⊖ with tense, tender lump and inflamed skin (strangulated, emergency). EHS defect size: 1 finger (< 1.5 cm) < 2 fingers < 3 fingers (> 3 cm). Nyhus I–II (ring only) vs III (posterior floor defect) vs IV (recurrent).

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "Femoral hernia is the most common hernia in women"**
>
> **What students think:** Because femoral hernia is far more common in women than men (F ≫ M), it must be the commonest hernia in women.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The female-to-male ratio is high, but the absolute number is small. Indirect inguinal hernia remains the most common hernia in women, just as in men. Femoral hernia is the hernia *most likely to strangulate*, not the most common.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Memorise the chain in the green callout: inguinal → indirect → indirect even in females. "F ≫ M" describes a ratio, not a rank.
>
> **Trap 2: Reducing an obstructed hernia by force "to save an operation"**
>
> **What students think:** If the lump can be pushed back, the problem is solved.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Forceful taxis in an obstructed or strangulated hernia can reduce the sac together with its constricting ring (reduction en masse), or rupture ischaemic bowel. The obstruction continues inside the abdomen with no lump to warn you, and gangrenous bowel is returned to the peritoneal cavity.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Taxis is for reducible hernias only; once the cough impulse is lost, the treatment is surgery. "No impulse = no taxis."
>
> **Trap 3: Using a Prolene mesh in a strangulated hernia**
>
> **What students think:** Mesh gives the lowest recurrence, so it should always be used.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Synthetic mesh in a contaminated field (ischaemic bowel, resection) becomes infected and forms a chronic sinus that may need removal. Obstructed and strangulated hernias are repaired by herniorrhaphy (sutures) or, if a mesh is essential, a biological mesh (AlloDerm, porcine dermis) that tolerates infection.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Match the repair to the field: clean → synthetic mesh; contaminated → sutures or biological mesh; child → herniotomy alone.
>
> **Trap 4: Mixing up the two laparoscopic triangles**
>
> **What students think:** "Doom" and "pain" are interchangeable danger zones.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** They lie on opposite sides of the testicular vessels. Doom is medial (between vas and vessels) and holds the external iliac vessels — a tack bleeds. Pain is lateral (between vessels and the peritoneal reflection, below the iliopubic tract) and holds the femoral, lateral cutaneous and genitofemoral (femoral branch) nerves — a tack hurts.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Doom is in the middle" (vas–vessels), "Pain is on the side" (vessels–peritoneum); never fire a tacker below the iliopubic tract.
>
> **Trap 5: Confusing the nerve at the superficial ring with the nerve under the mesh**
>
> **What students think:** One nerve explains all post-herniorrhaphy pain.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The ilioinguinal nerve runs through the superficial ring with the cord and is the nerve most often *injured* during open repair (sensory loss). The iliohypogastric nerve runs above the ring in the posterior wall and is the one *entrapped under the mesh*, giving chronic inguinal pain.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Ilio-INGUINAL at the ring, ilio-HYPOgastric hiding under the mesh."

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Inguinal Canal and Spermatic Cord — Anatomy]]**
> The walls of the canal, the contents of the cord and the course of the ilioinguinal nerve explain every step of open repair and every complication (nerve injury, vas injury, ischaemic orchitis). The deep ring = fascia transversalis, superficial ring = external oblique aponeurosis is the anatomical core of the deep ring occlusion test.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Intestinal Obstruction]]**
> A hernia is the second most common cause of small-bowel obstruction after adhesions (and the most common in patients without previous surgery). Always examine the hernial orifices in any patient with obstruction; an obstructed hernia follows the same fluid, decompression and surgery pathway.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Femoral Hernia and Femoral Canal]]**
> The femoral canal lies below the inguinal ligament in the lower half of the myopectineal orifice; its rigid boundaries (lacunar ligament medially) make femoral hernias the ones most likely to strangulate, and the same preperitoneal mesh covers them.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Lumbar Plexus — Nerves of the Groin and Thigh]]**
> The ilioinguinal, iliohypogastric, genitofemoral, lateral cutaneous and femoral nerves are the vocabulary of hernia complications; meralgia paraesthetica and post-herniorrhaphy pain are lumbar plexus lesions.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Surgical Infection and Prosthetic Materials]]**
> Mesh choice (polypropylene vs PTFE vs biological), pore size, shrinkage and the rule against synthetic mesh in contaminated fields are shared with incisional hernia and abdominal wall reconstruction.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Hernias appear in general surgery (elective and emergency), paediatric surgery (herniotomy, hydrocele), urology (cord injury, ischaemic orchitis), sports medicine (Gilmore's groin), radiology (CT/MRI of the groin) and anaesthesia (day-case local / regional blocks for Lichtenstein repair).
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Exams combine the anatomy of Hesselbach's triangle and the inguinal canal with clinical signs (cough impulse, deep ring occlusion), surgical choice (herniotomy vs herniorrhaphy vs hernioplasty), laparoscopic anatomy (triangles of doom and pain, corona mortis) and classifications (EHS, Nyhus). A typical integrated question gives a vignette and asks for the structure at risk or the correct repair.

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Clinical Pearls
> 1. **Strangulated = obstructed + compromised blood supply** → skin inflamed, no cough impulse, no forceful taxis.
> 2. **Herniotomy** alone is the treatment of choice in children (congenital inguinal hernia, congenital hydrocele).
> 3. **No synthetic mesh in obstructed / strangulated hernia** — sutures or biological mesh.
> 4. **Ideal mesh:** lightweight, thin fibres, large pores; **≥ 2 cm overlap** all around.
> 5. **PTFE** is the only synthetic mesh for intraperitoneal placement; Prolene / Vypro cause bowel adhesions.
> 6. **Deep ring occlusion test** is the single best clinical test for inguinal hernia.
> 7. **Direct hernia goes through Hesselbach's triangle; indirect goes lateral to it** (lateral to the inferior epigastric vessels).
> 8. **Myopectineal orifice of Fruchaud** — one preperitoneal mesh covers inguinal, femoral and obturator hernias.
> 9. **Ilioinguinal nerve** = most commonly injured (at the superficial ring); **iliohypogastric** = entrapped under mesh → chronic pain.
> 10. **TEP** keeps the peritoneum intact (harder, better); **TAPP** breaches it.
> 11. **Triangle of doom** = vas / testicular vessels → external iliac vessels → bleeding. **Triangle of pain** = testicular vessels / peritoneal reflection, below the iliopubic tract → nerves → meralgia paraesthetica.
> 12. **Sliding hernia** = sigmoid colon in the posterior wall, left ≫ right, elderly men. **Sportsman's hernia** = Gilmore's groin, MRI, laparoscopic repair.
> 13. **Nyhus IIIb** = indirect hernia + posterior floor defect (pantaloon); **IIIc** = femoral; **IV** = recurrent.

[^2]: The "first part" rule is the classical bedside difference: an enterocele's first loop is the hardest to get back through the neck and the rest follows with a gurgle; an omentocele's first part goes easily and the last, adherent part is the hardest.
[^3]: Common exam trap. The femoral:inguinal ratio in women is about 1:4, so even in women the indirect inguinal hernia wins. Femoral hernia is, however, the groin hernia most likely to **strangulate** because of its rigid neck (lacunar ligament medially).

[^6]: The iliohypogastric nerve runs between internal oblique and external oblique above the inguinal canal and can be caught by the sutures that fix the upper edge of the mesh; the ilioinguinal nerve runs *inside* the canal with the cord and exits through the superficial ring, where it is cut or crushed during open dissection.
