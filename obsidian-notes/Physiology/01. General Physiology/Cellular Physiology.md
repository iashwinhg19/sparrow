# 🧫 Cellular Physiology

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The **cell**[^ety-cell] is the **fundamental unit of all living organisms**, and its outer boundary — the **cell membrane**[^ety-membrane] (**plasma membrane**[^ety-plasma]) — is a **selectively permeable**[^ety-permeability][^pre-per] barrier built as a **fluid mosaic** of three **macromolecules**[^ety-macromolecule][^pre-macro]:
> - ✅ **Proteins (55 %)** — the mosaic; they do the work (receptors, pumps, channels, shape, anchors)
> - ✅ **Lipids (40 %)** — the fluid; they set fluidity and asymmetry
> - ✅ **Carbohydrates (5 %)** — the identity tags (blood groups)
>
> Every exam question in this chapter asks one of two things: *which molecule sits where?* and *what disease follows when it is missing, mutated or misplaced?*

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Swimming Pool with Floats and Flags

Picture the membrane as a **swimming pool**: the **lipids** are the water (it flows — *fluid*), the **proteins** are floats scattered across it (*mosaic*) — some pierce the floor (transmembrane), some cling to the edge (peripheral), some are tied on with a rope (lipid-anchored) — and the **carbohydrates** are little flags on top that tell visitors whose pool it is (blood group).

```
CELL MEMBRANE  = FLUID (lipids 40 %) + MOSAIC (proteins 55 %) + FLAGS (carbohydrates 5 %)   — Singer & Nicolson

LIPIDS         → phospholipids : PC/lecithin (surfactant) · SM · PI (IP3) · PS (eat-me) · CL (mito, VDRL)
               → glycolipids   : cerebrosides (CNS) · gangliosides (GM1 → cholera toxin)
               → cholesterol   : vit D precursor · FLUIDITY BUFFER
PROTEINS       → transmembrane : GPCR · Na+-K+ ATPase · CFTR (Cl- channel)
               → peripheral    : spectrin (elliptocytosis) · ankyrin (spherocytosis) · dystrophin (DMD)
               → lipid-anchored: CD55 · CD59 · C8-binding protein (PIGA) → missing = PNH
CARBOHYDRATES  → glycoproteins · glycolipids → BLOOD GROUPS
```

## 🧱 Cell Membrane

- AKA **plasma membrane**.

**FUNCTION:**
- It is an **outer barrier with selective permeability** in the cell — it lets some substances through and keeps others out.

**STRUCTURE:**
- **Fluid mosaic model**[^ety-mosaic] of the cell membrane. **Proposed by Singer and Nicolson**[^ety-singer-nicolson].
- 🧠 *Fluid* because the lipids and many proteins drift sideways within the layer; *mosaic* because proteins are scattered through the lipid like tiles in a mosaic.

**COMPOSITION:** macromolecules[^1]

| # | Macromolecule | Share | Role on the page |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Proteins**[^ety-protein] | **55 %** | Arranged in **mosaic pattern** in the cell membrane |
| 2 | **Lipids**[^ety-lipid] | **40 %** | **Responsible for fluidity** of the cell membrane |
| 3 | **Carbohydrates**[^ety-carbohydrate][^pre-hydro] | **5 %** | (identity tags — see Carbohydrates below) |

## 🫧 Lipids

### INTRODUCTION

**Structure:**
- **Bilayer** — outer and inner lipids (two leaflets).
- **Asymmetry**[^ety-asymmetry][^pre-a][^pre-syn] — lipids are **arranged specifically either on the inner or outer side** (e.g. PS only on the inner leaflet).

**Types:**
- **Phospholipids**[^ety-phospholipid] (PO₄³⁻ group).[^2]
- **Glycolipids**[^ety-glycolipid][^pre-glyco] (carbohydrate side chain).
- **Cholesterol**[^ety-cholesterol][^suf-ol] (**critical lipid**).

### PHOSPHOLIPIDS

- **Major lipid** of the membrane.

| Types | Site | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Phosphatidyl choline (PC)** AKA **lecithin**[^ety-lecithin][^suf-in] | **Lungs: surfactant**[^ety-surfactant] | • **Prevents alveolar**[^ety-alveolar] **collapse** • **Major lipid in surfactant**[^3] |
| **Sphingomyelin**[^ety-sphingomyelin] | • Nerve cell membrane • Surfactant | **Minor lipid in surfactant** |
| **Phosphatidylinositol**[^ety-inositol] **(IP3)** | Endocrine cells | • Part of the **second messenger system** (IP3 → DAG → Ca²⁺)[^4] |
| **Phosphatidylserine (PS)** | **Inner surface of the cell membrane in all cells** | • If **expressed on the outer surface** → **"Eat me" signal** • **Apoptosis**[^ety-apoptosis][^pre-apo][^suf-ptosis] (programmed cell death) • **Detected by Annexin V**[^ety-annexin] — binds & forms a complex with PS (**Annexin V staining**)[^5] |
| **Cardiolipin (CL)**[^ety-cardiolipin] | **Heart: mitochondria**[^ety-mitochondria] | • In **syphilis**[^ety-syphilis] — serum **anti-CL antibody +** (**detected by VDRL test**[^ety-vdrl])[^6] |

![[Cellular Physiology - phosphatidylserine inner and outer leaflet.png]]
*Phosphatidylserine (red head, "Ps") — normally in the inner leaflet of every cell; when it is flipped to the outer leaflet it becomes the "eat me" signal of apoptosis, picked up by Annexin V staining (cropped from the handwritten page).*

> [!note] Note — L/S ratio
> **L (Lecithin) / S (Sphingomyelin) ratio:**
> - **To assess fetal lung maturity.**
> - **Mature lung: ≥ 2.**[^7]
>
> 🧠 Lecithin rises steeply late in pregnancy while sphingomyelin stays flat, so a ratio of 2 or more in amniotic fluid means the lungs are making enough surfactant to stay open after birth.

### GLYCOLIPIDS

| Types | Site | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Cerebrosides**[^ety-cerebroside][^suf-oside] | CNS | — |
| **Gangliosides**[^ety-ganglioside] | GIT | **GM-1 gangliosides: receptors for cholera**[^ety-cholera] **toxin**[^ety-toxin] **— fragment B**[^8] |

### CHOLESTEROL

- **Site:** cell membrane of **skin**.

**Significance:**
- **Precursor of vit. D**[^ety-vitamin].
- **7-dehydrocholesterol**[^pre-de] —(sunlight, in skin)→ **Vit. D**.[^9]
- **Fluidity buffer** — maintains membrane fluidity in the **optimal range**.[^10]

### MEMBRANE FLUIDITY[^ety-fluidity]

- **Lubricative property of lipid → maintains fluidity of membrane → allows mobility** (of proteins and lipids within the membrane).

**Characteristics of fluidity:**

| | **Increased fluidity** | **Decreased fluidity** |
|---|---|---|
| **Effect on health** | Good | Bad |
| **Type of fatty acids** | **Unsaturated**[^ety-saturated] **(essential)** | Saturated (trans)[^11] |
| **Examples** | • **Linoleic acid**[^ety-linoleic] • **Linolenic acid** • **Arachidonic acid**[^ety-arachidonic] • **Omega-3**[^ety-omega] **fatty acids: abundant in fish** | • **Stearic acid**[^ety-stearic] • **Palmitic acid**[^ety-palmitic] (abundant in junk food) |
| **Packing (page drawing)** | Kinked tails → **fluid** | Straight tails → **rigid** |

## 🧩 Proteins

### Classification based on location in the cell membrane

1. **Transmembrane**[^pre-trans] **proteins / integral**[^ety-integral] **membrane proteins** — present **throughout** (span the whole bilayer).
2. **Peripheral**[^ety-peripheral][^pre-peri] **proteins** — present **peripherally** (on the inner or outer surface).
3. **Lipid-anchored proteins** — **require lipids for attachment**.

- **Most important** class.

### PERIPHERAL PROTEINS

**Functions:**

**1. Support shape of cell**

| Cell | Protein | Diseases due to mutations |
|---|---|---|
| **RBC (biconcave disc)** | **Spectrin**[^ety-spectrin] | **Elliptocytosis**[^ety-elliptocytosis][^suf-cyte] |
| | **Ankyrin**[^ety-ankyrin] | **Spherocytosis**[^ety-spherocytosis][^14] |
| **Skeletal muscle** | **Dystrophin**[^ety-dystrophin][^pre-dys][^suf-trophy] | • **Duchenne**[^ety-duchenne] **muscular dystrophy (DMD)** • Muscular weakness — typical presentation • **Gowers' sign**[^ety-gowers] — climbs on own body to stand up (**knee → hip → stand**) • **Respiratory paralysis**[^ety-paralysis][^pre-para] (weakness of the **diaphragm**[^ety-diaphragm][^pre-dia]) **→ death**[^15] |

**2. Cell surface receptors.**

### LIPID ANCHORED PROTEINS

**In RBC:**
- **PIGA (Phosphatidylinositol Glycan-A) gene**[^ety-glycan] synthesises proteins:
  - **CD55**[^ety-cd] (**decay accelerating factor**).
  - **CD59** (**membrane inhibitor of reactive lysis**[^ety-lysis][^suf-lysis]).
  - **C8 binding protein**.[^16]
- **Proteins bind to lipid: phosphatidylinositol** (the GPI anchor).
- **Significance:**
  - **Normal gene → prevents hemolysis**[^ety-hemolysis][^pre-haemo].
  - **Mutated gene → Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria.**

![[Cellular Physiology - lipid anchored CD55 CD59 on RBC.png]]
*Lipid-anchored proteins in the RBC — CD59 and CD55 sit on the outer surface tethered to the membrane by a phosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor; they switch off complement on the red cell (cropped from the handwritten page).*

### Paroxysmal Nocturnal Hemoglobinuria[^ety-pnh][^suf-uria]

- **Pathology:** **mutated or absent PIGA gene**.
- 🧠 Without the anchor, CD55 and CD59 cannot be fixed to the RBC, so the red cell has no brake on **complement**[^ety-complement] — it lyses inside the vessels and the free haemoglobin spills into the urine.

```
Mutated or absent PIGA gene
        ↓
During sleep (night)
        ↓  ↓ pH
Activates complement proteins: C5–C9 (membrane attack complex)
        ↓
Attacks RBC membrane
        ↓
Hemolysis (Hemoglobinuria)
```
The night-time fall in pH is the page's trigger for complement activation.[^17]

**Treatment:**
- **Anti-C5 monoclonal**[^ety-monoclonal][^pre-mono] **antibody** — **supportive**:
  - **Eculizumab**[^ety-eculizumab][^suf-mab].
  - **Ravulizumab** (long acting).[^18]
- **Hematopoietic**[^ety-hematopoietic][^suf-poiesis] **stem cell transplant**[^ety-transplant] — **definitive**.

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> The membrane does what its molecules are, and is sick where a molecule is missing or misplaced. **Lipids** decide *how the membrane moves* (fluidity — unsaturated tails and cholesterol) and *which face is which* (asymmetry — PS inside); **proteins** decide *what the membrane does* (receptors, pumps, channels, scaffolds, complement brakes); **carbohydrates** decide *who the cell is* (blood group). Every disease on these pages is a single faulty molecule: no lecithin → hyaline membrane disease; PS on the outside → apoptosis; CFTR → cystic fibrosis; spectrin/ankyrin → elliptocytosis/spherocytosis; dystrophin → DMD; PIGA → PNH; antibodies to cardiolipin → VDRL-positive syphilis.
>
> So for any membrane question ask two things: *which of the three macromolecules is it?* and *on which side of the bilayer, in which cell, does it sit?* — the disease follows.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> An asymmetric, fluid bilayer (PC and SM outside, PS inside) studded with proteins: GPCRs and pumps work, spectrin–ankyrin keep the RBC a biconcave disc, dystrophin anchors muscle fibres, and GPI-anchored CD55/CD59 keep complement off the red cell. Cholesterol buffers fluidity; surfactant lecithin keeps alveoli open.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> One molecule is short or faulty but the cell copes: a premature lung with an L/S ratio just under 2 still ventilates with effort; a small PNH clone causes only intermittent dark morning urine; a carrier of a spectrin or ankyrin defect has mild haemolysis balanced by a busy marrow; a young boy with DMD walks but rises from the floor with a Gowers' manoeuvre.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> The defect outruns the compensation: alveoli collapse at each expiration (hyaline membrane disease in the pre-term baby); complement destroys large numbers of PIGA-deficient RBCs (haemoglobinuria, anaemia, thrombosis); spherocytes are trapped in the spleen (anaemia, jaundice, splenomegaly); DMD progresses to diaphragmatic weakness and respiratory failure; thick CFTR-poor secretions block the airways.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal membrane → one molecule mutated/absent/misplaced → partial function and compensation (marrow, effort, steroids) → compensation fails → organ-level disease (lung collapse, haemolysis, paralysis)
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Treatment targets the missing molecule or its consequence: antenatal steroids and exogenous surfactant replace lecithin; anti-C5 antibodies (eculizumab, ravulizumab) stand in for the missing complement brakes in PNH while stem cell transplant replaces the faulty clone; splenectomy removes the trap for spherocytes.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Apoptosis:** "eat me signal", "Annexin V staining", "PS on the outer leaflet" → phosphatidylserine flipped out of the inner leaflet; macrophages recognise it and engulf the cell without inflammation.
>
> **Fetal lung maturity / hyaline membrane disease:** "L/S ratio", "≥ 2", "pre-term baby", "lecithin deficiency" → surfactant is mostly phosphatidylcholine; without it alveoli collapse.
>
> **Syphilis serology:** "anti-cardiolipin antibody", "VDRL" → a non-treponemal test using cardiolipin, a phospholipid of (heart) mitochondria.
>
> **Cholera:** "GM-1 ganglioside", "fragment B" → the B subunit of cholera toxin binds GM1 on intestinal cells.
>
> **Duchenne muscular dystrophy:** "Gowers' sign", "climbs up his own legs", "dystrophin", "respiratory paralysis" → absent peripheral protein in skeletal muscle.
>
> **PNH:** "PIGA", "CD55/CD59 absent", "dark urine in the morning", "complement C5–C9", "eculizumab" → lipid-anchored proteins missing on RBC.
>
> **Cystic fibrosis:** "CFTR", "Cl⁻ channel", "transmembrane conductance regulator" → integral (transmembrane) ion channel mutation.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> Kinked fatty-acid tails = fluid (unsaturated); straight tails = rigid (saturated); a red PS head on the outer leaflet = apoptotic cell; round dense RBCs without central pallor = spherocytes; oval RBCs = elliptocytes.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> L/S ratio < 2 → immature lungs; membrane composition 55 % protein : 40 % lipid : 5 % carbohydrate (myelin 20 : 80); in DMD, diaphragm weakness signals the terminal (respiratory) phase.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: "Lipids are the main component of every membrane"**
>
> **What students think:** Because the membrane is a "lipid bilayer", lipids must be the largest share.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** By weight the typical membrane is **55 % protein, 40 % lipid, 5 % carbohydrate**. Lipid wins only in myelin (80 % lipid : 20 % protein), which is built for insulation, not work. The protein-richest membrane of all is the inner mitochondrial membrane, packed with electron-transport and ATP-synthase complexes.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Workers are protein (55), insulators are lipid (myelin 80), power plants are the most protein (inner mitochondrial membrane)."
>
> **Trap 2: Swapping spectrin and ankyrin**
>
> **What students think:** Spectrin defects cause spherocytosis because both start with "sp".
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The page pairs **spectrin → elliptocytosis** and **ankyrin → spherocytosis**. Ankyrin is the anchor that ties the spectrin net down to the membrane; lose the anchor and the membrane sheds in vesicles, leaving a smaller sphere. Spectrin's horizontal net, when weak, lets the cell stretch into an ellipse.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**A**nkyrin = **A**nchor lost → ball (sphere)"; "**S**pectrin **S**tretches (ellipse)."
>
> **Trap 3: "Cholesterol always makes the membrane stiffer"**
>
> **What students think:** Cholesterol only rigidifies membranes.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The page calls it a **fluidity buffer**: at body or high temperature it restrains phospholipid movement (↓ fluidity), at low temperature it stops the tails packing into a gel (↑ fluidity). It keeps fluidity in the **optimal range** rather than pushing it one way.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Buffer = resists change in *both* directions, like a pH buffer.
>
> **Trap 4: Thinking PNH is treated definitively with eculizumab**
>
> **What students think:** Anti-C5 therapy cures PNH.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Eculizumab and ravulizumab block C5 and so the membrane attack complex — they are **supportive**; the PIGA-mutant stem cell clone persists. Only **haematopoietic stem cell transplant** removes it, so it is the **definitive** treatment.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "-mab mops up complement; transplant replaces the clone."
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing up lecithin and sphingomyelin in surfactant**
>
> **What students think:** Sphingomyelin is the main surfactant lipid because it is in the L/S ratio.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Lecithin (phosphatidylcholine) is the **major** surfactant lipid; sphingomyelin is **minor** and stays constant through pregnancy, which is exactly why it is used as the denominator — the ratio rises only when lecithin production matures.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "L for Lungs, S for Steady."

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Cell = **fundamental unit of all living organisms**; cell membrane AKA **plasma membrane** — an outer barrier with **selective permeability**.
> 2. **Fluid mosaic model** — **Singer and Nicolson**; **proteins 55 %** (mosaic pattern), **lipids 40 %** (fluidity), **carbohydrates 5 %**.
> 3. Lipid bilayer shows **asymmetry**; types — phospholipids (major), glycolipids, **cholesterol (critical lipid)**.
> 4. **Lecithin (PC)** = **major lipid in surfactant** (prevents alveolar collapse); **sphingomyelin** = minor; **L/S ratio ≥ 2 = mature fetal lung**; lecithin deficiency → **hyaline membrane disease**.
> 5. **Phosphatidylserine** — inner leaflet normally; on the outer surface = **"eat me" signal of apoptosis**, detected by **Annexin V**.
> 6. **Cardiolipin** — heart/**mitochondria**; anti-CL antibody in **syphilis** → **VDRL**. **Phosphatidylinositol** → IP3 second messenger.
> 7. **GM-1 ganglioside** (GIT) = receptor for **cholera toxin fragment B**; cerebrosides in CNS.
> 8. **Cholesterol** — skin: **7-dehydrocholesterol → (sunlight) → vit D**; **fluidity buffer**.
> 9. ↑ fluidity = **unsaturated (essential)** fatty acids (linoleic, linolenic, arachidonic, omega-3 — fish); ↓ fluidity = **saturated (trans)** — stearic, palmitic (junk food).
> 10. Transmembrane (integral) proteins — **most important**: **GPCR** (m/c hormone receptor), **Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase**, **CFTR** (Cl⁻ channel; mutated in cystic fibrosis).
> 11. Peripheral proteins — **spectrin → elliptocytosis**, **ankyrin → spherocytosis**, **dystrophin → DMD** (**Gowers' sign**, death from respiratory paralysis).
> 12. Lipid-anchored (RBC): **PIGA** → **CD55 (DAF), CD59 (MIRL), C8-binding protein** on **phosphatidylinositol**; mutated → **PNH** (night ↓ pH → C5–C9 MAC → haemolysis). Rx: **eculizumab / ravulizumab (supportive)**, **HSCT (definitive)**.
> 13. **Glycolipids on RBC determine blood groups**; highest-protein membrane = **inner mitochondrial membrane**; myelin = **80 % lipid**; damage repaired by **resealing of the bilayer**.

[^1]: Guyton gives the membrane as roughly 55 % protein, 42 % lipid (25 % phospholipid, 13 % cholesterol, 4 % other) and 3 % carbohydrate by weight; the page rounds this to 55 / 40 / 5. Lipid molecules still outnumber protein molecules about 50 : 1, because each protein is so much heavier.
[^2]: A phospholipid is **amphipathic**: a water-loving head (phosphate + a group such as choline, serine or inositol on glycerol or sphingosine) and two water-fearing fatty-acid tails. In water the tails hide from water by pairing tail-to-tail — which is why the membrane forms a bilayer on its own and reseals when torn.
[^3]: The single most important surface-active molecule is **dipalmitoyl-phosphatidylcholine (DPPC)**, secreted by type II pneumocytes; it lowers alveolar surface tension so small alveoli do not empty into large ones (law of Laplace).
[^4]: Strictly, membrane phosphatidylinositol is phosphorylated to PIP₂, which phospholipase C splits into **IP₃** (releases Ca²⁺ from the ER) and **DAG** (activates protein kinase C). The page's chain "IP3 → DAG → Ca²⁺" names the three products of this pathway — see [[Cellular Messengers and Receptors]].
[^5]: Living cells keep PS inside with ATP-driven **flippases**; in apoptosis caspase-3 switches the flippases off and a **scramblase** on, so PS appears on the surface and macrophages engulf the cell without inflammation. Annexin V binds PS (Ca²⁺-dependent), so fluorescent Annexin V on flow cytometry marks early apoptotic cells. Activated platelets also expose PS — it is the surface on which clotting factors assemble.
[^6]: Cardiolipin is the signature lipid of the **inner mitochondrial membrane** of all cells; it was first extracted from beef heart, hence "heart". VDRL and RPR are **non-treponemal** tests whose antigen is cardiolipin + lecithin + cholesterol; false positives occur in SLE, antiphospholipid syndrome and pregnancy, so a positive VDRL is confirmed with a treponemal test.
[^7]: Lecithin and sphingomyelin are about equal until ~32–34 weeks; then lecithin rises sharply. An L/S ratio ≥ 2 predicts a low risk of respiratory distress; the presence of phosphatidylglycerol in amniotic fluid adds confidence (especially in diabetic mothers).
[^8]: Cholera toxin is an AB₅ toxin: the five **B** (binding) subunits attach to GM1 ganglioside on enterocytes, the **A** subunit enters and ADP-ribosylates Gsα → permanent ↑ cAMP → CFTR opens → Cl⁻ and water pour out as "rice-water" diarrhoea.
[^9]: UVB light (about 290–315 nm) converts 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin to previtamin D₃ → cholecalciferol (vit D₃), which is hydroxylated at C-25 in the liver and at C-1 in the kidney to the active calcitriol — see [[Calcium Homeostasis]].
[^10]: Cholesterol slips between phospholipids: at high temperature its rigid ring restrains tail movement (↓ fluidity), at low temperature it keeps tails from packing into a gel (↑ fluidity). This two-way effect is why it is called a *buffer*.
[^11]: Trans fatty acids are technically unsaturated, but the *trans* double bond keeps the chain straight, so they pack like saturated fats — that is why the page groups "saturated (trans)" as rigid and unhealthy. Linoleic (ω-6) and α-linolenic (ω-3) acids are the true **essential** fatty acids; arachidonic acid becomes essential only when linoleic acid is lacking.
[^12]: G-protein-coupled receptors are the largest receptor family (about 800 human genes; seven transmembrane helices) and the target of roughly a third of all drugs — hence "most common" (m/c) hormone receptor.
[^13]: Cystic fibrosis is autosomal recessive (chromosome 7q31); the commonest mutation is **F508del (ΔF508)**, which misfolds CFTR so it is destroyed in the ER. CFTR is an ATP-gated Cl⁻ channel of the ABC-transporter family; diagnosis is a sweat chloride ≥ 60 mmol/L.
[^14]: Textbooks: hereditary spherocytosis is most often due to **ankyrin** defects (also band 3, β-spectrin, protein 4.2) — vertical membrane–skeleton links fail and membrane is lost; hereditary elliptocytosis is most often due to **spectrin** (or protein 4.1) defects — horizontal skeleton links fail and the cell stays stretched. The page's pairing matches the commonest cause of each.
[^15]: DMD is X-linked recessive (dystrophin gene at Xp21, the largest human gene); frameshift deletions give **absent** dystrophin, whereas Becker dystrophy (in-frame) gives a shortened, partly working protein. Expect markedly raised CK, calf pseudohypertrophy, and death in the 20s–30s from respiratory or cardiac failure.
[^16]: The C8-binding protein is also called **homologous restriction factor (HRF)**. All three are attached by a **glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI)** anchor, the first step of which needs the PIGA enzyme. PIGA is X-linked and the mutation in PNH is **acquired** (somatic) in a haematopoietic stem cell, so RBCs, WBCs and platelets of that clone all lack GPI-anchored proteins.
[^17]: The classic explanation is mild respiratory acidosis during sleep (CO₂ retention) enhancing complement activity, so the first morning urine is dark. Modern texts point out that haemolysis is in fact continuous and only *visible* overnight because urine concentrates; diagnosis is flow cytometry showing absent CD55/CD59 (or FLAER binding).
[^18]: Eculizumab binds C5 and stops formation of the membrane attack complex (C5b–C9); patients need meningococcal vaccination because terminal complement is what kills *Neisseria*. Ravulizumab is an engineered longer-acting version given every 8 weeks instead of every 2 weeks.
[^19]: The A and B antigens are oligosaccharide tips carried on both glycolipids and glycoproteins of the RBC membrane; group O carries only the H antigen (the unconverted precursor). The page highlights glycolipids.
[^20]: Myelin is about 70–80 % lipid (rich in cholesterol, cerebrosides and sphingomyelin), which makes it an electrical insulator; the inner mitochondrial membrane is about 75 % protein (electron transport chain and ATP synthase).
[^21]: When the membrane is torn, Ca²⁺ rushing in through the tear triggers fusion of internal vesicles (including lysosomes) to patch the hole; the bilayer then reseals on its own because exposed hydrophobic tails are unstable in water.
[^22]: Hyaline membrane disease = neonatal respiratory distress syndrome; the "hyaline membranes" are glassy pink fibrin–protein layers lining collapsed alveoli on histology. Antenatal corticosteroids to the mother speed up surfactant production; exogenous surfactant is given to the baby.

[^ety-cytoplasm]: **cytoplasm** — Greek *kytos*, "hollow vessel, cell" + *plasma*, "formed substance" → the living material of the cell outside the nucleus. **Differentiate:** *cytoplasm* (everything between membrane and nucleus, organelles included) · *cytosol* (only the fluid part) · *nucleoplasm* (the content of the nucleus).

[^ety-protein]: **protein** — Greek *prōteios*, "of the first rank, primary" (*prōtos*, first) → named by Gerardus Mulder in 1838 on Jöns Jacob Berzelius's suggestion, because proteins seemed the primary substance of living matter.
[^ety-lipid]: **lipid** — Greek *lipos*, "fat, grease" + *-id*, "belonging to" → fat-like substances that dissolve in organic solvents, not in water (fats, phospholipids, steroids). Same root as *lipoma*, *lipase*.
[^ety-carbohydrate]: **carbohydrate** — *carbon* (Latin *carbo*, charcoal) + *hydrate* (Greek *hydōr*, water) → "watered carbon", because simple sugars fit the formula Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ.
[^ety-asymmetry]: **asymmetry** — Greek *a-*, "not" + *syn-* (*sym-*), "together, alike" + *metron*, "measure" → not measured alike on both sides; the two leaflets of the bilayer carry different lipids (PC, SM, glycolipids outside; PS, PE, PI inside).
[^ety-phospholipid]: **phospholipid** — *phospho-* from phosphorus (Greek *phōsphoros*, "light-bearer", because white phosphorus glows) + *lipid* → a lipid carrying a phosphate group in its head.
[^ety-glycolipid]: **glycolipid** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" + *lipos*, "fat" → a lipid with a sugar chain attached. **Differentiate:** *glycolipid* (sugar on lipid) · *glycoprotein* (sugar on protein) · *proteoglycan* (mostly sugar, a little protein core).
[^ety-cholesterol]: **cholesterol** — Greek *cholē*, "bile" + *stereos*, "solid" + *-ol*, an alcohol → "solid bile alcohol", first crystallised from gallstones in the 18th century and named *cholestérine* by Chevreul (1815); renamed *-ol* when its alcohol group was recognised.
[^ety-lecithin]: **lecithin** — Greek *lekithos*, "egg yolk" + *-in* → named by the French chemist Théodore Gobley (mid-19th century) after the egg yolk from which he isolated it; chemically phosphatidylcholine.
[^ety-surfactant]: **surfactant** — a contraction of **surf**ace-**act**ive **a**ge**nt** (coined 1950) → a substance that lowers surface tension where air meets water; in the lung it stops alveoli collapsing at the end of expiration.
[^ety-alveolar]: **alveolar** — Latin *alveolus*, "small hollow, little trough", diminutive of *alveus*, "cavity" → the tiny air sacs of the lung; also the tooth socket (*alveolar* process) — context decides.
[^ety-sphingomyelin]: **sphingomyelin** — *sphingo-* from the **Sphinx** of Greek myth (J. L. W. Thudichum, 1884, named its backbone *sphingosine* for its enigmatic nature) + Greek *myelos*, "marrow" (myelin) → the riddle-lipid of nerve sheaths.
[^ety-inositol]: **inositol** — Greek *is, inos*, "sinew, muscle fibre" + *-ose* (sugar) + *-ol* (alcohol) → a sugar-alcohol first isolated from muscle (1850); phosphatidyl*inositol* is the membrane lipid whose split products (IP₃, DAG) are second messengers.
[^ety-apoptosis]: **apoptosis** — Greek *apo-*, "off, away" + *ptōsis*, "falling" → "falling off", like leaves from a tree in autumn; coined by Kerr, Wyllie and Currie (1972) for programmed, orderly cell death. **Differentiate:** *apoptosis* (energy-dependent, cell shrinks, PS flips out, no inflammation) vs *necrosis* (Greek *nekros*, corpse — swelling, membrane rupture, inflammation) vs *autophagy* (self-eating to survive).
[^ety-annexin]: **annexin** — Latin *annectere*, "to tie to, attach" (*ad-* + *nectere*, to bind) + *-in* → a family of proteins that bind (attach to) negatively charged membrane phospholipids in the presence of Ca²⁺; Annexin V binds phosphatidylserine.
[^ety-cardiolipin]: **cardiolipin** — Greek *kardia*, "heart" + *lipos*, "fat" → "heart lipid", because Mary Pangborn first extracted it from beef heart in the early 1940s; it is the lipid of the inner mitochondrial membrane, which heart muscle has in abundance.
[^ety-mitochondria]: **mitochondria** — Greek *mitos*, "thread" + *chondrion*, "little granule" (diminutive of *chondros*, grain) → "thread-granules", named by Carl Benda (1898) for their appearance under the microscope; singular *mitochondrion*.
[^ety-syphilis]: **syphilis** — from *Syphilus*, the shepherd struck by the disease in Girolamo Fracastoro's Latin poem *Syphilis sive morbus gallicus* (1530); the name of the hero became the name of the disease.
[^ety-vdrl]: **VDRL** — **V**enereal **D**isease **R**esearch **L**aboratory (US Public Health Service), where the slide flocculation test was developed; *venereal* is from Latin *Venus*, goddess of love.
[^ety-cerebroside]: **cerebroside** — Latin *cerebrum*, "brain" + *-oside*, a glycoside → a sugar-lipid first found in brain (Thudichum, 1870s); abundant in myelin and the CNS, as the page says.
[^ety-ganglioside]: **ganglioside** — Greek *ganglion*, "knot, swelling (of nerve cells)" + *-oside* → a sialic-acid-rich glycolipid first isolated from ganglion cells (Ernst Klenk, 1942). *GM1* = **G**anglioside, **M**ono-sialo, migration band **1**.
[^ety-cholera]: **cholera** — Greek *cholera*, from *cholē*, "bile" → the ancient name for a disease of bile-like vomiting and diarrhoea; the modern disease is caused by *Vibrio cholerae* toxin.
[^ety-toxin]: **toxin** — Greek *toxikon (pharmakon)*, "(poison) for arrows", from *toxon*, "bow" + *-in* → a poison made by a living organism. **Differentiate:** *toxin* (biological) · *toxicant* (man-made poison) · *venom* (a toxin injected by bite or sting).
[^ety-vitamin]: **vitamin** — Latin *vita*, "life" + *amine* → coined as *vitamine* by Casimir Funk (1912), who thought these vital factors were all amines; the *-e* was dropped when most proved not to be.
[^ety-fluidity]: **fluidity** — Latin *fluidus*, "flowing" (*fluere*, to flow) + *-ity*, quality → the quality of flowing; in a membrane, the ease with which lipids and proteins move sideways. Opposite *rigidity* (Latin *rigidus*, stiff).
[^ety-saturated]: **saturated / unsaturated** — Latin *saturare*, "to fill full" (*satur*, full) → a saturated fatty acid has every carbon *filled* with hydrogen (no double bonds, straight chain); an *un*saturated one has one or more C=C double bonds, which kink the chain. **Differentiate:** *mono-* (one double bond) · *poly-unsaturated* (several) · *trans* (straight despite a double bond).
[^ety-linoleic]: **linoleic / linolenic** — Latin *linum*, "flax" + *oleum*, "oil" → acids first obtained from linseed (flax-seed) oil; linoleic is ω-6 (two double bonds), α-linolenic is ω-3 (three).
[^ety-arachidonic]: **arachidonic** — Latin *arachis*, "peanut" (Greek *arakos*, a leguminous plant) → named from arachidic acid of peanut oil; the 20-carbon ω-6 parent of prostaglandins and leukotrienes (eicosanoids).
[^ety-omega]: **omega-3** — *omega* (ω), the last letter of the Greek alphabet → the **last** (methyl) carbon of the fatty-acid chain; *omega-3* means the first double bond is 3 carbons from that end (EPA, DHA in fish oil; α-linolenic acid in plants).
[^ety-stearic]: **stearic** — Greek *stear*, "tallow, hard fat" → an 18-carbon saturated fatty acid named from animal tallow; solid at room temperature.
[^ety-palmitic]: **palmitic** — from **palm** oil (Latin *palma*), where it was first found → a 16-carbon saturated fatty acid, the commonest saturated fat in the diet.
[^ety-integral]: **integral** — Latin *integer*, "whole, untouched" + *-alis* → forming part of the whole; an integral protein cannot be removed without dissolving the bilayer (detergents). **Differentiate:** *integral* (embedded; all transmembrane proteins are integral) vs *peripheral* (loosely attached, removed by salt washes).
[^ety-peripheral]: **peripheral** — Greek *periphereia*, "circumference", from *peri-*, "around" + *pherein*, "to carry" → lying on the outer edge; peripheral proteins sit on a surface of the bilayer, not within it.
[^ety-receptor]: **receptor** — Latin *recipere*, "to take back, receive" (*re-* + *capere*, to take) + *-tor*, the agent → the molecule that receives a chemical signal.

[^ety-spectrin]: **spectrin** — from *spectre*, "ghost" (Latin *spectrum*, apparition) + *-in* → first extracted (1968) from red-cell "ghosts", the empty membranes left after haemoglobin is washed out.
[^ety-elliptocytosis]: **elliptocytosis** — Greek *elleipsis*, "a falling short" (the shape of an ellipse) + *kytos*, "cell" + *-osis*, condition → a condition of oval red cells. **Differentiate:** *elliptocyte* (oval, spectrin) · *spherocyte* (ball, ankyrin) · *stomatocyte* (mouth-like slit) · *acanthocyte* (spiky).
[^ety-ankyrin]: **ankyrin** — Greek *ankyra*, "anchor" + *-in* → the protein that anchors the spectrin skeleton to band 3 in the membrane.
[^ety-spherocytosis]: **spherocytosis** — Greek *sphaira*, "ball" + *kytos*, "cell" + *-osis* → a condition of round (spherical) red cells that have lost membrane, lack central pallor and are trapped in the spleen.
[^ety-dystrophin]: **dystrophin** — Greek *dys-*, "bad, faulty" + *trophē*, "nourishment" + *-in* → the protein whose absence causes muscular *dystrophy* (named 1987 by Kunkel's group after the disease). **Differentiate:** *dystrophy* (faulty development/degeneration) · *atrophy* (*a-*, without — wasting of a once-normal tissue) · *hypertrophy* (bigger cells) · *pseudohypertrophy* (calves enlarged by fat and fibrous tissue in DMD).
[^ety-duchenne]: **Duchenne** — Guillaume-Benjamin-Amand Duchenne (de Boulogne) (1806–1875), French neurologist and pioneer of electrophysiology, who described the pseudohypertrophic muscular paralysis of boys in the 1860s.
[^ety-gowers]: **Gowers' sign** — Sir William Richard Gowers (1845–1915), British neurologist, who described in 1879 how boys with DMD rise from the floor by "climbing up" their own legs with their hands because the hip and knee extensors are weak.
[^ety-paralysis]: **paralysis** — Greek *para-*, "beside, abnormal" + *lyein*, "to loosen" + *-sis* → an abnormal loosening (of the muscles), i.e. loss of power. **Differentiate:** *paralysis / -plegia* (complete loss) · *paresis* (partial weakness) · *palsy* (an old English contraction of *paralysis*).
[^ety-diaphragm]: **diaphragm** — Greek *dia-*, "across, through" + *phragma*, "fence, partition" → the muscular partition stretched across between chest and abdomen; the main muscle of breathing, so its failure is fatal in DMD.
[^ety-glycan]: **glycan** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" + *-an*, a polysaccharide → a chain of sugars; *phosphatidylinositol glycan* = the sugar chain built on phosphatidylinositol that forms the GPI anchor (PIGA makes its first link).
[^ety-cd]: **CD (cluster of differentiation)** — the naming system for cell-surface molecules agreed at the first international workshop on leucocyte differentiation antigens (Paris, 1982): antibodies that recognise the same molecule are "clustered" under one CD number.
[^ety-lysis]: **lysis** — Greek *lysis*, "loosening, dissolving" (*lyein*, to loosen) → the breaking open of a cell; *reactive lysis* = lysis by complement attached to a bystander cell (which CD59 prevents).
[^ety-hemolysis]: **hemolysis** — Greek *haima*, "blood" + *lysis*, "loosening" → bursting of red cells. **Differentiate:** *intravascular* haemolysis (in the vessels — haemoglobinuria, as in PNH) vs *extravascular* (in the spleen — spherocytosis); *haemolysis* vs *haemorrhage* (blood leaving the vessel).
[^ety-pnh]: **paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria** — *paroxysmal*: Greek *para-* + *oxynein*, "to sharpen" → sudden sharp attacks; *nocturnal*: Latin *nox, noctis*, "night"; *hemoglobinuria*: *haima*, "blood" + Latin *globus*, "ball" + Greek *ouron*, "urine" → attacks of haemoglobin in the urine, seen after a night's sleep.
[^ety-complement]: **complement** — Latin *complementum*, "that which fills up, completes" (*com-* + *plere*, to fill) → named by Paul Ehrlich (1890s) for the heat-labile serum factor that *completes* the killing begun by antibodies; C1–C9 cascade, C5b–C9 = membrane attack complex.
[^ety-monoclonal]: **monoclonal** — Greek *monos*, "single" + *klōn*, "twig, cutting" → from a single clone of plasma cells (hybridoma — Köhler and Milstein, 1975), so every antibody molecule is identical and binds one epitope. **Differentiate:** *polyclonal* (many clones, many epitopes).
[^ety-eculizumab]: **eculizumab** — drug-name stems: *ecu-* (a fantasy prefix) + *-li-* (immunomodulator) + *-zu-* (humanised) + *-mab* (monoclonal antibody) → a humanised immunomodulating monoclonal antibody (anti-C5). *Ravu-li-zu-mab* is built the same way.
[^ety-hematopoietic]: **hematopoietic** — Greek *haima*, "blood" + *poiein*, "to make" + *-etic* → blood-making; *haematopoietic stem cell transplant* replaces the PIGA-mutant clone with normal marrow.
[^ety-transplant]: **transplant** — Latin *trans-*, "across" + *plantare*, "to plant" → to plant across (from donor to recipient). **Differentiate:** *transplant* (living tissue moved — organ, marrow) · *transfusion* (*fundere*, to pour — blood poured across) · *graft* (tissue placed, often from the same person).
[^ety-glycoprotein]: **glycoprotein** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" + *prōteios*, "primary" → a protein carrying sugar side-chains, added in the Golgi by glycosylation; most membrane proteins facing the outside are glycoproteins.
[^ety-antigen]: **antigen** — *anti(body)* + Greek *-gen*, "producing" → a substance that generates antibodies; the A and B blood-group antigens are sugar tips on RBC glycolipids and glycoproteins.
[^ety-myelin]: **myelin** — Greek *myelos*, "marrow" + *-in* → named by Rudolf Virchow (1854) for the fatty "marrow" around nerve fibres; it is 80 % lipid, an electrical insulator.
[^ety-hyaline]: **hyaline** — Greek *hyalos*, "glass" + *-ine* → glassy, translucent; the "hyaline membranes" are glassy pink fibrin–protein layers lining collapsed alveoli in surfactant-deficient babies.
[^pre-per]: **per-** — through, thoroughly: permeability, perfusion, percutaneous. Don't confuse with **peri-** around (peripheral, pericardium) · **para-** beside · **pre-** before.
[^pre-macro]: **macro-** — large, long: macromolecule, macrophage, macrocytic. Don't confuse with **micro-** small (microfilament) · **mega-** very large (megaloblast).
[^pre-eu]: **eu-** — good, true, normal: eukaryotic (true nucleus), euthyroid, euphoria. Don't confuse with **dys-** bad, faulty (dystrophin) · **a- / an-** without.
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, in front of, forward: prokaryotic (before a nucleus), prodrome, progeria. Don't confuse with **post-** after · **proto-** first (protoplasm) · **pre-** before (Latin, in time).
[^suf-elle]: **-elle / -ella / -ule / -ole** — small, diminutive: organelle (little organ), vacuole (little empty space), vesicle (little bladder), alveolus. Don't confuse with **-oma** a tumour · **-oid** resembling (sphenoid).
[^pre-cyto]: **cyto- / -cyte** — cell (Greek *kytos*, hollow vessel): cytoplasm, cytoskeleton, erythrocyte. Don't confuse with **cysto-** bladder, sac (cystic fibrosis, cystitis) · **cito** quickly (Latin).
[^pre-hydro]: **hydro- / hydr-** — water: carbohydrate, dehydrocholesterol, hydrolase, hydrophobic. Don't confuse with **hydroxy-** an –OH group (hydroxylase) · **hygro-** moisture.
[^pre-a]: **a- / an-** — not, without: asymmetry, atrophy, anaemia, apnoea. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from (abduction) · **ad- (a-)** towards (*a*scend) · **ana-** up, again (anabolism).
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym- (before b, p, m)** — together, alike: symmetry, synapse, syndrome, synthesis. Don't confuse with **sin-** (Latin *sinister*, left) · **semi-** half.
[^pre-glyco]: **glyco- / gluco-** — sweet, sugar: glycolipid, glycoprotein, glycosylation, glucose. Don't confuse with **glycero-** glycerol (glycerophospholipid) · **glia-** glue (glial cells) · **gly-** in *glycine* (an amino acid that tastes sweet).
[^suf-ol]: **-ol** — an alcohol (–OH group): cholesterol, inositol, glycerol, ethanol, calcitriol. Don't confuse with **-ole** small (arteriole, vacuole) · **-ose** a sugar (glucose) · **-one** a ketone (testosterone, cortisone).
[^suf-in]: **-in / -ine** — a substance, often a protein or chemical: lecithin, spectrin, ankyrin, dystrophin, annexin, myelin. Don't confuse with **-ase** an enzyme (ATPase) · **-ogen** an inactive precursor (pepsinogen).
[^pre-apo]: **apo-** — off, away from, detached: apoptosis, apoenzyme, apocrine (secretes by shedding part of the cell). Don't confuse with **epi-** upon (epithelium) · **ana-** up · **hypo-** under.
[^suf-ptosis]: **-ptosis** — falling, drooping: apoptosis (falling off), ptosis (drooping eyelid), nephroptosis. Don't confuse with **-plasia** formation · **-stasis** standing still · **-osis** a condition.
[^suf-oside]: **-oside** — a glycoside, a sugar joined to another molecule: cerebroside, ganglioside, cardiac glycosides (digoxin). Don't confuse with **-ose** a free sugar (glucose) · **-ide** a binary compound (chloride).
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, from, removing: 7-**de**hydrocholesterol (cholesterol with hydrogen removed), deacetylase, degradation, dehydration. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis-** apart (dissection) · **dia-** through (diaphragm).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through, beyond: transmembrane, transplant, transcellular. Don't confuse with **inter-** between (intercellular) · **intra-** within (intracellular) · **para-** beside (paracellular).
[^pre-peri]: **peri-** — around, surrounding: peripheral, pericardium, peritoneum. Don't confuse with **para-** beside · **per-** through · **pre-** before.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: receptor, resealing, recycle, reabsorption. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards, behind · **pre-** before.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: ATPase, phosphatase, lipase, hydrolase (from *diastase*, the first enzyme named, 1833). Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (lactose — the *substrate* of lact*ase*) · **-in** a protein that is not necessarily an enzyme.
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a condition, state or process (often abnormal or increased): fibrosis, spherocytosis, elliptocytosis. Don't confuse with **-itis** inflammation (cystitis) · **-iasis** an infestation (amoebiasis) · **-ation** an active process.
[^suf-cyte]: **-cyte** — a cell: elliptocyte, spherocyte, erythrocyte, astrocyte. Don't confuse with **-cyst** a sac (Greek *kystis*) · **-site / -cide** (parasite, bactericide).
[^pre-dys]: **dys-** — bad, faulty, difficult: dystrophy, dyspnoea, dysplasia. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart, reversal (Latin: dissect) · **a-** without (atrophy) · **eu-** good.
[^suf-trophy]: **-trophy / -trophin** — nourishment, growth: dystrophy, hypertrophy, atrophy, gonadotrophin. Don't confuse with **-tropic / -tropin** turning towards, acting on (thyrotropin) · **-plasia** formation of cells (hyperplasia = more cells; hypertrophy = bigger cells).
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, alongside, abnormal: paralysis, paroxysmal, paracellular, parathyroid. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **per-** through · **pre-** before.
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, across, apart: diaphragm, dialysis, diarrhoea. Don't confuse with **di-** two (disaccharide) · **dys-** bad.
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis / -lytic** — loosening, breaking down, dissolving: lysis, haemolysis, glycolysis, paralysis, dialysis. Don't confuse with **-lithiasis** stones · **-ptosis** falling · **-stasis** stopping.
[^pre-haemo]: **haemo- / hemo- / haemat-** — blood: hemolysis, hemoglobinuria, hematopoietic. Don't confuse with **hemi-** half (hemidesmosome, hemiplegia) · **hepato-** liver.
[^suf-uria]: **-uria** — a condition of the urine: hemoglobinuria, hematuria, proteinuria, hypercalciuria. Don't confuse with **-uresis** passage of urine (diuresis, natriuresis) · **-emia / -aemia** a condition of the blood (anaemia).
[^pre-mono]: **mono-** — single, one (Greek): monoclonal, monosaccharide, monocyte. Don't confuse with **uni-** one (Latin equivalent) · **poly-** many · **multi-** many (Latin).
[^suf-mab]: **-mab** — **m**onoclonal **a**nti**b**ody: eculizumab, ravulizumab, rituximab. The letters before it tell its source: **-ximab** chimeric · **-zumab** humanised · **-umab** fully human. Don't confuse with **-nib** a small-molecule kinase inhibitor (imatinib) · **-cept** a receptor fusion protein (etanercept).
[^suf-poiesis]: **-poiesis / -poietic** — making, production: hematopoiesis, erythropoiesis, erythropoietin. Don't confuse with **-plasia** growth of tissue · **-genesis** origin, creation (gametogenesis).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against, opposite: antigen (generates *anti*bodies), antibody, anticoagulant. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (antenatal) · **anterior** in front.
