# Acid Base Balance

## The basic equation
- CO₂ + H₂O → (carbonic anhydrase) → H₂CO₃
  - H₂CO₃ = weak acid
  - → H⁺ (acid) + HCO₃⁻ (base)

## Maintenance of Acid Base Balance
### pH
- Normal 7.35–7.45
  - > 7.8 or < 6.9 → death
- Derangement of pH
  - → Demineralization of proteinaceous enzymes (↓ activity)
  - → Alkalosis: ↑ CNS excitability
  - → Acidosis: ↓ CNS excitability

### First line defense — buffers
- Maintains pH without correcting the cause
- Extracellular buffer
  - CO₂ + H₂O → (CA) → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
  - HCO₃⁻ = most important buffer
  - Tightly regulates CO₂ in lung and H⁺ in kidney
- Intracellular buffer
  - Proteins with histidine
    - Hb in RBCs = most plentiful intracellular buffer
  - Phosphate → urinary buffer
- Note: ammonia → urinary buffer

### Second line defense — lungs
- Alters pCO₂ in lungs
  - Can correct up to 75 %
- Alveolar ventilation ∝ 1 / pCO₂
  - Hyperventilation → ↑ CO₂ washout → ↓ pCO₂
  - Hypoventilation → ↑ CO₂ → ↑ pCO₂

### Third line defense — kidneys
- Correct the cause → normalize pH by
  - Generation of HCO₃⁻ (unique to kidney)
  - H⁺ excretion
  - HCO₃⁻ excretion / reabsorption

### PCT
- 1. H⁺ excretion and HCO₃⁻ reabsorption
  - Na⁺–H⁺ exchanger → H⁺ into lumen
  - Filtered HCO₃⁻ acts as buffer for H⁺
  - H₂CO₃ → (CA) → CO₂ + H₂O → CO₂ recycled into cell
  - Cell: CO₂ + H₂O → (CA II) → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
  - Generated HCO₃⁻ reabsorbed
  - No net gain → filtered = generated
- 2. New HCO₃⁻ with substitute buffers (unique to kidney)
  - a. HPO₄²⁻ as buffer
    - H⁺ + HPO₄²⁻ → H₂PO₄⁻
    - H₂PO₄⁻ = titratable acid excreted in urine
    - Generated HCO₃⁻ reabsorbed → net gain
  - b. NH₃ as buffer
    - Glutamine → 2 HCO₃⁻ + NH₃
    - NH₃ → lumen by non-ionic diffusion
    - H⁺ + NH₃ → NH₄⁺ → ammonium excreted in urine
    - New HCO₃⁻ generated → net gain
- Limiting pH
  - pH at which PCT stops H⁺ excretion = 4.5
  - Never seen in PCT (strict buffering)
  - Seen in collecting duct → urine acidified

### Collecting duct
- Regulated by protein hensin
- 1. H⁺ excretion — acidosis — type A intercalated cell
  - Luminal H⁺–K⁺ ATPase → excretion of H⁺
  - Under control of aldosterone
  - Basolateral Cl⁻–HCO₃⁻ exchanger
  - → HCO₃⁻ reabsorption & replenishment
- 2. HCO₃⁻ excretion — alkalosis — type B intercalated cell
  - Luminal HCO₃⁻–Cl⁻ exchanger → HCO₃⁻ excretion
  - Basolateral H⁺–K⁺ ATPase → H⁺ reabsorption

## Acid Base Disorders
### Normal values
- pH 7.35–7.45
- pCO₂ 35–45 mm Hg
- HCO₃⁻ 22–26 mEq/L (avg 24)

### Metabolic acidosis (↓ pH)
- ↑ H⁺
  - Lactic acidosis
  - Diabetic ketoacidosis
  - Salicylic acid poisoning
- ↓ HCO₃⁻
  - Diarrhea (intestinal contents alkaline)
- Lungs
  - Hyperventilation → CO₂ washout → ↓ pCO₂ & ↓ H⁺
- Kidneys
  - H⁺ excretion by type A cell in CD
  - Can lead to hyperkalemia

### Metabolic alkalosis (↑ pH)
- ↓ H⁺
  - Vomiting (loss of acidic stomach contents)
  - Hyperaldosteronism (↑ H⁺ excretion)
  - Diuretics → hypovolemia → (+) RAAS → ↑ aldosterone
    - → H⁺ excretion → volume contraction alkalosis
- ↑ HCO₃⁻
  - Sodium bicarbonate ingestion
- Lungs
  - ↓ rate and depth (hypoventilation) → CO₂ build up → ↑ pCO₂
- Kidneys
  - Base excretion by type B intercalated cells

### Respiratory acidosis (↓ pH)
- ↑ pCO₂
  - Respiratory depression → opioids, barbiturates
  - Respiratory muscle paralysis → Guillain–Barré, polio
  - Airway obstruction → COPD
- Kidneys → ↑ HCO₃⁻
  - H⁺ excretion
  - Reabsorption and generation of HCO₃⁻

### Respiratory alkalosis (↑ pH)
- ↓ pCO₂ → hyperventilation
  - Hysteria
  - High altitude
- Kidneys
  - HCO₃⁻ excretion by type B cells → ↓ HCO₃⁻

### Calcium note
- H⁺ and Ca²⁺ compete for albumin
  - ↓ H⁺ in respiratory alkalosis → ↑ Ca²⁺ binding to albumin
  - → ↓ free Ca²⁺ (↓ actions) → hypocalcemic tetany

### Anion gap
- Law of electroneutrality
- Contributes to electroneutrality
  - Anions − cations d/t anions not routinely measured
  - Sulfate, protein anions, lactate, salicylate
- AG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)
  - Normally 144 − (108 + 24) = 12 mEq/L
  - Range 8–16 mEq/L
- HAGMA
  - ↑ unmeasured anions
  - DKA, lactic acidosis, salicylate poisoning
- NAGMA
  - ↑ measured anion → ↑ Cl⁻ → hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis
  - Diarrhea, renal tubular acidosis, hyperaldosteronism
