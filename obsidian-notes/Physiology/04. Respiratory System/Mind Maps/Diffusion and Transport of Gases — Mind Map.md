# Diffusion and Transport of Gases

## Diffusion of Gases
- Transit time of blood in alveolar capillaries → 0.75 s
  - Diffusion must take place in ≤ 0.75 s
- Simple diffusion → no ATP (passive)
- Respiratory membrane (Rm)
  - Alveoli (high O2) → Rm → plasma → RBC
  - RBC (high CO2) → plasma → Rm → alveoli

### Factors affecting diffusion (Fick's law)
- Directly proportional
  - Concentration gradient (high → low)
  - Surface area of Rm
  - Lipid solubility of gas
- Inversely proportional
  - Thickness of Rm
  - Size of particle

### Applied aspect
- Emphysema
  - α1 antitrypsin deficiency
  - → ↑ trypsin activity (protease)
  - → ↑ destruction of Rm
  - → ↓ membrane surface area
  - → ↓ diffusion of gases
  - Distended alveoli
- Pulmonary fibrosis
  - Fibrosis → ↑ thickness of Rm
  - → ↓ diffusion of gases

### DLCO
- Diffusion capacity of lung using CO
- CO affinity to Hb = 210 × O2
- Normal → 25 ml/min/mmHg
- ↑ DLCO (d/t ↑ blood flow)
  - Exercise (physiological)
  - Polycythaemia
  - Pulmonary haemorrhage
- ↓ DLCO
  - Anaemia (d/t ↓ blood flow)
  - Emphysema (↓ area of Rm)
  - Pulmonary fibrosis (↑ thickness of Rm)
- CO2 diffuses > O2 (20 × solubility)

## Transport of Gases
### Form of transport
- O2
  - Plasma → dissolved 3 % → determines PO2
  - Hb → combined 97 %
  - 1 g Hb → 1.34 ml O2
  - Hb ↑ O2 transport 70 times
- CO2
  - Bicarbonate 70 % → major form
  - Hb 30 % → CO2 + Hb → carbamino Hb

### O2–Hb dissociation curve
- Sigmoid curve
  - 1 Hb binds 4 O2
  - 1st O2 → difficult → Hb tight form
  - 2nd–4th O2 → easy → Hb relaxed form
  - = cooperative binding kinetics
- Plateau at PO2 100 mmHg → Hb fully saturated
- P50 → 50 % saturated at PO2 27 mmHg
- L shift → P50 ↓
- R shift → P50 ↑

### Shifts
- L shift (in lungs)
  - O2 binding / loading onto Hb
  - ↑ affinity → load O2 → unload CO2 = Haldane effect
  - ↓ CO2 (hypocarbia), ↓ H+ (alkalosis)
  - Causes → "total" (fetal) Hb ↓ affinity to 2,3-DPG
  - Causes → CO poisoning
  - Causes → stored blood, chronic hypoxia → ↓ glycolysis → ↓ 2,3-DPG
- R shift (in tissues)
  - Release / unloading of O2
  - ↓ affinity → release O2 = Bohr effect
  - ↓ O2 (hypoxia) → ↑ CO2 (hypercarbia) → ↑ H+ (acidosis)
  - Hypoxia → anaerobic glycolysis → ↑ 2,3-DPG → binds β chain → O2 release
  - Thyroid hormones → ↑ metabolism
  - Exercise
  - High altitude
- CPDA → less fall in 2,3-DPG → used for blood storage

### O2–myoglobin dissociation curve
- O2 storage in muscles
- 1 myoglobin : 1 O2 → no cooperativity
- Rectangular hyperbola

### CO2 dissociation
- Mechanism (venous blood)
  - Tissue PCO2 45 → CO2 → plasma (PCO2 40) → RBC
  - CO2 + H2O → carbonic anhydrase → H2CO3
  - H2CO3 → H+ + HCO3−
  - H+ → buffered by Hb
  - Hb + CO2 → carbamino Hb
  - HCO3− → anion exchanger → plasma (70 %)
  - Cl− in → chloride shift (Hamburger phenomenon)
  - Cl− osmotically active → H2O in → RBC swells
  - → venous haematocrit / PCV ↑ by 3 %
- CO2 dissociation curve
  - PaCO2 40 → 48 ml / 100 ml
  - PvCO2 45 → 52 ml / 100 ml
  - → 4 ml CO2 transported / 100 ml blood
