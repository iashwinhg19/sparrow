# 🌸 Female Reproduction

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> Female reproduction is the monthly programme in which the **hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis** ripens **one egg** (oogenesis[^ety-oogenesis][^pre-oo][^suf-genesis] + follicular growth), releases it at **ovulation** and prepares the **endometrium, cervix and vagina** to receive a pregnancy — and, after birth, keeps the baby fed (**lactation**). Everything in the chapter answers three questions:
> - ✅ **Which hormone is in charge?** — FSH/estrogen in the first half, LH/progesterone in the second.
> - ✅ **What does each target tissue do under it?** — ovary, endometrium, cervical mucus, vaginal epithelium, breast.
> - ✅ **What happens if fertilisation does / does not occur?** — hCG rescues the corpus luteum, or it degenerates and menstruation follows.

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Monthly Hotel With Two Shifts

Think of the ovary as a hotel that prepares **one room for one guest every month**. The **day shift (follicular phase)** is run by **FSH + estrogen**: it builds the room (proliferative endometrium, thin "welcome" cervical mucus). Mid-month, estrogen flips the switch to **positive feedback** → the **LH surge** opens the door (ovulation). The **night shift (luteal phase)** is run by **LH + progesterone** from the corpus luteum: it furnishes the room (secretory endometrium, thick "closed" mucus, warmer body). If no guest checks in, the night manager (corpus luteum) leaves on **day 24**, progesterone falls, PGF2α tears the room down — **menstruation**. If a guest arrives, the guest's own manager (**hCG**) keeps the night shift working.

```
FOLLICULAR (variable)              OVULATION (day 14)             LUTEAL (constant 14 d)
FSH → follicles grow               ↑↑ estrogen → + feedback         LH → corpus luteum → PROGESTERONE
1 Graafian follicle → ESTROGEN     → LH surge (24–36 h before)      endometrium: secretory (15–28 d)
endometrium: proliferative (6–14)  → meiosis I completed            cervix: thick, less elastic
cervix: thin, watery, fern, spinn  → follicle wall lysis → egg out  BBT ↑ 0.5–1 °C
                                                                    no hCG → CL degenerates d24 → menses (5 d)
```

## 🥚 Oogenesis

The egg is made in steps labelled **A → I** on the page. Two "pauses" (meiotic arrests) are the whole exam point: the primary oocyte waits in **prophase I** from fetal life until puberty, and the secondary oocyte waits in **metaphase II** until a sperm arrives.

| Label | Stage / event | Ploidy | Key point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Oogonia**[^ety-oogonia] | 2n | Germ-line stem cells of the ovary |
| **B** | **Mitosis**[^ety-mitosis][^suf-osis] | — | Oogonia multiply (fetal life) |
| **C** | **Primary Oocyte**[^ety-oocyte][^suf-cyte] | 2n | **Meiosis**[^ety-meiosis] **I arrest till puberty** in the **diplotene**[^ety-diplotene][^pre-diplo] **stage of prophase**[^ety-prophase][^pre-pro][^1] |
| **D** | **Meiosis I at puberty**[^ety-puberty] | — | Completed just before ovulation (each cycle) |
| **E** | **Secondary Oocyte** | n | **Meiosis II arrest in metaphase**[^ety-metaphase][^pre-meta] |
| **F** | **First polar body**[^ety-polar-body] | n | Small cell that takes the discarded chromosomes |
| **G** | **Meiosis II resumes during fertilization**[^ety-fertilization] | — | Triggered by sperm entry |
| **H** | **Female pronucleus**[^ety-pronucleus] | n | Fuses with the male pronucleus → zygote |
| **I** | **Second polar body** | n | Second discarded set of chromosomes |

```
A  Oogonia (2n)
   │  B  mitosis
   ▼
C  PRIMARY OOCYTE (2n) ── meiosis I ARREST till puberty (diplotene stage of prophase)
   │  D  meiosis I at puberty
   ├──────────────► F  First polar body (n)
   ▼
E  SECONDARY OOCYTE (n) ── meiosis II ARREST in METAPHASE
   │  G  meiosis II resumes during fertilization
   ├──────────────► I  Second polar body (n)
   ▼
H  FEMALE PRONUCLEUS (n)
```

## 🔄 Regulation

### Hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis — **Two cell two gonadotropin model**[^ety-gonadotropin][^suf-tropin]

The hypothalamus[^ety-hypothalamus][^pre-hypo] releases GnRH, which drives the anterior pituitary[^ety-pituitary] to release **LH** and **FSH**. The ovary needs **two cells working together** to make estrogen: the **theca**[^ety-theca] **cells** (driven by LH) make the androgen **androstenedione**[^ety-androstenedione][^pre-andro], which diffuses into the **granulosa**[^ety-granulosa] **cells** (driven by FSH), where the enzyme **aromatase**[^ety-aromatase][^suf-ase] converts it into **estradiol**[^ety-estradiol].[^2] Granulosa cells also release progesterone[^ety-progesterone], **activin**[^ety-activin] and **inhibin**[^ety-inhibin]**-B**, which feed back on the pituitary.

```
                       HYPOTHALAMUS
                            │
                          GnRH
                            │ (+)
                    ANTERIOR PITUITARY
               ┌────────────┴────────────┐
               ▼                         ▼
               LH                        FSH
               │                         │ (+)
               ▼                         ▼
          THECA CELLS              GRANULOSA CELLS
               │                         │
               ▼                         ├──► Androstenedione ──(Aromatase)──► ESTRADIOL
        Androstenedione ─────────────────┘    Progesterone · Activin · Inhibin-B

FEEDBACK ON THE PITUITARY
   Estradiol     ──(−)──►  LH
   Progesterone  ──(−)──►  LH
   Activin       ──(+)──►  FSH
   Inhibin-B     ──(−)──►  FSH
```

![[Female Reproduction - two cell two gonadotropin model.png]]
*Two cell two gonadotropin model: LH → theca cells → androstenedione → passes to granulosa cells (FSH-driven) → aromatase → estradiol; estradiol and progesterone inhibit LH, activin stimulates and inhibin-B inhibits FSH (cropped from the handwritten page; the page spells it "Androstenidione").*

| Cell | Hormone driving it | What it makes | Feedback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Theca cells** | **LH** | Androstenedione | — |
| **Granulosa cells** | **FSH** | Androstenedione → **estradiol** (aromatase), progesterone, activin, inhibin-B | Estradiol (−) LH · progesterone (−) LH · activin (+) FSH · inhibin-B (−) FSH |

## 🩸 Menstrual Cycle

- **28-day cycle** in females.
- **Note:** **Estrous**[^ety-estrous] **cycle** — seen in mammals other than humans.[^3]

### 🥚 Changes in Ovary

#### Follicular[^ety-follicle] phase

**FSH** starts follicular growth; of the 10–15 follicles that grow, **one becomes dominant** (the **Graafian**[^ety-graafian] **follicle**), makes most of the estrogen, and the rising **estrogen** switches the pituitary into **positive feedback** → **LH surge**[^4], which completes meiosis I and causes ovulation[^ety-ovulation][^suf-ation].

```
FSH
 ↓
Follicular growth
 ↓
10–15 follicles grow ─────────► 1 follicle becomes dominant: GRAAFIAN FOLLICLE
 ↓                                 • maximum FSH receptors
ESTROGEN  ◄────┐                   • maximum aromatase activity
 ↓             │ + feedback        • maximum estrogen production
↑↑ LUTEINIZING HORMONE (LH surge) ─┘
 │
 ├──► Acts on 1° oocyte → meiosis I is completed → OVULATION
 ├──► Progesterone synthesis
 └──► Synthesis of prostaglandins
```

- **FSH** → **follicular growth** → **10–15 follicles grow** → **1 follicle becomes dominant: Graafian follicle**
  - **Maximum FSH receptors**
  - **Maximum aromatase activity**
  - **Maximum estrogen production**
- **Estrogen** ⇄ **↑↑ Luteinizing**[^ety-luteinizing] **Hormone (LH surge)** — **+ feedback** loop.
- The LH surge:
  1. **Acts on 1° oocyte** → **meiosis I is completed** → **Ovulation**
  2. **Progesterone synthesis**
  3. **Synthesis of prostaglandins**[^ety-prostaglandin]

#### Luteal[^ety-luteal] phase

After ovulation the empty follicle bleeds and clots; under **LH** the remaining cells fill with yellow lipid (luteal cells) and form the **corpus luteum**, the gland that makes **progesterone**.

```
Bleeding inside Graafian follicle
 ↓
Clots
 ↓  LH
Luteal cells (yellow-coloured)
 ↓
CORPUS LUTEUM ── if fertilization does not occur ──► degenerates on 24th day of cycle ──► menstrual blood loss
 ↓
PROGESTERONE
```

- **Bleeding inside Graafian follicle** → **clots** → (LH) → **luteal cells (yellow-coloured)** → **corpus luteum** → **progesterone**.
- **If fertilization does not occur** → the corpus luteum **degenerates on 24th day of cycle** → **menstrual**[^ety-menstrual] **blood loss**.[^7]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> The female cycle is **one feedback switch and one fixed timer**. The switch: estradiol from the dominant Graafian follicle normally *inhibits* LH, but once it is high enough for long enough it flips to **positive feedback** → the **LH surge** (24–36 h before ovulation) completes meiosis I, makes progesterone and prostaglandins and ruptures the follicle. The timer: the **corpus luteum lives a fixed ~14 days** (degenerates on day 24 of a 28-day cycle) unless **hCG** — an LH look-alike with a 37-h half-life — rescues it. So the luteal phase is constant and every variation in cycle length lies in the follicular phase.
>
> Every target tissue simply reads the hormone that dominates: **estrogen builds and opens** (proliferative endometrium, thin stretchy ferning mucus, superficial vaginal cells, ductal breast growth), **progesterone matures and closes** (secretory endometrium, thick mucus, intermediate cells, lobulo-alveolar growth, warmer BBT). Withdrawal of progesterone → PGF2α → spiral-artery spasm → menstruation.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> FSH recruits 10–15 follicles; one Graafian follicle (maximum FSH receptors and aromatase) dominates and raises estradiol; positive feedback gives the LH surge, ovulation on day 14 and a corpus luteum that secretes progesterone for 14 days. BBT rises 0.5–1 °C, the fern pattern disappears and the endometrium turns secretory; without hCG the corpus luteum degenerates and a 5-day menstruation follows.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> A cycle can fail silently: if estradiol never reaches the threshold (stress, low body weight, high prolactin suppressing GnRH, PCOS-type androgen excess), there is no LH surge and no ovulation. Bleeding may still occur from estrogen withdrawal, so the woman notices only irregular cycles — but the BBT chart stays flat, mid-luteal progesterone is low and the cervical mucus keeps ferning.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Persistent anovulation leaves the endometrium under **unopposed estrogen** — continuous proliferation → hyperplasia, heavy irregular bleeding and infertility. Absent estrogen (menopause, hypogonadism) removes osteoprotegerin and NO effects → osteoporosis, rising LDL and cardiovascular risk; estrone becomes the main estrogen.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal ovulatory cycle → trigger (stress / hyperprolactinaemia / androgen excess / ageing ovary) → estradiol fails to switch to positive feedback → no LH surge, no corpus luteum → unopposed estrogen, flat BBT → anovulatory bleeding, hyperplasia, infertility
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Ovulation is proven indirectly by the things progesterone does — a BBT rise, loss of ferning and spinnbarkeit, secretory endometrium, mid-luteal progesterone. Ovulation timing (LH surge 24–36 h before) is the basis of urine LH kits and of timing hCG triggers in IVF; hCG's long half-life and LH-like β-subunit explain both pregnancy tests and its use as an "LH substitute".

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Mixing up the two meiotic arrests**
>
> **What students think:** The oocyte is arrested in metaphase until puberty and completes meiosis at ovulation.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** There are two arrests. The **primary oocyte** stops in the **diplotene stage of prophase I** from fetal life to puberty; meiosis I is completed only by the LH surge just before ovulation. The **secondary oocyte** then stops in **metaphase II** and finishes meiosis II only if fertilisation occurs. So what is ovulated is a secondary oocyte plus the first polar body.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**P**rimary — **P**rophase (diPlotene) till **P**uberty; **S**econdary — **M**etaphase II till **S**perm."
>
> **Trap 2: Thinking cycle length varies because of the luteal phase**
>
> **What students think:** In a 35-day cycle, ovulation is still on day 14.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The luteal phase has a **constant duration of 14 days** because the corpus luteum has a fixed lifespan. The extra days are added to the follicular phase, so in a 35-day cycle ovulation is on day 21.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Day of ovulation = cycle length − 14.
>
> **Trap 3: Confusing which estrogen belongs to which stage**
>
> **What students think:** Estradiol is the main estrogen throughout life.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Estradiol dominates in the reproductive age group, **estriol** in pregnancy (from the fetal–placental unit) and **estrone** after menopause (made from androstenedione by aromatase in fat). The question "main estrogen in pregnancy / post-menopause" is a classic.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** estr**I**ol — **I**n pregnancy; estr**ONE** — when the cycles are d**ONE** (menopause); estr**ADI**ol — **AD**ult reproductive life.
>
> **Trap 4: Assuming all sex steroids retain salt and water**
>
> **What students think:** Progesterone, like estrogen and aldosterone, causes salt retention.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Estrogen causes salt and water retention, but progesterone causes **natriuresis** — it is the **only steroid hormone to cause natriuresis**, by competing with aldosterone at the mineralocorticoid receptor. It also stimulates respiration, lowering alveolar PCO₂ (the mild respiratory alkalosis of pregnancy).
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Estrogen "keeps", progesterone "pees and pants".
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing up the four lactation words**
>
> **What students think:** Lactogenesis and galactopoiesis mean the same thing, and oxytocin makes milk.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Mammogenesis** = breast growth (estrogen, progesterone); **lactogenesis** = starting milk production (prolactin, insulin, GH); **galactokinesis** = release of milk (oxytocin, the let-down reflex); **galactopoiesis** = maintaining lactation (prolactin). Oxytocin only ejects milk that prolactin has made.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** -genesis = make, -kinesis = move, -poiesis = keep making.

> [!tip]+ **🔗 Related Concepts & Cross-Topic Connections**
>
> Understanding how this topic connects to other areas of medicine deepens your knowledge and helps you see the bigger picture. These connections also help you answer integrated questions on exams.
>
> **Related Topic 1: [[Male Reproduction]]**
> The same GnRH → LH/FSH axis drives the testis: LH → Leydig cells (testosterone), FSH → Sertoli cells (inhibin B) — the male version of the two-cell model; hCG acts on fetal Leydig cells for exactly that reason.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Pituitary Gland]]**
> FSH, LH and TSH share an α-subunit with hCG; prolactin and oxytocin, the two lactation hormones, come from the anterior and posterior pituitary respectively.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Homeostasis]]**
> The LH surge is the only true positive feedback in endocrine regulation, and the oxytocin loops of parturition (Ferguson reflex) and milk ejection are positive-feedback / neuroendocrine reflexes.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Regulation of Body Temperature]]**
> Progesterone's thermogenic action resets the hypothalamic set point upward by 0.5–1 °C — the basis of BBT charting.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Calcium Homeostasis]]**
> Estrogen's osteoprotegerin–RANKL brake on osteoclasts explains post-menopausal osteoporosis.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Chemical Regulation of Respiration]]**
> Progesterone stimulates respiration and lowers alveolar PCO₂ — the reason for hyperventilation in the luteal phase and pregnancy.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Ovulation tests (LH kits, BBT, mucus), oral contraceptives (estrogen + progestin suppress the LH surge and thicken mucus), IVF triggering with hCG, pregnancy tests (β-hCG), hormone replacement at menopause and lactation counselling (stress inhibits let-down) all apply this chapter.
>
> **Exam Integration:**
> Expect one-liners on the meiotic arrests, the two cell two gonadotropin model, LH surge timing, constant luteal phase, fern/spinnbarkeit, estradiol/estriol/estrone by stage, the 2/60/38 % binding split, progesterone natriuresis and thermogenesis, hCG half-life and the four lactation processes.

[^ety-progesterone]: **progesterone** — Latin *pro-*, "for, in favour of" + *gestation* (*gerere*, to carry) + *ster(ol)* + *-one* (ketone) → the steroid "for pregnancy", named in 1934 when it was purified from the corpus luteum.
[^ety-activin]: **activin / inhibin** — named for opposite actions on FSH: *activin* (Latin *activus*, active) stimulates FSH, *inhibin* (*inhibere*, to hold in) suppresses it; both are TGF-β family dimers secreted by granulosa cells.
[^ety-inhibin]: **inhibin** — Latin *in-*, "in" + *habere*, "to hold" → *inhibere*, "to hold in, restrain" + *-in* (a substance) → the substance that holds FSH back. **Differentiate:** *inhibin* (protein, gonads, ↓ FSH) · *follistatin* (binds activin, ↓ FSH) · *activin* (↑ FSH).
[^ety-estrous]: **estrous / estrus** — Greek *oistros*, "gadfly; frenzy" → the frenzied, receptive period ("heat") of animals stung into mating; *estrous cycle* = cycle of heat. Same root gives *estrogen*.
[^ety-follicle]: **follicle** — Latin *folliculus*, "little bag", diminutive of *follis*, "bellows, leather bag" → the small sac of cells that holds the oocyte. **Stages:** primordial → primary → secondary → tertiary (antral) → **Graafian** (pre-ovulatory).
[^ety-graafian]: **Graafian follicle** — Regnier de Graaf, Dutch physician and anatomist (1641–1673), who described the mature ovarian follicles in 1672 (he thought the follicle itself was the egg).
[^ety-ovulation]: **ovulation** — Latin *ovulum*, "little egg" (diminutive of *ovum*) + *-ation*, the process → the process of releasing the (little) egg. **Differentiate:** *ovulation* (release) · *ovum* (egg) · *ovary* (*-ary*, the place for eggs).
[^ety-luteinizing]: **luteinizing hormone (LH)** — Latin *luteus*, "yellow" + *-ize*, "to make" → the hormone that makes the follicle yellow, i.e. turns it into the *corpus luteum*.
[^ety-prostaglandin]: **prostaglandin** — named by Ulf von Euler (1935) because he found it in seminal fluid and thought it came from the **prostate gland** (it is actually made mainly by the seminal vesicles, and by almost every tissue). PGF2α = prostaglandin F, two double bonds, α-configuration.
[^ety-lysis]: **lysis** — Greek *lysis*, "a loosening, dissolving" → breakdown of a wall or membrane by enzymes. **Differentiate:** *lysis* (dissolution) · *rupture* (tearing) · *necrosis* (cell death).
[^ety-luteal]: **luteal / corpus luteum** — Latin *corpus*, "body" + *luteum*, "yellow" → the yellow body formed from the ruptured follicle (yellow from lutein and lipid). Opposite: *corpus albicans* (*albicare*, to be white) — the white scar after it dies.
[^ety-menstrual]: **menstrual / menstruation** — Latin *mensis*, "month" → *menstruus*, "monthly" → the monthly bleed. Same root as *menses*, *menarche* (*archē*, beginning) and *menopause*.
[^ety-endometrium]: **endometrium** — Greek *endon*, "within" + *mētra*, "womb" + *-ium*, a tissue/layer → the inner lining of the womb. **Differentiate:** *endometrium* (inner, cyclic) · *myometrium* (*mys*, muscle — middle) · *perimetrium* (*peri*, around — outer serosa).
[^ety-ischemia]: **ischemia** — Greek *ischein*, "to hold back" + *haima*, "blood" + *-ia* → blood held back. **Differentiate:** *ischemia* (reduced supply, reversible) → *infarction* (tissue death from it) · *hypoxia* (low O₂ for any reason) · *necrosis* (death of tissue).
[^ety-degeneration]: **degeneration** — Latin *de-*, "down from" + *genus*, "kind, race" + *-ation* → falling away from its kind; deterioration of tissue structure and function. **Differentiate:** *degeneration* (deterioration, often reversible early) · *regeneration* (*re-*, again — regrowth) · *necrosis* (death).
[^ety-proliferative]: **proliferative** — Latin *proles*, "offspring" + *ferre*, "to bear" → bearing offspring; cells multiplying. **Differentiate:** *proliferation* (more cells) · *hypertrophy* (bigger cells) · *hyperplasia* (more cells as a pathological/compensatory response).
[^ety-secretory]: **secretory / secretion** — Latin *se-*, "apart" + *cernere*, "to sift, separate" → separating a product out of a cell. **Differentiate:** *secretion* (a useful product released) · *excretion* (*ex-*, out — waste removed) · *exocytosis* (the membrane mechanism).
[^ety-cervix]: **cervix** — Latin, "neck" → the neck of the uterus that opens into the vagina; its glands make the cyclic mucus.
[^ety-spinnbarkeit]: **spinnbarkeit** — German *spinnbar*, "spinnable" + *-keit*, "-ness" → "spinnability": estrogen-rich mucus can be drawn into a thread 10 cm or more long between two slides or fingers.
[^ety-vagina]: **vagina** — Latin, "sheath, scabbard" → the sheath (for the sword); its stratified squamous epithelium matures with estrogen.
[^ety-parabasal]: **parabasal** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + Latin *basis*, "base" → cells lying just beside the basal layer (immature, small, round). Order of maturation: basal → parabasal → intermediate → superficial.
[^ety-thermogenic]: **thermogenic** — Greek *thermē*, "heat" + *-genic*, "producing" → heat-producing. **Differentiate:** *thermogenesis* (making heat) · *thermolysis* (losing heat) · *thermoregulation* (balancing the two).
[^ety-steroid]: **steroid** — Greek *stereos*, "solid" (→ *sterol*, a solid alcohol like cholesterol) + *-oid*, "like" → like a sterol; all built on the four-ring cyclopentanoperhydrophenanthrene nucleus. Count the carbons: estrogens C-18, androgens C-19, progesterone and corticoids C-21.
[^ety-estrogen]: **estrogen** — Greek *oistros*, "frenzy, heat" + *-gen*, "producing" → the substance that produces estrus (heat). American *estrogen* = British *oestrogen*.
[^ety-relaxin]: **relaxin** — Latin *relaxare*, "to loosen" (*re-* + *laxus*, loose) + *-in* → the substance that loosens the pelvic ligaments; discovered by Frederick Hisaw (1926) in guinea pigs.
[^ety-placenta]: **placenta** — Latin, "flat cake" (Greek *plakous*) → named for its flat, round shape; the organ of exchange and a major endocrine gland of pregnancy.
[^ety-menopause]: **menopause** — Greek *mēn*, "month" + *pausis*, "a stopping" → the stopping of the monthly bleeds (12 months of amenorrhoea, average age ~50).
[^ety-albumin]: **albumin** — Latin *albumen*, "egg white" (*albus*, white) → the main plasma protein, named after the white of egg; carries 60 % of circulating estradiol loosely.
[^ety-globulin]: **globulin** — Latin *globulus*, "little ball" + *-in* → globular proteins; SHBG is a specific globulin that binds sex steroids tightly (so they are inactive while bound).
[^ety-contractility]: **contractility / excitability** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *trahere*, "to draw" → drawing together; *ex-*, "out" + *citare*, "to set in motion" → being set in motion by a stimulus. Estrogen raises both in the myometrium; progesterone lowers both.
[^ety-osteoprotegerin]: **osteoprotegerin (OPG)** — Greek *osteon*, "bone" + Latin *protegere*, "to protect" (*pro-* + *tegere*, to cover) + *-in* → the bone-protecting protein; a decoy receptor for RANK ligand.
[^ety-rank-ligand]: **RANK ligand (RANKL)** — *Receptor Activator of Nuclear factor κ-B* + Latin *ligare*, "to bind" → the molecule that binds RANK on osteoclast precursors and switches them on.
[^ety-osteoclast]: **osteoclast** — Greek *osteon*, "bone" + *klastos*, "broken" → the bone-breaker. **Differentiate:** *osteoclast* (resorbs bone) · *osteoblast* (*blastos*, germ — builds bone) · *osteocyte* (*kytos*, cell — the mature bone cell in its lacuna).
[^ety-resorption]: **resorption** — Latin *re-*, "back, again" + *sorbere*, "to suck in" → sucking back in; osteoclasts dissolve bone mineral and matrix into the blood. **Differentiate:** *resorption* (taking back a tissue) · *reabsorption* (taking back filtered fluid, e.g. in the nephron) · *absorption* (taking in from outside, e.g. gut).
[^ety-vasodilation]: **vasodilation** — Latin *vas*, "vessel" + *dilatare*, "to spread out, widen" (*dis-* + *latus*, wide) + *-ion* → widening of the vessel. **Differentiate:** *vasodilation* (lumen widens, resistance falls) · *vasoconstriction* (narrows) · *venoconstriction* (veins narrow → ↑ venous return).
[^ety-neuroprotective]: **neuroprotective** — Greek *neuron*, "nerve, sinew" + Latin *protegere*, "to cover in front, protect" → protecting nerve cells from injury and death (estrogen reduces neuronal apoptosis).
[^ety-lobulo-alveolar]: **lobulo-alveolar** — Latin *lobulus*, "small lobe" + *alveolus*, "small cavity, little trough" → the milk-producing units of the breast (alveoli clustered into lobules). Estrogen grows the *ducts*, progesterone the *lobules and alveoli*.
[^ety-natriuresis]: **natriuresis** — Latin *natrium*, "sodium" (from Egyptian/Greek *nitron*, soda) + Greek *ourēsis*, "urination" → excretion of sodium in the urine. **Differentiate:** *natriuresis* (↑ Na⁺ excretion) · *diuresis* (↑ urine volume) · *kaliuresis* (↑ K⁺ excretion).
[^ety-follistatin]: **follistatin** — *folli(cle)* (FSH) + Greek *statos*, "standing, stopped" (*-statin*, "that which stops") + *-in* → the protein that stops FSH secretion by binding activin.
[^ety-symphysis]: **symphysis** — Greek *syn-*, "together" + *physis*, "growth" → grown together; a secondary cartilaginous joint in the midline (pubic symphysis), which relaxin loosens before labour.
[^ety-glycoprotein]: **glycoprotein** — Greek *glykys*, "sweet" (→ sugar) + *prōteios*, "of first rank" (→ protein) → a protein carrying sugar chains; FSH, LH, TSH and hCG are the four glycoprotein hormones (common α, specific β).
[^ety-syncytiotrophoblast]: **syncytiotrophoblast** — Greek *syn-*, "together" + *kytos*, "cell" (*syncytium*, cells fused together, many nuclei in one cytoplasm) + *trophē*, "nourishment" + *blastos*, "germ, bud" → the fused, nourishing outer layer of the placental villus that makes hCG. **Differentiate:** *syncytiotrophoblast* (outer, fused, endocrine) vs *cytotrophoblast* (inner, separate cells, its stem cells).
[^ety-leydig]: **Leydig cells** — Franz Leydig, German anatomist and zoologist (1821–1908), who described the interstitial cells of the testis in 1850; they make testosterone under LH (or hCG in the fetus).
[^ety-immunosuppressive]: **immunosuppressive** — Latin *immunis*, "exempt, free from duty" + *sub-*, "under" + *premere*, "to press" → pressing the immune response down. hCG (with progesterone) helps the mother tolerate the half-foreign fetus.
[^ety-lactation]: **lactation** — Latin *lac, lactis*, "milk" + *-ation*, the process → the process of producing and giving milk. Greek equivalent: *gala, galaktos* (→ *galacto-*).
[^ety-mammogenesis]: **mammogenesis** — Latin *mamma*, "breast" + Greek *genesis*, "creation" → growth of the breast (ducts and lobulo-alveolar units) in puberty and pregnancy.
[^ety-lactogenesis]: **lactogenesis** — Latin *lac*, "milk" + Greek *genesis*, "creation" → the start of milk production after delivery.
[^ety-prolactin]: **prolactin** — Latin *pro-*, "for" + *lac*, "milk" + *-in* → the hormone "for milk"; from the anterior pituitary lactotrophs, kept inhibited by dopamine.
[^ety-insulin]: **insulin** — Latin *insula*, "island" + *-in* → the hormone of the *islets* of Langerhans; needed (with prolactin, GH and cortisol) for milk synthesis.
[^ety-galactokinesis]: **galactokinesis** — Greek *gala, galaktos*, "milk" + *kinēsis*, "movement" → movement (ejection) of milk; the oxytocin let-down reflex.
[^ety-reflex]: **reflex** — Latin *re-*, "back" + *flectere*, "to bend" → *reflexus*, bent back: a stimulus "bent back" into an automatic response. **Differentiate:** *neural reflex* (nerve in, nerve out) · *neuroendocrine reflex* (nerve in, hormone out — milk ejection).
[^ety-oxytocin]: **oxytocin** — Greek *oxys*, "swift" + *tokos*, "childbirth" → swift birth; named by Henry Dale (1906) for its uterine effect. It also contracts the myoepithelial cells of the breast (milk ejection).
[^ety-neuroendocrine]: **neuroendocrine** — Greek *neuron*, "nerve" + *endon*, "within" + *krinein*, "to secrete" → a nerve cell that secretes a hormone into the blood (hypothalamic neurons releasing oxytocin from the posterior pituitary).
[^ety-galactopoiesis]: **galactopoiesis** — Greek *gala*, "milk" + *poiēsis*, "making" → continued making of milk, i.e. maintenance of established lactation (prolactin, regular emptying of the breast).
[^pre-oo]: **oo-** — egg (Greek *ōion*): oogenesis, oogonium, oocyte. Don't confuse with **oophor-** ovary (oophorectomy) · **ovi- / ovo-** egg (Latin: oviduct, ovum).
[^suf-genesis]: **-genesis** — origin, formation: oogenesis, mammogenesis, lactogenesis. Don't confuse with **-genic** producing (thermogenic, pyrogenic) · **-poiesis** making continuously (galactopoiesis, haematopoiesis) · **-plasia** growth of tissue (hyperplasia).
[^suf-osis]: **-osis** — a process or condition: mitosis, meiosis, fibrosis. Don't confuse with **-asis / -esis** (a process: diuresis) · **-itis** inflammation · **-ation** an action.
[^suf-cyte]: **-cyte** — cell: oocyte, osteocyte, leukocyte. Don't confuse with **-blast** a germ/precursor cell (osteoblast, trophoblast) · **-clast** a breaker cell (osteoclast).
[^pre-diplo]: **diplo-** — double: diplotene, diploid, diplopia. Don't confuse with **haplo-** single (haploid) · **bi- / di-** two (Latin/Greek numerals: bilateral, disaccharide).
[^pre-pro]: **pro-** — before, forward, for: prophase, pronucleus, progesterone, prolactin. Don't confuse with **pre-** before (Latin, in time: preload) · **post-** after · **proto-** first (protoplasm).
[^pre-meta]: **meta-** — after, between, change: metaphase, metabolism, metastasis. Don't confuse with **meso-** middle (mesoderm) · **para-** beside.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypothalamus, hypophysis, hypothermia. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep · **sub-** below (Latin equivalent).
[^suf-tropin]: **-tropin / -tropic** — "turning towards", stimulating a target gland: gonadotropin, corticotropin, thyrotropin. Don't confuse with **-trophin / -trophic** nourishing (often used interchangeably in British spelling) · **-tropy** in *isotropy* (direction).
[^pre-andro]: **andro-** — man, male: androstenedione, androgen, android. Don't confuse with **anthropo-** human being (anthropology) · **gyno- / gyneco-** woman.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: aromatase, kinase, lipase, amylase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (glucose, lactose) · **-ase** in *phase* (Greek *phasis*, an appearance — not an enzyme).
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -ition / -ion** — the process or result of an action: ovulation, lactation, fertilization, degeneration. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition (mitosis) · **-ism** a state (metabolism).
[^suf-lysis]: **-lysis** — loosening, breakdown: lysis of the follicle wall, haemolysis, thrombolysis. Don't confuse with **-lytic** (adjective: proteolytic) · **-lyte** a substance that dissolves into ions (electrolyte).
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endometrium, endocrine, endothelium. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside · **ecto-** outer · **myo-** muscle (myometrium) · **peri-** around (perimetrium).
[^suf-ium]: **-ium** — a tissue, layer or structure: endometrium, myometrium, pericardium, epithelium. Don't confuse with **-ia** a condition (ischemia) · **-ism** a state.
[^suf-emia]: **-emia / -aemia** — a condition of the blood: ischemia, anaemia, hyperkalaemia. Don't confuse with **-uria** in the urine (proteinuria) · **-oma** a tumour.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down from, away, undoing: degeneration, denaturation, dehydration. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart (dissociation) · **dys-** bad, abnormal (dysmenorrhoea) · **di-** two.
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, alongside, abnormal: parabasal, paraventricular, paracrine. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **pre-** before · **post-** after.
[^pre-thermo]: **thermo-** — heat: thermogenic, thermoregulation, thermometer. Don't confuse with **thrombo-** clot · **therm-** in *hypothermia* (same root, as the second element).
[^suf-oid]: **-oid** — like, resembling: steroid (like a sterol), android, thyroid (shield-like). Don't confuse with **-ode** path/way (electrode, Greek *hodos*) · **-ide** a chemical compound (chloride).
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with: contractility, conduction, coagulation. Don't confuse with **contra-** against (contraception, contraindication) · **counter-** against.
[^pre-osteo]: **osteo-** — bone: osteoprotegerin, osteoclast, osteoporosis. Don't confuse with **ostium / os** mouth, opening (*os* of the cervix — Latin *os, oris*) · **oste-** vs **ost-** (*ostracod*, shell).
[^suf-clast]: **-clast** — breaker: osteoclast, chondroclast, iconoclast. Don't confuse with **-blast** germ, builder (osteoblast) · **-cyte** mature cell (osteocyte).
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: resorption, reflex, relaxin, reperfusion. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards, behind (retroverted uterus) · **pre-** before.
[^pre-vaso]: **vaso-** — vessel: vasodilation, vasoconstriction, vasopressin. Don't confuse with **veno-** vein (venoconstriction) · **vas deferens** (the duct, same root "vessel").
[^pre-neuro]: **neuro-** — nerve: neuroprotective, neuroendocrine, neuron. Don't confuse with **nephro-** kidney · **neo-** new.
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against, opposing: anti-estrogenic, antipyretic, antibody. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (antenatal, antepartum) · **anti-** in *anticipate* (actually *ante-*).
[^suf-uresis]: **-uresis / -uria** — passing in urine: natriuresis, diuresis, kaliuresis; *-uria* for a substance found in urine (glycosuria). Don't confuse with **-emia** in the blood · **uretero-** the ureter.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym- / syl-** — together, with: symphysis, syncytiotrophoblast, synapse. Don't confuse with **sin-** (Latin *sinister*, left) · **semi-** half.
[^pre-glyco]: **glyco- / gluco-** — sweet, sugar: glycoprotein, glycogen, glucose. Don't confuse with **glia** glue (neuroglia) · **glyc-** in *glycerol* (same root, sweet alcohol).
[^suf-blast]: **-blast** — germ, bud, immature builder cell: trophoblast, osteoblast, erythroblast. Don't confuse with **-clast** breaker · **-cyte** mature cell · **-plast** formed thing (chloroplast).
[^pre-sub]: **sub- (sup-, suf-, sug-)** — under, below, less than: immunosuppressive (*sub-premere*), subendocardial, suffocate. Don't confuse with **supra- / super-** above · **hypo-** under (Greek equivalent).
[^pre-galacto]: **galacto-** — milk (Greek *gala*): galactokinesis, galactopoiesis, galactorrhoea, galactose. Don't confuse with **lacto-** milk (Latin: lactation, lactose) — same meaning, other language · **gastro-** stomach.
[^suf-kinesis]: **-kinesis** — movement: galactokinesis, cytokinesis, dyskinesia. Don't confuse with **-kinin** a substance that causes movement (bradykinin) · **kinase** an enzyme that moves a phosphate.
[^suf-poiesis]: **-poiesis** — making, production: galactopoiesis, haematopoiesis, erythropoiesis. Don't confuse with **-genesis** origin (a one-time start) · **-plasia** tissue growth.
