# 🔁 Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> The loop of Henle[^ety-henle] (LOH) is a **hairpin countercurrent**[^ety-countercurrent][^pre-counter] **multiplier**[^ety-multiplier][^pre-multi]: its water-permeable descending limb and salt-pumping, water-tight ascending limb build the **medullary hyperosmolarity**[^ety-osmolarity][^pre-hyper] (up to 1200 mOsm/L) that lets the kidney make urine from **50 to 1400 mOsm/L**. The distal[^ety-distal] convoluted tubule (DCT) fine-tunes Na⁺ (5 %) and Ca²⁺ (9 %) and, where it touches its own glomerulus, forms the **juxtaglomerular**[^ety-juxtaglomerular][^pre-juxta] **apparatus** that controls renin and GFR. Everything here answers:
> - ✅ **How is the medulla made salty?** — NKCC2 in the thick ascending limb (single effect), multiplied by countercurrent flow, preserved by the vasa recta, helped by urea (50 : 50)
> - ✅ **Which transporter, which drug, which syndrome?** — NKCC2 → loop diuretics / Bartter; NCC → thiazides / Gitelman; TRPV5 → PTH, vitamin D
> - ✅ **What does the JGA do?** — JG cells release renin (RAAS), macula densa senses NaCl (TGF), lacis cells support

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Hairpin Salt Pump and a Heat-Saving Stork

Picture a **stork standing in cold water**: warm blood going down its leg hands its heat to the cold blood coming up, so the heat stays in the body and the foot stays cold. The loop of Henle does the same with **salt** instead of heat: the ascending limb pumps salt out (without water), the descending limb loses water to that salty interstitium, and because the two limbs run side by side in opposite directions, each small step is **multiplied** into a huge gradient from 300 (cortex) to 1200 mOsm/L (papilla). The vasa recta are the stork's leg veins — they run the same hairpin so they do not wash the salt away.

```
                PCT 300 ─►┐                        ┌─► 300→(dilute) DCT
DESCENDING LIMB           │ ↓ H₂O out (AQP-1)       ↑ │ Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ out (NKCC2)   ASCENDING LIMB
"concentrating"   600     │                         │ 600    "diluting"
permeable to H₂O  900     │                         │ 900    permeable to solutes
impermeable Na⁺  1200     └──────── tip 1200 ───────┘ 1200   impermeable to H₂O
SINGLE EFFECT = Na⁺ moved without H₂O  ×  countercurrent flow  =  MULTIPLIER  →  medulla 1200 (½ NaCl, ½ urea)
VASA RECTA = countercurrent EXCHANGER → keeps the gradient   ·   longer loop → higher medullary osmolarity
DCT: NCC (thiazide, Gitelman) 5 % Na⁺ · TRPV5 (PTH, vit D) 9 % Ca²⁺ · JGA: renin · macula densa · lacis cells
```

## 🦩 Counter Current Mechanism

**Requirements for counter current system:**
1. **2 tubes lying close to each other.**
2. **Fluid movement in opposite directions in the tubes.**

**Seen in:**
- **Human kidneys (loop of Henle).**
- **Testis**[^ety-testis].[^1]
- **Intestines**[^ety-intestine][^pre-intra].
- **Skin (temperature regulation).**

![Countercurrent heat exchange in a wading bird's leg — warm arterial blood going down warms the cold venous blood coming up (Wikimedia Commons)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Counter_current_exchange_in_birds.svg/800px-Counter_current_exchange_in_birds.svg.png)
*The page's stork: (1) exchange of heat between the two vessels, (2) arterial blood warm, (3) cold venous blood → warm (d/t heat exchange) before it reaches the body; the foot stands in cold water — the counter current system (Wikimedia Commons).*

🧠 Two tubes, opposite flow, close together — that is all a countercurrent system needs. Whatever crosses between the tubes (heat in the stork, salt in the kidney) gets **trapped** at the hairpin end instead of being carried away.

## 🔁 Loop of Henle (LOH)

- **Loop of Henle receives isotonic**[^ety-isotonic][^pre-iso] **fluid from PCT (Osmolarity = 300 mOsm/L).**
- **Function: concentration**[^ety-concentration][^pre-con] **and dilution**[^ety-dilution][^pre-dis] **of urine.**
- **Range of urine osmolarity = 50–1400 mOsm/L.**
- **Average = 100–800 mOsm/L.**[^2]

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - parts of loop of Henle.png]]
*Parts of the loop of Henle — descending LOH and ascending LOH — with the distal convoluted tubule returning to touch its glomerulus at the juxtaglomerular apparatus (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Parts of LOH | — |
|---|---|
| **Descending LOH** | Goes down into the medulla |
| **Ascending LOH** | Comes back up to the cortex → **DCT**, which touches the glomerulus at the **juxta glomerular apparatus** |

### COUNTER CURRENT SYSTEM

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - countercurrent multiplier.png]]
*The countercurrent multiplier as drawn in the notes — 300 mOsm/L enters from the PCT; the descending limb loses water through aquaporins (600 → 900 → 1200); the ascending limb pumps Na⁺, K⁺, 2Cl⁻ out (NKCC2, blocked by loop diuretics such as frusemide) and returns to the DCT at 300 mOsm/L; medullary hyperosmolarity at the tip (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| | **Descending LOH** | **Ascending LOH** |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | **Concentrating segment** | **Diluting segment** |
| **Water** | **Permeable to water** (H₂O leaves via **aquaporin**[^ety-aquaporin][^pre-aqua]) | **Impermeable to water** |
| **Solutes** | **Impermeable**[^ety-permeable][^pre-im] **to Na⁺** | **Permeable to solutes** (Na⁺ leaves) |
| **Tubular osmolarity** | 300 → 600 → 900 → **1200** (↑ concentration) | 1200 → 900 → 600 → **300** (↑ dilution) |
| **Key protein** | Aquaporin (AQP-1) | **Na⁺, K⁺, 2Cl⁻ cotransporter**[^ety-cotransporter][^pre-co] (NKCC2) |
| **Role** | **Countercurrent multiplier** | Drives the **single effect** |

- **↑↑ Na⁺** pumped into the interstitium by the ascending limb → **↑ concentration** in the descending limb.
- The tip of the loop = **1200 mOsm/L** → **medullary hyperosmolarity**.
- **Loop diuretic**[^ety-diuretic][^pre-dia] **(Eg: Frusemide**[^ety-frusemide]**)** ✗ the Na⁺, K⁺, 2Cl⁻ cotransporter → **excretion of Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻**.

> [!info] **Note: Single effect → transport of Na⁺ without being accompanied by H₂O.**
> 🧠 At any one level the ascending limb can make only a ~200 mOsm/L difference between itself and the interstitium (the "single effect"). Flow round the hairpin repeats that step over and over, stacking the small differences into a 300 → 1200 gradient — that is what "multiplier" means.[^3]

![Loop of Henle countercurrent multiplier system — descending limb loses water, ascending limb pumps out NaCl, interstitial osmolarity rises toward the papilla (OpenStax)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/2621_Loop_of_Henle_Countercurrent_Multiplier_System.jpg/800px-2621_Loop_of_Henle_Countercurrent_Multiplier_System.jpg)
*The same multiplier in OpenStax colours — water leaves the descending limb, NaCl leaves the impermeable ascending limb, and interstitial osmolarity climbs from 300 to 1200 mOsm/L at the tip (OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology, CC BY 3.0).*

### Medullary hyperosmolarity

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - medullary hyperosmolarity urea from CD.png]]
*Medullary hyperosmolarity — 1200 mOsm/L at the tip of the loop, built by ↑↑↑ Na⁺ from the ascending limb plus urea from the collecting duct; the longer the loop, the higher the osmolarity (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- **Created by: countercurrent multiplier.**
- **Purpose: ADH**[^ety-adh][^pre-anti] **mediated water reabsorption from CD.**
- **Maintained by: countercurrent exchanger (vasa recta).**
- **Length of LOH ∝ medullary hyperosmolarity.**
- **Contributed by:**
  - **Na⁺ (50 %).**
  - **Urea**[^ety-urea] **(50 %): from CD.**
    - **By urea transporters (UT1 and UT2).**[^4]
    - **↑ with high protein diet.**

### Vasa recta[^ety-vasa-recta]

- **Blood vessels along LOH of similar osmolarities.**

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - vasa recta.png]]
*The vasa recta run beside the loop at the same osmolarities (300, 600, 900, 1200) — solute and water swap passively between the descending and ascending vessels, so the blood leaves the medulla without washing the gradient away (cropped from the handwritten page).*

🧠 Blood going down the vasa recta picks up salt and loses water, blood coming up does the reverse; the vessel leaves the medulla only slightly saltier than it entered. A straight capillary would have washed the medulla out — the hairpin is a **passive exchanger**, not a pump.[^5]

### REABSORPTION OF Ca²⁺

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - calcium reabsorption in thick ascending limb.png]]
*Calcium reabsorption in the thick ascending limb — (1) Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ cotransporter, (2) K⁺ recycling back to the lumen makes the lumen positive, (3) the positive charge repels Ca²⁺, which is reabsorbed between the cells; a loop diuretic blocks step 1 → calciuria (cropped from the handwritten page).*

1. **Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ cotransporter.**
2. **K⁺ recycling**[^ety-recycling][^pre-re]**: positive charge in the lumen.**
3. **Repel the + charge; Ca²⁺ gets reabsorbed.**[^6]

```
Loop diuretic ⊖ Na⁺K⁺2Cl⁻ cotransporter → no K⁺ recycling → lumen not positive → Ca²⁺ not reabsorbed → CALCIURIA
```

> [!warning] Loop diuretic → **calciuria**[^ety-calciuria][^suf-uria]
> Blocking NKCC2 removes the lumen-positive voltage, so Ca²⁺ (and Mg²⁺) are lost in the urine — which is why loop diuretics are used to treat **hypercalcaemia**.

### REABSORPTION OF CHLORIDE (Cl⁻)

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - chloride reabsorption and Bartter syndrome.png]]
*Chloride reabsorption in the thick ascending limb — Cl⁻ enters through the Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ cotransporter and leaves on the blood side through the chloride channel (with its subunit barttin); a mutation here causes Bartter syndrome (cropped from the handwritten page).*

| Side | Protein |
|---|---|
| Lumen | **Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ cotransporter** |
| Blood side | **Chloride**[^ety-chloride] **channel (Barttin**[^ety-barttin]**)** → Cl⁻ to the peritubular capillary[^ety-peritubular][^pre-peri] |

**Bartter syndrome**[^ety-bartter][^ety-syndrome][^pre-syn] (**mutation**):

```
Salt & water loss (volume depletion)
        ↓
RAAS activation: ↑ aldosterone
        ↓
↑ K⁺ & H⁺ excretion
```

- **Salt & water loss (volume depletion**[^ety-depletion][^pre-de]**).**
- **RAAS activation: ↑ aldosterone**[^ety-aldosterone].
- **↑ K⁺ & H⁺ excretion.**[^7]

## 🧂 Distal Convoluted Tubule

| **Sodium reabsorption** | **Calcium reabsorption** |
|---|---|
| **5 % of filtered load** | **9 % of filtered load** |

### Sodium reabsorption — 5 % of filtered load

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - DCT sodium reabsorption.png]]
*Sodium reabsorption in the DCT — Na⁺ K⁺ pump on the blood side; on the lumen side the Na⁺–Cl⁻ cotransporter (2° active transport), blocked by thiazide diuretics and mutated in Gitelman's syndrome (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- Blood side: **Na⁺ K⁺ pump.**
- Lumen side: **Na⁺–Cl⁻ cotransporter (2° active transport).**
  - **Mutation → Gitelman's syndrome**[^ety-gitelman]**.**[^9]
  - ⊖ **Thiazide diuretics**[^ety-thiazide].

### Calcium reabsorption — 9 % of filtered load

![[Loop of Henle and Distal Convoluted Tubule - DCT calcium reabsorption.png]]
*Calcium reabsorption in the DCT — Ca²⁺ enters from the lumen through the TRPV5 channel (up-regulated by PTH and vitamin D) and leaves on the blood side through the Na⁺–Ca²⁺ exchanger (cropped from the handwritten page).*

- Blood side: **Na⁺–Ca²⁺ exchanger**[^ety-exchanger][^pre-ex].
- Lumen side: **Transient receptor potential vanilloid channel (TRPV 5)**[^ety-trpv]**:**[^10]
  - **Up regulated by parathormone (PTH)**[^ety-parathormone][^pre-para] **and vitamin D**[^ety-vitamin].

🧠 The two DCT stories are linked: thiazides block NCC → cell Na⁺ falls → the basolateral Na⁺–Ca²⁺ exchanger works harder → more Ca²⁺ is reabsorbed. So **thiazides cause hypocalciuria**, the opposite of loop diuretics.[^11]

### TYPES OF CELLS

#### 1. JG cells

- **Line the afferent**[^ety-afferent][^pre-ad] **arteriole.**
- **AKA granular**[^ety-granular] **cells; contain granules → release renin**[^ety-renin]**.**
- **JG cells sense pressure changes.**

| Conditions that **↑ renin release**[^12] | Conditions that **↓ renin release** |
|---|---|
| **Low pressure / hypotension**[^ety-hypotension][^pre-hypo] **in afferent arteriole** | **↑ Pressure in afferent arteriole** |
| **↓ Na⁺** | **Angiotensin II (−ve feedback)** |
| **Sympathetic stimulation**[^ety-sympathetic] | **ADH** |
| **Diuretics** | — |
| **Hemorrhage**[^ety-hemorrhage][^suf-rrhage] | — |

**Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS): in response to low BP**

```
Angiotensinogen ──(Renin)──► Angiotensin I ──(ACE in lung)──► Angiotensin II ──► Aldosterone from adrenal cortex
   (Liver)                                                          │                        │
                                                                    ▼                        ▼
                                                                  ↑ BP              ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption
```

- **Angiotensinogen**[^ety-angiotensin][^suf-ogen] is made in the **liver**; **renin** cuts it to **angiotensin I**; **ACE**[^ety-ace] in the **lung** converts it to **angiotensin II** → **↑ BP**, and → **aldosterone from adrenal**[^ety-adrenal] **cortex** → **↑ Na⁺ reabsorption**.

![Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system — liver angiotensinogen, kidney renin, lung ACE, angiotensin II acting on vessels, adrenal cortex (aldosterone), pituitary (ADH) and the kidney (Wikimedia Commons)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone_system.png/800px-Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone_system.png)
*The whole RAAS cascade on one figure — the same chain as the page's flow, with the extra targets of angiotensin II (arteriolar constriction, ADH release, Na⁺ reabsorption) (Wikimedia Commons).*

**Overactive RAAS: Hypertension (HTN)**[^ety-hypertension].
- **In treatment of HTN, ACE inhibitors are used to block RAAS.**
- **Direct renin blockers: Aliskiren**[^ety-aliskiren]**.**

#### 2. Macula densa[^ety-macula-densa]

- **GFR / NaCl sensors: chemical sensor.**
- **Function → Tubuloglomerular feedback (TGF):**

```
↑ GFR → ↑ NaCl filtration → sensed by macula densa in distal tubule → releases adenosine
                                                                          ↓
                                                     constriction of afferent arteriole → ↓ GFR
```

The messenger is **adenosine**[^ety-adenosine].

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> The loop of Henle separates **salt from water**: the thick ascending limb pumps NaCl out through NKCC2 while staying water-tight (the single effect), and the descending limb lets water leave into the salty interstitium. Countercurrent flow multiplies this ~200 mOsm/L step into a 300 → 1200 mOsm/L medullary gradient (half NaCl, half urea), the vasa recta keep it from being washed away, and ADH later uses it to pull water out of the collecting duct. The longer the loop, the higher the gradient — so juxtamedullary nephrons make concentrated urine.
>
> Every transporter is a drug target and a disease: **NKCC2** (loop diuretics, Bartter — calciuria), **NCC** (thiazides, Gitelman — hypocalciuria), **TRPV5** (PTH, vitamin D). Where the DCT meets its own glomerulus, the **JGA** turns pressure (JG cells → renin → RAAS) and NaCl delivery (macula densa → adenosine → TGF) into control of BP and GFR.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Isotonic 300 mOsm/L fluid enters the loop; the medulla reaches 1200 mOsm/L at the papilla; fluid leaves the ascending limb hypotonic (~100 mOsm/L) and the DCT dilutes it further while reabsorbing 5 % of Na⁺ and 9 % of Ca²⁺. Renin is released only when afferent pressure or distal NaCl falls.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> When NKCC2 or NCC is blocked (diuretics) or mutated (Bartter, Gitelman), salt is lost; volume contraction activates RAAS, and aldosterone rescues Na⁺ in the collecting duct at the price of K⁺ and H⁺ — BP stays normal but hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis appears. Hypotension, haemorrhage and sympathetic drive raise renin to defend BP.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> Loss of the medullary gradient (loop diuretics, medullary washout, short loops, osmotic diuresis) means the kidney can neither concentrate nor dilute → isosthenuria and polyuria. Bartter presents in infancy with polyhydramnios, salt wasting, hypercalciuria and nephrocalcinosis; Gitelman later with hypomagnesaemia, hypocalciuria and cramps. Overactive RAAS (renal artery stenosis, renin-secreting tumour) produces hypertension with hypokalaemia.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal → transporter block / mutation / ↓ perfusion → salt and water loss or ↓ afferent pressure → renin ↑ → angiotensin II and aldosterone compensation → K⁺ and H⁺ wasting → hypokalaemic alkalosis, polyuria, or RAAS-driven hypertension
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Choosing a diuretic is choosing a segment: loop diuretics for oedema and hypercalcaemia (most powerful, calciuric), thiazides for hypertension and calcium stones (hypocalciuric). ACE inhibitors, ARBs and aliskiren interrupt RAAS at different steps; knowing the renin stimuli explains why diuretics and haemorrhage raise renin.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Bartter syndrome:** "Neonate / polyhydramnios", "hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis with normal BP", "hypercalciuria, nephrocalcinosis", "looks like a patient on frusemide" → NKCC2 / ROMK / ClC-Kb / barttin defect in the thick ascending limb.
>
> **Gitelman syndrome:** "Adolescent with cramps, tetany", "hypomagnesaemia, hypocalciuria", "looks like a patient on thiazide" → NCC (SLC12A3) defect in the DCT.
>
> **Loop diuretic effect:** "Frusemide", "hypercalcaemia treatment", "ototoxicity", "calciuria" → NKCC2 block abolishes K⁺ recycling and the lumen-positive drive for Ca²⁺.
>
> **Thiazide effect:** "Hypocalciuria", "used for calcium stones", "hyponatraemia in elderly" → NCC block raises DCT Ca²⁺ reabsorption.
>
> **High-renin states:** "Hypotension, haemorrhage, diuretic use, sympathetic stimulation, low Na⁺" → JG cells release renin.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> A stork in cold water = countercurrent exchange; a hairpin diagram with 300 / 600 / 900 / 1200 = countercurrent multiplier; three numbered cells beside an afferent arteriole and a distal tubule = JGA (macula densa, JG cells, lacis cells).
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Urine osmolarity range 50–1400 mOsm/L; medullary tip 1200 mOsm/L (50 % Na⁺, 50 % urea); juxtamedullary nephrons 15 %, cortical 85 %; DCT reabsorbs 5 % of Na⁺ and 9 % of Ca²⁺.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Swapping the permeabilities of the two limbs**
>
> **What students think:** The ascending limb lets water out, the descending limb pumps salt.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The **descending** limb is permeable to water and impermeable to Na⁺ (concentrating segment); the **ascending** limb is permeable to solutes and impermeable to water (diluting segment). Fluid leaving the ascending limb is hypotonic — the only place in the nephron where fluid becomes dilute regardless of ADH.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**D**escending **D**rinks out water; **A**scending **A**xes the salt."
>
> **Trap 2: Calling the vasa recta the multiplier**
>
> **What students think:** The vasa recta create the medullary gradient.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The loop of Henle (NKCC2 + countercurrent flow) **creates** the gradient — the countercurrent **multiplier**. The vasa recta only **maintain** it as a passive countercurrent **exchanger**; they have no pumps.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Multiplier = loop = creates; exchanger = vasa recta = maintains.
>
> **Trap 3: Loop and thiazide diuretics both cause calciuria**
>
> **What students think:** All diuretics wash Ca²⁺ out.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** Loop diuretics abolish the lumen-positive voltage of the thick ascending limb → **calciuria** (used in hypercalcaemia). Thiazides lower DCT cell Na⁺, which speeds the basolateral Na⁺–Ca²⁺ exchanger → **hypocalciuria** (used for calcium stones). Bartter (= loop) and Gitelman (= thiazide) copy these urine-calcium patterns.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**L**oops **L**ose calcium; thiazides **T**rap calcium."
>
> **Trap 4: Forgetting urea's half of the gradient**
>
> **What students think:** Medullary hyperosmolarity is all NaCl.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** About half the inner-medullary osmolarity is **urea**, recycled from the collecting duct by urea transporters under ADH. A low-protein diet lowers urea and impairs concentrating ability; a high-protein diet improves it.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "50 : 50 — Na⁺ from the loop, urea from the CD."
>
> **Trap 5: Mixing up the JGA cells**
>
> **What students think:** The macula densa releases renin.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **JG (granular) cells** in the afferent arteriole release renin and sense **pressure** (baroreceptor); the **macula densa** in the DCT senses **NaCl** (chemoreceptor) and drives TGF via adenosine (and signals the JG cells); **lacis cells** are supporting cells.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** JG = **G**ranules = renin = pressure; macula densa = **D**ensa senses **D**istal NaCl.

>

> **Related Topic 1: [[Collecting Duct and Micturition Reflex]]**

>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Glomerular Filtration Rate]]**
> Tubuloglomerular feedback (macula densa → adenosine → afferent constriction) is one of the two arms of GFR autoregulation; angiotensin II squeezes the efferent arteriole.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[Proximal Convoluted Tubule]]**
> The isotonic 300 mOsm/L fluid that enters the loop is made there; together, PCT and loop reabsorb ~90 % of filtered Na⁺.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Regulation of Blood Pressure]]**
> RAAS is the long-term hormonal arm of BP control; ACE inhibitors, ARBs and aliskiren are antihypertensives because they cut it.

>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Regulation of Body Temperature]]**

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. Countercurrent system = **2 tubes close together + opposite flow**; seen in **kidney (LOH), testis, intestines, skin**.
> 2. LOH receives **isotonic 300 mOsm/L** fluid; function = **concentration and dilution**; urine **50–1400 mOsm/L** (average 100–800).
> 3. **Descending LOH = concentrating segment**: permeable to water, impermeable to Na⁺, **countercurrent multiplier**.
> 4. **Ascending LOH = diluting segment**: permeable to solutes, impermeable to water; **Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ cotransporter** ⊖ **loop diuretics (frusemide)**.
> 5. **Single effect** = Na⁺ transport without H₂O.
> 6. Medullary hyperosmolarity: **created by multiplier**, **maintained by vasa recta (exchanger)**, **purpose = ADH water reabsorption**; **∝ length of LOH**; **50 % Na⁺ + 50 % urea (UT, ↑ high-protein diet)**.
> 7. TAL Ca²⁺: NKCC2 → **K⁺ recycling → lumen positive → Ca²⁺ repelled into blood**; loop diuretic → **calciuria**.
> 8. **Bartter syndrome** (Cl⁻ channel / barttin, NKCC2 mutation): salt & water loss → RAAS → ↑ aldosterone → **↑ K⁺ & H⁺ excretion**.
> 9. **Juxtamedullary nephrons 15 %** (long loop, concentrate urine); **cortical 85 %** (short loop).
> 10. DCT: **Na⁺ 5 %** via **Na⁺–Cl⁻ cotransporter** ⊖ **thiazides**, mutation → **Gitelman**; **Ca²⁺ 9 %** via **TRPV5** ↑ by **PTH and vitamin D**, Na⁺–Ca²⁺ exchanger basolaterally.
> 11. JG cells: **granular, line the afferent arteriole, release renin, sense pressure**. **↑ renin**: hypotension, ↓ Na⁺, sympathetic, diuretics, haemorrhage; **↓ renin**: ↑ pressure, angiotensin II, ADH.
> 12. RAAS: **angiotensinogen (liver) → renin → Ang I → ACE (lung) → Ang II → ↑ BP + aldosterone (adrenal cortex) → ↑ Na⁺**; overactive → **HTN**; **ACE inhibitors**, **aliskiren (direct renin blocker)**.
> 13. **Macula densa = NaCl chemical sensor → TGF (adenosine → afferent constriction)**; **lacis / extraglomerular mesangial / Polkissen cells = supporting cells**.

[^1]: In the testis the pampiniform venous plexus wraps the testicular artery: arterial blood is cooled by the returning venous blood, keeping the testis 2–3 °C below core temperature for spermatogenesis. In the intestinal villi, the countercurrent arrangement of arteriole and venule shunts O₂ away from the villus tip.
[^2]: The page writes "100–800m Osm/L"; read as **100–800 mOsm/L**. Guyton: urine osmolarity can range from ~50 mOsm/L (maximal dilution, no ADH) to ~1200–1400 mOsm/L (maximal concentration); typical daily urine is 500–800 mOsm/L.
[^3]: Guyton's step-by-step model: the thick ascending limb can hold a gradient of ~200 mOsm/L between its lumen and the interstitium at any level ("single effect"); repeated with flow round the hairpin, this builds the medullary gradient to 1200–1400 mOsm/L. The NKCC2 transporter carries 1 Na⁺, 1 K⁺, 2 Cl⁻ (electroneutral).
[^4]: Urea transporters: UT-A1 and UT-A3 in the inner-medullary collecting duct (ADH-regulated — urea leaves the CD into the interstitium), UT-A2 in the thin descending limb (urea re-enters the loop — "urea recycling"), UT-B in the descending vasa recta and red cells. The page's "UT1 and UT2" refers to these families.
[^5]: Guyton: medullary blood flow is only 1–2 % of total renal blood flow and is sluggish, which also limits washout. Medullary washout (e.g. high flow in osmotic diuresis, loop diuretics) reduces the gradient and concentrating ability.
[^6]: K⁺ re-enters the lumen through ROMK channels; the lumen becomes ~+8 mV, which drives Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, Na⁺ and K⁺ between the cells (paracellular route, through claudin-16 / claudin-19 tight junctions). About 20–25 % of filtered Ca²⁺ and ~60 % of filtered Mg²⁺ are reabsorbed this way in the thick ascending limb.
[^7]: Bartter syndrome (autosomal recessive) types: I — NKCC2 (*SLC12A1*); II — ROMK (*KCNJ1*); III — ClC-Kb (*CLCNKB*); IV — barttin (*BSND*, with deafness); V — gain-of-function CaSR. All mimic chronic loop-diuretic use: hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis, high renin and aldosterone with normal BP, hypercalciuria (nephrocalcinosis), raised PGE2.
[^8]: Guyton: about 20–30 % of nephrons are juxtamedullary in the human kidney (the page gives 15 %, the commonly quoted Indian-text value). Their efferent arterioles give rise to the vasa recta.
[^9]: Gitelman syndrome (autosomal recessive, *SLC12A3* encoding NCC) mimics chronic thiazide use: hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis, **hypomagnesaemia**, **hypocalciuria**, normal BP; usually presents in adolescence or adulthood with cramps, tetany and fatigue — milder than Bartter.
[^10]: The page writes "vanilliod"; the channel is the transient receptor potential **vanilloid** 5 (TRPV5 — "vanilloid" from vanilla/capsaicin-sensitive TRPV1, the family's first member). The page also writes "parathornme" for **parathormone** (PTH). Inside the cell Ca²⁺ is buffered by calbindin-D28k; it leaves via the Na⁺–Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX1) and a Ca²⁺-ATPase (PMCA).
[^11]: Thiazides also cause a mild volume contraction that increases proximal Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ reabsorption. Hence thiazides are used in idiopathic hypercalciuria (calcium stones) and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, and can unmask hyperparathyroidism.
[^12]: The three classic stimuli of renin release (Guyton/Ganong): (1) ↓ pressure in the afferent arteriole (intrarenal baroreceptor in JG cells), (2) ↓ NaCl delivery to the macula densa, (3) sympathetic stimulation through β1 receptors. Diuretics and haemorrhage act through these. Inhibitors: angiotensin II (short-loop negative feedback), ↑ afferent pressure, ADH, ANP, high NaCl at the macula densa, β-blockers.
[^13]: Lacis cells (extraglomerular mesangial cells) fill the triangle between the afferent arteriole, the efferent arteriole and the macula densa; they are gap-junction–coupled to the JG cells and intraglomerular mesangial cells and are thought to relay the macula densa signal. Some texts note they can also make renin.
[^ety-henle]: **loop of Henle** — Friedrich Gustav Jakob Henle (1809–1885), German anatomist and pathologist (Göttingen), who described the loop in 1862; also of Henle's layer of the hair follicle and the Henle–Koch postulates (he taught Robert Koch).
[^ety-countercurrent]: **countercurrent** — Latin *contra*, "against" (→ *counter-*) + *currere*, "to run" → running against; two streams flowing in opposite directions side by side. **Differentiate:** *countercurrent* (opposite directions — efficient exchange, gradient multiplied) vs *concurrent / co-current* (same direction — exchange stops at equilibrium halfway).
[^ety-multiplier]: **multiplier / exchanger** — Latin *multus*, "many" + *plicare*, "to fold" → *multiplicare*, to make manifold: the loop **multiplies** a small single effect into a large gradient using energy (NKCC2). *Exchanger* (Latin *ex-* + *cambiare*, to barter) → the vasa recta only **swap** solute and water passively. **Differentiate:** multiplier = creates (active) vs exchanger = maintains (passive).
[^ety-osmolarity]: **osmolarity / hyperosmolarity** — Greek *ōsmos*, "push, thrust" + Latin *-molar* (from *moles*, a mass) + *-ity* → the number of osmotically active particles per litre (mOsm/L); *hyper-* = above normal (> 300). **Differentiate:** *osmolarity* (per litre of solution) vs *osmolality* (per kg of water) vs *tonicity* (only the particles that cannot cross the membrane).
[^ety-distal]: **distal** — Latin *distare*, "to stand apart" (*dis-* + *stare*) + *-al* → farther from the origin. The DCT is farther from the glomerulus along the tubule, yet it returns to touch it at the JGA.
[^ety-juxtaglomerular]: **juxtaglomerular** — Latin *juxta*, "beside, close to" + *glomerulus*, "little ball" + *-ar* → beside the glomerulus. The JGA (Goormaghtigh, 1930s) is where the distal tubule touches the vascular pole of its own glomerulus.
[^ety-testis]: **testis** — Latin *testis*, "a witness" (the organ "witnessing" virility — a disputed folk etymology; origin uncertain); plural *testes*. Its pampiniform plexus (Latin *pampinus*, tendril) is a countercurrent heat exchanger.
[^ety-intestine]: **intestine** — Latin *intestinus*, "internal" (*intus*, within) → the inner parts, the bowel. In its villi, arterioles and venules run countercurrently.
[^ety-isotonic]: **isotonic** — Greek *isos*, "equal" + *tonos*, "tension" + *-ic* → equal osmotic pull to plasma (~300 mOsm/L). **Differentiate:** *isotonic* (equal) · *hypotonic* (less — ascending limb fluid) · *hypertonic* (more — medullary interstitium).
[^ety-concentration]: **concentration** — Latin *con-*, "together" + *centrum*, "centre" + *-ation* → bringing to a centre; packing more solute per volume (removing water). **Differentiate:** *concentration* (water removed → urine hypertonic) vs *dilution* (solute removed → urine hypotonic).
[^ety-dilution]: **dilution** — Latin *dis-* (→ *di-*), "apart, away" + *luere*, "to wash" + *-tion* → washing away; making a fluid weaker by removing solute (ascending limb) or adding water. Same root as *ablution*.
[^ety-aquaporin]: **aquaporin** — Latin *aqua*, "water" + Greek *poros*, "pore" + *-in* (protein) → water-pore protein (Peter Agre, Nobel 2003). AQP-1 (descending limb, PCT — always open) vs AQP-2 (collecting duct — ADH-regulated).
[^ety-permeable]: **permeable / impermeable** — Latin *per-*, "through" + *meare*, "to pass" + *-able* → able to be passed through; *im-* (*in-* before *p*), "not" → cannot be passed through. **Differentiate:** *permeable* (lets a substance through) vs *semipermeable* (water but not solute).
[^ety-cotransporter]: **cotransporter (symporter)** — Latin *co-*, "together" + *trans-*, "across" + *portare*, "to carry" → carries solutes across together in one direction: Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻ (NKCC2) in the TAL, Na⁺–Cl⁻ (NCC) in the DCT. **Differentiate:** *cotransporter* (same direction) vs *exchanger / antiporter* (opposite directions — Na⁺–Ca²⁺).
[^ety-diuretic]: **diuretic** — Greek *dia-*, "through" + *ourein*, "to urinate" + *-tic* → promoting the passing of urine. **Differentiate:** *diuretic* (↑ urine volume, usually via Na⁺ loss — natriuretic) · *aquaretic* (↑ free water only — vaptans) · *antidiuretic* (↓ urine — ADH).
[^ety-frusemide]: **frusemide (furosemide)** — a coined drug name from *furfuryl* (Latin *furfur*, "bran" — furfural was distilled from bran) + *sulfamoyl* + *-semide*; the most used loop diuretic, blocking NKCC2 (Na⁺ K⁺ 2Cl⁻). "Frusemide" is the older British spelling, "furosemide" the international name.
[^ety-adh]: **ADH (antidiuretic hormone)** — Greek *anti-*, "against" + *dia-*, "through" + *ourein*, "to urinate" → against urine flow; also *vasopressin* (Latin *vas*, vessel + *premere*, to press). It uses the medullary gradient to pull water from the collecting duct.
[^ety-urea]: **urea** — Greek *ouron*, "urine" → the nitrogenous waste of protein breakdown (synthesised by Wöhler, 1828); half of inner-medullary osmolarity. Do not confuse with *uric acid* (purine waste) or *uraemia* (renal failure syndrome).
[^ety-vasa-recta]: **vasa recta** — Latin *vas* (plural *vasa*), "vessel" + *rectus*, "straight" → straight vessels; long hairpin capillaries from juxtamedullary efferent arterioles that run beside the loops. Same *rectus* as rectum, rectus abdominis.
[^ety-recycling]: **recycling** — Latin *re-*, "again" + Greek *kyklos*, "circle, wheel" + *-ing* → going round again; K⁺ that came in on NKCC2 leaks back into the lumen through ROMK so the cotransporter never runs out of K⁺ — and the lumen turns positive.
[^ety-calciuria]: **calciuria / hypercalciuria** — Latin *calx, calcis*, "lime, limestone" → *calcium* (Davy, 1808) + Greek *ouron*, "urine" + *-ia* → calcium in the urine; *hyper-* = excess. **Differentiate:** *hypercalciuria* (urine — loop diuretics, Bartter) vs *hypercalcaemia* (blood) vs *hypocalciuria* (thiazides, Gitelman).
[^ety-chloride]: **chloride** — Greek *chlōros*, "pale green, yellow-green" (the colour of chlorine gas; named by Davy, 1810) + *-ide*, a binary compound/anion → the anion of chlorine, Cl⁻. Same root as *chlorophyll*, *chlorosis*.
[^ety-barttin]: **barttin** — named after Frederic Bartter + *-in* (protein) → the β-subunit of the kidney chloride channels ClC-Ka/ClC-Kb, also expressed in the inner ear; its mutation (*BSND*) causes Bartter syndrome type IV with sensorineural deafness.
[^ety-peritubular]: **peritubular** — Greek *peri-*, "around" + Latin *tubulus*, "small tube" + *-ar* → around the tubules; the capillaries into which reabsorbed Cl⁻ and Ca²⁺ return.
[^ety-bartter]: **Bartter syndrome** — Frederic Crockett Bartter (1914–1983), American endocrinologist at the NIH, who described it in 1962 (hypokalaemic alkalosis with juxtaglomerular hyperplasia and high aldosterone but normal BP).
[^ety-syndrome]: **syndrome** — Greek *syn-*, "together" + *dromos*, "a running" → a running together of signs and symptoms. **Differentiate:** *syndrome* (a cluster) vs *disease* (defined cause and pathology).
[^ety-depletion]: **depletion** — Latin *de-*, "reversal, away" + *plere*, "to fill" + *-tion* → un-filling, emptying; *volume depletion* = loss of extracellular fluid (salt and water together). **Differentiate:** *volume depletion* (salt + water, ECF shrinks) vs *dehydration* (strictly, pure water loss → hypernatraemia).
[^ety-aldosterone]: **aldosterone** — *aldehyde* (from Latin *al(cohol) dehyd(rogenatum)*) + *sterone* (a steroid ketone) → the steroid with an aldehyde group at C-18 (isolated by Simpson and Tait, 1953). The main mineralocorticoid: ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption, ↑ K⁺ and H⁺ excretion in the collecting duct.
[^ety-juxtamedullary]: **juxtamedullary** — Latin *juxta*, "beside" + *medulla*, "marrow, pith" + *-ary* → beside the medulla; nephrons whose glomeruli sit at the corticomedullary junction and whose long loops reach the papilla.
[^ety-cortex]: **cortex / cortical** — Latin *cortex*, "bark, rind" + *-al* → of the outer layer; cortical nephrons have glomeruli in the outer cortex and short loops reaching only the outer medulla. Opposite *medulla* (marrow, inner part).
[^ety-gitelman]: **Gitelman syndrome** — Hillel J. Gitelman (1932–2015), American nephrologist (University of North Carolina), who described the familial hypokalaemia–hypomagnesaemia syndrome in 1966.
[^ety-thiazide]: **thiazide** — a chemical name: *thi-* (Greek *theion*, "sulphur") + *azide* (nitrogen ring, from French *azote*, "lifeless" — nitrogen) → a sulphur–nitrogen ring compound (benzothiadiazine); chlorothiazide (1957) was the first. Block NCC in the DCT.
[^ety-exchanger]: **exchanger (antiporter)** — Old French *eschangier* (Latin *ex-*, "out" + *cambiare*, "to barter") → swaps ions in opposite directions: 3 Na⁺ in for 1 Ca²⁺ out (NCX1) on the DCT basolateral membrane. **Differentiate:** *exchanger* (opposite directions) vs *cotransporter* (same direction).
[^ety-trpv]: **TRPV5 (transient receptor potential vanilloid 5)** — *transient receptor potential* from the *trp* mutant fruit fly whose photoreceptors gave only a transient response to light; *vanilloid* (vanilla + Greek *-oid*, "like") from the capsaicin/vanilloid-sensitive first member TRPV1 → a family of cation channels; TRPV5 is the gatekeeper of DCT Ca²⁺ entry.
[^ety-parathormone]: **parathormone (PTH)** — Greek *para-*, "beside" + *thyreos*, "shield" (→ thyroid) + *hormaein*, "to set in motion" → the hormone of the glands beside the thyroid (parathyroids). It raises blood Ca²⁺ partly by up-regulating DCT TRPV5.
[^ety-vitamin]: **vitamin D** — Latin *vita*, "life" + *amine* → *vitamine* ("vital amine", Casimir Funk, 1912; the *e* was dropped when not all proved to be amines); D = the fourth one named (McCollum, 1922). Calcitriol up-regulates TRPV5 and calbindin.
[^ety-afferent]: **afferent** — Latin *ad-* (→ *af-*), "towards" + *ferre*, "to carry" → carrying towards (the glomerulus); the arteriole where JG cells sit. Opposite *efferent* (*ex-* → *ef-*, away).
[^ety-granular]: **granular** — Latin *granulum*, "small grain" (*granum*, grain + *-ulum*, little) + *-ar* → containing small grains; the renin-storage granules of JG cells. **Differentiate:** JG cells are modified smooth muscle (myoepithelioid) cells, not epithelial cells.
[^ety-renin]: **renin** — Latin *ren, renis*, "kidney" + *-in* → the kidney substance (Tigerstedt and Bergman, 1898, from rabbit kidney extracts). **Differentiate:** *renin* (kidney enzyme, starts RAAS) vs *rennin / chymosin* (stomach enzyme that curdles milk — Old English *rennet*).
[^ety-hypotension]: **hypotension** — Greek *hypo-*, "under" + Latin *tensio*, "stretching" (*tendere*) → under-stretching, low pressure. Opposite *hypertension*.
[^ety-sympathetic]: **sympathetic** — Greek *syn-* (*sym-* before *p*), "together" + *pathos*, "feeling" → feeling together; sympathetic fibres release renin through β1 receptors on JG cells.
[^ety-hemorrhage]: **haemorrhage** — Greek *haima*, "blood" + *rhēgnynai*, "to burst forth" → blood bursting out; the fall in BP and volume is a strong renin stimulus.
[^ety-angiotensin]: **angiotensin / angiotensinogen** — Greek *angeion*, "vessel" + Latin *tensio*, "tension" + *-in* → vessel-tensing protein; *-ogen* (Greek *-genēs*, "producing") → angiotensinogen, the precursor made in the liver. **Differentiate:** angiotensinogen (precursor) → angiotensin I (inactive decapeptide) → angiotensin II (active octapeptide).
[^ety-ace]: **ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme)** — Latin *convertere*, "to turn round" (*con-* + *vertere*, to turn) → the enzyme that turns angiotensin I into angiotensin II; most abundant on pulmonary capillary endothelium. It also breaks down bradykinin — the cause of ACE-inhibitor cough. *Enzyme* = Greek *en*, "in" + *zymē*, "leaven".
[^ety-adrenal]: **adrenal** — Latin *ad-*, "near, at" + *ren*, "kidney" + *-al* → the gland near (on top of) the kidney; Greek equivalent *epinephric* (*epi-* + *nephros*), hence epinephrine. Its cortex (zona glomerulosa) makes aldosterone.
[^ety-hypertension]: **hypertension** — Greek *hyper-*, "over, excessive" + Latin *tensio*, "stretching" → excessive arterial pressure; an overactive RAAS is one cause (renovascular hypertension, renin-secreting tumour).
[^ety-aliskiren]: **aliskiren** — a coined drug name with the stem **-kiren** (for renin inhibitors; *ki-* from "kinase/inhibitor" and *-ren* from renin); the first oral direct renin inhibitor (approved 2007). It lowers plasma renin *activity* while renin concentration rises.
[^ety-macula-densa]: **macula densa** — Latin *macula*, "spot" + *densa*, "thick, crowded" → the dense spot: tall crowded cells in the DCT wall at the vascular pole that sense luminal NaCl (via NKCC2). Same *macula* as the retinal macula and skin macule.
[^ety-adenosine]: **adenosine** — Greek *adēn*, "gland" (adenine was first isolated from pancreas) + *-osine*, from ribose → adenine + ribose. In TGF it **constricts** the afferent arteriole (A1 receptors) — unlike most vascular beds where it dilates.
[^ety-mesangial]: **mesangial** — Greek *mesos*, "middle" + *angeion*, "vessel" + *-al* → in the middle of the vessels; *extraglomerular* mesangial cells lie outside the tuft, between the arterioles.
[^ety-lacis]: **lacis cells** — French *lacis*, "network, lacework" (Old French *lacier*, to lace) → cells whose interlacing processes form a lace-like network between the arterioles (Zimmermann). Synonyms: extraglomerular mesangial cells, Goormaghtigh cells, Polkissen.
[^ety-polkissen]: **Polkissen** — German *Pol*, "pole" + *Kissen*, "cushion, pillow" → the "polar cushion" at the vascular pole of the glomerulus (Zimmermann, 1933); used for the extraglomerular mesangial / JG cell cushion.
[^pre-counter]: **counter- / contra-** — against, opposite: countercurrent, counter-transport, contralateral. Don't confuse with **con- / co-** together (cotransporter, concentration) · **anti-** against (Greek equivalent: antiporter).
[^pre-multi]: **multi-** — many: multiplier, multiple, multicellular. Don't confuse with **poly-** many (Greek equivalent: polyuria) · **mono- / uni-** one.
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, above, excessive: hyperosmolarity, hypertension, hypercalciuria. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under (hypotension) · **supra-** above (Latin).
[^pre-juxta]: **juxta-** — beside, close to (Latin): juxtaglomerular, juxtamedullary, juxta-articular. Don't confuse with **para-** beside (Greek equivalent: parathyroid) · **peri-** around · **ad-** towards.
[^pre-intra]: **intra- / intro- / in-** — within, inside: intestine (*intus*, within), intracellular, intraglomerular mesangial cells. Don't confuse with **inter-** between (interstitium) · **extra-** outside (extraglomerular).
[^pre-iso]: **iso-** — equal: isotonic, iso-osmotic, isosthenuria. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **hyper-** over · **homo-** same.
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with: concentration, cotransporter, convoluted. Don't confuse with **contra- / counter-** against (countercurrent).
[^pre-dis]: **dis- / di-** — apart, away: dilution (washing away), distal (standing apart), diffusion. Don't confuse with **di-** two (Greek: dichloride) · **dia-** through (diuretic) · **dys-** bad.
[^pre-aqua]: **aqua-** — water (Latin): aquaporin, aquaretic, aqueous. Don't confuse with **hydro-** water (the Greek equivalent).
[^pre-im]: **im- / in- (il-, ir-)** — not (Latin negative): impermeable, insoluble, inactive. Don't confuse with **in- / im-** into (infusion, implant — same spelling, different prefix) · **un-** not (English).
[^pre-co]: **co-** — together (form of *con-*): cotransporter, coenzyme. Don't confuse with **counter-** against (counter-transport = exchanger).
[^pre-dia]: **dia-** — through, across: diuretic, dialysis, diaphragm. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis- / di-** apart (dilution) · **dys-** bad.
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: antidiuretic, antiporter, antihypertensive. Don't confuse with **ante-** before · **ana-** up, again.
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: recycling, reabsorption, release. Don't confuse with **ren-** kidney (renin, renal) · **retro-** backward (retrograde).
[^suf-uria]: **-uria** — a condition of the urine: calciuria, glycosuria, polyuria, hypocalciuria. Don't confuse with **-aemia** a condition of the blood (hypercalcaemia) · **-uresis** the passing of urine (diuresis, natriuresis).
[^pre-peri]: **peri-** — around: peritubular, pericardium. Don't confuse with **para-** beside · **per-** through (permeable).
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together: syndrome, sympathetic, symporter. Don't confuse with **semi-** half · **sin-** left.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — down, away, reversal: depletion, dehydration, depolarisation. Don't confuse with **di-** two · **dis-** apart · **dys-** bad.
[^pre-ex]: **ex- (ef-, e-)** — out of: exchanger, excretion, efferent. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek) · **extra-** beyond (extraglomerular).
[^pre-para]: **para-** — beside, near: parathormone (beside the thyroid), paracellular, paracrine. Don't confuse with **peri-** around · **juxta-** beside (Latin equivalent) · **pre-** before.
[^pre-ad]: **ad- (af-, ac-, ag- …)** — to, towards: afferent, adrenal (near the kidney), adhesion. Don't confuse with **ab-** away from · **a- / an-** without.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypotension, hypocalciuria, hypotonic. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep · **sub-** under (Latin).
[^suf-rrhage]: **-rrhage / -rrhagia** — bursting forth, heavy bleeding: haemorrhage, menorrhagia. Don't confuse with **-rrhoea** flow, discharge (diarrhoea) · **-rrhaphy** suturing · **-rrhexis** rupture.
[^suf-ogen]: **-ogen / -gen** — a precursor, that which produces: angiotensinogen, pepsinogen, fibrinogen, glycogen. Don't confuse with **-genic** producing or produced by (nephrogenic) · **-genesis** formation (spermatogenesis).
[^pre-extra]: **extra-** — outside, beyond: extraglomerular mesangial cells, extracellular. Don't confuse with **intra-** within (intraglomerular) · **ex-** out of · **exo-** outside (Greek).
[^pre-meso]: **meso- / mes-** — middle: mesangial, mesoderm, mesentery. Don't confuse with **meta-** after, change · **medi-** middle (Latin: median).
