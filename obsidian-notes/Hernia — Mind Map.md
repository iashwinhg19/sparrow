# Hernia

## Types of hernia
- Simple / uncomplicated
  - Reducible
  - Cough impulse ⊕
  - Taxis possible
- Obstructed
  - Irreducible
  - Cough impulse ⊖
  - Taxis contraindicated
- Strangulated
  - Obstructed + blood supply compromised
  - Skin inflamed
  - Cough impulse ⊖
  - Taxis contraindicated
- Why no forceful taxis
  - Reduction en masse
  - Contents go back with the constriction ring
  - Obstruction persists inside the abdomen

## Contents of the sac
- Omentocele
  - No peristalsis
  - Doughy
  - First part reduces easily
  - Dull on percussion
- Enterocele
  - Peristalsis present
  - First part reduces with difficulty
  - Tympanic on percussion

## Operations
- Herniotomy
  - Sac identified, opened, contents reduced
  - Defect not repaired
  - Highest recurrence
  - Treatment of choice in
    - Congenital inguinal hernia
    - Inguinal hernia in children
    - Congenital hydrocele
- Herniorrhaphy
  - Defect edges sutured
  - No mesh
  - Used in obstructed and strangulated hernia
- Hernioplasty
  - Defect closed with mesh
  - Least recurrence
  - All other hernias

## Mesh
- Ideal mesh
  - Low weight → less shrinkage
  - Thin fibres
  - Large pores
- Placement
  - ≥ 2 cm overlap all around the defect
- Synthetic
  - Avoid in infection and strangulation
  - Prolene
  - Vypro
  - PTFE (the only one for intraperitoneal use)
  - Prolene and Vypro cause bowel adhesions if intraperitoneal
- Biological
  - Can be used in infection
  - Acellular human dermis (AlloDerm)
  - Acellular porcine dermis

## Inguinal hernia
- Most common
  - Hernia overall
  - Inguinal subtype → indirect
  - Hernia in females → indirect inguinal
  - Femoral hernia is F ≫ M but still rarer than inguinal in women
- Clinical tests
  - Deep ring occlusion test → single best test
  - Zieman's three-finger test → low sensitivity
  - Ring invagination test → low sensitivity
- Types of indirect hernia
  - Bubonocele → sac within the canal
  - Funicular → sac reaches the top of the testis
  - Inguinoscrotal (complete) → sac reaches the bottom of the scrotum

## Anatomy
- Hesselbach's triangle
  - Lateral → inferior epigastric vessels
  - Medial → lateral border of rectus
  - Inferior → inguinal ligament
  - Direct hernia → through the triangle
  - Indirect hernia → lateral to the triangle
- Rings
  - Deep ring → opening in fascia transversalis
  - Superficial ring → opening in external oblique aponeurosis
- Myopectineal orifice of Fruchaud
  - Superior → arching fibres of internal oblique
  - Inferior → Cooper's (pectineal) ligament
  - Lateral → iliopsoas
  - Medial → lateral border of rectus
  - One mesh here covers inguinal, femoral and obturator hernias

## Hernioplasty
- Open
  - Lichtenstein tension-free mesh repair
- Complications of open repair
  - Haemorrhage
  - Injury to vas / cord
  - Ilioinguinal nerve injured at the superficial ring (most common nerve)
  - Iliohypogastric nerve trapped under mesh → chronic groin pain
  - Recurrence
  - Wound infection
- Laparoscopic
  - TEP
    - Totally extraperitoneal
    - Peritoneum intact
    - Harder, better repair
  - TAPP
    - Transabdominal preperitoneal
    - Peritoneum opened, mesh placed, peritoneum closed

## Laparoscopic danger zones
- Triangle of doom
  - Medial → vas deferens
  - Lateral → testicular vessels
  - Inferior → peritoneal reflection
  - Contains external iliac artery and vein, genital branch of genitofemoral nerve
  - Tacker here → torrential bleeding
- Triangle of pain
  - Medial → testicular vessels
  - Lateral → peritoneal reflection
  - Superior → iliopubic tract
  - Contains lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh, femoral nerve, femoral branch of genitofemoral nerve
  - Tacker here → meralgia paraesthetica (lateral cutaneous nerve of thigh, most common)
- Corona mortis
  - Abnormal link between obturator and external iliac vessels
  - Injury → torrential bleeding

## Special inguinal hernias
- Sliding hernia
  - Viscus forms the posterior wall of the sac
  - Sigmoid colon most common → left ≫ right
  - Elderly men
  - Viscus at risk during sac dissection
- Sportsman's hernia
  - Gilmore's groin
  - Athletes
  - Tear of posterior wall muscles
  - Severe pain, no palpable sac
  - Investigation of choice → MRI
  - Treatment → laparoscopic repair

## Classification
- European Hernia Society
  - Defect measured in finger breadths
  - Primary vs recurrent
  - Lateral (indirect), medial (direct), femoral
  - Ventral hernia midline → M1 subxiphoid, M2 epigastric, M3 umbilical, M4 infraumbilical, M5 suprapubic
  - Ventral hernia lateral → L1 subcostal, L2 flank, L3 iliac, L4 lumbar
- Nyhus
  - I → indirect, normal ring
  - II → indirect, enlarged ring
  - IIIa → direct, posterior wall defect
  - IIIb → indirect + posterior wall defect (pantaloon)
  - IIIc → femoral
  - IV → recurrent
