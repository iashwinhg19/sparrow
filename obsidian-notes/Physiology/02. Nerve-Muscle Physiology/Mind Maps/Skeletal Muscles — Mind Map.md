# Skeletal Muscles

## Muscle types and coverings
- Types → smooth, cardiac, skeletal
- Function → contraction & relaxation
- Connective tissue coverings
  - Epimysium → overall outer covering
  - Perimysium → covering of fascicle
  - Endomysium → covering of muscle fibre
- Muscle fibre cell membrane → sarcolemma

## Sarcomere
### Functional unit
- Sarcomere = functional unit of skeletal muscle
- 1 sarcomere = area between 2 Z lines

### Proteins
- Actin
  - Thin filament
  - Made of G-actin
- Myosin
  - Thick filament
  - 2 heavy chains + 4 light chains
  - 1.6 µm fixed length
  - Head region → ATPase activity

### Microscopic appearance
- A band → contains myosin
- I band → contains actin
- H band → no overlap of actin & myosin
- Z line → boundary
- M line → middle

### Accessory proteins
- Titin
  - Z line → M line
  - Spring-like appearance
  - Elastic support
- Myomesin → attaches myosin to M line
- α-actinin → attaches actin to Z line
- Nebulin
  - Follows entire length of actin
  - Molecular ruler
  - Regulates length of actin

### Mechanism of contraction
- Sarcomere shortens
- A band constant
- H zone shortens
- I band shortens
- Actin–myosin overlap ↑

## Skeletal muscle proteins
### Types
- Contractile → actin, myosin
- Supporting → desmin, titin, dystrophin
- Regulatory → troponin C, I, T
- Relaxation → SERCA (SR Ca2+ ATPase)
  - Ca2+ channel protein (page)
  - Activation → muscle relaxation

### Supporting proteins
- Desmin
  - Intermediate filament
  - Tumour marker for sarcomas
  - Connects Z line to sarcolemma
- Titin
  - Giant / largest protein
  - Elasticity & passive tension
  - Scaffolding protein
- Dystrophin
  - Dystrophin glycoprotein complex
  - Connects sarcolemma to actin
  - Laminin → α-dystroglycan → β-dystroglycan
  - Sarcoglycan, sarcospan in sarcolemma
  - Syntrophin with dystrophin
  - Gene mutation → Duchenne muscular dystrophy
  - → respiratory muscle paralysis → death

### Regulatory proteins
- Troponin
  - Troponin C → binds Ca2+
  - Troponin I → inhibitory
  - Troponin T → binds tropomyosin
- Tropomyosin
  - Rope-like protein
  - Covers myosin binding sites on actin
- Resting state → binding sites covered
- Contraction state
  - Ca2+ binds troponin
  - → myosin binding site exposed
  - → actin takes part in contraction

## Synaptic events in muscle contraction
### Excitation–contraction coupling
- Action potential → electrical event
- Contraction → mechanical event
- Linked by Ca2+-mediated coupling
- Motor units → one neuron + its muscle fibres

### Pre- and post-synaptic events
- AP arrives (RMP −70 mV)
- Na+ influx & K+ efflux
- Potential to −40 mV
- Sensed by Ca2+ channels → Ca2+ influx
- Exocytosis of ACh vesicles
- ACh binds nicotinic ACh receptors
- Nicotinic receptor activated → Na+ influx
- End-plate potential (graded)
- Summation crossing threshold → action potential
- Muscle contraction

### Channel legend
- Blue → voltage-gated Na+ channel
- Orange → K+ channel
- Green → voltage-gated Ca2+ channel
- Yellow → nicotinic ACh receptor

### Neuromuscular toxins
- Tetrodotoxin
  - Pufferfish
  - Weakness / paralysis → death
- Dendrotoxin → black mamba snake
- Conotoxin → snails

### Disorders of neuromuscular transmission
- Myasthenia gravis
  - Autoantibodies vs nicotinic ACh receptors
  - Postsynaptic
- Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome
  - Autoantibodies vs voltage-gated Ca2+ channels
  - Presynaptic
- Miniature end-plate potential → spontaneous (quantal) ACh release

## Muscle contraction
### Sequence of events
- 1 Action potential
- 2 Conformational change (lock & key)
- 3 Ca2+ efflux from SR
- 4 Ca2+ + troponin
- 5 Myosin head binds actin
- 6 Muscle contraction
- Sarcoplasmic reticulum → Ca2+ storage
- SERCA pump → Ca2+ back into SR → relaxation, Ca2+ recycled

### Ca2+ receptors
- DHPR → dihydropyridine receptor (T-tubule, key)
- RyR → ryanodine receptor (SR, lock)
- Mechanical interaction

### Applied aspect
- DHPR blockers → calcium channel blockers → nifedipine
- RyR blockers → dantrolene sodium
  - Used in malignant hyperthermia
  - Overactive RyR → ↑↑ Ca2+ release
  - → sustained contraction → ↑↑ body heat
- Brody's myopathy
  - SERCA pump coded by ATP2A1 gene
  - ATP2A1 mutation → impaired relaxation
  - → muscle cramps following exercise

### Molecular mechanism
- 1 ATP binds myosin head (ATPase activity, 45°)
- 2 ATP hydrolysis → ADP + Pi (90°)
- Ca2+ → exposes binding site
- 3 Cross-bridge formation → actin–myosin interaction
- 4 Power stroke → sliding of head → releases ADP + Pi
- 5 New ATP → detachment of myosin head
- Cross-bridge cycling + power stroke → enhanced contraction
