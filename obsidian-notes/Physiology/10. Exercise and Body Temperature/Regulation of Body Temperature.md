# 🌡️ Regulation of Body Temperature

> [!abstract] Core Definition
> Humans are **homeotherms**: the hypothalamus[^ety-hypothalamus][^pre-hypo] holds core temperature at a **set point** (36.8 ± 0.4 °C) by balancing **heat generation** (metabolism, shivering, vasoconstriction) against **heat loss** (evaporation, conduction, convection, radiation). The chapter answers:
> - ✅ **How is temperature sensed and controlled?** — TRP channels → anterior hypothalamus (heat) / posterior hypothalamus (cold), plus feedforward from the shell.
> - ✅ **What is fever?** — pyrogens raise the set point via PGE2 at the OVLT; the controller works normally around a new target.
> - ✅ **What happens when control fails or is overwhelmed?** — the heat disorders (cramps → syncope → exhaustion → heat stroke → malignant hyperthermia) and the cold disorders (hypothermia, accidental vs therapeutic).

---

## 🧠 Mental Model — A Home Thermostat

The hypothalamus is a **thermostat with a dial**. The skin and core are its **thermometers** (TRP channels). Below the dial setting it switches on the **furnace** (shivering, vasoconstriction — posterior hypothalamus); above it, the **air-conditioner** (sweating, vasodilation — anterior hypothalamus). **Fever** = somebody (pyrogens → PGE2) **turned the dial up** — the thermostat is fine, the furnace runs until the new setting is reached. **Heat stroke** = the **thermostat itself has broken** (no sweating). **Malignant hyperthermia** = the **furnace (muscle) has jammed on** regardless of the thermostat. **Therapeutic hypothermia** = deliberately turning the house down to save the brain and heart.

```
SENSE      TRP channels — heat: TRPV1, TRPA1, TRPM2, TRPM3 · cold: TRPM8 (menthol)
CONTROL    Hypothalamus — ANTERIOR (heat → sweat, vasodilate, thirst) · POSTERIOR (cold → shiver, vasoconstrict, ↑ micturition)
MAKE HEAT  metabolism (indirect calorimetry, RQ: CHO 1 · protein 0.8 · fat 0.7 · mixed 0.82)
LOSE HEAT  evaporation · conduction · convection · radiation
FEVER      pyrogens → OVLT (outside BBB) → PGE2 → EP3 → ↑ cAMP → set point ↑ · Rx antipyretics
FAILURE    heat: cramps → syncope → exhaustion → STROKE (no sweat) → MALIGNANT HYPERTHERMIA (RyR)
           cold: hypothermia — accidental / therapeutic (CBF ↓ 7 % per °C; cardiomyocyte cooling → RISK → Akt)
```

## 📖 Introduction

| Type | Definition | Example |

**Normal body temperature:**
- **36.8 ± 0.4 °C**
- **98.2 ± 0.7 °F**[^1]

**Changes with circadian**[^ety-circadian][^pre-circa] **rhythm:**
- **Lowest: 6 AM.**
- **Highest: 4–6 PM.**

| For heat | For cold |

| **TRPA1** | — |
| **TRPM2** | — |
| **TRPM3**[^2] | — |

**Unit of heat measurement: calories**[^ety-calorie]**.**
- **1 Calorie: amount of heat required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of water by 1 °C.**[^3]

**Pathology:**
- **Excessive heat → protein denaturation**[^ety-denaturation][^pre-de][^suf-ation] **→ loss of enzyme**[^ety-enzyme] **function.**
- **Excessive cold → mechanical tissue damage by ice crystals.**

## 🔥 Sources of Heat Generation

**Metabolism**[^ety-metabolism][^pre-meta][^suf-ism]**:** **breakdown of food with O₂ consumption → generates heat** (measured by **indirect calorimetry**[^ety-calorimetry][^suf-metry]).[^4]

```latex
\text{Respiratory Quotient (RQ)} = \frac{\text{Volume of CO}_2\ \text{produced}}{\text{Volume of O}_2\ \text{consumed}}
```

**Respiratory quotient**[^ety-quotient] **(RQ)** = volume of CO₂ produced ÷ volume of O₂ consumed.

| Nutrients | RQ |
|---|---|
| **Carbohydrates** | **1** |
| **Proteins** | **0.8** |
| **Fats** | **0.7** |
| **Mixed diet** | **0.82** |

> [!info] Respiratory exchange ratio (RER)
> **RER: equal to RQ at steady state** — **all the CO₂ produced is expelled by the lungs.**[^5]

### Feedforward[^ety-feedforward] / Anticipatory[^ety-anticipatory][^pre-ante] control

The **skin (shell)** cools long before the **core** does; the hypothalamus reads that early fall and starts heat production **before** the core temperature has dropped — feedforward control.

```
Skin: SHELL temperature
      ↓
Reduction
      ↓
Anticipation of ↓ in CORE temperature   ◄── internal organs: core temperature
      ↓
PREMATURE ↑ of core temperature
```

- **Skin: shell temperature** → **reduction** → **anticipation of ↓ in core temperature** → **premature ↑ of core temperature**.
- **Internal organs: core temperature.**

## 🤒 Fever[^ety-fever]

- **Body's defence mechanism enhanced by ↑ in temperature.**
- **Pyrogens**[^ety-pyrogen][^pre-pyro][^suf-gen]**: alteration of hypothalamic set point by pyrogens (fever-causing agents).**

```
                     PYROGENS
           ┌────────────┴─────────────┐
   EXOGENOUS PYROGENS          ENDOGENOUS PYROGENS
   • Endotoxins of             • IL-1     • TNF
     gram −ve bacteria         • IL-6
                               • INF-α (used in Rx of hepatitis)
```

| Exogenous[^ety-exogenous][^pre-exo] pyrogens | Endogenous[^pre-endo] pyrogens |
|---|---|
| **Endotoxins**[^ety-endotoxin] **of gram**[^ety-gram] **−ve bacteria** | • **IL-1**[^ety-interleukin][^pre-inter]<br>• **IL-6**<br>• **TNF**[^ety-tnf]<br>• **INF-α**[^ety-interferon] **(used in Rx of hepatitis**[^ety-hepatitis][^suf-itis]**)**[^9] |

**Pyrogens cannot cross blood brain barrier (BBB).**
- **Act on areas outside BBB like organum vasculosum laminae terminalis (OVLT)**[^ety-ovlt] **close to hypothalamic temperature regulation area.**
- **Produce prostaglandin**[^ety-prostaglandin] **E2 —EP3 receptor→ ↑ cAMP**[^ety-camp][^pre-mono] **→ fever, arthritis**[^ety-arthritis]**, myalgia**[^ety-myalgia][^pre-myo][^suf-algia]**.**[^10]
- **Rx: antipyretics**[^ety-antipyretic][^pre-anti] **(↓ PGE2).**

```
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
Prostaglandin E2 ──EP3 receptor──► ↑ cAMP
        ↓
↑ hypothalamic set point → FEVER (+ arthritis, myalgia)
        ↑

```

> [!danger] The line that matters
> Heat cramps, syncope and exhaustion are failures of **water, salt and circulation** — the **thermoregulatory mechanism is intact**, so they recover with rest and fluids. **Heat stroke** is failure of the **thermostat itself** — **sweating is absent**, the skin is hot and dry, the patient is comatose with a rectal temperature > 106 °F: **active cooling at once**.[^11] **Malignant hyperthermia** is a drug-triggered runaway of **muscle heat production** (ryanodine receptor → Ca²⁺ → sustained contraction) — treated with **dantrolene**.[^12]

## Core Dictum & Pattern Recognition

> [!check]+ **🎯 Core Dictum - The Unifying Principle**
>
> Body temperature is a **negative-feedback loop around an adjustable set point**: TRP-channel sensors report to the hypothalamus, which compares the reading with its set point (≈ 36.8 °C) and drives heat loss (anterior: sweating, vasodilation) or heat gain (posterior: shivering, vasoconstriction); feedforward from the shell lets it act before the core changes. Every disorder in the chapter is one of three failures: the **set point is moved** (fever — pyrogens → PGE2 at the OVLT), the **load exceeds an intact controller** (heat cramps, syncope, exhaustion; accidental hypothermia), or the **controller or effector itself fails** (heat stroke — no sweating; malignant hyperthermia — muscle RyR runaway).
>
> That is why antipyretics work in fever (they lower the set point) but not in heat stroke (the set point is normal — the body must be cooled physically), and why dantrolene, not paracetamol, treats malignant hyperthermia.

> [!example]+ **📊 Clinical Pattern Recognition**
>
> Understanding how the condition progresses through stages helps you recognize it at different phases and predict outcomes.
>
> **Stage 1 - Normal Physiology:**
> Core temperature 36.8 ± 0.4 °C (98.2 ± 0.7 °F), lowest at 6 AM and highest at 4–6 PM. Metabolism generates heat (RQ 0.82 on a mixed diet); evaporation, conduction, convection and radiation remove it; the hypothalamus matches the two.
>
> **Stage 2 - Compensated/Early Pathology:**
> In heat the controller works hard but correctly: profuse sweating loses electrolytes (heat cramps), vasodilation pools blood in the legs (heat syncope), extensive vasodilation lowers diastolic filling (heat exhaustion) — all with an intact thermoregulatory mechanism and normal or mildly raised temperature. In fever the set point is raised: chills and shivering (B) until the new set point (C), then sweating (D) when the pyrogen is removed.
>
> **Stage 3 - Decompensated/Overt Disease:**
> In heat stroke the thermoregulatory mechanism fails: sweating stops, the skin is hot and dry, rectal temperature exceeds 106 °F, coma follows and proteins denature (loss of enzyme function). In malignant hyperthermia succinylcholine or halothane opens a defective ryanodine receptor, Ca²⁺ floods the muscle, sustained contraction generates runaway heat. In severe cold, ice crystals cause mechanical tissue damage.
>
> **Progression Timeline:**
> Normal set point → heat load (or pyrogen / trigger drug) → compensated loss (sweat, vasodilation) → water–salt–circulatory failure (cramps, syncope, exhaustion) → thermoregulatory failure (heat stroke) → protein denaturation, coma, death
>
> **Why This Matters Clinically:**
> Distinguishing "intact controller" from "failed controller" decides treatment: rest and fluids for cramps, syncope and exhaustion; immediate active cooling for heat stroke; dantrolene and stopping the trigger for malignant hyperthermia; antipyretics only for fever. Controlled cooling (therapeutic hypothermia) is used deliberately to protect brain and heart.

> [!warning]+ **🔍 Diagnostic Buzzwords & Pattern Recognition**
>
> These are the key terms, phrases, and clinical patterns that should immediately trigger specific diagnoses in your mind. When you see these in a clinical vignette or real patient, you should think of the associated condition instantly.
>
> **Buzzwords by Condition:**
>
> **Heat cramps:** "profuse sweating", "muscle cramps", "loss of electrolytes" → replace electrolytes; very mild.
>
> **Heat syncope:** "prolonged standing in a hot environment", "venous pooling", "fainted, recovered on lying down" → mild, spontaneous recovery.
>
> **Heat exhaustion / collapse:** "extensive vasodilation", "↓ diastolic filling", "loss of consciousness", thermoregulation intact → lie down + fluids.
>
> **Heat stroke:** "hot dry skin", "coma", "rectal temperature > 106 °F", "sweating absent", "old age (classic) or young athletes/soldiers (exertional)" → medical emergency, active cooling.
>
> **Malignant hyperthermia:** "after succinylcholine / halothane", "muscle rigidity", "ryanodine receptor", "↑↑ calcium" → dantrolene sodium.
>
> **Fever:** "IL-1, IL-6, TNF, INF-α", "OVLT", "PGE2 via EP3", "set point raised" → antipyretics.
>
> **Visual/Imaging Buzzwords:**
> The set-point curve: a rising limb with shivering (B), a plateau at the new set point (C), a falling limb with sweating (D). A mid-sagittal MRI with dots at the lamina terminalis (OVLT), subfornical organ, median eminence, neurohypophysis, pineal and area postrema = the circumventricular organs.
>
> **Severity Indicators:**
> Heat disorders in order: very mild (cramps) → mild (syncope) → exhaustion → medical emergency (heat stroke) → most severe (malignant hyperthermia). Therapeutic hypothermia: CBF ↓ 7 % per 1 °C; cardiomyocyte cooling by 5 °C.

> [!danger]+ **⚠️ Common Traps & Pitfalls**
>
> These are the mistakes that students and clinicians commonly make with this topic. Being aware of these traps helps you avoid diagnostic errors and answer tricky exam questions correctly.
>
> **Trap 1: Treating heat stroke with antipyretics**
>
> **What students think:** High temperature = fever = give paracetamol.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In fever the set point is raised by PGE2, so blocking PGE2 lowers it. In heat stroke the set point is normal — the controller has failed (no sweating) and heat simply accumulates; antipyretics do nothing. Only **active cooling** (cool place, cool water, cold clothes) and fluids work.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Fever = dial turned up → antipyretic; heat stroke = thermostat broken → ice."
>
> **Trap 2: Mixing up anterior and posterior hypothalamus**
>
> **What students think:** The posterior hypothalamus handles heat.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** The **anterior** (preoptic) hypothalamus responds to **heat** → heat-dissipating responses (sweating, vasodilation, thirst); the **posterior** hypothalamus responds to **cold** → heat-generating responses (shivering, vasoconstriction). A lesion of the anterior area gives hyperthermia; of the posterior area, loss of cold defence.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "**A**nterior = **A**ir-conditioner; **P**osterior = **P**roduces heat."
>
> **Trap 3: Thinking pyrogens act directly on hypothalamic neurons**
>
> **What students think:** IL-1 and TNF enter the brain and reset the thermostat.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** **Pyrogens cannot cross the BBB**. They act at the **OVLT**, a circumventricular organ outside the BBB, which releases **PGE2** that diffuses to the nearby preoptic area and acts via **EP3** receptors. Antipyretics (COX inhibitors) work by lowering PGE2.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Follow the chain: pyrogen → OVLT → PGE2 → EP3 → set point.
>
> **Trap 4: Calling heat exhaustion a thermoregulatory failure**
>
> **What students think:** Heat exhaustion is a milder heat stroke with a failed thermostat.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** In heat cramps, syncope and exhaustion the **thermoregulatory mechanism is intact** — the problems are salt loss and circulatory collapse from vasodilation. Only heat stroke shows a "not intact" mechanism with absent sweating.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** Ask "is the patient still sweating?" — yes → exhaustion; dry → stroke.
>
> **Trap 5: RQ values**
>
> **What students think:** Fat has the highest RQ because it releases the most energy.
>
> **Why this is wrong:** RQ is CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ consumed. Carbohydrate already contains much oxygen, so it needs less extra O₂: RQ = 1. Fat is oxygen-poor and needs more O₂: RQ = 0.7; protein 0.8; mixed diet 0.82.
>
> **How to avoid this trap:** "Carbs 1, protein 0.8, fat 0.7, mixed 0.82."

> Temperature control is the textbook negative-feedback loop, and shell-to-core anticipation is one of the chapter's three feedforward examples.
>
> **Related Topic 2: [[Limbic System and Hypothalamus]]**
> The anterior (preoptic) and posterior hypothalamic nuclei are the heat-loss and heat-gain centres; thirst and osmoreceptors live there too.
>
> **Related Topic 3: [[CSF, CBF and BBB]]**
> Pyrogens cannot cross the BBB and act at the OVLT, a circumventricular organ; cerebral blood flow falls about 7 % per 1 °C of cooling.
>
> **Related Topic 4: [[Exercise Physiology]]**
> Exercise raises heat production; skin blood flow rises later for dissipation; exertional heat stroke strikes young athletes and soldiers.
>
> **Related Topic 5: [[Female Reproduction]]**
> Progesterone's thermogenic action raises basal body temperature by 0.5–1 °C after ovulation.
>
> **Related Topic 6: [[Skeletal Muscles]]**
> The ryanodine receptor releases Ca²⁺ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum in excitation–contraction coupling — its overactivity is malignant hyperthermia; shivering is skeletal-muscle heat production.
>
> **Clinical Integration:**
> Fever work-up and antipyretics, heat-illness triage in summer, anaesthetic safety (succinylcholine, halothane, dantrolene), targeted temperature management after cardiac arrest and cooling in neurosurgery all rest on this chapter.
>

## 🏆 Pearls (High-Yield Summary)

> [!success] Must-Know Pearls
> 1. **Poikilotherm** (frog) — temperature follows the environment; **homeotherm** (humans) — constant.
> 2. Normal temperature **36.8 ± 0.4 °C / 98.2 ± 0.7 °F**; **lowest 6 AM, highest 4–6 PM**.
> 3. Sensors = **TRP channels**: heat **TRPV1 (vanilloid), TRPA1, TRPM2, TRPM3**; cold **TRPM8 (menthol)**.
> 4. **1 Calorie** = heat to raise **1 kg** of water by **1 °C**; heat → protein denaturation; cold → ice-crystal damage.
> 5. Heat from **metabolism**, measured by **indirect calorimetry**; **RQ = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ consumed: CHO 1, protein 0.8, fat 0.7, mixed 0.82**; **RER = RQ at steady state**.
> 6. Heat loss: **evaporation (sweating), conduction (colder objects), convection (air currents), radiation (electromagnetic waves)**.
> 7. **Anterior hypothalamus → heat → sweating, vasodilation, ↑ thirst (dissipation); posterior → cold → shivering, vasoconstriction, ↑ micturition (generation).**
> 8. Feedforward: **↓ shell temperature → anticipation of ↓ core → premature ↑ of core temperature**.
> 9. Fever: pyrogens alter the set point — exogenous **endotoxin**, endogenous **IL-1, IL-6, TNF, INF-α**; **cannot cross BBB → OVLT → PGE2 → EP3 → ↑ cAMP**; **antipyretics ↓ PGE2**.
> 10. Heat disorders: **cramps (electrolyte loss) → syncope (venous pooling) → exhaustion (↓ diastolic filling) → heat stroke (sweating absent, hot dry skin, coma, rectal > 106 °F, thermoregulation not intact, active cooling) → malignant hyperthermia (succinylcholine, halothane; RyR → ↑↑ Ca²⁺; dantrolene)**.
> 11. Hypothermia: accidental (cold, **barbiturates**) vs therapeutic (**CBF ↓ 7 % per 1 °C**; neurosurgery; cardiomyocyte cooling).
> 12. **Cardiomyocyte cooling (↓ 5 °C) → RISK → survival kinase → Akt phosphorylation (+ HSP27) → mitochondrial protection → ↑ survival** after MI/ROSC.

[^1]: Oral temperature; rectal (core) temperature is about 0.5 °C higher. Guyton gives the normal range as ~36–37.5 °C (96.8–99.5 °F); the page's 36.8 ± 0.4 °C is the classic exam value.
[^2]: TRPV1 opens above ~43 °C and with capsaicin (chilli); TRPM8 below ~25 °C and with menthol; TRPM2 and TRPM3 sense warmth/noxious heat. The page lists TRPA1 with heat; many texts (e.g. Ganong) place **TRPA1** with **noxious cold** (< ~17 °C) and irritants such as mustard oil — the page's grouping is kept in the body.
[^3]: This "large" Calorie (capital C) is the kilocalorie used in nutrition; the small calorie raises 1 g of water by 1 °C. 1 kcal = 4.184 kJ.
[^4]: *Direct* calorimetry measures the heat given off in an insulated chamber; *indirect* calorimetry measures O₂ consumption and uses the energy equivalent of oxygen (~4.82 kcal per litre O₂ on a mixed diet) to calculate heat production.
[^5]: RER is measured at the mouth (CO₂ out ÷ O₂ in); it equals the cellular RQ only at steady state. Hyperventilation, metabolic acidosis or heavy exercise (buffering of lactic acid releases extra CO₂) push RER above 1 even though the cells' RQ is not.
[^6]: At rest in a room, Guyton's proportions of heat loss are roughly: radiation ~60 %, evaporation ~22 %, conduction to air ~15 % (with convection carrying it away), conduction to objects ~3 %. When air is hotter than skin, evaporation is the only route left.
[^7]: Standard texts name the **preoptic / anterior hypothalamus** as the heat-loss centre and the **posterior hypothalamus** as the heat-gain centre. Cold-induced ↑ micturition (*cold diuresis*) follows vasoconstriction: the central blood volume rises and ADH falls.
[^8]: In fever the B limb is the *chill* (shivering, cold skin, piloerection although the temperature is rising) and the D limb the *crisis* or *flush* (sweating, hot skin) when the cause is removed and the set point falls.
[^9]: The page writes "INF-α"; the standard abbreviation is **IFN-α** (interferon-α), used therapeutically in chronic hepatitis B and C, where fever and flu-like symptoms are its typical side effects.
[^10]: Molecular detail differs between sources: EP3 receptors on preoptic neurons are largely Gi-coupled, and PGE2 is often described as lowering cAMP to inhibit warm-sensitive neurons; many review and exam texts write "PGE2 → ↑ cAMP → ↑ set point", which is the page's version and is kept. PGE2 at peripheral sites also causes the arthralgia and myalgia of fever.
[^11]: Current definitions of heat stroke use a core temperature > 40 °C (104 °F) with CNS dysfunction; patients with *exertional* heat stroke may still be sweating. The page's classic triad (hot dry skin, coma, rectal > 106 °F) is the traditional exam description.
[^12]: Malignant hyperthermia is usually autosomal dominant (mutations of the skeletal-muscle ryanodine receptor RYR1); triggers are volatile anaesthetics (halothane and others) and succinylcholine. Signs: masseter/muscle rigidity, rising end-tidal CO₂, tachycardia, hyperthermia. Dantrolene blocks RyR1 Ca²⁺ release.
[^13]: Cerebral metabolic rate and blood flow fall by roughly 6–7 % for each 1 °C of cooling, which lowers O₂ demand and protects neurons; targeted temperature management after cardiac arrest keeps the core at 32–36 °C (a 1–5 °C reduction).
[^14]: The RISK pathway is the group of pro-survival kinases (PI3K–Akt and MEK1/2–ERK1/2) activated at reperfusion; they keep the mitochondrial permeability transition pore closed — the "mitochondrial protection" of the page — and reduce infarct size.

[^ety-electrolyte]: **electrolyte** — Greek *ēlektron*, "amber" (→ electricity) + *lytos*, "loosened, dissolved" → a substance that dissolves into electrically charged ions (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻); named by Michael Faraday (1834).
[^ety-ryanodine]: **ryanodine receptor (RyR)** — named after *ryanodine*, an alkaloid from the plant *Ryania speciosa*, which binds the channel; RyR1 is the Ca²⁺-release channel of skeletal-muscle sarcoplasmic reticulum.

[^ety-rectal]: **rectal / rectum** — Latin *rectum (intestinum)*, "straight (intestine)" → the straight last part of the large gut; rectal temperature is the practical measure of core temperature.
[^ety-dantrolene]: **dantrolene** — a coined pharmaceutical name (hydantoin-derived structure, *-lene*) with no classical root → a direct-acting skeletal-muscle relaxant that blocks RyR1 Ca²⁺ release; the specific antidote for malignant hyperthermia.

[^ety-reperfusion]: **reperfusion** — Latin *re-*, "again" + *per-*, "through" + *fundere*, "to pour" + *-ion* → pouring blood through again after a blockage; *reperfusion injury* = damage caused by the return of O₂ (free radicals, Ca²⁺ overload).
[^ety-kinase]: **kinase** — Greek *kinein*, "to move" + *-ase*, enzyme → an enzyme that moves a phosphate group from ATP onto a substrate. **Differentiate:** *kinase* (adds phosphate) · *phosphatase* (removes it) · *phosphorylase* (adds inorganic phosphate to break a bond, e.g. glycogen phosphorylase).
[^ety-phosphorylation]: **phosphorylation** — *phosphoryl* (the PO₃ group; Greek *phōsphoros*, "light-bearer") + *-ation* → adding a phosphate group to a protein to switch it on or off; Akt is activated by phosphorylation.
[^ety-mitochondria]: **mitochondrion** — Greek *mitos*, "thread" + *chondrion*, "little grain" → the thread-grain organelle (named by Carl Benda, 1898); its permeability-transition pore opening at reperfusion kills the cell, which the RISK pathway prevents.
[^pre-hypo]: **hypo-** — under, below, deficient: hypothalamus, hypothermia. Don't confuse with **hyper-** over · **hypno-** sleep · **sub-** below (Latin equivalent).
[^pre-poikilo]: **poikilo-** — varied, irregular: poikilotherm, poikilocytosis (red cells of varied shapes). Don't confuse with **pleo- / poly-** many · **homeo-** similar.
[^suf-therm]: **-therm / -thermia / -thermy** — heat: poikilotherm, homeotherm, hypothermia, hyperthermia, diathermy. Don't confuse with **thermo-** as a prefix (thermogenesis) · **-therapy** treatment.
[^pre-homeo]: **homeo- / homoeo-** — similar, like: homeotherm, homeostasis. Don't confuse with **homo-** same, identical (homozygous) · **hetero-** different.
[^pre-circa]: **circa- / circum-** — about, around: circadian (about a day), circumference, circumventricular (around the ventricles). Don't confuse with **peri-** around (Greek equivalent) · **cirrho-** tawny (cirrhosis).
[^pre-trans]: **trans-** — across, through: transient, transport, transmission. Don't confuse with **trans-** vs **cis-** (same side) in chemistry · **inter-** between · **dia-** through (Greek).
[^suf-oid]: **-oid** — like, resembling: vanilloid, steroid, thyroid. Don't confuse with **-ode** path (electrode) · **-ide** a chemical compound (chloride).
[^suf-ol]: **-ol** — an alcohol (–OH compound): menthol, cholesterol, glycerol, estradiol. Don't confuse with **-ole / -ule** small (arteriole) · **-ose** a sugar · **-one** a ketone.
[^pre-de]: **de-** — away from, down, undoing: denaturation, degeneration, dehydration. Don't confuse with **dis-** apart · **dys-** bad · **di-** two.
[^suf-ation]: **-ation / -ition / -ion** — the process or result: denaturation, evaporation, radiation, micturition. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition · **-ism** a state.
[^pre-meta]: **meta-** — change, after, beyond: metabolism, metaphase, metastasis. Don't confuse with **meso-** middle · **para-** beside.
[^suf-ism]: **-ism** — a state, process or doctrine: metabolism, catabolism, mechanism. Don't confuse with **-osis** a condition · **-ist** a person who does it.
[^suf-metry]: **-metry / -meter** — measuring / measuring instrument: calorimetry, thermometer, spirometry. Don't confuse with **-metra / metro-** womb (endometrium — Greek *mētra*) · **-metric** in *isometric* (equal measure).
[^pre-ex]: **ex- (e-, ef-)** — out of, away: evaporation, exhaustion, excretion. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside (Greek: exogenous) · **extra-** beyond.
[^pre-con]: **con- / com- / co-** — together, with: conduction, convection, constriction. Don't confuse with **contra-** against · **counter-** opposite.
[^pre-vaso]: **vaso-** — vessel: vasodilation, vasoconstriction, vasomotor. Don't confuse with **veno-** vein · **vagus** (wandering nerve).
[^pre-osmo]: **osmo-** — push, osmotic pressure (Greek *ōsmos*): osmoreceptor, osmolality, osmosis. Don't confuse with **osmo-** smell (Greek *osmē*: anosmia) — same spelling, different root.
[^pre-dis]: **dis- (di-, dif-)** — apart, away: dissipation, dissociation, diffusion. Don't confuse with **dys-** bad · **de-** down from.
[^pre-ante]: **ante-** — before: anticipatory (from *ante-* + *capere*), anterior, antenatal. Don't confuse with **anti-** against (antipyretic) · **post-** after.
[^pre-pyro]: **pyro- / pyr-** — fire, fever: pyrogen, pyrexia, antipyretic. Don't confuse with **pyo-** pus (pyogenic) · **pyelo-** renal pelvis (pyelonephritis).
[^suf-gen]: **-gen / -genic** — producing, producer: pyrogen, glycogen, estrogen. Don't confuse with **-genesis** the act of formation · **-gene** a unit of heredity.
[^pre-exo]: **exo-** — outside, outer: exogenous, exotoxin, exocrine. Don't confuse with **ex-** out of (Latin) · **ecto-** outer layer (ectoderm) · **extra-** beyond.
[^pre-endo]: **endo-** — within: endogenous, endotoxin, endocrine. Don't confuse with **exo-** outside · **ento-** within (rare synonym).
[^pre-inter]: **inter-** — between: interleukin, interferon, intercellular. Don't confuse with **intra-** within (intracellular) · **infra-** below.
[^suf-itis]: **-itis** — inflammation: hepatitis, arthritis, meningitis. Don't confuse with **-osis** non-inflammatory condition (arthrosis) · **-algia** pain (arthralgia).
[^pre-mono]: **mono-** — one, single: monophosphate, monocyte, monoamine. Don't confuse with **uni-** one (Latin equivalent) · **poly-** many · **di-** two.
[^pre-myo]: **myo- / my-** — muscle: myalgia, myocyte, myoglobin. Don't confuse with **myelo-** marrow, spinal cord · **myco-** fungus.
[^suf-algia]: **-algia / -algesia** — pain: myalgia, arthralgia, neuralgia; analgesia (no pain). Don't confuse with **-agra** sudden pain/seizure (podagra) · **-dynia** pain (Greek synonym: glossodynia).
[^pre-anti]: **anti-** — against: antipyretic, antibody, anticoagulant. Don't confuse with **ante-** before (anterior, anticipatory) · **counter-** opposite.
[^pre-syn]: **syn- / sym-** — together, with: syncope, synapse, symphysis. Don't confuse with **semi-** half · **sin-** left (*sinister*).
[^pre-hyper]: **hyper-** — over, excessive: hyperthermia, hyperpyrexia, hypertension. Don't confuse with **hypo-** under · **supra-** above (Latin).
[^suf-lyte]: **-lyte** — a substance that can be dissolved or broken up: electrolyte, ampholyte. Don't confuse with **-lysis** the process of breakdown · **-lytic** (adjective).
[^pre-neuro]: **neuro-** — nerve: neurosurgical, neuroendocrine, neuron. Don't confuse with **nephro-** kidney · **neo-** new.
[^pre-cardio]: **cardio-** — heart: cardiomyocyte, cardiology, cardioprotection. Don't confuse with **cardia** (part of the stomach) · **carpo-** wrist.
[^suf-cyte]: **-cyte** — cell: cardiomyocyte, leukocyte, osteocyte. Don't confuse with **-blast** a precursor cell · **cyto-** as a prefix (cytoplasm) · **-cytosis** an increase in cells.
[^pre-in]: **in- (im-, il-, ir-)** — into, in (also "not" in other words): infarction (stuffing into), injection; but *in-* = not in inactive, insipid. Don't confuse with **intra-** within · **en- / em-** in (Greek: embolism).
[^pre-re]: **re-** — back, again: reperfusion, regulation, receptor. Don't confuse with **retro-** backwards · **pre-** before.
[^pre-per]: **per-** — through, thoroughly: reperfusion, perfusion, permeability. Don't confuse with **peri-** around (pericardium) · **pre-** before.
[^suf-ase]: **-ase** — an enzyme: kinase, phosphatase, cyclo-oxygenase. Don't confuse with **-ose** a sugar (glucose) · **-ase** in *phase* (not an enzyme).
