# Male Reproduction

## Sex-determining region of Y chromosome (SRY)
- SRY gene → short arm of Y
  - Pseudoautosomal regions at both tips
  - Centromere between short and long arm
- Testis-determining gene → transcription factors
- Leydig cells → testosterone
  - → Wolffian system stimulation → male organs
- Sertoli cells → anti-mullerian hormone
  - → inhibits female organs development
- Absence of SRY → female

## Puberty
### Pre-pubertal
- Continuous release of GnRH → (–) LH, FSH
- Maintained by GABA, melatonin
### At puberty
- Adequate fat stores → ↑ leptin
  - Leptin → permissive role in puberty
- → ↑↑ kisspeptin
- → GPR54 in GnRH neuron
- → pulsatile release of GnRH
- When GnRH ↓ between pulses → (+) LH, FSH

## Male reproduction
### Anatomy
- Vas deferens, epididymis, testis
- Penis, glans penis
- Bladder, prostate, ureter, seminal vesicle
- Scrotum → 1–2 °C below body temp → favours spermatogenesis
### Spermatogenesis
- Location → seminiferous tubules
- Duration → 74 days
- Type B spermatogonium (2n, stem cell)
  - → mitosis → primary spermatocyte (2n)
  - → meiosis I (reductional) → 2 secondary spermatocytes (1n)
  - → meiosis II → 4 spermatids (1n)
  - → spermiogenesis (differentiation) → sperm
  - → release = spermiation
### Cells in testis
- Sertoli cells
  - AMH → regression of female organs
  - Blood–testis barrier → tight junctions b/w Sertoli cells
  - Nurse cells → nourishment & protection
  - Androgen-binding protein → binds testosterone
  - Aromatase → testosterone → estrogen
  - Inhibin → inhibits FSH
  - Phagocytosis of dead sperms
- Leydig cells → produce testosterone
### Semen
- Sperm + fluid
- Seminal vesicle 60 %, prostate 30 %, vas deferens 10 %
- pH 7.2–7.4
- Sperm count 90–120 million/mL
### Sperm
- Life span → 1–2 days after ejaculation
- Maturation in epididymis
  - ↑ forward motility → CatSper Ca²⁺ channel
  - Maturation of acrosome
- Vaginal secretion → ↑ sperm motility
- Structure (length 55–60 µ)
  - Head → acrosome (hyaluronidase, proteolytic enzymes)
  - Acrosome enzymes → penetrate zona pellucida
  - Head → surface membrane, vacuole, nucleus
  - Neck
  - Body → mitochondria → ATP for motility
  - Tail (flagellum) → motility, microtubules
  - Velocity → 1–4 mm/min
- Capacitation
  - After ejaculation into female genital tract
  - Acrosome reaction → zona pellucida → fertilization
### Fluid component
- Seminal vesicle (60 %)
  - Fructose → energy (GLUT-5)
  - Ascorbic acid → antioxidant
  - Prostaglandin → cervical mucus penetrable
  - Phosphorylcholine → Florence test (seminal stain)
- Prostate (30 %)
  - Fibrinolysin → liquefaction in 15–30 min
  - Acid phosphatase
  - Spermine → Barberio's test
- Vas deferens (10 %)
  - Bicarbonate, phosphate
- Buffers → bicarbonate, phosphate

## Male sexual act
### Erection
- Parasympathetic (S2–S4) → acetylcholine
- NANC system → nitric oxide
  - → dilatation of arterioles
  - → compression of outflow veins
  - → engorgement & erection (via cGMP)
- cGMP degraded by phosphodiesterase
- PDE inhibitor (sildenafil) → ↑ cGMP → erection
### Ejaculation
- Sympathetic (T12–L2)
- Emission → semen into urethra
- Ejaculation → semen out of urethra
### Resolution (detumescence)
- Endothelin & noradrenaline
- → constriction of arterioles → resolution

## Regulation of male reproduction
### Two cell two gonadotrophin model
- Hypothalamus → GnRH → (+) anterior pituitary
- LH → (+) Leydig cells → testosterone
  - Testosterone → (–) GnRH and LH
- FSH → (+) Sertoli cells → inhibin B
  - Inhibin B → (–) FSH
- Prolonged testosterone → azoospermia (–ve feedback on LH)
### Regulation of spermatogenesis
- Testosterone
- FSH
- LH
- Growth hormone → early division of spermatogonia

## Male sex hormones
### Testosterone → 5α reductase → DHT (more potent)
- Testosterone actions
  - Wolffian duct → male internal genital organs
  - ↑ erythropoietin → ↑ RBC
  - Protein anabolism → ↑ muscle mass
  - Bone growth
  - Initiation & maintenance of spermatogenesis
  - ABP synthesis by Sertoli cells
  - ↑ libido
- DHT actions
  - Puberty-associated changes
  - Masculinization of external genitalia
  - Prostate development → finasteride for BPH
  - Facial hair growth
  - Acne
  - Temporal hairline recession (male pattern baldness)
